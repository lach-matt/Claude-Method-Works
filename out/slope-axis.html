The Slope Axis

The Method 1.6 · §34.4–34.6 · Z = 3 to 108, four edges to 120 · candidate set to ℓ = 4

Every element in the index sits on one axis, and the axis is a — not a fitted constant but a slope. Each candidate subshell is a point in a plane; the incoming electron takes the point a line of slope a touches first, sweeping up from below. An element's place on the axis is the wedge of slopes for which its observed entrant is that first point of contact.

A subshell is a candidate whenever it is not full — that is the whole of the admissibility rule, and it says nothing about ℓ. So the g subshells are points here too, even though no element below Z = 121 puts an electron in one: 5g sits at the origin of the plane, at (0, 5), because a subshell with no radial node has √r = 0. Where it lies on the lower hull it gives the element below it a floor — which is why protactinium's wedge starts at zero rather than running off the edge.

The rule

ν = n − a·√r

with r = p + q/2(2ℓ+1), the node count and the filled fraction of capacity. Least ν takes the electron (§34.4).

The plane

s ↦ ( √r , n )

Minimising n − a·√r is minimising y − a·x. Each admissible subshell is a point; nothing else about it enters.

The axis

a ∈ ℝ — a slope

A line of slope a rising from below stops at one point. Only vertices of the lower convex hull can ever be that point.

What the picture says about state. A single a is a single vertical rule. The rule never crosses all 106 bars — the best it reaches is 86 (node-only) or 90 (finished), and the running intersection empties 14 times, resp. 11. That is what “the state is necessary” establishes: necessary to this rule, so that a may be re-set and remain exact. It is not a claim about the periodic table, and the index does not support one — the memoryless least-(n+ℓ) rule carries nothing and scores 96 of 106, and its pick lies inside the corridor at every step.
Why no fixed slope reproduces Madelung. k = n + ℓ and p = n − ℓ − 1, so k = 2n − p − 1 — and with x = √p, that is k = 2y − x² − 1. In this plane ν sweeps a family of lines and the Madelung rule sweeps a family of parabolas. They agree over most of the occupied region and cannot agree everywhere: the best fixed a matches the k-rule at 88 of 106 (node-only), 91 (finished).
