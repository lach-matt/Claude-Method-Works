    Glossary and keys

    Every term the site uses, in the site's own words. A definition here is a reading aid; the value it describes carries its own status wherever it appears.

    Statuses

    READ
    A value taken as recorded from a source table or paper: a measured level, an observed configuration, a stated figure. Nothing was computed to produce it.
    PINNED
    A standing definition stated at the precision a program needs: the channel equation's coefficients, the Pauli bound, the order operator, the standing caps.
    DERIVED
    Arithmetic on READ or PINNED quantities with nothing added: a count, a difference, a classification by a stated rule.
    RECOVERED
    A form stated nowhere in a program's terms, measured out of the index's own computed column and consistent with what is said of it qualitatively. The collapse ramp C(Z, ℓ) is the one such object here.
    RECONSTRUCTED
    An object whose behaviour is stated but not the form a program needs, rebuilt here and kept marked so a later decision can move it: the ionisation-ladder mapping, the reading of n₀, and the whole of the reconstructed walk.
    a status is never flattened
    A value keeps the weakest status of anything it depends on. A DERIVED count over RECONSTRUCTED rows is shown RECONSTRUCTED, and a solver's result carries the status of its weakest input.

    Cells and channels

    channel
    One ion at one orbital angular momentum ℓ: the series of levels an electron in that ion runs through as its principal quantum number n rises. A channel is addressed as element, ionisation stage, ℓ; for example Fe II d.
    cell
    One row of the spectra index: a channel at one multiplicity, carrying its quantum defect δ, its grade and its bound. Some channels hold several cells because the series splits by multiplicity.
    ionisation stage
    The ion's charge plus one, in spectroscopic Roman numerals: Fe I is the neutral atom, Fe II has lost one electron. The lattice draws the stage upward.
    quantum defect, δ
    How far a series departs from the hydrogenic ladder: the levels sit at −1/(2(n − δ)²) rather than −1/(2n²). A one-electron channel has δ = 0 exactly, by symmetry.
    grade: measured, exact, computed
    Measured: δ read from levels in a compilation, chiefly NIST ASD. Exact: a one-electron channel, δ = 0 by symmetry. Computed: δ from the channel equation, where no measurement is held.
    witnessed
    A cell whose measured δ has an independent second reading in the source data. Drawn with a ring in the lattice.
    bound, and its kind
    The note the spectra index records on each cell about where the series ends or why it cannot be keyed. The note is READ; its kind is DERIVED by a stated rule into eight: series limit printed · series unresolved · nuclear (no isotope or nuclide) · not keyable (no single 2S+1) · open-shell core · no analysis located · δ = 0 by symmetry · no note.
    multiplicity
    2S+1, the spin multiplicity of the term the series belongs to. It is the fourth coordinate of a cell after Z, charge and ℓ.

    The channel equation and its terms

    the channel equation
    The one equation that predicts δ for every channel from the ion's own configuration, in two branches: one where the core holds electrons at this ℓ (p > 0) and one where it does not (p = 0). Its five constants A, K, H, E₀ and E₁ are PINNED. Solver mode 1 evaluates it term by term.
    p
    The number of orbitals the ion's core already holds at this ℓ, read from the observed ground configuration.
    n₀
    The first principal quantum number at this ℓ that the core leaves entirely unoccupied. Its reading is RECONSTRUCTED; the helium s series settles it.
    B, the Pauli bound
    B = min(p, n₀ − ℓ − 1): how many core orbitals at this ℓ lie below the series, and so how many nodes exclusion forces on the running electron. Solver mode 2.
    C(Z, ℓ), the collapse coordinate
    A ramp from 0 to 1 across the eight elements where the ℓ-orbital collapses into the core (opening at Sc for d, at La for f), carried by the p = 0 branch. Its form is RECOVERED from the computed column. Solver mode 3.
    Nₑ and the exponent
    Nₑ is the ion's electron count; the equation's exponent falls as e(Nₑ) = E₀ − E₁ ln Nₑ. At Nₑ = 1 the (Nₑ − 1)/Nₑ factor vanishes and the equation returns 0 exactly.
    residual
    δ measured minus δ from the equation, on a measured cell. The Figures dialog draws every residual; the rms over all measured channels is a fixture the solver selftest checks.

    Layouts and closure

    the drawn periodic layout
    The eighteen-column table, period down and group across, with helium at group 18 and the lanthanides and actinides set aside below. Ninety main-table cells are held.
    ℛ, the order operator
    A cell is admitted when every cell below it on every axis is held: the downward closure of the held set. It is PINNED and computed in the closure solver (mode 4).
    held, admitted, E
    Held: the cells the layout draws. Admitted: the cells ℛ reaches from them. E = admitted − held: the cells the operator admits and the table does not hold. On the periodic layout E = 36; on the Janet layout E = 0.
    the table as a lattice
    The drawn periodic layout with a third axis: group across, period up, ℓ into the page, so each block is a layer. The set-aside lanthanides and actinides, which the layout gives no group, are drawn in their period rows at the long-form table's columns 3 to 16; that placement is DERIVED and the caption says so.
    ghost cell
    One of the thirty-six, drawn tinted in the gaps of the short periods. Tap one for its definition: 1d, 1p and 2d are forbidden by ℓ ≤ n − 1 (lighter tint), 3d and helium's vacated slot are deferred (fuller tint).
    the priced alternative
    Helium drawn at group 2 above beryllium, where its configuration puts it. E falls from 36 to 20, and the plate offers the toggle.
    set aside
    The twenty-eight lanthanides and actinides, drawn below the main table and not counted among its held cells.
    Janet cell, (n+ℓ, ℓ)
    Each element placed by the n+ℓ and ℓ of its differentiating electron: the left-step table. On it the closure is exact, E = 0.

    The lattice

    Λ_spectra
    The spectra index seen as a lattice: every element a slab at its Z, ionisation stage up, ℓ into the page, one node per cell. Rotate by dragging, zoom by wheel or pinch, pan with Shift-drag or two fingers.
    node marks
    A filled sphere is a measured cell, ringed when witnessed; a disc with a green ring is an exact cell; a small grey dot is a computed cell; the selected cell carries the accent ring. Colour by grade or by limit kind from the key.
    Λ₈ and the ionisation ladder
    The eight-coordinate cell (n, ℓ, k, q, e, f, g, 2S) an ion occupies, with seven constraints and standing caps (n, e, ℓ, k, f) = (3, 3, 1, 3, 1). The ladder is the sequence of Λ₈ cells an element's ions step through; the mapping is RECONSTRUCTED. Solver mode 5 tests a cell.
    the tower
    The layers above Λ₈ that the index is indexed by; a source table the site reads for its coordinates.

    The relativistic limit and the walk

    the Löwdin paper
    The released paper that derives the periodic table's filling order from the many-electron equation with one constant, and shows that at c → ∞ eleven elements are misplaced. Read it under Papers.
    the eleven
    The elements the paper's construction displaces when the speed of light is sent to infinity: Mn, Zn, Ag, Cd, Nd, Pm, Sm, Lu, Hg, Lr, Rf. Marked on the table by the relativistic colouring.
    entrant
    The channel the chain adds an electron to on the step from Z − 1 to Z: the deepest unfilled channel in the frozen field of the ion. The walk's entrant is compared with the observed gain from the configurations table.
    displaced
    An element whose entrant differs between the two settings, c = 137.035999 and c → ∞.
    margin
    The energy gap, in hartree, between the entrant and its runner-up. A small margin is a contested row.
    the walk, reconstructed
    The paper's own chain run here in two fields, the paper's average-of-configuration Hartree–Fock field with non-local exchange and a local-exchange field beside it, at both settings, into a 476-row table. Every value of it is RECONSTRUCTED and it is placed beside the paper's result, never in its place. Solver mode 7.
    converged
    Whether the self-consistent field of a row settled to 10⁻⁷. Three Hartree–Fock rows at c = 137.035999 (Ts, Og, Ubn) settled to 10⁻⁶ instead, and the table says so on the row.

    The particle indexes

    particle index
    An index whose members are particles rather than periodic atoms: the fundamental particles, the mesons and the baryons of the Particle Data Group's table, and the quasiparticle indexes as they land. Each declares its coordinates with their statuses, and its cells and closure channel are measured by the instrument that seats it, never here.
    member, cell, drawn position
    A member is one row of the index; its cell is its full coordinate tuple, and several members may share a cell. The explorer draws three coordinates on the axes and a fourth as colour, so a drawn position can hold several cells; members sharing one are fanned out inside it. Which coordinate goes on which axis is a choice of this page, marked DERIVED on the plate.
    Q3, 2J, 2I
    The charge in thirds, the spin doubled and the isospin doubled, each so that every member's value is an integer; the plate and the axis ticks read them back as Q, J and I.
    charted, set aside by name
    A member is charted when every coordinate is printed for it and so lands on a cell; one with a coordinate the table does not print is set aside by name, listed on the index's plate and drawn nowhere. That is a gap in the table, not in physics.
    sub-population, reach
    A sub-population is the members of an index with one coordinate held to one value; the sweep asks of each whether it is closed and which channel it reaches. A reach is a total order the table itself carries (a mass cut, or PDG's status flag) along which a chart is swept to see whether its channel is a property of the data or of the construction; a channel that moves with the reach is seated, and both readings are quoted with their condition.
    nuclear band state
    A nuclear excited state in a magnetic or antimagnetic rotational band, carrying spin I and parity as a particle carries J and P, read from a published data table captured in full. The bands themselves are families of levels and are measured as a sub-population rather than seated. Magnetic rotation (MR) steps by ΔI = 1, antimagnetic rotation (AMR) by ΔI = 2, and the capture checks both against the paper's own selection rule.
    demanded cell, E
    A cell an index's own join-closure demands and no member occupies; E is their count, and it is an upper bound on predictions, because a demanded cell is not a prediction until it is adjudicated. In a particle index's lattice the demanded cells are drawn as hollow ghosts beside the members, tinted by their bin. The element table's ghosts are the same idea under a different operator: an order deficit, where these are join deficits.
    forbidden, unplaced, open
    The three bins of a demanded cell. Forbidden: a bound derived from a stated physical law rules the combination out, so its emptiness is a theorem. Unplaced: the object exists and the source prints no value for one of its coordinates, so the cell is empty because of the source and not because of nature. Open: physical, placeable, and nothing there, the only bin that is a prediction. A fourth, undecided, marks an index whose source does not bank what would settle it.
    free channel
    A channel an index reaches only because a closer is vacuous at its arity: at arity 2 the statistics closer closes every chart, so a K2 there carries no content and the index really closes in nothing. The plate says so rather than banking the channel.
    closure channel K
    The channel the instrument reports for the index's cells under the closure test, with its height and width and the languages that close it; K0 means no language closes it. Read from the instrument, never computed here.

    The site

    edition
    The commit the data was built from, shown after the subtitle and in every citation line. The Provenance panel lists every edition.
    the explorer computes nothing
    Every value the explorer shows was serialised at build from the generator; the browser reads and draws. Only the solver suite computes, and each solver carries a selftest against exported numbers.
    machine check
    What the page does to a model's answer: resolves every cited path against the loaded data, repeats every stated computation with its own library, tallies every chemical equation for conservation of atoms and charge, and counts the numbers left unmarked. A mismatch is reported beside the model's words and never corrected.
    retrieval redundancy, ρ
    How many independent routes carried a fact: the number of open routes among those tried. Two survive the loss of either; one is fragile; none is a stated gap, recorded with the routes tried. A paywall blocks a route, not a fact, and a compilation can carry a better figure than the primary paper.
    route types
    The kinds of route a fact can be retrieved by: the primary paper, a preprint, a review, a compilation or table, a citing paper, a deposit or archive scan, a database record. A source is asked for as a catalogue entry — a DOI, an arXiv number, an archive identifier — not only as a text string.
    ⟦path⟧, ⟪f(args) = v⟫, ⦃equation⦄, ⟨⟨url⟩⟩
    The three markers a model is instructed to use so the page can check it: a citation of a figure by its path in the data, a computation the page can repeat, and a chemical equation the page can balance-check; and ⟨⟨url⟩⟩ after a figure taken from the web names the route that carried it, checked against the searches the API reported.
    reading an ion
    Charges are read in any usual notation: Fe3+, Fe^3+, Fe(3+), Fe+3, Fe³⁺, Fe(III); MnO4-, SO4^2-, SO4-2, SO₄²⁻. One form is ambiguous on its face, a plain digit and sign after a single element symbol: Fe3+ is iron(III), but N3- is azide and I3- triiodide. The chemistry modes read it as a charge, show every reading they made, and where that reading cannot balance and the other can, use the other and say so. The caret or superscript form is never ambiguous.
    the algebraic balance
    How the tenth solver balances an equation: each element and the charge gives one conservation equation over the species' coefficients, and the solutions of that system, found exactly, are every balance the species admit. One solution is the balance; none means the species cannot balance; more than one means they admit more than one reaction, and the mode shows the basis rather than choosing.
    a finding is recorded, never repaired
    Where two sources disagree, both values are shown with their statuses; the site does not choose.

    Navigation

    • Scroll or pinch to zoom, drag to pan, tap a node to fly to it; the detail plate follows the selection. In a lattice view drag sideways to rotate. Every scene opens fitted whole to its canvas; zoom in and, once it is taller than the canvas, a chip at the clipped edge says how many stages are hidden and a vertical drag scrolls it (Shift-drag or two fingers pan freely).
    • With the canvas focused (click or tab to it): Esc up one level · H whole index · + − zoom · arrow keys pan. / focuses search from anywhere outside a text field. Table rows in the detail plate open with Enter.
    • Search accepts a symbol, Z, name, or a path: Fe II, Fe II d, Fe II d 3 — and a particle's name (pi0, mu-, Lambda) or an index's (mesons) once the particle indexes have loaded.
    • The picker at the top left switches the explorer between the elements and each particle index. A particle index opens as its own lattice, three of its coordinates on the axes and one node per member; tap a node for the member's plate, which lists the cell's other members and the neighbours in the table. Its address is #/p/mesons, a member's #/p/mesons/pi0.
    • The address bar carries the path, so any node is a link.

    Query the index

    The console answers deterministically from the loaded data: help, go Fe II d 3, axes, closure, sources, caveats, count, measured Fe, residuals Fe, B Pd, ladder Fe. When the page runs as a claude.ai artifact a second answerer, Claude, is offered beside it and is always labelled; it is instructed to answer only from the selected record and to name the status of every number it quotes.

    Ask a model, with a machine check

    The console answers only from the loaded data. With an API key saved under Ask a model, the same button also sends the question to Claude with web search on. The model is told to search for context and method, to apply them to the data the page hands it, and to mark its work: every figure it takes from the index as ⟦path⟧, every computation as ⟪function(args) = value⟫, every chemical equation as ⦃equation⦄. The page then checks the answer rather than trusting it — each citation is resolved against the loaded data and its value compared, each computation is repeated with the page's own solver library, each equation is tallied for conservation of atoms and charge with every element linked to its record — and the count of numbers the model stated with no marker is reported as unverified. A mismatch is shown beside the model's words, never corrected. The model is also told how to search: enumerate the target facts first; for each list the routes that could carry it by type — primary, preprint, review, compilation, citing paper, deposit, database — and try the open ones first, since a paywall blocks a route and not a fact; read each source for the sources it names; move on from a blocked route rather than retry it; and end with a retrieval table, one line per target and route tried. The page counts the open routes per target as its redundancy ρ — two survive the loss of either, one is fragile, none is a stated gap — and checks every source the model names against the searches the API actually reported, flagging any it never returned. The console command check runs the same checker over any pasted text. The ninth solver mode checks a chemical equation on its own, and the tenth balances one exactly by the algebraic method — conservation of every element and of charge solved in rational arithmetic, with acidic or basic media for redox reactions in water and e⁻ for half-reactions — refusing to invent a species or choose between reactions the species leave open. Nothing here writes a laboratory procedure, by decision.

    If a view does not draw

    The page keeps an account of itself: the browser, the canvas, what is drawn, and the last drawing error if there was one. Copy it and send it with a report; on a phone with no developer console this is the only way to see what went wrong. The same text is the console command diag.
