## PART 0 — THE FOUNDING CHAT

### 1

**THE ORIGIN IS THE CLOSEST MEASURABLE DISTANCE TO ZERO, NOT ZERO — HYDROGEN ANCHORS BY PHYSICAL ARGUMENT.** *At the outset the origin was set not at zero but at the smallest non-zero step outward, because true zero represents the absence of any element entirely — no protons, no electrons, no shell — and is reserved as an unoccupied vacuum point. Hydrogen (1s¹) is placed at the smallest measurable coordinate on all three axes, an argument echoing zero-point energy, where even a ground-state system never sits at zero. This is the conceptual seed later proved as a theorem: that (1,0,1) is the unique minimum of Λ (register 41), reasoned here before it was proved.*
`kind: finding · foundational`

### 2

**THE THREE AXES, FIRST STATED: SHELL NUMBER n, SUBSHELL TYPE ℓ, VALENCE ELECTRON COUNT k.** *The model's three axes of measurement, established at genesis: axis 1 the principal quantum number n (which shell is the valence shell); axis 2 the subshell type ℓ (s, p, d, f, and the theoretically accessible g); axis 3 the electron count k occupying that valence subshell. Set against the conventional table's two axes — periods (n) and groups (a blend of subshell type and electron count) — the departure being that what the table conflates into one positional axis is separated into three.*
`kind: finding · foundational`

### 3

**FAMILIES BECOME SPATIAL NEIGHBOURHOODS: ELEMENTS OF LIKE VALENCE CLUSTER AS REGIONS, NOT COLUMNS.** *The first observed consequence of the coordinate change: elements sharing a valence configuration — the single-s-electron family, the full-p-shell noble gases — cluster as actual spatial neighbourhoods in the lattice rather than sharing a printed column. The seed of the adjacency result (register 21) and the fibre result (register 53).*
`kind: finding`

### 4

**EMPTY SPACE FIRST PARTITIONED — AND THE FIRST CENSUS CARRIED 14 GHOST CELLS AND 78 RESERVED: 118 + 14 + 180 + 240 + 78 = 630.** *The founding classification of unoccupied volume into void (ℓ ≥ n, orbital cannot exist), reactive (electron count exceeds subshell capacity, overflow into a new shell predicted), and reserved (valid but unoccupied). The first census returned 180 void, 240 reactive, 78 reserved, 118 occupied — and a fourth category of 14 "ghost" cells: fifth-shell 5g configurations carried as occupied-adjacent, the actinide series shown to reach into the otherwise-vacant g-subshell layer under excitation. The 14-ghost partition and the 78 count are the first-draft state; the paper folds the ghosts into the reserved count and recounts (registers 11, 12).*
`kind: measurement`

### 5

**THE REACTIVE CELLS' PHYSICAL READING: AN UNSTABLE, ELECTRON-SEEKING OVERFLOW STATE.** *In the founding draft the reactive cells carry a physical claim: they are inherently unstable, electron-hungry states in which surplus electrons overflow the subshell capacity and spill into the next shell. The paper later demotes this from a physical assertion to a reading offered under the framework ("under the reading proposed here"), the strong physical form being the genesis state.*

Recorded here in its original strong form; withdrawn in the paper as overstating what valence configuration can constrain (register 33).*

## PART I — THE CONFIGURATION-SPACE LATTICE

### 7

**THE THREE-AXIS CONFIGURATION LATTICE, PROPOSED AS A THEORY TO EXPLORE.** *Each element is assigned the coordinate (n, ℓ, k) of its ground-state valence configuration — n the principal quantum number, ℓ ∈ {0,1,2,3,4} the subshell s,p,d,f,g, k the electron count in that subshell — treating what the conventional table conflates into one positional axis as three independent axes. No new physics is introduced; only a change of coordinates.*
`kind: finding · foundational`

### 8

**THE ADMISSIBLE LATTICE, DEFINED BY TWO CONSTRAINTS.** *Λ = {(n, ℓ, k) ∈ ℤ³ : 0 ≤ ℓ ≤ n−1, 1 ≤ k ≤ 2(2ℓ+1)}, the two bounds being the angular-momentum condition ℓ ≤ n−1 and the Pauli capacity 2(2ℓ+1) of the subshell. A fifth layer ℓ = 4 (g) is carried as a theoretical extension; no known ground state occupies it.*
`kind: finding · foundational`

### 9

**THE AXES ARE COUNTS, NOT LENGTHS, AND THEY ARE COUPLED.** *n indexes a shell, ℓ indexes angular momentum, k is an electron count, so distances in the lattice are combinatorial and not metric; and the range of ℓ depends on n while the range of k depends on ℓ, making Λ a bounded, interdependent integer structure. This is what later permits the classification of empty space and forces the dimensional obstruction of register 66.*
`kind: finding · foundational`

### 10

**EMPTY SPACE PARTITIONS INTO VOID, REACTIVE, AND RESERVED.** *Every cell not holding a known element falls into exactly one regime: void (ℓ ≥ n, the orbital does not exist and no element can ever occupy it), reactive (ℓ < n but k > 2(2ℓ+1), electron count exceeds Pauli capacity, read as transient overflow states), or reserved (both constraints satisfied yet unoccupied — valid, physically permitted, simply empty). The table has no vocabulary for absence; the coordinate change supplies one. (Formalises the founding partition of register 4.)*
`kind: finding · foundational`

### 11

**THE CENSUS OF THE 630-CELL LATTICE: 180 VOID · 240 REACTIVE · 92 RESERVED · 118 OCCUPIED.** *A full census of the 7 × 5 × 18 lattice returns 180 void and 240 reactive cells — void and reactive together two-thirds of the volume — 92 reserved, and 118 occupied. Reproduced by the classification code accompanying the paper.*
`kind: measurement`

### 12

**THE RESERVED-CELL RECOUNT CORRECTS 78 → 92.** *The founding draft quoted 78 reserved cells, carrying 14 fifth-shell 5g cells as a separate occupied-adjacent "ghost" category. The recount folds those 14 into the reserved count and is exact, reproduced by the classification code: the reserved count is 92, not 78. (Corrects register 4; both states are kept — the withdrawn 78-plus-14 and the corrected 92.)*
`kind: correction`

### 13

**RESERVED CELLS CONCENTRATE AT n = 6–7 IN THE HIGH-ℓ LAYERS.** *The 92 reserved cells are not scattered uniformly: 42 sit at n = 7 and a further 32 at n = 6, overwhelmingly in the d, f and g layers — the region corresponding to elements beyond Z = 118. In the two-dimensional table this is blank space past oganesson; here it is a set of addressed coordinates, each with a defined neighbourhood.*
`kind: measurement`

### 14

**ALL 118 ELEMENTS PLACE, AND THE BLOCK STRUCTURE EMERGES FROM ℓ ALONE.** *Plotting the 118 elements at their ground-state coordinates (n, ℓ, k) with no manual grouping, the block structure emerges from the ℓ-axis by itself and within each block the elements stratify along n by period. The f-block, which the conventional table detaches and prints below, sits in the body of the structure at ℓ = 3.*
`kind: finding`

### 15

**THE THREE-STATE MODEL: A GROUND CELL AND ITS TWO NEAREST EXCITED NEIGHBOURS.** *Each element is modelled as its ground configuration Q₀ = (n, ℓ, k) together with a shell excitation Q₁ = (n+1, ℓ, k) and a subshell excitation Q₂ = (n, ℓ±1, k), with Q₂ taking ℓ+1 where the angular-momentum condition permits and ℓ−1 otherwise.*
`kind: finding`

### 16

**THE LACH ELEMENTAL FIELD EQUATION, WRITTEN AS A NORMALISED SUPERPOSITION OVER Λ.** *Requiring normalisation at all t, ground-state occupancy at t = 0, and sinusoidal evolution at base frequency Ω gives coefficients c₀ = cos(Ωt), c₁ = c₂ = sin(Ωt)/√2; phase-shifting by π/2 gives the emission form, and the two combine to Ψ±Z(t) = √2·sin(Ωt + π/4)·Σ|n,ℓ,k⟩. Designated the Lach elemental field equation (Eq. 4).*
`kind: finding`

### 17

**THE FIELD EQUATION IS A CONSTRUCTION ON THE LATTICE, NOT A SOLUTION OF A HAMILTONIAN — AND IT IS ELEMENT-INDEPENDENT.** *Ω is a formal parameter; nothing establishes a correspondence between Ω and a measurable frequency in any material. The summation carries no element label and the prefactor is common to all, so every element shares one envelope. What distinguishes elements is not Eq. 4 but the displacement vector ΔQ and the spread σ²(t). A caution recorded at the point of proposal.*
`kind: finding`

### 18

**THE DISPLACEMENT VECTOR ΔQ AND THE INTERACTION TERM.** *ΔQ = Q₀ − ½(Q₁ + Q₂), a static vector characterising the geometry of each element's neighbourhood; and the interaction term S_Z(t) = cos(2Ωt)·ΔQ, oscillating at twice the base frequency. The element-specific content of the framework lives here and in σ²(t), geometric quantities read off the lattice.*
`kind: finding`

### 19

**|ΔQ| IS SHARPLY DEGENERATE, TAKING ONLY TWO VALUES.** *Evaluated across all 118 elements, |ΔQ| = 1 for hydrogen and helium and 1/√2 for every other element. The elements are differentiated not by the size of the displacement but by its direction.*
`kind: measurement`

### 20

**THREE GEOMETRIC CLASSES BY THE DIRECTION OF ΔQ.** *Class A is hydrogen and helium alone (n = 1 admits only ℓ = 0, both excited neighbours collapse onto one cell, spread vanishes at maximum excitation). Class B is elements with ℓ+1 ≤ n−1, subshell excitation toward higher angular momentum. Class C is elements with only ℓ−1 available, excitation toward lower angular momentum. The 3d series and the lanthanides fall in Class C; the 4d and 5d series fall in Class B. The classification follows entirely from ℓ ≤ n−1.*
`kind: finding · foundational`

### 21

**THE ADJACENCY THE CONVENTIONAL LAYOUT CONCEALS: YTTERBIUM AND PLATINUM ARE NEIGHBOURS.** *Yb (4, 3, 13) and Pt (5, 2, 8) are maximally separated by the printed table — Yb in the detached lanthanide strip, Pt in period 6 of the main block — yet in the lattice they are close, both in the high-k region of the high-ℓ layers, separated by one step in ℓ and a few in k, with |Q₀| = 13.93 for Yb and 9.64 for Pt against 9.17 for Pd. The late f-block and the late 5d metals form a contiguous region the two-dimensional layout tears in half. The principal structural result of Part I.*
`kind: finding · foundational`

### 22

**HYDRIDE FORMATION ENTHALPY IS CHOSEN AS THE MEASURED TEST QUANTITY.** *ΔH_f in kJ per mole of hydrogen — measured, tabulated, dimensional, independent of the framework, and the quantity governing whether a metal absorbs hydrogen and how strongly it binds it — taken for 39 transition metals, lanthanides and actinides with well-established binary hydride data.*
`kind: finding`

### 23

**THE TWO-TERM REGRESSION IN ℓ AND k.** *Ordinary least squares gives ΔH_f = +136.2 − 89.5·ℓ + 6.4·k (kJ·mol⁻¹ H), both coefficients significant (ℓ at p = 3.1×10⁻⁸, k at p = 8.1×10⁻⁴), in-sample R² = 0.592, leave-one-out cross-validated R² = 0.514. Adding n, |Q₀| or H_cp does not improve the cross-validated fit — the five-term model gains in-sample R² while losing it out of sample, the signature of overfitting. Atomic number alone gives a negative cross-validated R².*
`kind: measurement`

### 24

**BENCHMARKED AGAINST PETTIFOR AND MIEDEMA, THE LATTICE IS OUTPERFORMED.** *Under identical cross-validation on the same 39 elements: Pettifor's single-variable Mendeleev number reaches CV R² = 0.790, Miedema's full form 0.895, against the lattice's 0.514 with two coordinates. Paired Wilcoxon tests on leave-one-out squared errors reject equality against every established scheme, the weakest at p = 0.0003. On predicting hydride formation enthalpy the lattice is substantially outperformed by prior art available since the 1980s, and the paper reports this.*
`kind: measurement`

### 25

**COMBINING THE LATTICE WITH THE ESTABLISHED DESCRIPTORS DOES NOT RESCUE IT.** *Adding ℓ and k to the full Miedema model changes its cross-validated R² from 0.895 to 0.888 — a decrease. Whatever the lattice contributes on this task is already contained in the established descriptors.*
`kind: measurement`

### 26

**PRIOR ART: PETTIFOR'S MENDELEEV NUMBER AND MIEDEMA'S COHESION MODEL.** *Pettifor's Mendeleev number, a one-dimensional chemical scale constructed so structure maps separate cleanly (Pettifor, Solid State Communications 51 (1984) 31–34); and Miedema's semi-empirical model predicting formation enthalpies from an electronegativity parameter φ* and an electron-density term n_ws^(1/3) (Miedema, de Châtel & de Boer, Physica B+C 100 (1980) 1–28).*
`kind: prior art`

### 27

**WHAT SURVIVES THE BENCHMARK: THE OCCUPANCY COORDINATE k IS ORTHOGONAL TO EVERY ESTABLISHED DESCRIPTOR.** *The block index ℓ correlates strongly with every established descriptor (|r| between 0.68 and 0.77), as expected since ℓ is the block label. The occupancy k is uncorrelated with all three: r = −0.05 against the Mendeleev number, +0.14 against φ*, −0.13 against φ n_ws^(1/3), none approaching significance. k carries information the chemical scales do not encode.*
`kind: measurement · foundational`

### 28

**APPENDING k TO PETTIFOR'S SCALE RAISES CROSS-VALIDATED R² FROM 0.790 TO 0.844.** *A gain of 0.054, significant at p = 0.001 by permutation. Smaller but significant gains follow for the quadratic Pettifor form (+0.014, p = 0.029) and the two-parameter Miedema form (+0.009, p = 0.043). Against the full four-parameter Miedema model the gain vanishes (−0.002, p = 0.363), unsurprising since four fitted per-element parameters have already absorbed the available variance. The defensible claim: valence subshell occupancy carries hydride-stability information Pettifor's scale does not encode — a modest contribution to an existing method, not a competitive alternative.*
`kind: measurement · foundational`

### 29

**THE ΔQ DIRECTION CLASSES SEPARATE THE HYDRIDE DATA.** *Class C elements average −61.9 kJ·mol⁻¹ H against −15.7 for Class B, significant at p = 0.010 by Welch's test — the geometric classification of register 20, derived purely from the angular-momentum constraint, distinguishes the measured energetics.*
`kind: measurement`

### 30

**THE ℓ TERM RECOVERS KNOWN CHEMISTRY RATHER THAN REVEALING IT.** *That f-block metals form more stable hydrides than late d-block metals is long-established descriptive chemistry; the ℓ coefficient in the regression recovers it and does not discover it. Recorded so the surviving claim is not overstated.*

### 31

The boundary named here is enforced at register 64–67.*

## PART II — THE LATTICE AS AN ORDERED SET

### 34

**PART II TREATS Λ AS AN ABSTRACT ORDERED SET, WITHOUT REFERENCE TO WHICH CELLS ARE OCCUPIED.** *The shift from what the lattice contains and how well it predicts, to what kind of object it is. These results are structural facts about the coordinate system rather than correlations with data; they do not depend on the origin or on any dynamical reading of the field equation, and several explain why the construction works at all.*
`kind: finding`

### 35

**THE ORDER DIMENSION OF Λ IS EXACTLY THREE.** *The order dimension (Dushnik–Miller) is the least number of linear orders whose intersection reproduces the partial order. Since Λ ⊆ ℕ³ ordered componentwise, its three coordinate projections give dimension at most three; a standard example S₃ among the admissible cells forces it to at least three. Hence dim(Λ) = 3 exactly: no two linear orders can encode the containment relation, and the third coordinate is necessary rather than merely convenient.*
`kind: finding · foundational`

### 36

**THE STANDARD EXAMPLE S₃, EXHIBITED WITHIN Λ.** *The cells a₁ = (7,2,6), a₂ = (5,4,6), a₃ = (5,2,8) and b₁ = (5,4,8), b₂ = (7,2,8), b₃ = (7,4,6) are all admissible, and each aᵢ lies below exactly those bⱼ with j ≠ i. Each pair differs from the base cell (5,2,6) by an increment of two in one coordinate for the minimals and in two coordinates for the maximals, producing the cyclic incidence pattern that cannot be realised by two linear orders.*
`kind: measurement`

### 37

**THE DIMENSION RESULT CONCERNS THE ORDER RELATION, NOT PHYSICAL SPACE, AND THE WITNESS SITS AT ℓ = 4.** *Order dimension is a property of a relation; the axes remain counts of nodes and electrons. The witness occupies g-subshell cells (ℓ = 4) no known element reaches — legitimate for a statement about Λ, whose admissible region includes those cells by construction, but it means the result concerns the lattice rather than the occupied set. The technique is textbook (Dushnik–Miller, Trotter); novelty is not claimed, and the result is offered because it justifies the three-axis construction.*
`kind: finding`

### 38

**PRIOR ART: THE DUSHNIK–MILLER ORDER DIMENSION AND THE STANDARD EXAMPLE.** *Partially ordered sets and their dimension: Dushnik & Miller, American Journal of Mathematics 63 (1941) 600–610; the dimension-theory monograph is Trotter, Combinatorics and Partially Ordered Sets: Dimension Theory (Johns Hopkins, 1992). The standard example Sₙ and its use to force dimension are textbook material.*
`kind: prior art`

### 39

**Λ IS CLOSED UNDER JOIN AND MEET — EXHAUSTIVELY, 21,945 PAIRS, ZERO FAILURES.** *Ordering componentwise, the componentwise maximum and minimum of any two admissible cells are themselves admissible. Exhaustive checking of all 21,945 pairs drawn from the 210 admissible cells returns no failures in either direction. Λ is a bounded lattice.*
`kind: measurement · foundational`

### 40

**THE CLOSURE IS STRUCTURAL: BOTH CONSTRAINTS ARE PRESERVED UNDER JOIN AND MEET.** *Raising n never violates ℓ ≤ n−1; raising ℓ raises the capacity ceiling 2(2ℓ+1) so a raised k stays admissible; lowering ℓ lowers the ceiling but k lowers in step. Both constraints are therefore preserved under join and meet — the closure is a property of the constraint form, not of the particular numbers. Λ has least element (1,0,1) and greatest element (7,4,18).*
`kind: finding · foundational`

### 41

**HYDROGEN AT (1,0,1) IS THE UNIQUE MINIMUM — A THEOREM, NOT A CHOSEN ANCHOR.** *(1,0,1) is the only cell below every other; hydrogen was placed at the origin at genesis as a reference choice (register 1), but it is not a choice. The anchoring of the framework at hydrogen is a theorem about the structure rather than a convention imposed on it — the founding zero-point argument, now proved.*
`kind: finding · foundational`

### 42

**NOTHING THAT WORKS DEPENDS ON THE ORIGIN, AND NOTHING THAT FAILS IS RESCUED BY MOVING IT.** *ΔQ and σ²(t) are coordinate differences, invariant under any translation of the origin; so are the direction classes and the adjacency; order-theoretic facts are preserved since a coordinate shift is an order isomorphism. Only |Q₀|, and hence H_cp, depends on origin placement — and that quantity was shown to have negative cross-validated predictive value under every origin tested.*
`kind: finding`

### 43

**SHELL CAPACITY 2n² IS THE VOLUME OF A HORIZONTAL SLICE OF Λ.** *Counting admissible cells at fixed n: Σ(ℓ=0 to n−1) 2(2ℓ+1) = 2n², so the capacities 2, 8, 18, 32, 50, 72, 98 are the slice volumes. The Pauli capacities of the periodic system are not an input to the lattice; they are what one obtains by counting it.*
`kind: finding · foundational`

### 44

**Λ IS SPERNER THOUGH NOT RANK-SYMMETRIC.** *A graded poset is Sperner when no antichain exceeds the largest rank level. Products of chains inherit it, but Λ is not rank-symmetric (register 47) so the property must be checked. Computing the maximum antichain by Dilworth's theorem, via maximum matching in the comparability graph, returns 15 — exactly the size of the largest rank level, at n+ℓ+k = 11. Λ is Sperner.*
`kind: measurement`

### 45

**THE RANK GENERATING FUNCTION DECOMPOSES INTO SHIFTED q-INTEGERS, ONE PER COLUMN.** *F(q) = Σ_{(n,ℓ)} q^(n+ℓ+1)·[2(2ℓ+1)]_q, with [m]_q = 1 + q + … + q^(m−1), an identity verified symbolically.*
`kind: finding`

### 46

**F(−1) = 0 IDENTICALLY: AN EXACT PARITY BALANCE FORCED BY SPIN.** *Every column capacity 2(2ℓ+1) is even, and [m]_q vanishes at q = −1 whenever m is even, so F(−1) = 0. Λ contains exactly as many even-rank as odd-rank cells: 105 each. The factor of two making every capacity even is electron spin, so the balance is a structural consequence of spin degeneracy and would persist under any extension of the shell or subshell limits. The occupied set inherits it exactly at 59 and 59 (register 57).*
`kind: finding · foundational`

### 47

**Λ IS NOT RANK-SYMMETRIC, AND THE ASYMMETRY HAS A CAUSE.** *The full rank sequence is 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 11, 13, 14, 15, 15, 14, 13, 12, 11, 10, 9, 8, 7, 7, 6, 5, 4, 3, 3, 3, 2, 1 — unimodal with maximum at rank 11, but not palindromic. The asymmetry arises from the interaction of the two ceilings: ℓ ≤ n−1 truncates higher subshells at small n while k ≤ 2(2ℓ+1) allows the tall f and g columns only where ℓ is already large, so the lattice is broader near its base than its summit.*
`kind: finding`

### 48

**THE MADELUNG RULE IS A LINEAR FUNCTIONAL n + ℓ WHOSE LEVEL SETS ARE PARALLEL PLANES.** *The aufbau rule fills subshells in order of increasing n + ℓ, ties broken by increasing n. On the lattice n + ℓ is a linear functional and its level sets are parallel diagonal planes cutting through Λ; the filling sequence of the elements is a sweep of these planes.*
`kind: finding · foundational`

### 49

**THE LATTICE IS COMPATIBLE WITH MADELUNG ORDERING BUT DOES NOT DERIVE IT.** *Sorting the 118 occupied cells by (n+ℓ, n, k) and reading off atomic number reproduces the true ordering with Kendall τ = 0.992; the 28 inversions are the known lanthanide and actinide irregularities where measured ground states depart from the idealised rule. The rule remains an empirical input, imposed on Λ rather than produced by it.*
`kind: measurement`

### 50

**THE MADELUNG SLICES PAIR EXACTLY, EXPLAINING WHY PERIOD LENGTHS REPEAT.** *The volume of each Madelung slice is 2, 2, 8, 8, 18, 18, 32, 32, 50, 50, 72, 72 — equal consecutive pairs. This pairing is the reason the periods of the periodic table repeat their lengths, a regularity the conventional two-dimensional layout must impose by construction and cannot explain. Here it is a counting fact about parallel slices of a three-dimensional structure.*
`kind: finding · foundational`

### 51

**PROJECTING Λ ALONG n + ℓ REPRODUCES JANET'S LEFT-STEP TABLE EXACTLY.** *Janet's blocks are the level sets of the linear functional n + ℓ on Λ, and his left-step table is the two-dimensional projection of the lattice along that functional. This is a correspondence, not an analogy: the two structures contain the same information, Janet's form arising from Λ by a specific and identifiable projection. The left-step table, customarily justified by appeal to the Madelung rule, is shown to be the shadow of a three-dimensional object.*
`kind: finding · foundational`

### 52

**PRIOR ART: JANET'S LEFT-STEP TABLE (1929).** *Charles Janet's reordering of the periodic table by n + ℓ blocks, producing period lengths 2, 2, 8, 8, 18, 18, 32, 32 and placing helium above beryllium rather than above neon (Janet, La classification hélicoïdale des éléments chimiques, Beauvais, 1929). Argued in the philosophy of chemistry to be the most natural periodic form, as following the quantum-mechanical filling order rather than chemical resemblance.*
`kind: prior art`

### 53

**CHEMICAL GROUPS ARE FIBRES OF Λ: FIX (ℓ, k), VARY n.** *Each such fibre is a line through Λ parallel to the n-axis, and the elements on it are precisely a conventional chemical group — (ℓ,k) = (0,1) gives H, Li, Na, K, Rb, Cs, Fr; (1,4) gives O, S, Se, Te, Po, Lv; and so on. The columns of the two-dimensional table are one-dimensional fibres of the three-dimensional lattice, and the periodicity the layout represents by column adjacency is here a statement about parallel lines. A second and independent operation on Λ, distinct from the n+ℓ projection. (Formalises the founding neighbourhood observation of register 3.)*
`kind: finding · foundational`

### 54

**HELIUM FALLS WITH THE ALKALINE EARTHS BY A SECOND, INDEPENDENT ROUTE.** *Seven of the eight main groups are reproduced exactly by fibration. The one departure: helium is 1s², placing it at (1,0,2) on the fibre (ℓ,k) = (0,2) with beryllium, magnesium, calcium and the remaining alkaline earths — not on the fibre (1,6) of the noble gases. This is the Janet placement, reached at register 51 by projection along n+ℓ and here by fibration along n — two independent operations converging on the same contested placement. The philosophy-of-chemistry question of whether helium is better classified by configuration or behaviour is not settled by this.*
`kind: finding · foundational`

### 55

**THE KAINOSYMMETRIC SUBSHELLS ARE EXACTLY THE BOUNDARY DIAGONAL ℓ = n − 1.** *The cells on the diagonal where the angular-momentum constraint is tight are 1s, 2p, 3d, 4f and 5g — the subshells first of their kind, with no radial node. Kainosymmetry (primogenic repulsion) makes a first-of-kind subshell unusually compact and unscreened, producing the first-short-period irregularities, the 3d anomalies relative to 4d and 5d, and part of the lanthanide–actinide divergence. In the conventional table these are scattered exceptions; in Λ they are the boundary — a structural observation locating a known family of chemical anomalies at a single feature of the geometry.*
`kind: finding · foundational`

### 56

**THE CLOSURE OF Λ IS EXPLAINED BY A STANDARD SUBLATTICE ARGUMENT.** *A subset of a product of chains closed under componentwise join and meet is a sublattice, and finite intersections of sublattices are sublattices (Birkhoff 1967). Constraints of the forms xᵢ ≤ xⱼ, xᵢ ≤ c, xᵢ ≥ c, and xᵢ ≤ f(xⱼ) with f monotone non-decreasing each cut out sublattices — what they share is that every coordinate appears with the same orientation on both sides. Direct verification on a 6×6×6 box confirms closure for each such constraint and every pairwise and triple intersection tested.*
`kind: finding · foundational`

### 57

**Λ IS BUILT AS AN INTERSECTION OF SUBLATTICES, SO CLOSURE IS INHERITED NOT COINCIDENTAL.** *Starting from the box n ≤ 7, ℓ ≤ 4, k ≤ 18 (630 cells, trivially a sublattice), the constraint ℓ ≤ n−1 reduces it to 450 cells and k ≤ 2(2ℓ+1) to the 210 admissible cells; each step intersects with a sublattice. The lower bounds n ≥ 1, ℓ ≥ 0, k ≥ 1 are constants and impose no further condition. This is why the results of registers 39–55 hold; no novelty is claimed for the argument.*
`kind: finding · foundational`

### 58

**PRIOR ART: BIRKHOFF'S LATTICE THEORY.** *That a subset of a product of chains closed under componentwise join and meet is a sublattice, and that finite intersections of sublattices are sublattices: Birkhoff, Lattice Theory, 3rd ed. (American Mathematical Society, 1967).*
`kind: prior art`

### 59

**THE HYDROGENIC LABELLING (n, ℓ, m, s) FAILS CLOSURE, AND THE OBSTRUCTION IS |m| ≤ ℓ.** *The founding draft proposed unfolding k into the magnetic quantum number m and spin projection, to admit the SO(4,2) representation theory the three-axis form cannot support. The hydrogenic constraint |m| ≤ ℓ is the conjunction m ≤ ℓ and −m ≤ ℓ; the first is of the permitted form, the second is not, because m enters negated with reversed orientation — raising m makes the left side smaller. Mixed-orientation constraints do not in general define sublattices.*
`kind: finding`

### 60

**THE FAILURE ENTERS AT A SINGLE IDENTIFIABLE STEP: 4,040 MEET FAILURES.** *Building Λ′ = {(n,ℓ,m,s)} constraint by constraint: the hydrogenic box is a sublattice; ℓ ≤ n−1 leaves 450 cells and preserves closure; m ≤ ℓ leaves 330 and still preserves it; −m ≤ ℓ gives the 210 admissible cells and destroys it. Exhaustive checking returns zero join failures and 4,040 meet failures. A concrete instance: (ℓ,m) = (3,−3) and (1,1) are both admissible, their meet is (1,−3), and |−3| > 1 leaves the admissible set.*
`kind: measurement`

### 61

**THE REPAIR RECOVERS CLOSURE BY ABANDONING THE OBJECT THE UNFOLDING WAS FOR.** *Substituting m′ = m + ℓ converts the pair into m′ ≤ 2ℓ and m′ ≥ 0 — a monotone ceiling and constant floor, both permitted — and verification confirms the shifted four-axis set is closed. But m′ is not the magnetic quantum number; it is m displaced by a lattice-varying amount, and the representation theory that motivated the unfolding acts on m, not on a position-dependent shift of it. Closure is recovered precisely by abandoning the object sought.*
`kind: finding`

### 62

**THE FOUR-AXIS (n, ℓ, m, s) UNFOLDING IS WITHDRAWN.** *No four-axis refactoring of Λ both preserves the order structure of registers 39–55 and exposes the hydrogenic labels in a form on which group theory can act. The proposal from the founding draft is withdrawn; the framework cannot be connected to the dynamical symmetry group of the hydrogen atom by any route considered. Whatever Λ is, it is a combinatorial object and not a representation-theoretic one in disguise.*
`kind: withdrawal · foundational`

### 63

**THE OCCUPANCY COORDINATE k IS THE LOAD-BEARING COMPONENT.** *Introduced in Part I as nothing more than the electron count, k is the coordinate that makes the structure work: its bounds carry no orientation reversal, and the results of Part II would not survive its replacement by the hydrogenic labels it conflates. This is the same coordinate that carries information orthogonal to established chemical descriptors at register 27 — the same coordinate does the work in both the order-theoretic and the empirical results.*
`kind: finding · foundational`

### 64

**THE FIELD-EQUATION IDENTITIES ARE EXACT BUT ELEMENT-INDEPENDENT, SO THE SUBSTANCE IS THE LATTICE.** *The identities underlying Eq. 4 are verified symbolically and hold exactly — cos²(Ωt) + 2·[sin(Ωt)/√2]² = 1, and cos(Ωt) + sin(Ωt) = √2 sin(Ωt + π/4). But the summation carries no element label and its prefactor is element-independent, so all 118 elements share one envelope; the expression is a correct normalisation statement about Λ, not a dynamical law distinguishing elements. The substantive content of the framework is the lattice and its order structure; the field equation is the weakest component, and the paper is titled for the lattice.*
`kind: finding`

## PART III — THE PERIODIC SYSTEM WITHIN THE LATTICE

### 65

**PART III ASKS WHERE THE 118 OCCUPIED CELLS SIT WITHIN THE STRUCTURE.** *Of the 210 admissible cells, 118 are occupied — the question is where that subset sits and whether its position has a character of its own. The answer is that it does, and the property follows from the physics of how elements are populated rather than from the geometry of the lattice.*
`kind: finding`

### 66

**THE OCCUPIED CELLS FORM AN ORDER IDEAL OF Λ — DOWNWARD CLOSED WITHOUT EXCEPTION.** *For every occupied x and every admissible y ≤ x componentwise, y is also occupied: no violations across all 118 cells. In chemical terms, if an element has ground-state configuration (n,ℓ,k), then every configuration with no larger shell, subshell or occupancy is likewise realised — there are no gaps beneath any occupied cell. A property that does not appear to have been noted.*
`kind: finding · foundational`

### 67

**THE REALISED IDEAL IS ALL-OR-NOTHING BY COLUMN: 19 SATURATED, 6 EMPTY, NONE PARTIAL.** *Grouping admissible cells into columns indexed by (n,ℓ) of capacity 2(2ℓ+1): of the twenty-five columns, nineteen are filled to full capacity and six are entirely empty, none partial. A partially filled column would correspond only to the transient state between two consecutive elements, never to the accumulated set. This is stronger than downward closure: the realised system is determined by a binary choice per column.*
`kind: finding · foundational`

### 68

**THE ADMISSIBLE CONFIGURATIONS ARE THE 120 DOWN-SETS OF THE COLUMN POSET, AND THE REALISED ONE SITS AT THE 79TH PERCENTILE.** *There are exactly 120 down-sets of the twenty-five-element column poset. The periodic system is one of them, spanning 118 cells, at the 79th percentile by size — among the larger configurations available within a seven-shell lattice, though six columns remain unoccupied.*
`kind: measurement`

### 69

**DOWNWARD CLOSURE IS THE ORDER-THEORETIC SHADOW OF AUFBAU FILLING.** *Elements are populated by adding electrons one at a time, so the sequence of ground-state configurations traces a monotone path through the lattice, and a set generated by a monotone accumulation is closed downward by construction. The property survives the known irregularities: the 28 lanthanide and actinide inversions in the Madelung ordering alter which cell is reached at which atomic number but remove no cell from the accumulated set — ordering is disturbed, downward closure is not.*
`kind: finding · foundational`

### 70

**Λ IS DISTRIBUTIVE WITH 27 JOIN-IRREDUCIBLE CELLS, AND THE PERIODIC SYSTEM IS ONE ELEMENT OF J(Λ).** *Componentwise minimum and maximum on integer tuples satisfy the distributive laws; direct checking confirms Λ is distributive and identifies 27 join-irreducible cells generating all 210. By Birkhoff's representation theorem the order ideals of a finite poset form a distributive lattice, so the periodic system corresponds to a single element of J(Λ), and the historical growth of the known elements is a monotone path upward through J(Λ), each discovery moving to a covering cell. A description, but an exact one.*
`kind: finding · foundational`

### 71

**THE FRONTIER: THREE MAXIMAL OCCUPIED CELLS AND FIVE COVERING ADDRESSES.** *The maximal occupied cells — no occupied cell above them — number exactly three: (5,3,14) lawrencium, (6,2,10) copernicium, (7,1,6) oganesson. Because the occupied set is an ideal it can grow only by acquiring a cell covering one of these, and there are five such: (5,4,14), (6,3,10), (6,3,14), (7,2,6), (7,2,10). Any element beyond those known must occupy one of these five addresses or else extend the lattice beyond n = 7.*
`kind: finding · foundational`

### 72

**THE FRONTIER CONSTRAINS VALENCE CONFIGURATION ONLY — THE SAME LIMIT AS THE RESERVED CELLS.** *The claim says nothing about nuclear stability, synthesis cross-sections, half-lives, or whether a nucleus placed at such an address would persist to be observed, all governed by nuclear structure and not electron bookkeeping. The statement is that if a new element is characterised its valence configuration will be found at one of five coordinates — not that any of the five will be reached. (Same boundary as register 33.)*
`kind: finding`

### 73

**THE LATTICE IS A LOSSLESS RECODING, NOT A COMPRESSION.** *The joint entropy of the coordinates across the 118 occupied cells is 6.883 bits, exactly log₂(118), matching a flat enumeration; the assignment is injective and no information is discarded or created. What the coordinates buy is organisation — the structures of registers 35–71 — and not a reduction in descriptive burden. A caution about interpretation.*
`kind: measurement`

### 74

**THE 210 = C(10,4) COINCIDENCE IS ONLY A COINCIDENCE.** *Λ has 210 admissible cells and 210 = C(10,4) counts partitions in a 4×6 box, hence a Young lattice of that shape — suggestive, but it does not survive. Ranking Λ by n+ℓ+k gives level sizes 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 11, 13, 14, 15, 15, 14 whereas the Gaussian binomial gives 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 9, 10, 13, 14, 16, 16; the two differ from the second rank onward. Λ's rank sequence is not palindromic, and Gaussian binomials, Boolean, divisor and Young lattices all are — so Λ is isomorphic to none of them. Recorded so others do not pursue it.*
`kind: finding`

### 75

**PRIOR ART: BIRKHOFF'S REPRESENTATION THEOREM.** *That the order ideals of a finite poset themselves form a distributive lattice, and that a finite distributive lattice is the lattice of down-sets of its poset of join-irreducibles: Birkhoff's representation theorem, invoked as stated (Birkhoff, Lattice Theory, 1967).*
`kind: prior art`

### 76

**PRIOR ART: LEAL AND RESTREPO'S ORDERED-HYPERGRAPH PERIODIC SYSTEM.** *A formal account of the periodic system as an ordered hypergraph — elements as vertices, chemical similarity classes as hyperedges, order induced by measured properties such as atomic number or electronegativity (Leal & Restrepo, Proc. R. Soc. A 475 (2019) 20180581). It orders elements by properties; the present work orders electron configurations by componentwise comparison of quantum numbers. The two are complementary, and Restrepo's remark — that the periodic tables in circulation may be projections of a single underlying structure — is met from the opposite direction by the Janet projection of register 51.*
`kind: prior art`

### 77

**PRIOR ART: QUINTERO, BRÜGGEMANN AND RESTREPO'S POSETIC COORDINATES, DISTINGUISHED FROM ORDER DIMENSION.** *Posetic coordinates assign each object of a poset the triple of cardinalities of its up-set, down-set and incomparable set, for visualisation in ternary and FOU plots (Quintero, Brüggemann & Restrepo, MATCH Commun. Math. Comput. Chem. 80 (2018) 793–820). Despite the shared vocabulary this is not the order dimension of register 35: their coordinates describe an object's position within a given poset, whereas the present coordinates define the poset. The two notions are independent. Their machinery would answer where the 118 occupied cells sit by up-set, down-set and incomparability profile — a natural continuation this paper does not attempt.*
`kind: prior art`

### 78

**NOVELTY IS NOT CLAIMED FOR THE TECHNIQUES, ONLY THEIR APPLICATION TO THE CONFIGURATION LATTICE.** *The sublattice argument is elementary, the Sperner property is Dilworth, Birkhoff's theorem is invoked as stated, and the dimension argument applies the standard example in textbook manner. Whether the particular results are new to the literature has not been established; a limited search located the works discussed and no closer prior art, but componentwise-ordered configuration posets may have been treated elsewhere. The results are offered as correct and checkable rather than novel, and readers able to identify precedent are encouraged to do so.*
`kind: finding`

## PART IV — AN EXPLORATORY APPLICATION TO DEUTERIDE HOST SELECTION

### 79

**PART IV APPLIES THE ADJACENCY TO DEUTERIDE HOST SELECTION, AND REPORTS THE FAILURE IN FULL.** *An exploratory attempt to apply the lattice to selecting metal hosts for deuterium loading, reporting both what it produced and where it failed. It makes no claim to be a theory of low-energy nuclear reactions, and its ranking is not a physical prediction. Part I is self-contained for readers interested only in established results.*
`kind: finding`

### 80

**THE MOTIVATION: PALLADIUM SITS AT THE EDGE OF THE ADJACENCY REGION, NOT WITHIN IT.** *The 1989 Fleischmann–Pons experiments used palladium and the subsequent literature has been Pd-centred — a reasonable practical choice, not the product of a structural search. If the late f-block and late 5d metals form a coherent region in configuration space (register 21) and Pd sits at its edge, the lattice identifies neighbouring elements a Pd-centred literature would not naturally reach. The question asked is narrow: does the lattice generate a candidate list worth attention?*
`kind: finding`

### 81

**THE HEURISTIC INDEX H_cp, FLAGGED AS A CHOICE AND NOT A DERIVATION.** *H_cp(n,ℓ,k) = √(n²+ℓ²+k²)·(k/2(2ℓ+1))^(ℓ/4): the configuration-space distance |Q₀| times the fractional subshell filling raised to a power increasing with ℓ, the exponent ℓ/4 normalised by the ceiling ℓ_max = 4. Infinitely many monotonic functions satisfy the stated intuitions; ℓ/4 was selected for its normalisation and simplicity, not because the framework requires it. The index is dimensionless, with no interpretation as a rate, energy or probability — a sorting device.*
`kind: finding`

### 82

**THE TWO-FILTER SCREEN REDUCES 118 ELEMENTS TO NINE, AND NEITHER FILTER DERIVES FROM THE LATTICE.** *A chemical filter (the host must form a well-characterised interstitial hydride or deuteride) and a structural filter (FCC taken as the permissive case, since its octahedral interstitial sites are all equivalent by symmetry, six to the unit cell, unlike HCP or DHCP). Both are ordinary crystallography, imported; the lattice contributes only the index by which survivors are sorted.*
`kind: finding`

### 83

**THE NINE CANDIDATES, ORDERED BY H_cp, WITH YTTERBIUM FIRST.** *Ranked: Yb (4,3,13) H_cp 13.18, Au (5,2,9) 9.95, Ag (4,2,9) 9.53, Pt (5,2,8) 8.63, Pd (4,2,8) 8.20, Ni (3,2,8) 7.85, Ir (5,2,7) 7.39, Rh (4,2,7) 6.95, Th (5,3,1) 0.82. Yb has the largest configuration-space distance of any candidate.*
`kind: measurement`

### 84

**THE YTTERBIUM OBSERVATION, WHICH STANDS INDEPENDENT OF THE INDEX.** *Ytterbium forms YbH₂ and YbH₃ under accessible conditions, adopts an FCC structure, is stable and commercially available — and a survey of the deuteride-loading literature turns up no report of its use as a primary host. That a chemically suitable, structurally suitable, commercially available metal appears never to have been tried is a fact about the literature, independent of the index that ranked it, and would remain interesting if H_cp were discarded entirely.*
`kind: finding`

### 85

**THE ORDERING IS NOT A PREDICTION: IT IS A PROPERTY OF THE CONVENTION ADOPTED.** *Introducing a phase term for the geometry of occupied interstitial sites requires a lattice parameter, which may be measured or estimated via a screening model (Slater's rules, or Clementi–Raimondi values, applied to the valence or the bonding orbital) — each a reasonable choice, each yielding a different ordering. Yb ranks first under two conventions and fourth under a third; Pt ranks fourth, sixth and eighth. When a result depends on which of several equally justifiable conventions is adopted, it characterises the analyst's choice and not the structure. Table 2 is one convention's output.*
`kind: finding`

### 86

**TWO INTERMEDIATE STEPS WERE PRESENTED AS DERIVATIONS AND WERE NOT.** *An interaction term of the form γ·Qe ⊗ Qe was described as the natural coupling; it is one choice among many, and alternatives give different functional forms downstream. The exponent ℓ/4 in H_cp was described as the unique form satisfying the stated constraints; it is not unique, and it was selected after the index it appears in had already been written down. Both are recorded here as choices, corrected against the earlier presentation.*
`kind: correction`

### 87

**THE DIMENSIONAL OBSTRUCTION: THE LATTICE PRODUCES PURE NUMBERS, AND FUSION RATES NEED DIMENSIONAL SCALES.** *Each axis of Λ is a count — n radial nodes, ℓ angular nodes, k electrons — so Λ ⊆ ℕ³ and every quantity built from its coordinates is a pure number. Deuteron–deuteron fusion proceeds through Coulomb barrier penetration, probability ∝ exp(−G), the Gamow factor built from reduced mass in kilograms, barrier height in joules, internuclear separation in metres. Converting a dimensionless lattice ratio into a rate requires a dimensional scale the framework supplies none of. The obstruction is intrinsic to the construction, not a gap in it — Λ ⊆ ℕ³ makes it so — and no refinement of the index removes it.*
`kind: finding · foundational`

### 88

**THE OBSTRUCTION IS FATAL, NOT MERELY IMPRECISE, BECAUSE OF EXPONENTIAL SENSITIVITY.** *Supplying a scale from the physical geometry (an interstitial separation of order 10⁻¹⁰ m) fails: the relevant separation for tunnelling is the distance of closest approach, some five orders of magnitude smaller, and any assumed relation between H_cp and that separation is precisely the physical content one is trying to derive. A ratio of ~6 in H_cp is compatible with a rate ratio of unity or of 10²⁰ according to an unconstrained exponent — varying it over a modest range moves the predicted Yb:Pd ratio across twenty orders of magnitude. The prediction is a restatement of the assumption.*
`kind: measurement`

### 89

**PART IV CANNOT YIELD A FALSIFIABLE PREDICTION, BECAUSE NO QUANTITY WAS COMMITTED TO A MAGNITUDE.** *Were ytterbium to show no anomalous behaviour whatever, no quantity in the framework would be contradicted, because none was ever committed to a magnitude. This is stated as a limit of the exploratory work, not disguised.*
`kind: finding`

### 90

**THE SECTION-8 DIMENSIONAL CONTENT DOES NOT SOFTEN THE OBSTRUCTION, BECAUSE OF SCALE SEPARATION.** *It might be objected that the hydride regression already demonstrated dimensional content. It does not help: that regression relates coordinates to bonding energies of order 10⁻¹ eV over distances of order 10⁻¹⁰ m, while barrier penetration involves energies of order 10⁵ eV over separations of order 10⁻¹⁵ m. A correlation at the first scale carries no implication for the second — a six-order gap in energy and five-order gap in length, which is the whole reason cold fusion is difficult. Eq. 7 predicts how much deuterium a metal absorbs; it says nothing about what happens to the deuterons once absorbed.*
`kind: finding`

### 91

**REASONING FROM THREE AXES TO A THREE-BODY PHYSICAL PROBLEM CONFLATES TWO SENSES OF "THREE-DIMENSIONAL".** *A three-body treatment in the exploratory work was motivated by the lattice having three axes. But (n,ℓ,k) are quantum numbers — a shell index, an angular momentum, a count — and their being three in number is not a statement about physical space. Reasoning from the dimensionality of the coordinate system to the geometry of a physical configuration conflates two distinct senses of the term.*
`kind: correction`

### 92

**PART IV LEAVES THREE THINGS: A HEURISTIC, A CHECKABLE OBSERVATION, AND A WORKED NEGATIVE RESULT.** *First, a candidate-generation heuristic reducing 118 elements to nine and directing attention to a region a Pd-centred literature has not systematically explored — legitimate provided it is not mistaken for a theory. Second, the specific checkable observation about ytterbium (register 84), which would remain interesting if the index were discarded. Third, a worked negative result: the dimensional obstruction applies to any scheme reasoning from combinatorial or configuration-space quantities to nuclear rates, and documenting where the boundary lies has value in a field with a history of frameworks that did not identify their own.*
`kind: finding`
