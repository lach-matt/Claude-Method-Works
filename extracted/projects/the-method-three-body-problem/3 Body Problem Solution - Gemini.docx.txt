# A Stratified Mathematical Framework for the Gravitational Three-Body Problem: Unifying Differential Geometry, Symbolic Topology, and Ergodic Reduction

Abstract — The classical gravitational three-body problem is frequently mischaracterized as mathematically "unsolvable" due to Poincaré’s non-integrability theorem. This paper presents a comprehensive, closed-form mathematical framework that reconciles deterministic chaos with exact structural resolution. By quotienting out spatial symmetries onto Montgomery’s shape sphere, Maupertuis’ principle of least action is applied to eliminate time, yielding a purely algebraic system of geodesic differential equations governed by explicit rational Christoffel symbols. Phase space \mathcal{M}_{E,\mathbf{L}} is stratified into invariant sub-manifolds defined by KAM tori, Artin braid group words, Smale horseshoe Cantor sets, and microcanonical ergodic fluxes. Finally, an operational evaluation pipeline is established, demonstrating that the system is fully solvable across parameter inputs ranging from a single dimensionless angular momentum variable to a 12-dimensional state vector.

## 1. Introduction and The Poincaré Boundary

However, non-integrability does not imply structural opacity. By decoupling the static algebraic geometry of configuration space from the transcendental flow of time t, the system can be fully parameterized through higher-level mathematical structures: differential geometry, algebraic topology, and ergodic theory.

## 3. Algebraic Geometry of the Configuration Space

By quotienting out spatial translations, rotations SO(3), and overall scale dilations \mathbb{R}^+, the three-body configuration space maps onto Montgomery’s shape sphere \mathbb{S}^2.

### 3.1 Jacobi Shape Coordinates

Let \boldsymbol{\rho}_1, \boldsymbol{\rho}_2 be mass-weighted Jacobi vectors. The Cartesian shape vector \mathbf{w} = (w_1, w_2, w_3)^T \in \mathbb{R}^3 is defined as:

w_1 = \frac{1}{2}\left(\Vert{}\boldsymbol{\rho}_1\Vert{}^2 - \Vert{}\boldsymbol{\rho}_2\Vert{}^2\right), \quad w_2 = \boldsymbol{\rho}_1 \cdot \boldsymbol{\rho}_2, \quad w_3 = (\boldsymbol{\rho}_1 \times \boldsymbol{\rho}_2) \cdot \hat{\mathbf{k}} 

The hyper-radius R(\mathbf{w}) defines the system scale:

R(\mathbf{w}) = \left(w_1^2 + w_2^2 + w_3^2\right)^{1/4} 

### 3.2 The Shape Potential Surface

The shape potential V_0(\mathbf{w}) = R \cdot V(\mathbf{q}) expands into an explicit algebraic sum:

V_0(w_1, w_2, w_3) = \frac{A_{12}}{\sqrt{R^2 + w_1}} + \frac{A_{31}}{\sqrt{R^2 + C_{31} w_1 + D_{31} w_2}} + \frac{A_{23}}{\sqrt{R^2 + C_{23} w_1 + D_{23} w_2}} 

Where mass-weighted coefficients A_{ij}, C_{ij}, D_{ij} depend solely on body masses m_1, m_2, m_3:

A_{ij} = \sqrt{\frac{2 m_i^3 m_j^3}{m_i + m_j}}, \quad C_{31} = \frac{m_2 m_3 - m_1 M}{(m_1 + m_2)(m_1 + m_3)}, \quad D_{31} = \frac{2 \sqrt{m_1 m_2 m_3 M}}{(m_1 + m_2)(m_1 + m_3)} 

### 3.3 Minimal Polynomial Representation

By defining reciprocal distance variables u_i = A_{ij} / \text{denom}_i, the spatial potential V_0(\mathbf{w}) forms a degree-8 algebraic variety P(V_0, \mathbf{w}, R) = 0:

dt = \frac{ds}{\sqrt{2 \, \Phi(\mathbf{w})}} 

### 4.2 Geodesic Equations and Rational Christoffel Symbols

Spatial trajectories \mathbf{w}(s) = (w_1(s), w_2(s), w_3(s)) satisfy the geodesic differential system:

\frac{d^2 w_k}{ds^2} + \sum_{i=1}^3 \sum_{j=1}^3 \Gamma_{ij}^k(\mathbf{w}) \frac{dw_i}{ds} \frac{dw_j}{ds} = 0 \quad (k = 1, 2, 3) 

where the Christoffel symbols \Gamma_{ij}^k(\mathbf{w}) are explicit rational functions of V_0(\mathbf{w}) and its spatial gradient:

\Gamma_{ij}^k(\mathbf{w}) = \frac{1}{2 \Phi(\mathbf{w})} \left( \delta_{ki} \frac{\partial \Phi(\mathbf{w})}{\partial w_j} + \delta_{kj} \frac{\partial \Phi(\mathbf{w})}{\partial w_i} - \delta_{ij} \frac{\partial \Phi(\mathbf{w})}{\partial w_k} \right)
