## 1 · WHAT THE CORRIDOR IS, GEOMETRICALLY

ν = n − a·v[p] is a **linear functional** on the plane. Plot each candidate subshell of
a step as the point **(v[p], n)**. The entrant is minimal for some a exactly when it is
a **vertex of the LOWER CONVEX HULL** of that point set, and **the corridor (lo, hi) is
that vertex's normal cone** — the slopes of the two hull edges meeting there.

This is not an analogy. It is what `brack.py` computes.

## 2 · WHY THAT MAKES THE CONDITION LINEAR

A point is a lower-hull vertex iff it lies strictly below every chord that straddles it.
For a chord from p₁ to p₂ across the entrant at p_g:

> **(n₂ − n₁)·v[p_g] + (n_g − n₂)·v[p₁] − (n_g − n₁)·v[p₂] > 0**

The n are integer data; v is the unknown. **The inequality is linear in v**, and its
coefficients sum to zero — which is exactly why translation and scaling cancelled in the
last session's sampling, observed there without explanation.

**Therefore the feasible set is a convex polyhedral cone.** That was not known; the
region had been treated as an uncharacterised subset probed by sampling.

## 4 · CONCAVITY ⇒ FEASIBLE IS NOW PROVED, NOT MEASURED

`BRIDGE` §8 owed *"a proof that concavity ⇒ feasible"*. Each facet is a chord inequality:

- **1, 4, 5** are midpoint concavity at stride 2 — immediate from strict concavity.
- **2** is v₂ above the chord from index 0 to 6 at its own abscissa — immediate.
- **3** requires monotonicity as well: concavity gives v₄ ≥ (2v₁ + 3v₆)/5, and
