In the ideal picture of fractional quantum Hall (FQHE) states (Wen’s Chiral Luttinger Liquid
theory), the edge is treated as a sharp, abrupt boundary where the electron density drops
abruptly from the bulk value n_b = \nu \frac{e B}{h} to zero.
In real experimental devices, however, smooth electrostatic confining potentials cause edge
reconstruction: the edge breaks up into alternating compressible and incompressible strips,
often creating counter-propagating edge modes, inter-mode mixing, and energy dissipation.
1. Electrostatic Origin: The Chklovskii & Chamon-Wen Model
Edge reconstruction is driven by the competition between two energy scales:
 * Electrostatic Potential Energy (U_{\text{Coulomb}}): Long-range electron-electron repulsion
prefers a broad, smoothly decaying density tail to minimize charging energy.
 * Bulk Incompressible Energy Gap (\hbar \omega_c or \Delta_{\text{FQHE}}): Deforming the
local filling factor \nu_{\text{local}} = \frac{2\pi \ell_B^2 n(y)}{e B} away from a quantized
topological plateau requires overcoming the bulk excitation gap.

Compressible vs. Incompressible Strips
 * Incompressible Strips: Regions where \nu_{\text{local}} is pinned to a quantized value (e.g.,

varies continuously. Here, electrons screen the background potential, making the effective
electrostatic potential V_{\text{eff}}(y) flat while the density n(y) slopes smoothly.

Edge reconstruction and subsequent mode mixing fundamentally alter electrical, thermal, and

At QPCs or voltage contacts, current is injected into individual modes. As current propagates

 * Short Distances (L \ll \ell_{\text{eq}}): Modes do not have space to mix. Hall conductance G is

conductance returns to its exact bulk quantization G = \nu \frac{e^2}{h}, but energy is dissipated
as heat along the boundary into the contacts.
