# THE LÖWDIN CHALLENGE — THE OUTSIDE LITERATURE

*Compiled 2026-08-11 by live search. Every claim here is attributed; where a
paper's own abstract states a limitation, that limitation is quoted in substance
rather than softened. Nothing in this file is a result of this work.*

---

## 1 · THE CHALLENGE AS POSED

**Löwdin, P.-O. (1969).** *Some comments on the Periodic System of Elements.*
Int. J. Quantum Chem. Symp. **IIIS**, 331–334. Points out that the Madelung
(n+ℓ, n) rule has not been derived from quantum mechanics. This became "the
Löwdin challenge."

**Klechkovskii, V. M. (1951).** *(n+ℓ) groups in successive filling of atomic
electron configurations.* Doklady Akademii Nauk **80**, 603–606. The rule's
Russian originator; proposed an explanation in 1962. **Madelung, E. (1936).**
*Die Mathematischen Hilfsmittel des Physikers*, 3rd ed., p. 359. And earlier
still, **Janet (1928/1929)**, whose left-step table is the rule made a table —
unrecognised until Stewart (2010), which is why it carries Madelung's name.

## 2 · THE PRINCIPAL ATTEMPT, AND THE OBJECTION TO IT

**Demkov, Yu. N. & Ostrovsky, V. N. (1971/72).** *(n+ℓ) filling rule in the
Periodic System and focusing potentials.* Zh. Eksp. Teor. Fiz. **62**, 125–132
[Sov. Phys. JETP **35**, 66–69, 1972].

The potential **U(r) ∝ −r⁻¹(r + R)⁻²** — a "focusing" potential closely
resembling the Thomas–Fermi potential — produces **additional degeneracy for
levels with identical N = n + ℓ at energy E = 0**. This is the closest thing in
the literature to a derivation, and it is what most later work builds on or
argues against. Related: Ostrovsky (2001, 2003) on what physics contributes to
the periodic law; Rosu *et al.* (1995) on the supersymmetry of the DO potentials;
the κ = ½ member of the family is the one obeying Madelung, κ = 1 being the
Maxwell fish-eye.

**THE OBJECTION.** Thyssen & Ceulemans (*Shattered Symmetry*, OUP 2017) conclude
that although the Demkov–Ostrovsky results reproduce the Madelung rule correctly,
**the way they were obtained cannot be considered a solution of the Löwdin
challenge — because the effective potential was GUESSED.** Kholodenko & Kauffman
(2019) make the same point: DO obtained correct results without rigorous
justification of the potential.

*This objection applies with equal force to any posited ordering functional,
including ν. See §6.*

## 3 · THE OTHER CLAIMANTS

**Allen, L. C. & Knight, E. T. (2002/2003).** *The Löwdin challenge: Origin of
the n+ℓ, n (Madelung) rule…* Int. J. Quantum Chem. **90**, 80–88. Treats the
table as an s/p framework internally perturbed by d and f electrons; a
perturbation operator locates the blocks; verified against Desclaux's 1973
Dirac–Fock calculations for Z = 1–118. **Scerri's commentary (2006) concludes the
explanation is inadequate on several grounds.**

**Kholodenko, A. L. & Kauffman, L. H. (2019); Kholodenko (2020).** *How the
modified Bertrand theorem explains regularities of the periodic table* (arXiv
1906.05278) and *…Regularities and Anomalies…* (arXiv 2002.12128). Uses Perlick's
extension of Bertrand's theorem to static spherically symmetric spacetimes
supporting closed orbits; shows the Tietz-type Schrödinger equation (Tietz 1956)
describes quantum motion in Bertrand spacetimes; claims an **analytic solution of
the Löwdin challenge.**

**Their own stated limitation:** the solution is **not universal** — the Madelung
exceptions among transition metals, lanthanides and actinides are **not described
in detail**, though they argue the method is capable of it.

**Group-theoretic approaches.** Kibler (2007), SO(4,2) ⊗ SU(2). Fet (2016),
Varlamov (2018). Thyssen (2013 dissertation) and Thyssen & Ceulemans (2017, 2020)
— SO(4,2) broken to the anti-de Sitter SO(3,2) explains period doubling, and the
Madelung rule is **rationalised via nonlinear Lie algebras reflecting the
screening of the Coulomb hole**. A 2026 arXiv preprint extends this to SO(4,4)
double towers. These approaches generally require some empirical input
(Ostrovsky's own assessment).

**Tangour, B. (2015).** *Beyond the Madelung–Klechkowski rule.* World J. Chem.
Educ. **3**(6), 160–167. Proposes a descriptor **K(ZX) built from all four
quantum numbers n, ℓ, mℓ, ms of the most recently added electron**, incrementing
with Z, claiming **no exceptions remain**, and connecting it to the first
ionisation energy. *Closest in spirit to putting the occupancy inside the
ordering functional.*

## 4 · THE DISSENTING POSITION

**Schwarz, W. H. E.** (and Cao, Hu, Li, Schwarz, *Pure Appl. Chem.* **91**,
1969–1999, 2019; Schwarz & Rich 2010). Argues the Madelung rule **does not truly
reflect the order of orbital occupation** in the way generally believed. The
(n+ℓ, n) rule was originally used by spectroscopists to remember which
*differentiating* orbital is added in free neutral atoms as Z increases; chemical
educators then applied it to ions, where it is a different claim. **The rule
usually fails where d and f orbitals are involved.**

*This is the objection that bears most directly on this work's own results: the
walk is precisely the neutral-atom differentiating-orbital reading, and its
failures are concentrated in d and f contests.*

## 5 · THE FIELD'S CONSENSUS

**Scerri, E. R. (2020).** *Recent attempts to change the periodic table.* Phil.
Trans. R. Soc. A **378**, 20190300. States that **numerous authors claim to have
met the challenge, but the generally held opinion is that no such derivation has
succeeded in the way Löwdin intended.**

Scerri was for many years the leading proponent of the "not derived" position and
has more recently said he has **somewhat changed his mind** (Inference Review,
2021). Others hold that the rule is empirical and that a first-principles
derivation would be surprising, and that obtaining configurations from first
principles is "a mathematical experiment rather than a mathematical deduction."

**So the challenge is open by consensus, with two live claimants (Bertrand
spacetimes; group theory) and one standing objection to the premise (Schwarz).**

## 6 · ATTRIBUTION OF THE 2026-08-11 "BRIDGE" FINDINGS

*Searched specifically to test whether the connections found in this session are
already in the literature. THEY LARGELY ARE, and the attributions are older and
more explicit than the session's claims assumed. Recorded here so that nothing
downstream repeats them as new.*

**The coordinates are not ours.** Demkov & Ostrovsky (1972) write the E = 0
degeneracy condition as **N − 1 = n_r + 2ℓ, where n_r = n − ℓ − 1 is the radial
quantum number** — the node count. Belokolos (2017) states it as an identity:

> **M = n + ℓ = n_r + 2ℓ + 1**

So the pair (node count, angular momentum) is the standard coordinate system of
this literature, not a discovery of this work. That ν's radicand is built from
n − ℓ − 1 places it in the same variables the field has used since 1972.

**The period-2 structure in N is not ours either.** Belokolos gives the period
lengths in closed form:

> **L_M = 2( ⌊M/2⌋ + 1 )²**, yielding 2, 8, 8, 18, 18, 32, 32, …

The floor function ⌊M/2⌋ IS the period-two structure. The session's observation —
that (n, ℓ) → (n+1, ℓ+1) leaves n − ℓ − 1 and Δn invariant while raising N by
two, so the crossing sets repeat with period two — is a restatement of the same
arithmetic in a different object. It is correct and it is not new.

**The odd/even N split** appears in the Rumer–Fet / SO(4,2) line: the SO(4,4)
double-tower construction marks rings of odd n+ℓ separately, and the "doubling of
the representation space" is the same parity distinction.

**What this session's search did NOT find in the literature.** No source located
reports (i) the corridor construction — an interval L(Z) < a < U(Z) on a carried
ordering parameter, with endpoints as exact surds in node counts; (ii) a
falsification test of the ordering form against a case where the corridor is
EMPTY, as the nuclear shell ordering is; or (iii) an out-of-sample or
forward-horizon measurement of a proposed rule against a Madelung null.
**Absence from these searches is not absence from the literature**, and (i) in
particular should be searched again with better terms before any claim is made.

**The corrected reading of the "bridge".** This work has independently arrived at
the coordinate system the field has used for fifty years. That is a validation of
the coordinates and it makes comparison possible — it is not a contribution.
Belokolos additionally states an intention to compare his spectra against the
NIST Atomic Spectra Database, which is the same test this work has been running.

## 7 · WHERE THIS WORK SITS

**Different in kind.** Every attempt above seeks a POTENTIAL or a SYMMETRY GROUP
from which the n+ℓ ordering follows. This work posits an ordering functional
directly — ν(n, ℓ, q) = n − a√(n−ℓ−1 + q/2(2ℓ+1)) — and tests it by whether the
resulting corridor is non-empty at each element.

**The guessed-potential objection applies to ν too**, and must be answered rather
than dodged. The answer available here is not that ν is derived but that it is
FALSIFIABLE in a way a guessed potential is not: the same corridor construction
applied to the nuclear shell ordering gives an EMPTY feasible set (registers
1367–1371, 1394), while the atomic corridor is non-empty at 106 of 106. A form
that can fail and does not is a different epistemic object from a form fitted to
succeed. *That is a defence of the method, not a derivation of ν.*

**Points of contact worth pursuing.**

- Demkov–Ostrovsky locate their content at **degeneracy at E = 0 for equal
  N = n+ℓ**. This work locates its content at **crossings** — the values of a
  where two orderings become indistinguishable, the nineteen surds. Both say the
  physics lives where the ordering is degenerate. Whether these are the same
  degeneracy in two languages is a concrete question and has not been asked.
- Tangour's K(ZX) puts the occupancy of the newly added electron into the
  descriptor; ν puts q into the radicand as the Pauli fraction q/cap. Two
  attempts to remove the exceptions by carrying occupancy. **His claim of no
  exceptions should be checked against our 93 of 100.**
- Schwarz's objection predicts failures concentrated in d and f. Ours are: Mn,
  Tc, Gd, Cm are all s-vs-d or d-vs-f contests, and the half-capacity mechanism
  is a d and f phenomenon. **The objection and our miss classification agree**,
  which is either a confirmation of his reading or a symptom of the same cause.

**What appears NOT to be in the literature.** No source found reports an
**out-of-sample generation test against a Madelung null**. The claimants ask
whether the rule FOLLOWS from their formalism; none appears to ask how far past
the record their formalism can DERIVE, or to score it against plain Madelung
run under identical conditions. This work has measured that:

    conditional, one step ahead      law 90 · Madelung 96   (of 106)
    free-running from hydrogen       law 72 · Madelung 94
    forward horizon from a prefix    law mean 3.9 · Madelung mean 6.0 steps

**On that measurement the law currently loses to the rule it seeks to explain.**
That is a negative result, but the MEASUREMENT is the thing not previously made,
and it converts the challenge from a yes/no into a distance.

## 8 · THE MATHEMATICS THE CORRIDOR BELONGS TO

*Found 2026-08-11 when the person named the object: an old-school question about
ranked order pairs. It is, and it is not in the chemistry literature at all.*

The corridor asks whether an observed ranked order is **additively representable**
by a candidate form. Each adjacent pair gives one strict linear inequality in the
parameters; the order is representable iff the system is feasible; and an
infeasible system has a **Farkas certificate**, called in this literature a
**CANCELLATION CONDITION**.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Kraft, Pratt & Seidenberg (1959)** | *Intuitive probability on finite sets*, Ann. Math. Statist. **30**, 408–419. De Finetti's conjecture disproved: a five-element comparative probability order satisfying every obvious condition and admitting no representing measure. The founding counterexample. |
| **Scott (1964)** | *Measurement structures and linear inequalities*, J. Math. Psych. **1**, 233–247. The general criterion, proved by linear programming. |
| **Krantz, Luce, Suppes & Tversky (1971)** | *Foundations of Measurement* I. Farkas' lemma **alone** yields the cancellation law as the characterisation of additively representable relations. |
| **Fishburn (1996a,b,c; 1997)** | How high the cancellation order must climb before infeasibility appears. **The standing warning: feasibility checked at low order is NOT representability.** |
| **Motzkin (1936) · Kuhn (1956)** | Solvability and consistency for linear inequalities — the machinery underneath. |

**WHERE THIS WORK'S TWO CORRIDORS SIT.**

- **Atomic, ν's family** — 106 of 106 corridors non-empty, but that is **106
  separate one-dimensional feasibility results, not one system.** Solved as one
  system the table is **INFEASIBLE**: the running intersection empties fourteen
  times. **Each element's local order is representable; the table is not, by any
  single parameter** (R 1310, 1409, 1463). In dimension one Helly's theorem makes
  pairwise feasibility global, so nothing hides — the infeasibility is plain.
- **Nuclear, Nilsson's family** — infeasible. The six constraint vectors are all
  positive multiples of (4, 1), so the constraint matrix has **RANK ONE**, and
  three point each way. The certificate is a **second-order cancellation**, the
  shortest such a system can have — where KPS's counterexample needs a higher one.

**AND THE FIELD REACHES THE SAME ANSWER BY FITTING.** arXiv 1110.6134 Table I
gives κ = 0.0250 for ℓ = 1 within N = 5 where ℓ = 3 and ℓ = 5 get 0.0570 — a
factor of 2.3 inside one shell. ℓ-independence abandoned empirically, which is
what the rank-one certificate says is forced. Two routes to one answer.

**CONSEQUENCE FOR THE METHOD.** The corridor is not a new instrument; it is a
classical one, and naming it imports its theorems and its warnings. Any future
corridor must be SOLVED as a system rather than spot-checked, because Fishburn
shows low-order feasibility can hide high-order infeasibility.

   M = n + ℓ = n_r + 2ℓ + 1 and L_M = 2(⌊M/2⌋+1)² explicitly, which between them
   attribute this session's coordinate and period-two findings. Also proposes
   comparing his spectra to the NIST ASD, which is this work's own test.

   taken to be, which if right narrows what the challenge even asks.
