## 1. Setting

### 1.1 The operator

We use the notation of III.G: $v\in\mathbb R$, $\omega\in\mathbb S^3$, $R := v^{-1}$ near $v = \infty$,
$N_{\mathrm{sf}}(\lambda)$ of III.G (1.3), the spaces of III.G Definition 1.3, and the admissible pairs of III.G (2.6).
Let $\iota^+ := [0,\infty]_v\times\mathbb S^3$. It is a compact manifold with two boundary hypersurfaces,
$\mathscr I^+\cap\iota^+ = \{v = 0\}$ and $K^+\cap\iota^+ = \{R = 0\}$, which do not meet. Their defining functions are
$\rho_I := \frac v{1+v}$ and $\rho_K := \frac R{1+R}$. On $\iota^+$ we use the unweighted b-density
$|\frac{dv}v\,d\slashed g| = |\frac{dR}R\,d\slashed g|$ and $H^{k,\alpha_I,\alpha_K}_{\mathrm b}(\iota^+) :=
\rho_I^{\alpha_I}\rho_K^{\alpha_K}H^k_{\mathrm b}(\iota^+)$ (III.A Definition 2.6). $\dot C^\infty(\iota^+)$ is the
space of smooth sections vanishing to infinite order at $\partial\iota^+$. The $\iota^+$-normal operator ([Hin26]
(9.10)-(9.11) at $n = 4$) is
$$N_{\iota^+}(\lambda) := R^{-2-\lambda}N_{\mathrm{sf}}(\lambda)R^\lambda = -2v(v\partial_v-\lambda)\big(v\partial_v
-\tfrac32-\bar S\big)+v^2\tilde L(0)(\omega,v\partial_v,\partial_\omega)\tag{1.1}$$
on $(0,\infty)_v\times\mathbb S^3$. This is III.G (1.6) in the coordinate $v$ (*computed:* `sf5.py` S9; the control
$v^{2-\lambda}N_{\mathrm{sf}}(\lambda)v^\lambda$ fails). So on $\{0<v<\infty\}$, $N_{\iota^+}(\lambda)h = f$ is
equivalent to
$$(v\partial_v-\lambda)\big(v\partial_v-\tfrac32-\bar S\big)h = -\tfrac1{2v}f
+\tfrac v2\tilde L(0)(\omega,v\partial_v,\partial_\omega)h,\tag{1.2}$$
which is the source's (9.22) at $n = 4$. Near $R = 0$,
$$R^2N_{\iota^+}(\lambda) = A_0+RA_1,\qquad A_0 := \tilde L(0)(\omega,-R\partial_R,\partial_\omega),\quad A_1 :
 = -2(R\partial_R+\lambda)\big(R\partial_R+\tfrac32+\bar S\big).\tag{1.3}$$
Since $R^2N_{\iota^+}(\lambda) = R^{-\lambda}N_{\mathrm{sf}}(\lambda)R^\lambda$, it is b-elliptic at $R = 0$ (III.G
Lemma 1.2 (b); conjugation by $R^\lambda$ preserves b-ellipticity). Its indicial family is
$N(R^2N_{\iota^+}(\lambda),\zeta) = \tilde L(0)(\omega,-\zeta,\partial_\omega)$, which does not depend on $\lambda$. So
its boundary spectrum is $\{-\mu\}$, $\mu$ over the indicial roots of III.F$'$ Proposition 2.3: those of
$\widehat{L_b}(0)$, whose b-normal operator at $\partial X$ is
${}^{\mathrm b}\bar L(0) = \rho^2\tilde L(0)(\omega,\rho\partial_\rho,\partial_\omega)$.

### 1.2 The endomorphism

**Lemma 1.1 ($\bar S$ is semisimple).** Let $\gamma_\Upsilon<\min(\frac12s_C,\frac38)$, $s_C := \gamma_C(1-e_C)(1-v_C)$.
At every $\omega\in\mathbb S^3$, $\bar S(\omega)$ is diagonalisable, with the eigenvalues of III.F (3.1), which do not
depend on $\omega$. So $\bar S = \sum_{s\in\sigma}s\,\Pi_s$, $\sigma := \mathrm{spec}\,\bar S$, where the spectral
projections $\Pi_s$ are smooth on $\mathbb S^3$, $\Pi_s\Pi_{s'} = \delta_{ss'}\Pi_s$ and $\sum_s\Pi_s = \mathrm{Id}$.

*Proof.* III.F Lemma 3.1's proof writes $\rho\bar S = UV$, with $U:T^*\oplus T^*\to S^2T^*$, $V:S^2T^*\to T^*\oplus T^*$
and
$$VU = \begin{pmatrix}\rho\bar S^C&0\\V_2U_1&\rho\bar S^\Upsilon\end{pmatrix}.$$
There, $\bar S^C$ and $\bar S^\Upsilon$ leave the angular covectors invariant and act on them as $\gamma_C(1-v_C)$ and
$\gamma_\Upsilon$ times the identity. On the span of $dt_*$ and $k$ they are triangular with the distinct diagonal
entries $\gamma_C(1-e_C)(1-v_C)$, $2\gamma_C(1-v_C)$ and $2\gamma_\Upsilon$, $\gamma_\Upsilon(1-e_\Upsilon)$
($0<e_C,e_\Upsilon,v_C<1$). So both are diagonalisable. Their spectra are disjoint, since
$\mathrm{spec}\,\bar S^\Upsilon\subset(0,2\gamma_\Upsilon]$ and $\mathrm{spec}\,\bar S^C\subset[s_C,\infty)$. Let $p_1$,
$p_2$ be the minimal polynomials of the diagonal blocks $B_1$, $B_2$ of $T := VU$. They have simple roots and no common
root. $p_1(T)$ has first block row 0 and $p_2(T)$ has second block column 0. So $p_2(T)p_1(T) = 0$, and $T$ is
diagonalisable. All its eigenvalues are non-zero, so $VU$ is invertible. Let $q$ be the minimal polynomial of $VU$; then
$q(0)\ne0$. From $(UV)^{j+1} = U(VU)^jV$ we get $UV\,q(UV) = U\,q(VU)\,V = 0$. Hence the minimal polynomial of $UV$
divides $t\,q(t)$, which has simple roots, so $UV$ and $\bar S = \rho^{-1}UV$ are diagonalisable. Their eigenvalues are
III.F (3.1), independent of $\omega$. The projections are $\Pi_s = \prod_{s'\ne s}(\bar S-s')/(s-s')$, polynomials in
$\bar S$ with constant coefficients, hence smooth. (*Computed:* `sbar5.py` S4. At two parameter points, the product of
$\bar S-s$ over the seven distinct eigenvalues vanishes. Four-dimensional control: the same at $n = 3$. Control: at
$\gamma_\Upsilon = \gamma_C(1-v_C)$, where the two spectra meet, $\bar S$ has a Jordan block.) $\square$

We write
$$\sigma_\Upsilon := \{0,\ (1-e_\Upsilon)\gamma_\Upsilon,\ \gamma_\Upsilon,\ 2\gamma_\Upsilon\},\qquad\sigma_C :
 = \gamma_C\{(1-e_C)(1-v_C),\ 1-v_C,\ 2(1-v_C)\},\qquad\Pi_C := \sum_{s\in\sigma_C}\Pi_s,\tag{1.4}$$
so $\sigma = \sigma_\Upsilon\cup\sigma_C$, $\sigma_\Upsilon\subset[0,2\gamma_\Upsilon]\subset[0,\frac34)$ and
$\min\sigma_C = s_C$. The source works in his splitting (5.27). There $\bar S$ is lower triangular ((8.8)), and one
component carries two small exponents (his $\mathcal E_{\iota^+,\mathscr I,11}$). In the eigenbasis each component
carries one, and the $n = 4$ counterpart of (5.27) (Part III.C) is not needed here.

### 1.3 Index sets

As in III.A Definition 2.7, $(z,k)$ denotes the smallest index set containing $(z,k)$, that is
$\{(z+j,l):j\in\mathbb N_0,\ 0\le l\le k\}$, and $\bar\cup$ is the extended union.

**Definition 1.2 ([Hin26] Definition 9.3 at $n = 4$).** (1) (*Index sets at $\mathscr I^+\cap\iota^+$.*)
$$\mathcal E^{\mathrm{tot}}_{\iota^+,\mathscr I} := \bigcup_{s\in\sigma_\Upsilon}(\tfrac32+s,0),
 \qquad\mathcal E^C_{\iota^+,\mathscr I} := \mathcal E^{\mathrm{tot}}_{\iota^+,\mathscr I}+1,\qquad\mathcal E_s :
 = \mathcal E^C_{\iota^+,\mathscr I}\cup(\tfrac32+s,0)\quad(s\in\sigma_\Upsilon).\tag{1.5}$$
(2) (*Function spaces.*) For an index set $\mathcal E_K$ at $K^+\cap\iota^+$ and $\ell_I\in\mathbb R$,
$\mathcal A^{(\langle\mathcal E^C_{\iota^+,\mathscr I}\rangle,\ell_I),\mathcal E_K}(\iota^+)$ is the space of sections
$h$ with $\Pi_Ch\in\mathcal A^{(\mathcal E^C_{\iota^+,\mathscr I},\ell_I),\mathcal E_K}$ and
$\Pi_sh\in\mathcal A^{(\mathcal E_s,\ell_I),\mathcal E_K}$ for $s\in\sigma_\Upsilon$. Here III.A Definition 2.8 and
Notation 2.9 are used, with the index sets referring to $\{v = 0\}$ and $\{R = 0\}$ in this order.
$H^{k,(\langle\mathcal E^C_{\iota^+,\mathscr I}\rangle,\ell_I),(\mathcal E_K,\ell_K)}_{\mathrm b}(\iota^+)$ is defined
in the same way. The source's $\mathcal E_{\iota^+,\mathscr I,01}$, $\mathcal E_{\iota^+,\mathscr I,1/}$ and
$\mathcal E_{\iota^+,\mathscr I,0/}$ are the $\mathcal E_s$ for $s = (1-e_\Upsilon)\gamma_\Upsilon$, $\gamma_\Upsilon$
and $0$. His $\mathcal E_{\iota^+,\mathscr I,11}$ is the union of those for $2\gamma_\Upsilon$ and
$(1-e_\Upsilon)\gamma_\Upsilon$. Throughout, $\frac32$ replaces his $1$.

(3) (*The index set at $K^+\cap\iota^+$.*) Fix $\alpha\in(-1,0)$ and put $A := R^2N_{\iota^+}(\lambda)$. $\mathcal E_K$
is the index set $\mathcal E = \bigcup_j\mathcal E_j$ of III.A Lemma 1.4 for $A$ with $\mathcal F = \emptyset$, given by
$$\mathcal E_0 := \mathcal E(\alpha),\qquad\mathcal E_{j+1} := \mathcal E(\alpha)\,\bar\cup\,(\mathcal E_j+1),\tag{1.6}$$
where $\mathcal E(\alpha)$ is the smallest index set containing the $(\zeta,k)\in\mathrm{Spec}_{\mathrm b}(A)$ with
$\mathrm{Re}\,\zeta>\alpha$.

**Lemma 1.3 (the index set at $K^+$).** (a) $\mathcal E_K$ depends neither on $\lambda$ nor on $\alpha\in(-1,0)$. (b)
$\mathcal E_K\subset\{\mathrm{Re}\,z\ge0\}$ and $\mathcal E_K\cap\{\mathrm{Re}\,z = 0\} = \{(0,0)\}$. The exponents of
$\mathcal E(\alpha)$ in $(0,1)$ are $\lambda^\Upsilon_{\mathrm s0,2}-2\in(0,\frac{z_\Upsilon}3]$ and
$\lambda^\Upsilon_{\mathrm s1,2}-2\in(0,\frac14)$, so $\min\mathrm{Re}(\mathcal E_K\setminus\{(0,0)\})>0$. (c) All
exponents of $\mathcal E(\alpha)$ with real part $\le101$ are real.

*Proof.* (a) $\mathrm{Spec}_{\mathrm b}(A)$ is determined by $N(A,\zeta) = \tilde L(0)(\omega,-\zeta,\partial_\omega)$,
which does not depend on $\lambda$ (section 1.1). Its elements are the $(-\mu,k)$ with $\mu$ a root of III.F$'$
Proposition 2.3, $k$ below the order of the root. No root has real part in $(0,1]$ (III.F$'$ Proposition 2.3 (a)), so
$\mathrm{Re}(-\mu)>\alpha$ holds exactly when $\mathrm{Re}\,\mu\le0$, for every $\alpha\in(-1,0)$. (b), (c) The roots
with $\mathrm{Re}\,\mu\le0$ are those before the semicolons in III.F$'$ Proposition 2.3. They are: $0$ at s0 and s2; the
$\lambda^C_{\cdot,\ll,\cdot}-1$, with real part $<-101$ (there, (d)); $-l$, $1-l$ and $2-l$ at the types listed; $-1$ at
s1 and v1; $-2-2\gamma_Cv_C$ at v1; and $2-\lambda^\Upsilon_{\mathrm sl,l+j}$ ($j = 1,2$) and
$2-\lambda^\Upsilon_{\mathrm s0,2}$. By III.D Lemma 4.1 the last are real, with
$\lambda^\Upsilon_{\mathrm s0,2}\in(2,2+\frac{z_\Upsilon}3]$ and $\lambda^\Upsilon_{\mathrm sl,l+j}\in(l+j,l+j+\frac14)$
for $0<z_\Upsilon\le\frac12$, $z_\Upsilon := 4e_\Upsilon\gamma_\Upsilon^2$ (III.D's $z$). So every root with
$\mathrm{Re}\,\mu\le0$ other than $0$ has $\mathrm{Re}\,\mu<0$. The root $0$ occurs only at s0 and s2 and is simple
there (III.F$'$ Proposition 2.3 (c)), giving $(0,0)$ only. The exponents $-\mu\in(0,1)$ are
$\lambda^\Upsilon_{\mathrm s0,2}-2$ and $\lambda^\Upsilon_{\mathrm s1,2}-2$; the next ones are $\ge1$. The iterates
$\mathcal E_j+1$ only add points with real part $\ge1$. $\square$

## 2. Two lemmas at the light cone

Words $W^\alpha$ and the module $\mathcal D^m$ are as in III.G section 2.4 (products of $v\partial_v$ and the Lie
derivatives $\mathcal L_{\Omega_a}$).

**Lemma 2.1 (conormality at the light cone, every order).** Let $s\in\mathbb R$ with $s-\frac12\notin\mathbb Z$. There
is $m(s)\in\mathbb N$ such that the following holds for every $k\in\mathbb N_0$. Let
$u\in H^s(\mathbb R\times\mathbb S^3)$ be supported in $[0,M]\times\mathbb S^3$, with $W^\beta u\in H^s$ for
$|\beta|\le k+m(s)$. Then for $|\alpha|\le k$,
$$|W^\alpha u(v,\omega)|
 \le C\,v^{s-\frac12}\sum_{|\beta|\le k+m(s)}\|W^\beta u\|_{H^s}\quad\text{on }(0,1)\times\mathbb S^3,\tag{2.1}$$
with $C$ depending on $s$, $k$ and $M$. In particular, if $W^\beta u\in H^s$ for every word, then $u$ is conormal at
$\{v = 0^+\}$ with the bound $v^{s-\frac12}$.

*Proof.* *(i) Values in $\mathcal H := H^2(\mathbb S^3;\mathbb C^N)\subset C^0$.* Expand in spherical harmonics, with
eigenvalues $\kappa_j$ of $\Delta_{\mathbb S^3}$. For $s\ge0$, $(1+\xi^2+\kappa_j)^s\ge(1+\xi^2)^s$. For $s<0$,
$(1+\xi^2)^s\le(1+\xi^2+\kappa_j)^s(1+\kappa_j)^{-s}$, since $(1+\xi^2+\kappa_j)/(1+\xi^2)\le1+\kappa_j$. In both cases
$\|g\|_{H^s(\mathbb R;H^2(\mathbb S^3))}\le C\sum_{|\gamma|\le2+\lceil\max(0,-s)\rceil}\|\mathcal L_\Omega^\gamma
g\|_{H^s}$. This uses $\Delta_{\mathbb S^3} = -\sum_a\Omega_a^2$ and $\Omega_a = \mathcal L_{\Omega_a}-A_a$ with
constant $A_a$ (III.G Lemma 2.5's proof). So $W^\alpha u\in H^s(\mathbb R;\mathcal H)$ for
$|\alpha|\le k+m(s)-2-\lceil\max(0,-s)\rceil$.

*(ii) $s>\frac12$.* III.G Lemma 3.1's Taylor argument, applied to $g = W^\alpha u\in H^s(\mathbb R;\mathcal H)$
supported in $\{v\ge0\}$, gives $\|g(v)\|_{\mathcal H}\le Cv^{s-\frac12}\|g\|_{H^s(\mathbb R;\mathcal H)}$ on $(0,1)$;
this is (2.1).

*(iii) $s<\frac12$.* Let $j\in\mathbb N$ with $s+j>\frac12$. For $g\in H^\sigma(\mathbb R;\mathcal H)$ supported in
$[0,M']$, let $Ig$ be the distribution with $\partial_vIg = g$ and $Ig = 0$ on $\{v<0\}$, and let
$\zeta\in C^\infty(\mathbb R)$ equal 1 on $(-\infty,M'+\frac12]$ and 0 on $[M'+1,\infty)$. Then:

(α) $\zeta Ig$ is supported in $[0,M'+1]$, and $\partial_v(\zeta Ig) = g+\zeta'c$ with
$c := \langle g,1\rangle\in\mathcal H$, since $Ig = c$ on $\{v\ge M'\}$. Its Fourier transform is entire and equals
$\widehat{\partial_v(\zeta Ig)}(\xi)/(i\xi)$. This gives
$\|\zeta Ig\|_{H^{\sigma+1}(\mathbb R;\mathcal H)}\le C\|g\|_{H^\sigma(\mathbb R;\mathcal H)}$: use the quotient for
$|\xi|\ge1$, and for $|\xi|\le1$ the maximum principle on the complex unit disc with the Paley-Wiener bound for
compactly supported elements of $H^\sigma$.

(β) $v\partial_vIg = I((v\partial_v+1)g)$ and $\mathcal L_\Omega Ig = I(\mathcal L_\Omega g)$, since both sides vanish
on $\{v<0\}$ and have the same $v$-derivative ($\partial_v(v\partial_vIg) = g+v\partial_vg$). So
$W^\alpha(\zeta Ig) = \zeta I(\tilde W^\alpha g)+r$, where $\tilde W^\alpha$ is $W^\alpha$ with every $v\partial_v$
replaced by $v\partial_v+1$, and $r$ is supported in $[M'+\frac12,M'+1]$, built from $v^i\zeta^{(i)}$ and
$I(W^\gamma g)$ with $|\gamma|<|\alpha|$.

Applying (α)-(β) $j$ times, $U := \zeta_jI\cdots\zeta_1Iu$ satisfies $W^\alpha U\in H^{s+j}(\mathbb R;\mathcal H)$ for
the same $\alpha$, with bounds. By (ii) at order $s+j$ (with $s+j-\frac12\notin\mathbb N_0$),
$\|W^\alpha U(v)\|_{\mathcal H}\le Cv^{s+j-\frac12}$ on $(0,1)$. On $(0,1)$, where every $\zeta_i = 1$, we have
$u = \partial_v^jU = v^{-j}\prod_{i=0}^{j-1}(v\partial_v-i)U$. Also $W^\alpha v^{-j} = v^{-j}W^\alpha_{(-j)}$, with
$v\partial_v$ shifted by $-j$. So $W^\alpha u$ is $v^{-j}$ times a combination of words of length $\le|\alpha|+j$
applied to $U$. This gives (2.1) with $m(s) := 2+\lceil\max(0,-s)\rceil+j$. $\square$

It is III.G Lemma 3.1 (for every $s$ since D238) with the number of words counted. It replaces the source's
identifications (9.5) and (9.21) of supported and extendible Sobolev spaces with b-spaces (see (G$'$5)).

For an index set $G$ and $\gamma\in\mathbb R$, $\mathcal A^{(G,\gamma)}([0,1)_v\times\mathbb S^3)$ consists of the sums
$\sum_{(z,k)\in G,\,\mathrm{Re}\,z<\gamma}v^z(\log v)^kg_{z,k}(\omega)+r$, with $g_{z,k}$ smooth and
$|W^\alpha r|\le C_\alpha v^\gamma$ for all $\alpha$. The statements below hold for all orders in open ranges, so this
$L^\infty$-based version and III.A's $L^2$-based one can be used interchangeably
($H^{\infty,\gamma}_{\mathrm b}\subset\mathcal A^\gamma\subset H^{\infty,\gamma-\delta}_{\mathrm b}$ near $\{v = 0\}$).

**Lemma 2.2 (integration at the light cone).** Let $a\in\mathbb C$, $\gamma_0<\gamma$ with $\gamma\ne\mathrm{Re}\,a$,
$G$ an index set with no exponent of real part $\gamma$, and $w$ on $(0,1)\times\mathbb S^3$ with
$|W^\alpha w|\le C_\alpha v^{\gamma_0}$ for all $\alpha$. If $(v\partial_v-a)w = g\in\mathcal A^{(G,\gamma)}$, then
$w\in\mathcal A^{(G\bar\cup\{(a,0)\},\gamma)}$. If moreover $\mathrm{Re}\,a<\gamma_0$ and $a\notin\pi_1G$, then
$w\in\mathcal A^{(G,\gamma)}$. The same holds with $H^{k,(G,\gamma)}_{\mathrm b}$ in place of $\mathcal A^{(G,\gamma)}$
and $w\in\rho_I^{\gamma_0}H^k_{\mathrm b}$.

*Proof.* Since $(v\partial_v-a)(v^z(\log v)^j) = (z-a)v^z(\log v)^j+jv^z(\log v)^{j-1}$, each term
$v^z(\log v)^k\varphi$ of $g$ has a particular solution $v^zP(\log v)\varphi$. Here $P$ is a polynomial of degree $k$ if
$z\ne a$ and $k+1$ if $z = a$. Subtracting these leaves $(v\partial_v-a)w' = r$ with
$|W^\alpha r|\le C_\alpha v^\gamma$, and $w'$ conormal. If $\mathrm{Re}\,a<\gamma$, put
$w'' := v^a\int_0^vt^{-a}r(t)\frac{dt}t$. If $\mathrm{Re}\,a>\gamma$, put $w'' := -v^a\int_v^1t^{-a}r(t)\frac{dt}t$. In
both cases $|w''|\le Cv^\gamma$. Also $v\partial_vw'' = aw''+r$ and the rotations pass through the integral, so by
induction $|W^\alpha w''|\le C_\alpha v^\gamma$. Then $(v\partial_v-a)(w'-w'') = 0$, so $w'-w'' = c(\omega)v^a$, with
$c = v^{-a}(w'-w'')$ at any fixed $v$, smooth in $\omega$. If $\mathrm{Re}\,a>\gamma$, $cv^a$ is of order $\gamma$. This
proves the first claim. If $\mathrm{Re}\,a<\gamma_0$ and $a\notin\pi_1G$, the term $cv^a$ is the only term of order
$v^a$ in the expansion of $w = O(v^{\gamma_0})$, so $c = 0$. In the $L^2$ version the two integrals are bounded on
$\rho_I^\gamma H^k_{\mathrm b}$ by Hardy's inequality: after the Mellin transform in $v$ they are multiplication by
$(\kappa-a)^{-1}$ on the line $\mathrm{Re}\,\kappa = \gamma$. They commute with the rotations and intertwine
$v\partial_v$, so the b-regularity is preserved. $\square$

## 3. The inverse of the $\iota^+$-normal operator

**Theorem 3.1 ([Hin26] Proposition 9.4 at $n = 4$).** Assume the hypotheses of III.G Theorem 5.1 and
$\gamma_\Upsilon<\min(\frac12s_C,\frac38)$ (the source's $0<\gamma_\Upsilon\ll1$; D240). Let $\lambda\in\mathbb C$ be
such that $N_{\mathrm{sf}}(\lambda)$ is invertible; by III.G Theorem 5.1 this does not depend on the admissible pair.
For $f\in\dot C^\infty(\iota^+;S^2T^*)$, extend $R^{2+\lambda}f$ by 0 to $\{v\le0\}$ and set
$$h := N_{\iota^+}(\lambda)^{-1}f :
 = R^{-\lambda}N_{\mathrm{sf}}(\lambda)^{-1}(R^{2+\lambda}f)\big|_{(0,\infty)_v\times\mathbb S^3}.\tag{3.1}$$
Then $N_{\iota^+}(\lambda)h = f$, and:

(1) (*Conormality and polyhomogeneity.*) For every $\ell_I<\frac32+s_C$ and every $\ell<\frac52+s_C$, with
$\mathcal E_K$ of Definition 1.2 (3),
$$h\in\mathcal A^{(\langle\mathcal E^C_{\iota^+,\mathscr I}\rangle,\ell_I),\mathcal E_K}(\iota^+),\qquad\big(v\partial_v
-\tfrac32-\bar S\big)h\in\mathcal A^{(\mathcal E^C_{\iota^+,\mathscr I},\ell)}([0,1)_v\times\mathbb S^3).\tag{3.2}$$

(2) (*Finite regularity.*) Let $\ell_I<\frac32+s_C$ with
$\ell_I\notin\mathrm{Re}\,\pi_1\mathcal E^{\mathrm{tot}}_{\iota^+,\mathscr I}$, and let $\ell_K>-1$ with
$\ell_K\notin\mathrm{Re}\,\pi_1\mathcal E_K$. For $\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda<\ell_I$ there is $d\in\mathbb N_0$, depending
only on $\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda$, $\ell_I$ and the parameters, such that for every $k\in\mathbb N_0$ the map $f\mapsto h$
extends to a continuous linear map
$$N_{\iota^+}(\lambda)^{-1}:H^{k+d,\ell_I+1,\ell_K-2}_{\mathrm b}(\iota^+;S^2T^*)
\to H^{k,(\langle\mathcal E^C_{\iota^+,\mathscr I}\rangle,\ell_I),(\mathcal E_K,\ell_K)}_{\mathrm b}(\iota^+;S^2T^*),
\tag{3.3}$$
with $(v\partial_v-\frac32-\bar S)h\in H^{k,(\mathcal E^C_{\iota^+,\mathscr I},\ell_I)}_{\mathrm b}([0,1)_v\times\mathbb
S^3)$ (order $\ell_I$, as the source prints; $\ell_I+1$, stated here before D240, is false, the $f$-term of (1.2) being
of order $\ell_I$ only). On $\{\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda<\ell_I\}$, (3.3) is finite-meromorphic in $\lambda$. Its principal
parts at poles do not depend on $k$, $\ell_I$ or $\ell_K$, and their ranges lie in the space of (1).

*Proof.* *Step 0: $h$ solves.* Let $F := R^{2+\lambda}f$, extended by 0. As $f$ vanishes to infinite order at $v = 0$
and at $R = 0$, $F$ is smooth on $\mathbb R_v\times\mathbb S^3$, supported in $\{v\ge0\}$, and $O(R^\infty)$ with all
b-derivatives at $R = 0$. So $F\in\dot H^{(s-1;\infty),\mu}_{\mathrm b;\mathrm b}$ for every $(s,\mu)$. By III.G Theorem
5.1 (2)-(3), $\tilde h := N_{\mathrm{sf}}(\lambda)^{-1}F$ lies in $\dot H^{(s;\infty),\mu}_{\mathrm b;\mathrm b}$ for
every pair admissible for $\lambda$, and the inverses agree. For $0<a<1$, $N_{\mathrm{sf}}(\lambda)\tilde h = 0$ on
$\{-1<v<-a\}$, so III.G Lemma 2.3 on the slab $\Omega_{a,1}$, with the support at $v = -1$, gives $\tilde h = 0$ there.
Hence $\operatorname{supp}\tilde h\subset\{v\ge0\}$ and $h = v^\lambda\tilde h|_{v>0}$. By (1.1),
$N_{\iota^+}(\lambda)h = v^{2+\lambda}N_{\mathrm{sf}}(\lambda)\tilde h = v^{2+\lambda}F = f$.

*Step 1: a priori bounds.* Fix $\epsilon\in(0,\frac14)$ with $\frac32-\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda-\epsilon\notin\mathbb Z$, and
put $s := 2-\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda-\epsilon$; then $(s,\mu)$ is admissible for
$\mu\in(\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda-1,\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda)$. *At $v = 0$:* take $\chi\in C^\infty_c((-1,2))$ equal to 1 on
$[-\frac12,\frac32]$. On $\operatorname{supp}\chi$ the norm of $\dot H^{s,\mu}_{\mathrm b}$ is that of $H^s$, so
$\chi\tilde h$ satisfies Lemma 2.1's hypotheses for every $k$. Hence
$|W^\alpha\tilde h|\le C_\alpha v^{\frac32-\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda-\epsilon}$ on $(0,1)$. Since
$W^\alpha v^\lambda = v^\lambda W^\alpha_{(\lambda)}$ (with $v\partial_v$ shifted by $\lambda$),
$$|W^\alpha h|\le C_\alpha v^{\frac32-\epsilon}\quad\text{on }(0,1)\times\mathbb S^3.\tag{3.4}$$
*At $R = 0$:* $R\partial_R = -v\partial_v$ and the rotations span the b-vector fields there. So
$\tilde h\in R^\mu H^\infty_{\mathrm b}$ near $R = 0$ (III.G Definition 1.3), and
$h = R^{-\lambda}\tilde h\in R^{\alpha}H^\infty_{\mathrm b}$ with $\alpha := \mu-\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda\in(-1,0)$. III.A
Lemma 1.4 applies with $A := R^2N_{\iota^+}(\lambda)$ (b-elliptic, section 1.1), $Ah = R^2f\in\dot C^\infty$,
$\mathcal F = \emptyset$ and every $\beta$. It gives $h\in H^{\infty,(\mathcal E_K,\beta)}_{\mathrm b}$ near $R = 0$ for
every $\beta\notin\mathrm{Re}\,\mathrm{spec}_{\mathrm b}(A)$. That is, $h$ is polyhomogeneous at $R = 0$ with index set
$\mathcal E_K$ (Lemma 1.3 (a)).

*Step 2: the expansion at $v = 0$ for $\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda<\frac32-\epsilon$.* $\Pi_s$ commutes with $v\partial_v$ and
with $\bar S$. So (1.2) gives, for each $s\in\sigma$,
$$(v\partial_v-\lambda)\big(v\partial_v-\tfrac32-s\big)\Pi_sh = \Pi_sg,\qquad g := -\tfrac1{2v}f
+\tfrac v2\tilde L(0)(\omega,v\partial_v,\partial_\omega)h.\tag{3.5}$$
$\tilde L(0)(\omega,v\partial_v,\partial_\omega)$ is a combination of words with smooth coefficients (III.G Lemma 2.5's
proof). Fix $\ell_I<\frac32+s_C$ with $\ell_I\notin\mathrm{Re}\,\pi_1\mathcal E^{\mathrm{tot}}_{\iota^+,\mathscr I}$; it
suffices to prove (3.2) for such $\ell_I$, since $\mathcal A^{(\mathcal E,\ell')}\subset\mathcal A^{(\mathcal E,\ell)}$
for $\ell<\ell'$. Let $\vartheta_j := \min(\frac32-\epsilon+j,\ell_I)$. We show by induction that
$h\in\mathcal A^{(\langle\mathcal E^C\rangle,\vartheta_j)}$ at $v = 0$, where
$\mathcal E^C := \mathcal E^C_{\iota^+,\mathscr I}$. For $j = 0$ this is (3.4), as
$\min\mathrm{Re}\,\mathcal E_s = \frac32>\vartheta_0$.

For the step: $\mathcal E^C$ and the $\mathcal E_s$ lie in $\mathcal E^{\mathrm{tot}}_{\iota^+,\mathscr I}$, and words
preserve $\mathcal A^{(\mathcal E^{\mathrm{tot}}_{\iota^+,\mathscr I},\vartheta_j)}$. So
$g\in\mathcal A^{(\mathcal E^C,\vartheta_j+1)}$, with the $f$-term vanishing to infinite order. Apply Lemma 2.2 to
$w_1 := (v\partial_v-\frac32-s)\Pi_sh$, which is $O(v^{\frac32-\epsilon})$ by (3.4), with $a = \lambda$. Here
$\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda<\frac32-\epsilon$ (Lemma 2.2's $\gamma_0$) and $\lambda\notin\pi_1\mathcal E^C$ (whose real parts
are $\ge\frac52$), so $w_1\in\mathcal A^{(\mathcal E^C,\vartheta_{j+1})}$. Apply Lemma 2.2 again to $\Pi_sh$ with
$a = \frac32+s$. For $s\in\sigma_\Upsilon$, $\frac32+s\notin\pi_1\mathcal E^C = \frac52+\sigma_\Upsilon+\mathbb N_0$, as
$|s-s'|\le2\gamma_\Upsilon<1$. So $\Pi_sh\in\mathcal A^{(\mathcal E^C\cup(\frac32+s,0),\vartheta_{j+1})} = \mathcal
A^{(\mathcal E_s,\vartheta_{j+1})}$. For $s\in\sigma_C$,
$\mathrm{Re}(\frac32+s)\ge\frac32+s_C>\ell_I\ge\vartheta_{j+1}$, so
$\Pi_sh\in\mathcal A^{(\mathcal E^C,\vartheta_{j+1})}$. (Each use has $\vartheta_{j+1}\ne\mathrm{Re}\,a$ since
$\epsilon+s\in(0,1)$ for $s\in\sigma_\Upsilon$, and $\ell_I$ is chosen, as the monotonicity allows, with
$\ell_I\ne\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda$, $\ell_I+1\ne\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda$ and
$\ell_I\notin\mathrm{Re}\,\pi_1\mathcal E^{\mathrm{tot}}_{\iota^+,\mathscr I}$.) After finitely many steps
$\vartheta_j = \ell_I$. At that step $g\in\mathcal A^{(\mathcal E^C,\ell_I+1)}$, and the first application of Lemma 2.2
gives $(v\partial_v-\frac32-\bar S)h = \sum_s w_1\in\mathcal A^{(\mathcal E^C,\ell_I+1)}$. With Step 1, this proves (1)
for $\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda<\frac32-\epsilon$, and so for $\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda<\frac32$.

*Step 3: general $\lambda$, no $v^\lambda$-terms.* Let $\mathcal P$ be the set of poles of
$N_{\mathrm{sf}}(\cdot)^{-1}$. It is closed and discrete, with finitely many points in each half plane
$\{\mathrm{Re}\le C\}$ (III.G Theorem 5.1 (2)). Let $\Lambda_0 := \mathcal P\cup(\frac32+\sigma+\mathbb N_0)$, also
closed and discrete; so $\Omega := \mathbb C\setminus\Lambda_0$ is open and connected. For $\mu\notin\mathcal P$ put
$h(\mu) := N_{\iota^+}(\mu)^{-1}f$.

(a) *Holomorphy.* Let $\mu_0\notin\mathcal P$. Choose a disc $D\ni\mu_0$ disjoint from $\mathcal P$, and a pair
$(s,\mu_w)$ admissible for every $\mu\in D$ with $s-\frac12\notin\mathbb Z$ (as in III.G Theorem 5.1's proof). Let
$\mathcal X_k := \{u\in\dot H^{(s;k),\mu_w}_{\mathrm b;\mathrm b}:N_{\mathrm{sf}}(\mu)u\in\dot
H^{(s-1;k),\mu_w}_{\mathrm b;\mathrm b}\}$ and $\mathcal Y_k := \dot H^{(s-1;k),\mu_w}_{\mathrm b;\mathrm b}$. The
difference $N_{\mathrm{sf}}(\mu)-N_{\mathrm{sf}}(\mu_0)$ is $(\mu-\mu_0)$ times $-2\partial_v$ plus a module element.
Since $[v\partial_v,\partial_v] = -\partial_v$, $\partial_v$ commutes with the rotations, and
$\partial_v = -R^2\partial_R$ at $R = 0$, this difference maps $\dot H^{(s;k),\mu_w}$ to $\mathcal Y_k$. So
$\mathcal X_k$ does not depend on $\mu\in D$. By III.G Theorem 5.1 (3),
$N_{\mathrm{sf}}(\mu):\mathcal X_k\to\mathcal Y_k$ is a holomorphic family of bijections, so
$\mu\mapsto N_{\mathrm{sf}}(\mu)^{-1}\in\mathcal L(\mathcal Y_k,\mathcal X_k)$ is holomorphic. So is
$\mu\mapsto R^{2+\mu}f\in\mathcal Y_k$. With Lemma 2.1, whose bound is linear in the norms,
$\mu\mapsto v^{-(s-\frac12)}W^\alpha\tilde h(\mu)\in L^\infty((0,1)\times\mathbb S^3)$ is holomorphic on $D$, and
$h(\mu) = v^\mu\tilde h(\mu)$.

(b) *The Mellin recursion.* Let $\chi\in C^\infty_c([0,1))$ equal 1 on $[0,\frac12]$, and
$\mathcal M(u)(\kappa) := \int_0^\infty v^{-\kappa}u(v)\frac{dv}v$. By (a), shrinking $D$,
$(\mu,\kappa)\mapsto\mathcal M(\chi h(\mu))(\kappa)$ is holomorphic on
$D\times\{\mathrm{Re}\,\kappa<\frac32-2\epsilon_0\}$ for some $\epsilon_0>0$. We have
$\mathcal M(v\partial_vu) = \kappa\mathcal M(u)$ and $\mathcal M(vu)(\kappa) = \mathcal M(u)(\kappa-1)$. Applying
$\mathcal M$ to (3.5) for $\chi h(\mu)$ gives
$$(\kappa-\mu)\big(\kappa-\tfrac32-s\big)\Pi_s\mathcal M(\chi h(\mu))(\kappa) = \tfrac12\Pi_s\tilde L(0)(\omega,\kappa
-1,\partial_\omega)\mathcal M(\chi h(\mu))(\kappa-1)+H_s(\mu,\kappa).\tag{3.6}$$
Here $H_s$ is the transform of $-\chi\Pi_sf/(2v)$ and of the commutator of the operators in (3.5) with $\chi$; it is
supported in $[\frac12,1)$ apart from the term vanishing to infinite order. So $H_s$ is entire in $\kappa$ and
holomorphic in $\mu\in D$. By induction on strips $\{\mathrm{Re}\,\kappa<\frac32-2\epsilon_0+j\}$, (3.6) continues
$\mathcal M(\chi h(\mu))(\kappa)$ meromorphically to $D\times\mathbb C$. Its singularities lie on
$\{\kappa\in\mu+\mathbb N_0\}\cup\{\kappa\in\frac32+\sigma+\mathbb N_0\}$, and it decays rapidly as
$|\mathrm{Im}\,\kappa|\to\infty$ on vertical lines away from them, since
$\mathcal M(\chi(v\partial_v)^Nh) = \kappa^N\mathcal M(\chi h)+\ldots$. By Mellin inversion and the circle integrals of
III.A Lemma 1.5, the expansion of $\chi h(\mu)$ at $v = 0$ is read off from the principal parts. A term
$c\,v^z(\log v)^k$ corresponds to the principal part $-k!\,c\,(\kappa-z)^{-k-1}$ at $\kappa = z$, since
$\mathcal M(\chi v^z)(\kappa) = -(\kappa-z)^{-1}+(\text{entire})$.

(c) *The $v^\mu$-coefficient vanishes on $\Omega$.* Let $\mu_0\in\Omega$. Choose $r\in(0,\frac12)$ smaller than the
distance from $\mu_0$ to $\frac32+\sigma+\mathbb N_0$, let $C := \{|\kappa-\mu_0| = r\}$, and let
$|\mu-\mu_0|<\frac r2$. Inside $C$, the only singularity of $\mathcal M(\chi h(\mu))$ is at $\kappa = \mu$. It is at
most simple: in (3.6) at $\kappa = \mu$, $\mathcal M(\chi h(\mu))(\mu-1)$ is finite, because
$\mu-1\notin\mu+\mathbb N_0$ and $\mu-1\notin\frac32+\sigma+\mathbb N_0$, as $\mu_0\in\Omega$. So
$$c(\mu) := \frac1{2\pi i}\oint_C\Pi_s\mathcal M(\chi h(\mu))(\kappa)\,d\kappa\tag{3.7}$$
is minus the coefficient of $v^\mu$ in $\Pi_sh(\mu)$, and is holomorphic in $\mu$, the integrand being jointly
holomorphic near $C$. If $\mathrm{Re}\,\mu<\frac32-2\epsilon_0$, $\mathcal M(\chi h(\mu))$ is holomorphic at
$\kappa = \mu$, so $c(\mu) = 0$. As $\Omega$ is connected and $c$ is intrinsically the residue at $\kappa = \mu$,
$c\equiv0$ on $\Omega$. Then (3.6) has no singularity at $\kappa = \mu$, so none at $\kappa\in\mu+\mathbb N_0$ either.
For $\lambda\in\Omega$ the induction of Step 2 therefore goes through with $\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda$ arbitrary. In Lemma
2.2 with $a = \lambda$, the possible term $c_1v^\lambda$ in $w_1$ equals $(\lambda-\frac32-s)$ times the
$v^\lambda$-coefficient of $\Pi_sh$, which is 0. And no logarithm arises at $\lambda$, as
$\lambda\notin\frac32+\sigma+\mathbb N_0\supset\pi_1\mathcal E^C$. So (1) holds for $\lambda\in\Omega$.

*Step 4: $\lambda\in(\frac32+\sigma+\mathbb N_0)\setminus\mathcal P$, no extra logarithms.* Choose $r\in(0,\frac12)$
such that the closed disc $\bar D_r(\lambda)$ meets $\Lambda_0$ only at $\lambda$. Let $C := \{|\kappa-\lambda| = r\}$
and $0<|\mu-\lambda|<\frac r2$. Then $\mu\in\Omega$, and by Step 3 the only singularity of
$\Pi_s\mathcal M(\chi h(\mu))$ inside $C$ is at $\kappa = \lambda$. Its order is at most some $k_s$ for all such $\mu$:
at $\kappa = \lambda$ the recursion (3.6) involves $\mu$ only through $(\kappa-\mu)^{-1}$, which is regular there. Its
Laurent coefficient of order $k_s$ is the coefficient of the generic expansion of $\Pi_sh(\mu)$, of the form found in
Step 3. $(\mu,\kappa)\mapsto\mathcal M(\chi h(\mu))(\kappa)$ is jointly holomorphic on
$D_{r/2}(\lambda)\times(\text{a neighbourhood of }C)$, by (a)-(b): the singular sets $\kappa = \mu$ and
$\frac32+\sigma+\mathbb N_0$ stay away from $C$. So for every $j\ge0$,
$$\oint_C(\kappa-\lambda)^j\,\Pi_s\mathcal M(\chi h(\mu))(\kappa)\,d\kappa\to\oint_C(\kappa
-\lambda)^j\,\Pi_s\mathcal M(\chi h(\lambda))(\kappa)\,d\kappa\qquad(\mu\to\lambda).\tag{3.8}$$
For $j\ge k_s$ the left side vanishes, so the pole of $\Pi_s\mathcal M(\chi h(\lambda))$ at $\kappa = \lambda$ has order
$\le k_s$. With $j = k_s-1$, its leading Laurent coefficient is the limit of the generic ones. So $\Pi_sh(\lambda)$
carries, at the exponent $\lambda$, no logarithm and no component beyond those that $\Pi_sh(\mu)$ carries. For
$\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda<\ell_I$ (respectively $<\ell$), $\lambda\in\frac32+\sigma_\Upsilon+\mathbb N_0 = \pi_1\mathcal
E^{\mathrm{tot}}_{\iota^+,\mathscr I}$ (respectively also $\lambda = \frac32+s$, $s\in\sigma_C$, for the second
statement). At such $\lambda$ the generic exponent structure is that of (3.2), and the remaining exponents of the
expansions are unaffected (Step 3). So (1) holds at $\lambda$. Exponents with real part beyond the order of (3.2) do not
enter.

*Step 5: finite regularity.* Let $f\in H^{k+d,\ell_I+1,\ell_K-2}_{\mathrm b}(\iota^+)$ and
$\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda<\ell_I$.

(i) *Near $v = 0$:* $v^{-2-\lambda}f\in\rho_I^{\ell_I-1-\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda}H^{k+d}_{\mathrm b}$ with
$\ell_I-1-\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda>-1$, so it is in $L^1$ near $v = 0$ with values in $H^{k+d}(\mathbb S^3)$. Its extension
$F$ by 0 lies in $H^{-\frac12-\delta}(\mathbb R;H^{k+d}(\mathbb S^3))\subset H^{-\frac12-\delta}(\mathbb R\times\mathbb
S^3)$ (the inclusion as in Lemma 2.1 (i) for negative order). Moreover $v\partial_vF$ is the extension of
$v\partial_v(v^{-2-\lambda}f)$: the boundary term $vu\varphi$ at $v = 0$ vanishes because
$|u|\le Cv^{\ell_I-1-\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda}$, by b-Sobolev embedding in $v$.

(ii) *Near $R = 0$:* $F\in R^{\ell_K+\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda}H^{k+d}_{\mathrm b}$. Choose $\delta>0$ and
$s := \min(\frac12,2-\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda)-\delta$ with $s-\frac12\notin\mathbb Z$, and
$\mu\in(\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda-1,\min(\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda,\ell_K+\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda))$, which is non-empty as
$\ell_K>-1$. Then $(s,\mu)$ is admissible, $F\in\dot H^{(s-1;k+d-d_1),\mu}_{\mathrm b;\mathrm b}$ with $d_1 = 0$
(as $s-1\le-\frac12-\delta$; carried in LOSS-D.md section 2b, item 6, D357-D358; the earlier text had "$d_1$ depending
on $s$"), and $\tilde h := N_{\mathrm{sf}}(\lambda)^{-1}F\in\dot H^{(s;k+d-d_1),\mu}_{\mathrm b;\mathrm b}$, vanishing on
$\{v<0\}$ as in Step 0.

(iii) *The expansion at $v = 0$:* Lemma 2.1 gives $|W^\alpha h|\le C\|f\|\,v^{\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda+s-\frac12}$ for
$|\alpha|\le k+d-d_1-m(s)$, hence the weighted $L^2$ bound with a loss $\delta$ in the weight. The induction of Step 2
then runs with the $L^2$ version of Lemma 2.2. Each step loses two b-derivatives (through $\tilde L(0)$), and the number
of steps is at most $\lceil\ell_I-\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda\rceil+2$. This fixes $d$ in terms of $\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda$ and
$\ell_I$. Here the $f$-term of $g$ stops the expansion at $\ell_I$. In the first application of Lemma 2.2, now with
$\gamma_0 = \mathrm{Re}\,\lambda+s-\frac12-\delta<\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda$, terms $c_\lambda(f)v^\lambda$ and, when
$\lambda\in\pi_1\mathcal E^C_{\iota^+,\mathscr I}$, $c'_\lambda(f)v^\lambda\log v$ may appear, with $c_\lambda$,
$c'_\lambda$ continuous linear functionals. By Steps 3-4, $c'_\lambda(f)$ and $c_\lambda(f)$ vanish (or $c_\lambda(f)$
is a coefficient permitted by (3.2), when $\lambda\in\pi_1\mathcal E^{\mathrm{tot}}_{\iota^+,\mathscr I}$) for
$f\in\dot C^\infty(\iota^+)$. That space is dense, so the same holds for all $f$.

(iv) *At $R = 0$:* the proof of III.A Lemma 1.4 applies at finite order. b-ellipticity gains two b-derivatives, and the
expansion terms have smooth coefficients (they come from residues of $N(A,\cdot)^{-1}$ and from $RA_1$, with
$\mathcal F = \emptyset$). It gives $h\in H^{k+d+2,(\mathcal E_K,\ell_K)}_{\mathrm b}$ near $R = 0$, as
$\ell_K\notin\mathrm{Re}\,\pi_1\mathcal E_K$. This proves (3.3), and the statement on $(v\partial_v-\frac32-\bar S)h$ is
the last application of Lemma 2.2 in (iii), with $\gamma = \ell_I$, for $\lambda\notin\mathcal P$.

*Meromorphy.* $f\mapsto F$ is holomorphic in $\lambda$, and
$\lambda\mapsto N_{\mathrm{sf}}(\lambda)^{-1}\in\mathcal L(\mathcal Y_k,\mathcal X_k)$ is finite-meromorphic (III.G
Theorem 5.1 (2), and (a) above). Multiplication by $v^\lambda$ is holomorphic into weights reduced by any $\delta>0$.
The expansion coefficients of (iii)-(iv) are holomorphic in $\lambda$ as well, although Lemma 2.2's decomposition is not
(its particular solutions have poles where $\lambda\in\pi_1G$). Each coefficient at an exponent $z$,
$\mathrm{Re}\,z<\ell_I$, is $\frac1{2\pi i}\oint(\kappa-z)^j\Pi_s\mathcal M(\chi h(\lambda))(\kappa)\,d\kappa$ over a
fixed small circle around $z$. It is holomorphic by the joint holomorphy of Step 3 (b), which for $f$ in the weighted
space holds on $\{\mathrm{Re}\,\kappa<\ell_I\}$: there $H_s$ is the transform of
$-\chi\Pi_sf/(2v)\in\rho_I^{\ell_I}H_{\mathrm b}$ and of terms supported in $[\frac12,1)$. The remainder is $h$ minus
the finite expansion (D240). It is holomorphic in the weaker weight in which $h(\lambda)$ is, and bounded in the
$\ell_I$-weighted space locally uniformly in $\lambda$, the constants of Steps 1-5 being built from
$N_{\mathrm{sf}}(\lambda)^{-1}$ and fixed-circle integrals. A locally bounded map into a Hilbert space that is
holomorphic into a larger space continuously containing it is holomorphic (W. Arendt and N. Nikolski, Vector-valued
holomorphic functions revisited, Math. Z. 234 (2000), 777-805, Theorem 3.1, with the functionals of the larger space as
the separating set) (D241). So (3.3) is finite-meromorphic on $\{\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda<\ell_I\}$. Its principal part at
$\lambda_0$ is obtained by expanding $R^{\pm\lambda}$ about $\lambda_0$: its Laurent coefficients are finite
combinations of $R^{-\lambda_0}(\log R)^iA_{-j}(\log R)^{i'}R^{2+\lambda_0}$, with $A_{-j}$ those of
$N_{\mathrm{sf}}^{-1}$. These have finite rank and do not depend on $(s,\mu)$, as the inverses agree (III.G Theorem 5.1
(2)); so they do not depend on $k$, $\ell_I$ or $\ell_K$. For $f\in\dot C^\infty(\iota^+)$, the coefficient of order $j$
is $\frac1{2\pi i}\oint(\lambda-\lambda_0)^{j-1}N_{\iota^+}(\lambda)^{-1}f\,d\lambda$ over a small circle in $\Omega$.
By (1), with expansion coefficients and remainders continuous in $\lambda$ on the circle, this lies in the space of (1).
The coefficients have finite rank and $\dot C^\infty(\iota^+)$ is dense, so their ranges lie there for all $f$.
$\square$

## 4. The boundary spectrum ([Hin26] Definition 9.6, Remark 9.7)

**Definition 4.1 ([Hin26] Definition 9.6 at $n = 4$).** $\mathrm{spec}_{\iota^+}(\bar L)$ is the set of poles of
$\lambda\mapsto N_{\iota^+}(\lambda)^{-1}$ acting on $\dot C^\infty(\iota^+;S^2T^*)$; that is, of the $\lambda_0$ at
which $\lambda\mapsto N_{\iota^+}(\lambda)^{-1}f\in C^\infty((0,\infty)\times\mathbb S^3)$ fails to be holomorphic for
some $f$. $\mathrm{Spec}_{\iota^+}(\bar L)$ is the set of pairs $(\lambda,k)$ with $\lambda$ a pole of order $k+1$.

**Lemma 4.2.** (a) $\mathrm{spec}_{\iota^+}(\bar L)\subset\mathcal P\subset\{\mathrm{Re}\,z\ge2\}$, with finitely many
points in every half plane $\{\mathrm{Re}\,z\le C\}$. (b) For $\ell_I$, $\ell_K$ as in Theorem 3.1 (2),
$\mathrm{Spec}_{\iota^+}(\bar L)\cap\{\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda<\ell_I\}$ is the divisor of (3.3) on
$H^{\infty,\ell_I+1,\ell_K-2}_{\mathrm b}$. (c) ([Hin26] Remark 9.7 at $n = 4$.) Let
$\lambda_0\in\mathcal P\setminus\mathrm{spec}_{\iota^+}(\bar L)$, and suppose the principal part of
$N_{\mathrm{sf}}(\lambda)^{-1}$ at $\lambda_0$ does not vanish on the extensions by 0 of $\dot C^\infty(\iota^+)$. Then
$\ker N_{\mathrm{sf}}(\lambda_0)$ contains a non-zero distribution supported in $\{v = 0\}$. By Lemma 4.3 this forces
$\lambda_0\in\frac52+\mathrm{spec}\,\bar S+\mathbb N_0$.

*Proof.* (a) $N_{\iota^+}(\lambda)^{-1} = R^{-\lambda}N_{\mathrm{sf}}(\lambda)^{-1}R^{2+\lambda}$ is holomorphic off
$\mathcal P$ (Theorem 3.1). $\mathcal P\subset\{\mathrm{Re}\ge2\}$ with finitely many points in each half plane, by
III.G Theorem 5.1 (2). (b) $\dot C^\infty(\iota^+)$ is dense in $H^{\infty,\ell_I+1,\ell_K-2}_{\mathrm b}$ (in its
Fréchet topology). The Laurent coefficients of (3.3) are continuous and of finite rank, and they agree with those on
$\dot C^\infty$, so the pole orders agree. (c) Let
$N_{\mathrm{sf}}(\lambda)^{-1} = \sum_{i=1}^mA_{-i}(\lambda-\lambda_0)^{-i}+(\text{holomorphic})$, and let $j$ be the
largest $i$ with $A_{-i}F\ne0$ for some $F$ in the extensions by 0 of $\dot C^\infty(\iota^+)$. Write
$R^{2+\lambda}f = \sum_i\frac{(\lambda-\lambda_0)^i}{i!}(\log R)^iR^{2+\lambda_0}f$ and use
$(\log R)^iR^{2+\lambda_0}f\in\dot C^\infty(\iota^+)$. Then the coefficient of $(\lambda-\lambda_0)^{-j}$ in
$R^{-\lambda}N_{\mathrm{sf}}(\lambda)^{-1}R^{2+\lambda}f$ is $R^{-\lambda_0}A_{-j}(R^{2+\lambda_0}f)$. As
$\lambda_0\notin\mathrm{spec}_{\iota^+}(\bar L)$, this vanishes on $\{v>0\}$.
$A_{-j}F = \frac1{2\pi i}\oint(\lambda-\lambda_0)^{j-1}N_{\mathrm{sf}}(\lambda)^{-1}F\,d\lambda$ is supported in
$\{v\ge0\}$ (Step 0), so $A_{-j}F$ is supported in $\{v = 0\}$. The coefficient of $(\lambda-\lambda_0)^{-j}$ in
$N_{\mathrm{sf}}(\lambda)N_{\mathrm{sf}}(\lambda)^{-1} = \mathrm{Id}$ is
$N_{\mathrm{sf}}(\lambda_0)A_{-j}+\partial_\lambda
N_{\mathrm{sf}}(\lambda_0)A_{-j-1}+\frac12\partial_\lambda^2N_{\mathrm{sf}}(\lambda_0)A_{-j-2} = 0$ ($N_{\mathrm{sf}}$
is quadratic in $\lambda$). Applied to such $F$, with $A_{-i}F = 0$ for $i>j$, this gives
$N_{\mathrm{sf}}(\lambda_0)A_{-j}F = 0$. $\square$

**Lemma 4.3 (kernels supported at the light cone).** If $0\ne u\in\ker N_{\mathrm{sf}}(\lambda)$, in $\mathcal X$ for an
admissible pair, is supported in $\{v = 0\}$, then $\lambda\in\frac52+\mathrm{spec}\,\bar S+\mathbb N_0$.

*Proof.* A distribution supported in $\{0\}\times\mathbb S^3$ and lying in $H^s$ is
$u = \sum_{i=0}^j\delta^{(i)}(v)\otimes a_i$, with distributions $a_i$ on $\mathbb S^3$; take $a_j\ne0$. With
$v\delta^{(i)} = -i\delta^{(i-1)}$, so that $v\partial_v\delta^{(i)} = -(i+1)\delta^{(i)}$,
$$N_{\mathrm{sf}}(\lambda)\big(\delta^{(i)}\otimes a\big) = -2\,\delta^{(i+1)}\otimes\big(\lambda-i-\tfrac52
-\bar S\big)a+\delta^{(i)}\otimes\tilde L(0)(\omega,\lambda-i-1,\partial_\omega)a.\tag{4.1}$$
(*Computed:* `sf5.py` S10, through the transpose on a general polynomial. The control with the factor
$\lambda-i-\frac32-\bar S$ fails.) The coefficient of $\delta^{(j+1)}$ in $N_{\mathrm{sf}}(\lambda)u = 0$ is
$-2(\lambda-j-\frac52-\bar S)a_j = 0$. By Lemma 1.1, $(\lambda-j-\frac52-s)\Pi_sa_j = 0$ for every $s$, and
$\Pi_sa_j\ne0$ for some $s$; so $\lambda = j+\frac52+s$. $\square$

At $n = 3$ the same computation gives $2+\mathrm{spec}\,\bar S+\mathbb N_0$. This fits the source's observation that
(9.5) is available for $\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda<2$. Such poles are invisible on $\iota^+$ and belong to the radiation field
([BVW15, Remarks 7.5 and 8.2], as the source says).
