## 2. The 26 at source

- **Baez, "How Many Fundamental Constants Are There?" (math.ucr.edu/home/baez/constants.html, READ).** "my best guess is
  26". The list, verbatim in order: the masses of the up, down, charmed, strange, top and bottom quarks; "4 numbers for
  the Kobayashi-Maskawa matrix"; the masses of the electron, electron neutrino, muon, mu neutrino, tau and tau neutrino;
  "4 numbers for the Pontecorvo-Maki-Nakagawa-Sakata matrix"; "the mass of the Higgs boson"; "the expectation value of
  the Higgs field"; the U(1), SU(2) and strong coupling constants; and "the cosmological constant". That is 6 + 4 + 6 +
  4 + 2 + 3 + 1 = 26.
- **Not yet READ:** Baez's passage on how a mass becomes dimensionless (the unit), and whether a strong CP angle
  counts.
- **Tegmark, Aguirre, Rees and Wilczek, astro-ph/0511774 (READ, abstract):** "We identify 31 dimensionless physical
  constants required by particle physics and cosmology". This is a different count; cosmology's parameters are added.
- **Adams, 1902.03928 (READ, abstract):** the constants "must fall within a range of values in order for the universe
  to develop astrophysical structures and ultimately support life ... viable universes exist over a range of parameter
  space, which is delineated herein". These are the anthropic ranges.
