## 1. The theorem

**Hypotheses.**
- (H1) General relativity in five dimensions with the bulk's cosmological constant Lambda (established).
- (H2) Inside the horizon the README's hole is vacuum and spherically symmetric: the record's five-dimensional forms at
  both ends, as transition5q uses them.
- (H3) Quantum mechanics holds for the interior's geometry: its states form a Hilbert space and evolve unitarily in a
  physical clock (Stone's theorem: a strongly continuous unitary group has a self-adjoint generator).
- (H4) The clock is the time conjugate to Lambda (Henneaux and Teitelboim's form, classically general relativity).
- (H5) The state is symmetric under time reversal (273 (a), the Kruskal isometry); the mass is positive (E(N) > 0).

**Theorem QB.** Under (H1)-(H5), with the self-adjoint extensions of section 2 (L5):
1. the evolution is unitary and no state reaches the singularity: every state is reflected at r = 0 from the black
   hole's branch into the white hole's;
2. the white hole has the black hole's mass, and the quantum region makes no net energy;
3. there are exactly two horizons, the black hole's and the white hole's, and no inner or Cauchy horizon;
4. the curvature is bounded;
5. the proper time across is finite, 2 r_h to within 0.3 Y_min/r_h^2 plus 3 (sigma_k/k_c)^2;
6. before the bounce, the quantum radius departs from the classical one by 0.2736 (x/x_b)^2 + O((x/x_b)^4);
7. 1-6 hold for every place x_b of the bounce well inside the horizon (x_b >> x_h), so no row on 273 depends on
   x_b = 1.

## 2. The lemmas and their proofs

**L1 (the interior is a minisuperspace; proved).** By Birkhoff's theorem in five dimensions with Lambda, a
spherically symmetric vacuum metric -e^(2a(t,r)) dt^2 + e^(2b(t,r)) dr^2 + r^2 dOmega_3^2 is Schwarzschild-AdS5
(bounce_close C2, computed exactly):
- E_tr = -3 d_t b/r forces d_t b = 0;
- the remaining equations give e^(-2b) = f = 1 - mu/r^2 - Lambda r^2/6 and a + b equal to a function of t alone.

Inside the horizon (f < 0) the Killing field d_t is spacelike, so the interior is homogeneous: exactly the
Kantowski-Sachs metric ds^2 = -(N^2/xi) dt^2 + (xi/Y) dz^2 + Y dOmega_3^2, with Y = r^2 and xi = -r^2 f. No degree of
freedom of the spherically symmetric sector is dropped.

**L2 (the reduced dynamics; proved).** For this metric, sqrt(-g)(R - 2 Lambda) = L + dF/dt with
L = -(3/2N) xi' Y' + 6N - 2 Lambda N Y. Proof: the Euler-Lagrange operator of the difference vanishes identically
(bounce_qm Q1, computed). In one independent variable, a Lagrangian whose Euler-Lagrange expressions vanish identically
is a total derivative.

Add Henneaux and Teitelboim's term Lambda T'. Then:
- Lambda is a momentum, conjugate to the clock T, and varying Lambda gives T' = 2 N Y = 2 sqrt(-g).
- The Legendre transform gives H = N(-(2/3) pi_xi pi_Y - 6 + 2 Lambda Y) = 2NY (H_S + Lambda), with
  H_S = -pi_xi pi_Y/(3Y) - 3/Y.
- With the lapse 2NY = 1 the constraint is pi_T + H_S = 0, so the clock's evolution is generated by H_S
  (bounce_qm Q1).

**L3 (the ordering; proved).** The kinetic term defines the minisuperspace metric g = 12 Y dxi dY. With w = 6 Y^2 this
is dxi dw, null coordinates of the flat plane (bounce_proof P1). Covariance under changes of minisuperspace coordinate
fixes the ordering as the Laplace-Beltrami operator plus a multiple of the minisuperspace curvature (Hawking and Page;
Gielen and Ried section 3, READ), and that curvature is zero. So H_S = (1/3Y) d_xi d_Y - 3/Y, uniquely, and the inner
product is <psi, phi> = int dxi int_0^inf 6Y dY conj(psi) phi.

**L4 (the eigenfunctions; proved).** psi_(Lambda,k) = exp i[k xi + (3/k)(Lambda Y^2/2 - 3Y) + Lambda T] satisfies
i d_T psi = H_S psi. Substituting gives (1/3Y)(ik)(i(3/k)(Lambda Y - 3)) - 3/Y = -Lambda (bounce_qm Q3).

At k = 3 and xi0 = mu, the stationary-phase trajectory in k is the classical solution
xi = xi0 + (3/k^2)(Lambda Y^2/2 - 3Y) = -Y f (Schwarzschild-AdS5, bounce_qm Q2). It reaches Y = 0 at a finite T:
Y^2 = (2k/3)(T0 - T).

**L5 (self-adjointness and unitarity; proved).**
1. *H_S is not self-adjoint.* The solutions of H_S phi = +-i phi are
   phi = int dk gamma(k) exp i[k xi - (3/k)(+-i Y^2/2 + 3Y)], with |phi_k|^2 = exp(+-3Y^2/k). These are normalisable
   in the Y direction exactly when +-1/k < 0. The +i space is therefore the functions gamma on k < 0 and the -i space
   those on k > 0, with norm int |k| |gamma|^2 dk. The two spaces are isomorphic (gamma(k) -> gamma(-k)), so the
   deficiency indices are equal and nonzero, and self-adjoint extensions exist (von Neumann) (bounce_qm Q4).
2. *The extensions used.* For each odd real chi, the boundary condition alpha(Lambda, -k) = e^(i chi(k)) alpha(Lambda, k)
   (Gielen and Ried's multiplicative family, READ eq. 63). Other unitary maps between the deficiency spaces exist;
   the theorem is stated for this family.
3. *Unitarity, directly.* Write psi = int dLambda dk alpha(Lambda, k) e^(i Lambda T) e^(i k xi) e^(i F_k(Y, Lambda))
   and alpha~_k(s) = int dLambda alpha(Lambda, k) e^(i Lambda s)/(2 pi).
   - The xi integral gives 2 pi delta(k1 - k2).
   - With y = 3Y^2/(2k), 6Y dY = 2|k| dy, and y runs over (0, inf) for k > 0 and over (-inf, 0) for k < 0.
   - Hence N(T) = int dk (|k|/3) int over sign(k) R+ of |alpha~_k(T + y)|^2 dy, up to a constant.
   - The k > 0 term is int_T^inf |alpha~_k(s)|^2 ds and the k < 0 term is int_(-inf)^T |alpha~_(-k)(s)|^2 ds.
   - Their sum is independent of T if and only if |alpha~_(-k)| = |alpha~_k|, which the boundary condition gives.

   The black hole's branch alone keeps int_T^inf |alpha~|^2 ds, which falls to zero as the packet reaches Y = 0
   (bounce_qm Q5: 1, 0.5, 1e-17 at T = -40, 0, 40). Unitarity therefore requires the white hole's branch, and the
   boundary condition is the reflection at r = 0. This proves 1.

**L6 (the expectation values; proved).** For alpha = N exp(-i beta k/2) G_k(|k|) exp(-(Lambda - Lambda_c)^2/(2 sigma^2)):
- *The Lambda integrals* are Gaussian: |alpha~_k(s)|^2 is proportional to exp(-sigma^2 s^2).
- *The integral identity.* I_a(T) = int |y|^a exp(-sigma^2 (y+T)^2) dy = Gamma((1+a)/2) sigma^(-1-a)
  1F1(-a/2, 1/2, -sigma^2 T^2).

  Proof. Write z = sigma^2 T^2. Keep the even part:
  I_a = 2 e^(-z) int_0^inf y^a e^(-sigma^2 y^2) cosh(2 sigma^2 T y) dy. Expand the cosh and integrate term by term
  with int_0^inf y^(a+2n) e^(-sigma^2 y^2) dy = Gamma((a+1)/2 + n) sigma^(-a-1-2n)/2. Using (2n)! = 4^n n! (1/2)_n:
  I_a = sigma^(-a-1) Gamma((a+1)/2) e^(-z) Sum_n ((a+1)/2)_n z^n/(n! (1/2)_n)
      = sigma^(-a-1) Gamma((a+1)/2) e^(-z) 1F1((a+1)/2, 1/2, z),
  and Kummer's transformation gives the stated form (bounce_proof P2: quadrature and series agree to 2e-30).
- *The radius.* With Y = sqrt(2|k||y|/3):
  <Y>(T) = sqrt(2/3) <|k|^(1/2)> g_(1/2)(T), where g_a(T) = I_a(T) sigma/sqrt(pi) and
  <f> = int |k| G_k^2 f/int |k| G_k^2.
- *The metric function.* xi acts on the k-representation as i d_k, through the delta' of the xi integral. Integrating
  by parts, the phase exp(-i beta k/2) gives beta/2, the imaginary parts cancel, and d_k F_k gives the classical
  relation:
  <xi>(T) = beta/2 + Lambda_c <1/|k|> g_1(T) - 9 sqrt(2/3) <|k|^(-3/2)> g_(1/2)(T).
- *The check.* Both closed forms agree with a brute-force wavefunction on a (xi, Y) grid to 1e-4 at every T
  (bounce_qm Q5-Q6).

**L7 (the bounce; proved).**
- *Monotone.* d/dz 1F1(-a/2, 1/2, -z) = a 1F1(1 - a/2, 3/2, -z) = a e^(-z) 1F1((1+a)/2, 3/2, z), and every
  coefficient of the last series is positive (bounce_proof P3). So each g_a is strictly increasing in |T|, and
  <Y>(T) >= <Y>(0) = sqrt(2/3) <|k|^(1/2)> Gamma(3/4)/(sqrt(pi) sigma^(1/2)) > 0.
- *Classical on both sides.* As |T| -> inf, 1F1(-a/2, 1/2, -z) ~ (Gamma(1/2)/Gamma((1+a)/2)) z^(a/2), so <Y> tends to
  the classical branch on both sides.
- *Black hole before, white hole after.* The black hole's share of the norm is
  int_T^inf exp(-sigma^2 s^2) ds/(sqrt(pi)/sigma),
  which tends to 1 as T -> -inf and to 0 as T -> +inf.

**L8 (the mass; proved).**
- *Classical.* The mass function m = Y(1 + xi Y Y'^2) - Lambda Y^2/6 (r^2(1 - grad r . grad r) - Lambda r^4/6 in these
  variables, with g^TT = -4 xi Y^2) equals xi0 k^2/9 = mu on every classical solution (bounce_close C3, exact).
- *In and out.* <Y> and <xi> depend on T through T^2 only (L6), so for the state of L6 every asymptotic quantity is
  the same at T -> -inf and T -> +inf. The departures of g_a from the classical forms vanish as |T| -> inf, so
  xi0_in = xi0_out = beta/2, and m_in = m_out (bounce_close C3: equal to 1e-10).
- *A general state.* With alpha = A(|k|) e^(i theta(k)), stationary phase gives xi0(k) = -theta'(k). Differentiating
  the boundary condition gives xi0(-k) + xi0(k) = chi'(k). So xi0_out = chi'(k) - xi0_in, and the mass is kept if and
  only if xi0_in = chi'(k_c)/2 -- the time-symmetric state of (H5). An asymmetric state changes it (bounce_qm Q6;
  bounce_close C3: 70 in, 130 out, made at the bounce), which 386 (only information crosses) and 183 exclude.
- *No net energy.* <Y>'(0) = 0 by evenness, so m_eff(0) = <Y>(0), about 3e-6 mu: the quantum region carries the whole
  mass as negative effective energy and returns it in full -- 273 (c)'s pairing.

This proves 2.

**L9 (two horizons only; proved).** For Lambda_c <= 0, both T-dependent terms of <xi> are strictly decreasing in |T|
(L7), and <xi> -> -inf (g_(1/2) grows like |T|^(1/2)). So <xi> has exactly two zeros when <xi>(0) > 0, that is when
the bounce lies inside the horizon, and none otherwise. A third horizon would need a third zero. This proves 3
(bounce_qm Q7).

**L10 (bounded curvature; proved).**
- *The form of K.* The effective metric is -dT^2/(4 <xi> <Y>^2) + (<xi>/<Y>) dz^2 + <Y> dOmega_3^2. Its Kretschmann
  scalar is a polynomial in <xi>, <xi>', <xi>'', <Y>, <Y>', <Y>'' divided by <Y>^2 alone (bounce_proof P5), so it is
  finite wherever <Y> > 0, the horizons included.
- *Bounded everywhere.* <Y> >= <Y>(0) > 0 (L7), all the functions are smooth (entire in T), and as |T| -> inf K tends to
  the classical exterior's value. So K is bounded on the whole line.
- *Its maximum.* In the semiclassical limit the maximum is 0.7424 of the classical value at <Y>(0) (bounce_close C5,
  bounce_proof P6: the same from x_b = 0.3 to 100; a computed constant).

This proves 4.

**L11 (finite crossing time; proved, with a computed constant).**
- *Finite.* tau = int dT/(2 sqrt(<xi>) <Y>) over <xi> > 0. For T != 0, <xi>'(T) = 2 sigma^2 T (d/dz terms) has the
  sign of -T and is nonzero, so the horizons are simple zeros. The integrand diverges there only like
  |T - T_h|^(-1/2), which is integrable.
- *Its value.* tau/2 r_h = 1 - 0.25-0.3 Y_min/r_h^2 - O((sigma_k/k_c)^2) (bounce_qm Q9, bounce_proof P6; computed).

This proves 5.

**L12 (the departure before the bounce; proved).** DLMF 13.7.1, the second sum, at z = sigma^2 T^2 -> inf:
1F1(-1/4, 1/2, -z) = (Gamma(1/2)/Gamma(3/4)) z^(1/4)(1 - 1/(16z) + O(z^-2)). The first-order coefficient is
a(a - b + 1) = (-1/4)(1/4), with z^(-s) and no alternating sign (bounce_proof P4: -0.06250 at z = 1e4).

The bounce sits at <Y>(0)^2 = (2/3) <|k|^(1/2)>^2 Gamma(3/4)^2/(pi sigma), and the classical curvature variable is
x proportional to 1/Y^2. Hence x/x_b = Gamma(3/4)^2/(pi sigma |T|), and |delta Y|/Y = 1/(16 sigma^2 T^2) =
(x/x_b)^2/(16 (Gamma(3/4)^2/pi)^2) = 0.2736 (x/x_b)^2. This proves 6 (bounce_close C4).

**L13 (the place; proved).** L5-L11 hold for every sigma and therefore for every x_b. Only two conditions involve the
place:
- the bounce inside the horizon, <xi>(0) > 0, which in the semiclassical limit means Y_min < r_h^2, that is
  x_b > x_h = sqrt(72) l5^2/r_h^2;
- the exterior and the crossing classical to the stated accuracy, which needs x_b >> x_h (L11, L12).

bounce_proof P6 computes 1-6 for x_b = 0.3, 1, 3, 10 and 100 with x_h = 8.5e-10. The record's anchors are
x_h = 1.6e-9 (small end) and 1.2e-40 (large end).

So no row needs x_b = 1. What the rows need is that quantum gravity acts only well inside the horizon:
- effective field theory with order-one coefficients gives x_b >= 0.27 (Donoghue, gr-qc/9405057, READ: "the
  expectation that Lambda_grav is of order of the Planck mass would correspond to c_1, c_2 ~ O(1)");
- the candidate theory gives x_b = 1 (transition5q).

This proves 7.

**L14 (the clock; proved).**
- *Monotone.* Along the interior dT/dtau = 2 sqrt(xi) Y > 0 (from g_TT = -1/(4 xi Y^2)), so T is a monotone function of
  proper time.
- *Finite at the singularity, unbounded overall.* At Lambda = 0, T_left = (2 r_h tau - tau^2)^2/2 with tau the proper
  time left (bounce_close C1). Globally T is the five-volume and runs over the whole line.
- *Hence slow.* It is a slow clock, not a reparametrisation of a fast one: Gielen and Menendez-Pidal, 2205.15387, READ,
  "the clock variable must be valued over the entire real line"; "unitarity for a clock that encounters a classical
  singularity at finite time implies quantum singularity resolution".

## 4. What is theorized, and why the status is COMPUTED-THEORIZED

- **(H3) beyond its tested range.** Quantum mechanics holds for gravity where effective field theory applies
  (Donoghue: "gravity is a well behaved quantum field theory at ordinary energies"). At the bounce, effective field
  theory's expansion parameter is of order one, and no established theory has been tested there. Unitarity is the
  postulate the record already requires of the passage (418, 491); its holding at Planckian curvature is the
  theorized step.
- **The minisuperspace off symmetry.** Within spherical symmetry the truncation is exact (L1). Off symmetry, the local
  homogeneity of a generic spacelike singularity is the BKL conjecture (READ through 2409.03006).
- **The clock and the extension.** The record requires a slow clock (L14). The bulk's clock is one. The family of L5 is
  a choice among unitary extensions (Gielen and Ried's), and the time-symmetric state is the record's 273 (a).
- **The place.** That it lies well inside the horizon rests on effective field theory's natural coefficients, which
  are unmeasured (bounded only by 1e74).

Every computation above is proved or exactly checked, apart from three numbers that are computed rather than derived:
the constants 0.7424 and 0.25-0.3 and the 1e-4 grid agreement. None of these bears on 1-7 qualitatively.

**L15 (no Baez constant enters QB except through ell; proved).** QB's equations are L1-L5: the vacuum Einstein equations
