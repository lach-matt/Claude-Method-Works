## 2. The route the literature now carries: a semiclassical black-to-white transition (READ)

**Barceló, Boyanov, Carballo-Rubio and Garay, 2011.07331.** Quantum fields on the collapse, with their backreaction:
- "the inner horizon has a tendency to evaporate outward much more quickly than the outer one evaporates inward
  through the Hawking effect".
- On collapse: "the final stages of the Oppenheimer-Snyder collapse correspond to a value n=1", and "For n≥1 the
  semiclassical backreaction always ends up overcoming the contribution of the classical background, resulting in
  a bounce".
- It happens "well before either the surface gravity or the radial position of the inner horizon get close to the
  Planck scale".

**Boyanov, Hilditch and Semião, 2506.04845.** A full nonlinear self-consistent evolution:
- "the outer horizon of the trapped region contracts, and the inner horizon expands, ultimately pushing past
  extremality and evaporating the trapped region completely".
- "an anti-trapped region forms beneath the trapped one … settling to a size similar to that of the original BH".
- "A black-to-white-hole transition is thus obtained from purely semiclassical dynamics."

**2608.03538** gives the analytic account: the anti-trapped region is "a generic consequence of the amplification of
negative energy fluxes".

**Limits stated by the papers themselves:**
- The Polyakov (s-wave) approximation is used throughout.
- The anti-trapped region is likely "unstable under additional perturbations".
- The scales studied are not astrophysical.

**The condition the mechanism needs.** It is avoided "if the classical trajectory of the inner horizon goes to zero
quickly". The inner trapping horizon must linger.
