## What follows

- **E1. A horizon takes all that crosses it, without being fed.** It takes everything, not a selection; what brings the
  information to it is a separate question. *H-CORRIDOR-TAKES has a physical form in READ physics.* Capacity is no
  obstacle: the board's constants give 1.38×10⁶⁹ bits/m², matching Bousso, so a body needs only 10⁻⁴¹ m².
- **E2. Observation defines a horizon.** Each position has its own. *H-OBSERVED-NOT-LOADED has a READ counterpart.*
- **E3. One horizon, two sides: position 2 incorporates.**
  - What crosses a horizon leaves the view of observers on the side it *leaves*. It enters the region the horizon
    *encloses*, and an observer there receives it. One horizon serves both positions: position 1's horizon is position
    2's enclosure.
  - The horizon grows by what it takes (the area theorem, S = A/4). The information is incorporated, not destroyed.
    *That is your "it is incorporating that information into itself".*
  - For an illustrative 1 m enclosure: incorporating a body's 9.5×10²⁷ bits adds 7×10⁻⁴² m² of area and needs at least
    **17 J** at the enclosure's temperature, half of Bekenstein's floor. The enclosure's own mass is large (7×10²⁶ kg), but
    it is positive.
  - **To view itself as a whole, the enclosure needs a mirror,** exactly as you said. In Hayden and Preskill the rapid
    return requires prior entanglement.
    - A few qubits come back in the scrambling time, 3×10⁻⁷ s here.
    - A whole body's information comes back only in about **3×10¹⁹ s, roughly 70 Hubble times**.
    - These are their order-of-magnitude estimates. *Seeing a part is fast, and seeing the whole is slow.*
  - **Costs:** capture is one-way, position 2 cannot signal back out, and the information still reaches the horizon at no
    more than light speed.
- **E4. Expansion and contraction is one warp geometry, and it is not where the price sits.**
  - Computed from Alcubierre's own metric: expansion +4 behind and −4 ahead, at his Figure 1 parameters (only the signs
    carry meaning).
  - **The pattern is optional; the price is not.** Natário shows the pattern is not necessary for warp transport, but
    negative energy is.
  - **The negative energy is not in the contraction.** Natário's formula reproduces the printed energy density exactly
    (check 4), and in it expansion and contraction add *positive* energy. The density is the same behind and ahead, zero
    on the axis where the expansion is strongest, and largest at the sides where the expansion is zero.
  - So the negative energy belongs to the faster-than-light transport, not to drawing in.
  - The board's Pfenning–Ford figure, for their own 100 m example at light speed with the thickest wall the quantum
    inequality allows, is a floor: at least **8.5×10⁷⁹ J** of negative energy.
- **E5. Expansion alone takes, but it delivers no body.**
  - A positive vacuum energy whose horizon around position 1 lies at Proxima's distance needs **8.9×10⁹ J/m³**. The
    region then holds 2.4×10⁶⁰ J, about 10¹³ solar masses: the mass whose Schwarzschild radius is that distance.
  - Comoving bodies keep their comoving separation, so nothing is delivered. A pattern starting 1 m away crosses position
    1's horizon after about 160 years.
  - Alcubierre's "relative speed" between comoving observers is a speed defined between two positions, not a local one.
    *That is a READ counterpart of H-POSITION-RELATIVE-SPEED and of your "Expansion".*
- **E6. But a signal can be taken from position 1 and observed at position 2.**
  - In an expanding region whose horizon radius lies between one and two times the distance, a signal from position 1
    reaches position 2 *after* position 2 has passed beyond position 1's horizon (checked: emitted at separation 0.75 of
    the horizon radius, it arrives at scale factor 4; the horizon passes at 1.33).
  - Position 1's horizon takes it out of view, and position 2 observes it, with positive energy only (2.2–9.0×10⁹ J/m³).
  - It arrives at 1.85 times the light time: no speed gain.
- **E7. Whether a detector at position 2 registers it** is one local event for every observer. Where the information *is*
  can differ between inside and outside; complementarity treats that as a hypothesis.

## For M

  The corridor's speed advantage still has to come from the higher dimension (the two planes), not from horizons in our
  plane.

  positive energy; the negative energy belongs to faster-than-light transport itself, whatever its shape (Natário).
- **So the question narrows to one:** can the two-plane corridor supply the faster route while horizons supply the taking
  and the incorporating, without the transport's negative energy?
