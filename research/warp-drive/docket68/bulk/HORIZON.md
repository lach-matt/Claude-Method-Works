## What follows

- **E1. A horizon takes without being fed.** Whatever crosses a horizon is taken, with no loading act.
  *H-CORRIDOR-TAKES has a physical form in READ physics.*
  - Capacity is no obstacle. From S = A/4, the board's constants give **1.38×10⁶⁹ bits/m²**, matching Bousso's figure
    (check 1).
  - A body's 9.5×10²⁷ to 1.1×10²⁹ bits need a horizon area of only 10⁻⁴¹ m².
- **E2. Observation defines a horizon.** Jacobson defines a horizon as the boundary of what an observer can see, and
  Bousso and Crispino et al. show it depends on the observer. Each position has its own. *H-OBSERVED-NOT-LOADED has a
  READ counterpart: what an observer can see fixes the horizon that takes.*
- **E3. The direction matters.** What crosses an observer's horizon leaves *that* observer's view.
  - So position 2 cannot receive through its own horizon, in the sense the sources use.
  - The consistent reading of your exit uses two horizons: position 1's horizon takes the information out of position
    1's view, and the information arrives within position 2's view. That needs space to be drawn toward position 2.
  - Hayden–Preskill's return goes back out through the same horizon, and it needs prior entanglement and decoding. No
    READ source has a second horizon receiving what a first one took. *That is a limit of the sources, not a refutation
    of your hypothesis.*
- **E4. Expansion takes, contraction draws in: this is Alcubierre's warp pattern.**
  - Expansion where the information leaves and contraction where it arrives is exactly the warp metric. Computed from
    Alcubierre's own metric, the expansion is +4 behind and −4 ahead, and it equals his eq. 12 (check 3).
  - In his words, the ship is "pushed away ... and pulled towards a distant star by spacetime itself", with nothing
    moving locally faster than light. *That is a READ counterpart of H-EXPANSION, and of H-NO-SPEED's no-travel.*
  - **Its price is negative energy.** The board's own Pfenning–Ford figure, for a 100 m bubble at light speed with the
    thinnest wall quantum physics allows, is **−8.5×10⁷⁹ J**.
  - *Your picture arrives back at the board's first subject, the warp drive, from the other direction.*
- **E5. Expansion alone takes, but it does not deliver.**
  - A positive vacuum energy whose horizon sits between here and Proxima needs **8.9×10⁹ J/m³**. That is finite and
    positive, but 1.7×10¹⁹ times today's dark energy.
  - Expansion carries everything apart, though. Comoving positions keep their comoving separation, so nothing is
    delivered to position 2.
  - A pattern starting 1 m from position 1 crosses its horizon only after **38 light-crossing times, about 160 years**.
  - Drawing in is the contraction half, and in E4 that is what costs negative energy.
- **E6. What both positions agree on.** The description of a horizon depends on the observer; scalar facts do not.
  Two observing horizons can describe one crossing differently, but whether the information arrives is one fact for
  both. That is where your two-perspective tension (H-TWO-PERSPECTIVE-TENSION) and position-relative speed meet E3.

## For M

- **Your first half is READ physics.** A horizon takes without being fed, and an observer's view is what defines a
  horizon. "We are being observed, not loading" has a real counterpart.
- **The exit is where it gets hard.** In the sources, what crosses an observer's horizon is lost to that observer, so
  position 2 cannot draw it in through its own horizon in that sense. What fits is two horizons: position 1's takes it out
  of position 1's view, and space must be drawn toward position 2 for it to arrive there.
- **Expansion taking and contraction drawing in is Alcubierre's warp drive.** Space expands where you leave and contracts
  where you arrive, and nothing moves faster than light locally. That is your expansion and your no-travel. But its price
  is negative energy, the same wall the board has met from the start.
- **Expansion on its own costs only positive energy, but it delivers nothing.** It carries the information away from
  position 1, never toward position 2.
- **So the open question is sharp:** can the drawing-in half (contraction at position 2) be had without negative energy,
  for instance through the higher dimension (the two planes) rather than in our own plane? That is where your corridor
  and the warp pattern would have to meet.
