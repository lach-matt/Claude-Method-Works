## Which values can be exact

A README can carry two kinds of continuous value about a molecule.

- **(P) a parameter the copy is built around, such as its equilibrium shape.**
  - In the simplest picture (point nuclei, Born–Oppenheimer, α shared) it is the same in every universe.
  - So it can be exact by construction, setting aside small corrections from nuclear size and from mₑ/M.
- **(S) a state value: where the copy's nuclei actually are.**
  - Quantum mechanics forbids an exact one. Pinning a nucleus to a width d costs kinetic energy ħ²/(2md²) (the
    uncertainty principle; standard, not READ here).
  - **For the hydrogen molecule, pinning the nuclei better than 3.0 pm costs more than the bond itself** (V1;
    D_e = 4.7446 eV, standard, not READ). The molecule would come apart.
    - **Control:** at that width the cost equals the bond energy exactly.
  - At a nuclear-size width, 10⁻¹⁵ m, the cost is 41.5 MeV, nearly 10⁷ times the bond (V2, STRUCTURAL).
  - **That 3.0 pm is the same order as the copy's own zero-point spread** (2.3 pm, exactcopy.py; V3).
- **So the motion your item 147 leaves to position 2 is exactly what keeps a state value from being exact.** Your two
  answers fit, if the values in the README are of kind (P).
