## What follows

- **Z1. It does not decay.** It is the lightest state and massless, so it cannot decay into anything massive.
- **Z2. It lives against the far plane.** Its density across the fifth dimension falls off exponentially away from the
  hidden plane. It sits within about **5×10⁻³⁵ m** of the hidden plane, and its density at our plane is **10⁻³⁰** of its
  density there. That is Randall–Sundrum's "small overlap". *So a lasting stasis in the zero mode resides, in the bulk,
  at the far side of the corridor.*
- **Z3. Each plane couples to it differently, as read from that plane.**
  - In one common frame it couples to both planes equally (ratio 1, check 5). The massive modes' ratio is 2.5×10⁻³⁰.
  - Read against each plane's own natural scale, though, its strength is **0.82 at the hidden plane** and
    **8×10⁻¹⁶ at ours**, 10¹⁵ apart.
  - So loading it is natural from the hidden plane and very weak from ours. *This is again an inhomogeneity between
    the two perspectives at the same time, the same shape as the tension candidate in `crossing.py`.*
- **Z4. Its own clock reads zero.** From our clock it covers the Proxima span in 4.25 years along the plane. From its own
  clock, no time passes between leaving and arriving. *That is a candidate counterpart of your "position 1, then
  position 2, with no in between", seen from the carrier's perspective.* It holds for any massless quantum, light
  included.
- **Z5. Stasis is not rest.** A massless quantum has no rest frame. It persists (Z1) and does not age (Z4), but it moves at
  the speed of light along the planes. Holding it in one place would need confinement (OPEN).
- **Z6. Loading it from our plane is the price.**
  - The board's Peters power matches the quadrupole formula exactly (check 3).
  - A laboratory rotor (two 500 kg masses 1 m apart at 100 Hz, H-LAB-ROTOR) radiates **7×10⁻³¹ W**, about **5 gravitons a
    second**.
  - At one bit per graviton (H-BIT-PER-GRAVITON), loading the 9.5×10²⁷ bits that define a body takes **2×10²⁷ s, about 4×10⁹
    Hubble times**.
  - A neutron-star binary radiates 2×10²⁵ W, but what it writes is its own orbit, not a body. The real bottleneck is
    shaping a source's emission with the defining information, not the number of gravitons (OPEN).
