## What follows the work

- **1. One shared "now" makes your teleportation causally safe, however often it is repeated.**
  - Suppose every teleport is instantaneous in *one* frame for the whole dimension (the board's reading R-SHARED).
  - Then no chain of teleports returns before it left: a reply keyed to that frame arrives at t = 0, never earlier.
  - Every network of corridors keyed to one frame is free of closed loops. That is Sylvester's theorem; frame.py's
    0 loops in 200 networks is a regression check, not new evidence.
  - So your H-CORRIDOR-REPEATABLE (item 90) is safe on this reading, in the corridor-as-identification model
    (H-CORRIDOR-MODEL, H-KEYING; frame.py's own grade).
  - This is the published resolution. Paradoxes need that one can send a signal backward in time in *any* frame, and
    "there can be no paradox if, in one particular reference frame, tachyons can only propagate forward in time"
    (Liberati, Sonego and Visser, p.12).
  - Disagreement over time order, they add, "should not be more disturbing than the jet lag" (p.12).
- **2. The board already holds a candidate for that frame, and in exact FRW the geometry forces it.**
  - The candidate is the cosmic rest frame, against which the Sun moves at 369.82 km/s (Planck 2018, READ in
    cmbframe.py).
  - In an exactly uniform expanding universe (flat FRW), only identifications at equal cosmic time preserve the
    geometry. So there R-SHARED is not a choice: frame.py credits it to the geometry, not to any hypothesis.
- **3. Your charter's clause 2 is met without a loop.**
  - H-FRAME clause 1, "a preferred frame exists", is what R-SHARED needs.
  - Clause 2, "messages may travel into the past", survives in frame.py's sense 2a. A moving observer sees the message
    arrive in their own past: that is point 4's separation, with no loop.
  - Only 2b, the past of the shared clock itself, is excluded.
  - *First written* "Your charter's own hypothesis H-FRAME, 'a preferred frame exists', is exactly what R-SHARED
    needs", which hid clause 2.
- **4. What "all observers" costs.**
  - For two separated places, "instantaneous" holds only for the observers of one family of frames. This is the
    relativity of simultaneity: for such a pair, there is always a frame where they are simultaneous and others where
    their order reverses (LSV p.11).
  - Observers moving relative to the shared frame read the departure and the arrival as apart.
  - For the Sun's frame, along Proxima's actual direction (66° off our motion through the cosmic frame), they are
    6.7×10⁴ s apart (0.77 days).
  - For a span of Proxima's length laid along that motion (H-ALONG-DIPOLE), the maximum is 1.65×10⁵ s (1.9 days).
  - For observers on Earth the figure shifts by up to ±9.5×10³ s over the year.
  - On R-SHARED they disagree about the timing, but never see a loop.
  - *First written* "over the board's Proxima span, 1.65×10⁵ s (1.9 days); over Earth's year, 1.52×10⁵ to 1.78×10⁵ s".
    That was the along-dipole maximum, unnamed, and mixed the Sun's frame with Earth's.

## For M: the readings, and the question

Under the relativity of simultaneity, "instantaneous" for two separated places cannot hold for observers moving
relative to one another. That is H-LORENZ-OBSERVERS, the board's premise. It can hold in one shared frame (R-SHARED),
or in each object's own frame (R-OBJECT). **Neither honours "all observers" literally.** That would need your
dimension's observers not to follow the usual relativity, or a reading in which departure and arrival are not two
separated events at all:

| reading | what "instantaneous" means | causality |
|---|---|---|
| R-SHARED | one shared now for the whole dimension | safe (no loop); moving observers see a separation |
| R-OBJECT | each object's own now (its rest frame) | loops (the anti-telephone) |
| R-PROPER | zero time on the object's own clock: a statement about the object, with no claim about simultaneity | compatible with R-SHARED or R-IDENTIFY |
| R-IDENTIFY | the corridor makes the two places one point; nothing moves and no one can measure a duration; only the names of the two ends depend on the frame | safe, by exactly point 1 |

*First written* "these agree only if one frame serves the whole dimension". R-SHARED does not deliver "all
observers" either.
