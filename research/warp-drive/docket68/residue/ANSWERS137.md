## Particle by particle

All READ at source (alphaXiv): Salam–Wang–Zanderighi, "The Higgs boson Turns Ten", arXiv:2207.00478v1; and Yang et
al., arXiv:1808.08677v2.

| particle | how much of its mass the Higgs field gives | source |
|---|---|---|
| **electron** | **all of it**, as the Standard Model *"suggests"*: with no Higgs coupling the electron would be massless. Its mass comes from its interaction with the Higgs field. That interaction is not yet confirmed by experiment | 2207.00478 p.2, Fig. 1: *"the Standard Model suggests that the corresponding particle masses arise from interactions of those particles with the Higgs field"*; the electron's row reads *"No"* under "experimentally confirmed" |
| **up and down quarks** | all of their own small masses: about 2.2 and 4.7 MeV/c². Not yet experimentally confirmed, like the electron | p.2, Fig. 1 |
| **proton** (uud) | about **9%**: the quark-mass term's share in one lattice decomposition (N_f = 2+1). The Higgs field's reach is wider than that term: the quark masses also enter the trace anomaly, and the heavy quarks set the strong scale | 1808.08677 p.1: the u, d, s scalar condensates give *"9(2)(1)%"*; *"the total proton mass is 938 MeV"* |
| **neutron** (udd) | about **9%**, the same as the proton's | the lattice ensembles use equal light-quark masses, so the 9% is the nucleon's, proton and neutron alike. 2207.00478 p.2: *"Differences in quark masses (m_up < m_down) contribute to protons … being lighter than neutrons"* |
| **W and Z bosons** | all of it; established to the 5–20% accuracy of the measured couplings (pp.6–7) | p.2; p.10: *"responsible not only for the masses of the W and Z bosons"* |

**The board's arithmetic for a 70 kg body** (step1b/balance.py's counts):
- **Electrons:** 2.3132×10²⁸ × 9.1094×10⁻³¹ kg = **0.02107 kg**, all of it from the Higgs field.
- **Nucleons:** about 69.98 kg, of which the quark-mass term is about 9% (6.8–11.2%, both errors): **about 6.3 kg**.
- **So the quark-mass and electron terms come to about 6.3 kg of a 70 kg body, roughly 9%.** Most of the rest is the
  strong force's field energy inside protons and neutrons. That energy also depends on the quark masses indirectly, so
  "the Higgs field does not supply it" holds only in this decomposition.
- **Counting only the valence quarks' own masses**, which the Higgs field gives:
  - proton: 2.2 + 2.2 + 4.7 = 9.1 MeV of 938 MeV, about 1.0%;
  - neutron: 2.2 + 4.7 + 4.7 = 11.6 MeV, about 1.2%.
  - The 9% also counts the quark–antiquark sea.
