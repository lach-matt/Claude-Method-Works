## Particle by particle

All READ at source (alphaXiv): Salam–Wang–Zanderighi, "The Higgs boson Turns Ten", arXiv:2207.00478v1; and Yang et
al., arXiv:1808.08677v2.

| particle | how much of its mass the Higgs field gives | source |
|---|---|---|
| **electron** | **all of it**, in the Standard Model. Its mass comes from its interaction with the Higgs field. **The board's arithmetic for a 70 kg body** (step1b/balance.py's counts):
- **Electrons:** 2.3132×10²⁸ × 9.1094×10⁻³¹ kg = **0.02107 kg**, all of it from the Higgs field.
- **Nucleons:** about 69.98 kg, of which about 9% (7–11%) is from the Higgs field: **about 6.3 kg**.
- **So about 6.3 kg of a 70 kg body, roughly 9%, is Higgs-given mass.** The other ~91% is the strong force's field
  energy inside protons and neutrons.
- **Counting only the valence quarks' own masses**, which the Higgs field gives:
  - proton: 2.2 + 2.2 + 4.7 = 9.1 MeV of 938 MeV, about 1.0%;
  - neutron: 2.2 + 4.7 + 4.7 = 11.6 MeV, about 1.2%.
  - The 9% also counts the quark–antiquark sea.

**What this means for answer 2 (item 136):**
- "Activating the Higgs field" sets the electron's mass in full and about a tenth of the nucleons'.
- Most of the copy's mass is the strong force's field energy inside its nucleons, which the Higgs field does not
  supply.

## Reorganization

- **Item 137:** *"matter is neither created nor destroyed, only reorganized"*. Item 120 says the same.
- **Carried:** H-REORGANIZATION.
  - The copy's matter is the stock reorganized.
  - No baryon or lepton number is created, so the conservation law READ in ANSWERS136.md is kept.
  - The released energy forms the copy's mass as energy (binding, motion and field energy), not as new particles.
  - That is the "what fits both" reading the board offered.
