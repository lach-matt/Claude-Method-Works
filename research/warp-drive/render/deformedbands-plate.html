A member is one level, and it is not the other nuclear index’s

A nuclear excited state in a two-quasiparticle rotational band of a deformed odd-odd nucleus, Z 67–71. It carries spin I and parity exactly as a particle carries J and P, so the quantum numbers are the level’s own — not its band’s and not its nuclide’s.

nucbands holds the levels of MAGNETIC and ANTIMAGNETIC rotational bands — the shears mechanism in weakly-deformed and near-spherical nuclei. These are a DEFORMED ROTOR’s tower: a different mechanism, in different nuclei, from a different paper. The disjointness is measured rather than asserted — 23 nuclides here, 123 there, 0 in common.

WITHDRAWN in the instrument, and the plate carries the corrected ground rather than the first one

The zero above was first explained by saying the two mass ranges are disjoint. They are not. nucbands spans A 58–205 and contains this index’s A 156–174 entirely. The separation survives anyway, and is sharper than the ranges suggest: of this index’s 10 distinct mass numbers, 0 occur in nucbands at all. But that is a measured fact about which nuclides each paper happens to tabulate, not a consequence of where they sit on the chart — a weaker ground, and the true one.

The source’s title overclaims, and the index reports the table

The paper is titled “… Deformed Odd-Odd Nuclei, 156 ≤ A ≤ 168”, and its Table 3 does not respect that: 132 of the 1,907 seated levels sit above A = 168, up to A = 174. This index describes what the table holds, not what the title claims, and the discrepancy is recorded rather than smoothed.

03

Three refusals, each measured rather than asserted

  • The energy is not a coordinate. It is a magnitude, not a quantum number. It is also not even a number for much of this table: 61 bandheads are printed as A+134.27 or 1135.7+y, relative to an unknown offset. Both reasons hold and the second is peculiar to this source.
  • The band number is not a coordinate — it is the table’s row label. Measured against figure.py’s rule that a coordinate separating 90 % or more of its members groups nothing: 234 distinct band numbers over 234 entries, a ratio of 1.00. It separates every entry and groups none.
  • Z and N are not coordinates, and this one is a judgement rather than a rule, so it is charted anyway and refused against a number. (2I, parity, Z, N) would be 853 cells at K0, cell (0, 52, 40). They are properties of the HOST nuclide, not of the level, which is DOCKET 28’s trap exactly.

And its K2 is the free one. At arity 2 there is exactly one 2-subset of the coordinates, so kdet returns True for every set whatever and statistics closes vacuously — measured, not assumed: arity 2 ≤ 2, so the closer is vacuous. This index really closes in NOTHING, which is where mesons, baryons and nucbands sit. It is said rather than banked.

The cell, against the register

Measured, not assumed: (2, 49, 2) is held by no other seated index.

56 levels are REFUSED, and counted apart

They carry a spin and no parity. That is the same kind of gap nucbands reports and it is counted separately for the same reason: “no parity printed” and “no spin printed” are different facts about the source, and flattening them would lose one. The capture itself is total against three of the paper’s own numbers — 234 entries, 173 bands, 61 bandhead states, all exact.
