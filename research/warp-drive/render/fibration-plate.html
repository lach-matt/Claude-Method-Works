The address loses nothing: 170 elements onto 170 distinct triples, one each. That matters because of what it is not.

This is not Janet relabelled

Janet charts the same electrons as (n+ℓ, ℓ, k). This charts them as (n, ℓ, k). One coordinate differs and it is the one that matters — Janet's first coordinate is the fill order, this one's is the shell. Same 170 elements, same three-coordinate shape, and Janet closes in all five languages while this closes in two. That is the chart criterion's point made once more, from the other side.

02

The fibres are a palindrome

At each shell, the fibre is the set of (ℓ, k) that occur there. Their sizes are symmetric — and the symmetry is forced by two constraints changing hands.

02½

The bundle in three dimensions

All 170 cells at once. The base n runs left to right, ℓ into the depth, and the slot k bottom to top — so each vertical comb is one subshell and each slice across the depth is one fibre. Drag to rotate.

03

The sliding scale, and it is the period detector again

Sweep n, take the cumulative index up to each shell, and measure which languages close it. There is exactly one transition, and it lands somewhere specific.

Where the transition lands

On the first shrinking shell, at every reach tested — nine of nine. n = ⌊S/2⌋ + 2, which is one past the palindrome's last peak. While the fibres are still growing the cumulative index closes everything; the shell at which they begin to shrink is the shell at which order, algebra and information break.

A correction the selftest made. This first read ⌈(S+1)/2⌉ — the peak — off by one, because the number came from reading the last shell still at K7 instead of the first at K3. The pin failed. It is recorded because the off-by-one had a meaning: peak and first shrink are different claims about what causes the break, and only the second is true.

05

Are ℓ and k two different languages? No.

The hypothesis was that ℓ and k might carry order and algebra respectively. Put it to the test by projecting each axis out and measuring what breaks.

Refuted, and here is what replaces it

Order, algebra and information move as one block at every projection — 140/140/140, then 40/40/40, then 10/10/10. No projection separates order from algebra, so the order-versus-algebra reading of ℓ against k does not survive. Order and algebra agreeing is already a theorem; that information joins them here is the new part, and it is what K3 means.

The language of n is carried by n. Drop n and all five close, E = 0 across the board. Drop ℓ or drop k and the verdict does not move at all. The three broken languages are broken by the shell coordinate — ℓ and k are not two languages between them, they are two ways of being inside one.

But ℓ and k are still not interchangeable, and the asymmetry is real: dropping ℓ leaves a deficit of 40, dropping k leaves 10, on 82 cells against 25. The (n, k) chart is four times as far from closing. That is a genuine difference and it is not a difference of language.

Which measurement depends on which axis

Since (n, ℓ, k) is a bijection onto the elements, everything is determined by all three and that fact is empty. What is not empty is the minimal subset.

Three things this table says

The shape of a subshell is an ℓ fact, its filling is a k fact, and whether it is finished needs both. That is the exclusion principle showing up as a dependence.

No axis but n itself needs n alone. n never appears without ℓ — which is §05's finding in table form: n carries the broken languages but never acts by itself.

Group needs all three; period needs only (n, ℓ). The two coordinates of the drawn layout have different depths in this bundle and the layout does not say so. And n₀ needs all three despite being named for ℓ — register 1141 defines it as "the first entirely unoccupied n at this ℓ", but it reads the observed configuration of that particular element. A quantity's name is not its dependence.

07

What this refuses to measure

Two examples were offered for the dependence question. Neither is testable here, and saying so is the point.

Gravity on the ℓ axis — not measured

There is no gravitational measurement per element anywhere in this corpus. The G slot in bounds.py codes whether a bound mentions gravity — one integer in it was re-coded under an earlier ruling — not a quantity an atom has. To test this, an atomic gravitational axis would have to exist and be banked. None is. Not measured, and not because the answer is no.

Spectra on the k axis — not measured

The spectra axis is the series limit, and populate.LIMITS banks four of them: (Z, charge) = (3,3), (5,4), (5,5), (15,4), with three more reachable through banked deficits. All are ions at charges 3 to 5; not one is a neutral atom, and no two share an n. Four points cannot establish a functional dependence on anything.

What would settle it: series limits banked for a run of neutral atoms spanning at least two subshells at one n, and two n at one ℓ. Twelve would probably do. The measurement is cheap once the data exists — it does not exist.

08

Same laws, checked

The claim that the same mechanisms apply is not a formality — it is what makes the two indexes comparable at all, so it is verified rather than assumed.

  • Seven lawful containments hold on the index and on every one of the nine fibres. Zero broken.
  • The channel is a lawful down-set — K3 = down(geometry), a principal down-set and therefore join-irreducible.
  • Dilworth holds: 170 ≤ 26 × 17.
And one thing the check turned up

Every fibre closes in all five languages, without exception. A fibre is a complete rectangle of slots — ℓ runs 0…L and k runs 0…2(2ℓ+1)−1 with nothing missing — and a complete staircase closes everything. So the fibres carry no obstruction at all: every bit of this index's structure lives in how the fibres stack, not in what they contain.
