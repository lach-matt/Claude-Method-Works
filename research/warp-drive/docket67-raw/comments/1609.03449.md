## Summary

The detection stands, and so does its minimum-mass character, which two later instruments have confirmed. The semi-major axis stands, and so do the rounded figures in the abstract. Our one finding is a **discrepancy** inside Table 1: the minimum mass printed there is not what Table 1's own inputs give. The value we compute still lies inside the paper's 68% interval. It is not a refutation.

## Finding

### Table 1: m_p sin i from the table's own inputs (discrepancy)

- **The paper states** (Table 1, p. 10): the maximum a posteriori (MAP) values and 68% intervals
  - Doppler amplitude K = 1.38 [1.17, 1.59] m s⁻¹;
  - period P = 11.186 d;
  - stellar mass M* = 0.120 [0.105, 0.135] M_⊙;
  - eccentricity < 0.35;
  - semi-major axis a = 0.0485 [0.0434, 0.0526] AU;
  - minimum mass m_p sin i = 1.27 [1.10, 1.46] M_⊕.
- **What we find:**
  - **The MAP value.** The radial-velocity mass function with K = 1.38 m s⁻¹, P = 11.186 d, M* = 0.120 M_⊙ and e = 0 gives m_p sin i = **1.174 M_⊕**, not 1.27. The ratio is 1.082.
  - **The interval edges.** K at its interval edges gives 0.996 and 1.353 M_⊕, against the printed 1.10 and 1.46: a similar offset (ratios 1.10 and 1.08).
  - **Eccentricity does not help.** Allowing e > 0 lowers the value further, by a factor √(1 − e²): it is 1.100 at e = 0.35.
  - **Stellar mass does not help.** M* = 0.135 M_⊙ (the top of the interval) would return 1.27. But Kepler's third law would then give a = 0.0502 AU, not the printed 0.0485, which our computation reproduces at M* = 0.120 (0.0483).
  - **A later analysis agrees with our value.** Suárez Mascareño et al. (2020) used K = 1.377 m s⁻¹ with M* = 0.120 M_⊙ and published 1.15 M_⊕ (we compute 1.163).
  - **We do not suggest a cause.** The text does not say how the derived quantities were computed, for instance whether M* was propagated sample by sample through the posterior.
  - **Our value is inside the paper's interval.** 1.174 lies within the 68% interval [1.10, 1.46].

## What stands

- **The detection.** The 11.2-day Doppler signal, with frequentist false-alarm probability 7 × 10⁻⁸ (Table 1) and Bayesian odds 2.1 × 10⁷, and its interpretation as a planet candidate.
- **The minimum mass.** m_p sin i is a lower bound on the mass. Two independent instruments have since confirmed the planet. The same mass function reproduces every later published m sin i we checked to within 0.013 M_⊕: 1.29, 1.173, 1.15, 1.07 and 1.055 M_⊕ (the largest difference, 0.013, is for 1.15; the others agree to 0.004).
- **The orbit.** a = 0.0485 AU.
- **The abstract.** "a minimum mass of 1.3 Earth masses" and "~0.05 AU", as rounded figures.
