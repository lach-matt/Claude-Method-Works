## Summary

We do not assess the paper's main arguments: its critique of the dispersion relations used for the Scharnhorst effect, its argument concerning special relativity, and its proposal of an invariant length scale. Its conclusion that the effect is unmeasurable agrees with the literature it cites. Our one finding is a **numerical discrepancy**. The quoted magnitude of the Scharnhorst effect at a 1 μm plate separation, Δc/c = 1.6 × 10⁻³⁶, is the value of the formula at 10 μm. At 1 μm the formula gives about 1.6 × 10⁻³². This includes the paper's own expression on p. 6. It is a discrepancy, not a refutation, and it strengthens the paper's point that the effect is far too small to detect.

## Finding

### p. 9: the magnitude at 1 μm (discrepancy)

- **The paper states** (p. 9): "At micron length scale (1 μm) the increase in the velocity of light predicted by the Scharnhorst effect is much too small to detect Δc/c = 1.6 × 10⁻³⁶. At nuclear length scales of 1 fm would imply a Scharnhorst increase in the velocity of light by Δc/c = 1.6."
- **What we find:**
  - **Scharnhorst's formula.** Scharnhorst's low-frequency result (hep-th/9810221, eq. (10)) is Δc/c = (11π²/8100) α² (λ_c/L)⁴, with λ_c the reduced electron Compton wavelength. It gives:
    - **1.59 × 10⁻³²** at L = 1 μm;
    - **1.59 × 10⁻³⁶** at L = 10 μm.
  - **The paper's own expression.** On p. 6 the paper writes δc = k c α² (λ_c/L)⁴, with λ_c = 3.9 × 10⁻¹¹ cm, α = 1/137 and k ≈ 10⁻². At L = 1 μm this gives 1.2 × 10⁻³². That agrees with the exact coefficient 11π²/8100 ≈ 0.013, given the rounding of k.
  - **The 1 fm figure.** It follows from the 1 μm figure by the L⁻⁴ scaling (a factor of 10³⁶), so it carries the same factor of 10⁴. The formula itself gives about 1.6 × 10⁴ at 1 fm, a separation well outside the regime L ≫ λ_c in which the low-frequency result is derived.

The p. 6 cross-check is the one-line evaluation above, (10⁻²)(1/137)²(3.9 × 10⁻¹³ m/10⁻⁶ m)⁴ = 1.23 × 10⁻³², done for this draft.
- **Class:** discrepancy.

## What stands

- **The conclusion that the effect cannot be measured** (Secs. III-IV). It agrees with Milonni and Svozil and with Ben-Menahem as the paper quotes them. The corrected magnitude is still far below any detectable level.
- **The L⁻⁴ dependence** stated on pp. 6-7.

## Not assessed

- The paper's dispute of the dispersion relations (Sec. II), its argument about special relativity (Sec. III) and its proposal of an invariant length scale (Sec. IV). Nothing in this comment bears on them, in either direction.
