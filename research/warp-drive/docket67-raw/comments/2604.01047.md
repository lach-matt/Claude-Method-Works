## Findings

### 1. Theorem 3.6, S half only (α̃^S_1 = 1/(64π²)). Class: WRONG, in this part only

**What the paper states.** On pp. 33 and 35-37, α̃^S_1 = 1/(64π²) is obtained "in the hypothesis of Proposition 3.1 and by the principle of general local covariance", with perturbative agreement as the matching step. The late-time stress tensor (3.21) is evaluated "using the explicit form of :T^(0)_ab: given in (3.3) with square mass equal to m² + δm² and α_1 as fixed in Proposition 3.1" (p. 35). The p. 37 comparison then gives −4m²δm²/(64π²) = −8α̃^S_1 m⁴ W.

**What we found.**

- *The late region is Minkowski in disguise.* In the late S region the metric is Ω²η with constant Ω = 1 + W, and the field has mass m. The dilation x → Ωx is an isometry of (Ω²η, m) onto Minkowski with the same m.
- *The late state is the vacuum.* The paper's own convergence step puts the late state in the vacuum. The late state printed on pp. 33-35, with its 1/(1 + χ_ν W) factors, is exactly the mass-m vacuum of Ω²η written with σ_g = Ω²σ.
- *Covariance gives zero.* Under the general local covariance that the theorem invokes (pp. 32-33), the renormalised ⟨T_ab⟩ in the late region is the Minkowski vacuum value, which is 0 by Prop. 3.1, at every order in W. The identity that carries this is Ω² H_g(m, μ) = H_η(Ωm, Ωμ).
- *The TT half uses the same procedure.* The paper applies exactly this procedure to the other constant late metric, η + W r: the parametrix is taken in the perturbed spacetime with the same μ, and the result is ⟨T_ab⟩ = 0 (p. 39).
- *(3.21) holds μ fixed instead.* Read against this, (3.21) is (3.3) on η at mass Ωm with μ unchanged. That is the covariant formula with μ held fixed relative to the background η, i.e. μ replaced by μ/Ω.
- *The printed value measures the μ-running.* The m⁴/(64π²) that the comparison extracts equals dT_vac/d log μ², the running coefficient.
- *Zero in, zero out.* Putting the covariant value 0 into the p. 37 comparison gives α̃^S_1 = 0.

We do not think this is a misprint. The paper's algebra from (3.21) onward reproduces 1/(64π²) exactly. The adjudication also read p. 36: it derives (3.22) = −8α̃^S_1 W m⁴ with lim K_0 = lim S K_0 = 0, so (3.22) carries no compensating term.

The finding is therefore an inconsistency inside the paper's own procedure: the S and TT halves treat two structurally identical constant late metrics by different conventions.

**A consistency observation (not relied on).** The late perturbation 2Wη equals L_ξη, so it is pure gauge, while Prop. 3.4 proves Π gauge invariant. A nonzero α̃^S_1 therefore gives a nonzero response to a pure-gauge perturbation about T^(0) = 0. This supports the finding but is not decisive, because the S perturbation used is not in de Donder gauge (p. 37).

**What shows it.**

  - C3a-C3b reproduce the paper's 1/(64π²) from (3.21).
  - C4a-C4g give the covariance identity and the exact vanishing at every order in W.
  - C5a gives α̃^S_1 = 0 from the p. 37 comparison.
  - C5b gives m⁴/(64π²) = dT_vac/d log μ².
- `adjudications/2604.01047_thm3.6.json`: the decision, points (1)-(7), and the attempts to break the grade, (a)-(e).
- `shards/shard09.json`, key `2604.01047#thm3.6`.
- `VERIFIED-GRADES.tsv`, row `2604.01047#thm3.6`. The audit graded WRONG, three independent verifiers proposed WRONG, and the adjudication upheld it.

**What this finding does not say.**
- It does not say the instability reported in the paper is wrong in general.
- It does not say that the renormalisation of Anderson, Molina-París and Mottola is right in general.
- It does not touch the TT half or Prop. 3.1.

If the S-half evaluation is meant to use a scale fixed relative to η on purpose, that is a different choice from the covariance invoked on pp. 32-33 and from the p. 39 procedure, and we would welcome correction.

**Downstream.** These rest on α̃^S_1 = 1/(64π²) and so go with the S half:
- the Sec. 5.1 S zero "slightly smaller than 0";

At α̃^S_1 = 0 we have b_0 = 0, and γ = 0 is an exact root (`rederive/amm-gmmps-linearised-stability.out`, line 33). Whether anything grows from γ = 0 at α̃^S_1 = 0 is open. The roots near −b_1/b_2, which are set by the free α̃^S_3 and α̃^TT_4, are untouched.

### 4. Section 5.1, "For sufficiently large −b_2, this is the only negative zero" (p. 60). Class: discrepancy

**What the paper states.** For b_2 < 0 the S zero γ_0 "slightly smaller than 0" is present, and "for sufficiently large −b_2, this is the only negative zero that can be found in D".

**What we found.** With the printed signs b_0 < 0 and b_1 < 0 from (5.2), and b_2 < 0, the small-γ quadratic b_0 + b_1γ + b_2γ² has either two negative real roots or a complex pair. It never has exactly one negative root. The full F_S, with J kept, behaves the same way near γ = 0. The counts below are each for the window stated (reduced-Planck units, κ = 1, ξ = 0, m = 7.885 × 10⁻³ eV, the paper's α̃^S_1):
- At b_2 = −1 × 10¹⁰⁰, F_S has two negative real zeros in (−10⁻⁴⁰, −10⁻¹³⁰) M_P². The second is near −b_1/b_2.
- At b_2 = −1.08 × 10¹²⁰ there are still two negative real zeros there.
- At b_2 = −8.6 × 10¹²¹, ten times the threshold b_1²/(4|b_0|) ≈ 8.62 × 10¹²⁰, there is no negative real zero anywhere in (−10¹⁰, −10⁻¹³⁰) M_P²: γ_0 and its partner have become a complex pair.
- At the test point m = κ = 1, ξ = 0, counting sign changes on [−10⁶, −10⁻⁶]: b_2 = +100 gives one negative zero, b_2 = −100 gives two, and b_2 = −1000 gives none.

These windows do not reach the far negative axis. There, for every b_2 < 0, F_S has one further real zero, far beyond the Planck scale (log(|γ|/m²) ≈ 16π²|b_2|): since J(−X) ~ log(X/m²)/(16π²X) for large X, F_S(−X) ≈ −X²(b_2 + log(X/m²)/(16π²)), which changes sign once when b_2 < 0. So below the threshold there are three negative real zeros in D, and above it the only negative real zero is that far one, not γ_0. Either way γ_0 is never the only negative zero for b_2 < 0, so the far zero does not rescue the sentence.

The statement does hold for b_2 > 0, where that asymptotic form keeps one sign and the far zero does not occur. This agrees with Fig. 1, with Sec. 5.2.1's "α̃^S_3 sufficiently large and negative", and with the paper's own Sec. 5.3 regime: b_2^S = −4α̃^S_3/(6(1/6 − ξ)²), so α̃^S_3 negative means b_2 positive. We read it as probably a sign slip.

### 6. TT sector: existence results cited outside their stated hypotheses (Sec. 5.2, pp. 60-62). Class: discrepancy (a gap in the source)

**What the paper states.**
- Sec. 5.2 has a = 4m², b_0 = 0 and b_1 = 60/κ, and says "since b_0 = 0, there is no need to find h_0".
- p. 62 then invokes "Proposition 4.10, if we find three independent zeros".
- p. 62 also says "the existence of solutions is provided by Theorem 4.14".

**What we found.**
- **The a = 4m² endpoint.** a = 4m² is the endpoint that Prop. 4.12 and Thm 4.14 exclude: their hypothesis is a ∈ (−∞, 4m²). At a = 4m², Lemma 4.13's limit A′(4m²⁻) = +∞ fails, because A′ < 0 on all of I. So Prop. 4.12's tangent construction is unavailable for every b_2 ≥ 0. For b_2 > 1/(8π²) ≈ 0.0127 (a threshold located numerically and independent of m), no auxiliary (b_0, b_1) gives the three real roots that Thm 4.14's proof uses. This was checked on a grid in γ at b_2 = 0.1, 1, 10 and 10³ with m = 1; it is not a proof over the continuum.
- **The b_0 = 0 case.** Prop. 4.10 requires b_0, b_1, b_2 ∈ ℝ∖{0}, but the TT sector has b_0 = 0.
- **Net.** As stated, the existence results prove unique past-compact solutions for the S sector (a < 4m²) and not for the TT parameters.

We do not show that any TT source is ill-posed. The finding is that the cited results do not cover these parameters as stated.

**What shows it.**
- `rederive/2604.01047_setting-thm1.2.py`, with its log `rederive/2604.01047_setting-thm1.2.log`: checks C2a-C2b, C3c-C3d, C4lim and C4a.
