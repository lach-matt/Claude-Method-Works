## Summary

The observation the paper reports stands:

- broadband photon generation by a SQUID-terminated transmission line, driven at ~11 GHz;
- two-mode squeezing correlations with the structure the theory predicts;
- the qualitative identification of the effect as the dynamical Casimir effect.

Our one finding is a **discrepancy** among three of the paper's own numbers for the expected and measured squeezing. It is not a refutation, and it does not touch the observation.

## Finding

### Expected against measured two-mode squeezing: discrepancy

- **The paper states:**
  - p. 3: the effective velocity of the boundary can approach "v/c ∼ 0.05 for a 10% modulation of the SQUID inductance";
  - p. 4: c₀ ∼ 0.4c, the speed of light in the transmission line;
  - pp. 8-9: "σ₂ ≈ v_e/2c₀, which implies a squeezing of about 2% for a 10% modulation of the SQUID inductance";
  - p. 9: "The correlations imply a value of σ₂ ∼ 0.04 consistent with our expectations";
  - Fig. 3c: the axis "Squeezing [%]" spans 0.0-2.0, as extracted.
- **What we find:**
  - If 0.05 is v_e/c, then v_e/c₀ = 0.125 and σ₂ ≈ 0.0625, about 6%.
  - If 0.05 is v_e/c₀, then σ₂ ≈ 0.025, about 2.5%, close to "about 2%". Exactly 2% would need v_e/c₀ ≈ 0.04.
  - Independently of that reading, the measured σ₂ ∼ 0.04 (p. 9) is about twice the "squeezing of about 2%" that the paper derives from σ₂ ≈ v_e/2c₀ (pp. 8-9), and it is called consistent with that expectation. Under the v_e/c reading it would instead be about two-thirds of the expected 0.0625. Fig. 3c's squeezing axis, as extracted, stops at 2.0%.
  - The text does not settle whether 0.05 is taken against c or against c₀. As printed, the three statements are not mutually consistent, and we cannot tell which reading is intended.
- **Shown by:** `research/warp-drive/docket67-raw/adjudications/wilson-2011-dynamical-casimir.json`, `corrections_to_record` (the entry "Internal discrepancy in the source"); every quotation above re-read at source in this stage.
- **Class:** discrepancy.

## What stands

- **The photon generation.** It was observed for drive frequencies across the 8-12 GHz band (Fig. 2a), and over broad analysis bands at fixed drives of 8.70 and 11.30 GHz (Figs. 2b-c). It is broadband in both, which distinguishes it from a single-mode parametric amplifier.
- **The boundary velocity.** The effective velocity, "a few percent of the speed of light", is defined as the rate of change of the electrical length.
- **Thermal effects.** They are negligible: at 50 mK, n = 0.008 at 5 GHz (we recompute 0.0083).
- **The two-mode squeezing signature.**
  - ⟨I₊I₋⟩ = −⟨Q₊Q₋⟩, with correlations about 100 times the amplifier's own.
  - The single-mode fields remain unsqueezed.
  - Ψ transforms as e^{−2iθ}Ψ under phase rotations.
- **The authors' conclusion,** "the first experimental observation of the dynamical Casimir effect".
- **The absolute photon rate** rests, as the paper says, on the Q² parasitic-resonance model (Q ∼ 30-50, an enhancement of 1000-2000). The qualitative observation does not depend on it.
