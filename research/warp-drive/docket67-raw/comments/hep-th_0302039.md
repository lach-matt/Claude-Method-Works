## Finding

### (B3): the term −21 r⁸ f′⁴ printed inside the (ξ − 1/6)² log bracket (discrepancy)

- **The paper states** (Appendix B, eq. (B3), p. 16): the (ξ − 1/6)² log bracket opens "( 2880 r⁴ f⁴ − 21 r⁸ f′⁴ − 8640 r⁴ f⁴ …" and closes "… + 7020 r⁸ f′⁴ ) (ξ − 1/6)²".
- **What we find:**
  - **The ξ-independent log bracket needs the term.** With the term as unknowns, conservation ∇_μ T^μ_ν = 0 of the ξ-independent log part holds only for the coefficient pair (−116, −21), in the paper's sign convention. As printed, (B3)'s ξ-independent log bracket carries no −21 r⁸ f′⁴ term, and that part is not conserved.
  - **The (ξ − 1/6)² bracket does not want it.** That bracket is conserved only if its net r⁸ f′⁴ coefficient is **7020**. With the −21 inside it, the net is 7020 − 21 = **6999**, and the bracket is not conserved.
  - **At ξ = 1/6, m = 0**, the printed (B1)-(B3) are therefore not conserved. With the term moved to the ξ-independent bracket, they are conserved identically, for arbitrary f, r and arbitrary log scale. The scale (log) part is then separately conserved and traceless.
  - So the term is misplaced, not missing.
  - **A caution on what this shows.** Conservation decides the placement *given the other printed terms*. If every log coefficient were left free, conservation alone would leave 18 free directions. With the non-log part fixed, conservation plus tracelessness give the one reading above.

## What stands

All of this is at ξ = 1/6, m = 0, the only case checked.

- **(B1) and (B2).** (B1) carries the coefficient 116 and (B2) the power r². Where the 1997 printing differs (16, and /(f² r)), conservation selects Popov's (B1) and (B2), given the other printed terms.
- **The non-log parts.** They agree term by term with the 1997 printing. Their trace is the conformal anomaly with coefficient 1/(2880π²). This is agreement and consistency, not a uniqueness proof: conservation and the anomaly trace leave seven non-scale directions in the non-log part undetermined, so they cannot by themselves confirm every shared term (adjudication JSON, `decision` (3)). Nothing we found calls any shared term into question.
- **The high-frequency expression.** It contains no state-dependent quantity. With the (B3) term restored, it is identically conserved, and its scale part is separately conserved and traceless.
- **The AHS identification.** We raise nothing against the p. 10 statement that (T)^(4) is "equivalent to" the Anderson-Hiscock-Samuel approximation, with m_DS = μ and w₀ = λ. The AHS paper itself was not reached, so we have not compared the two.
