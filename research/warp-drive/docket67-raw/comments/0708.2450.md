## Summary

The paper's results stand. These are the state-dependent QEIs (Theorems 4.2 and 4.3), their non-triviality (Theorems 5.1 and 5.3, Cor. 5.4), the Sec. 3 proof that no state-independent bound exists for ξ > 0, the reduction to Fewster-Eveson at ξ = 0, and the conclusion of Sec. 5.2 that q ≥ 3. We record two **discrepancies** in the illustrative calculation of Sec. 5.2, eqs. (71)-(72). They appear to be linked. Neither touches a theorem or the q ≥ 3 conclusion. Since both rest on a text extraction, part or all of either may be an extraction artefact.

## Findings

### Eq. (71): the exponent of the first term (discrepancy, possibly extraction)

- **The paper states** (eq. (71), p. 15), as extracted: for the one-particle state (70), ⟨ρ_quant ∘ γ⟩(t) = (κ⁴/π²){4/(1 + t²κ²) − 4ξ · 6(t²κ² − 1)/(1 + t²κ²)⁴}.
- **What we find:**
  - From state (70) we reproduce ⟨H^j⟩ = (κ/2)^j (j + 1)! and the ξ-term of (71) exactly.
  - The ξ-independent term comes out as **4/(1 + t²κ²)³**, not 4/(1 + t²κ²).
  - The extraction may have dropped the superscript 3.
- **Shown by:** `research/warp-drive/docket67-raw/rederive/fewster-osterbrink-qei.out.json`, checks B1, B2, B3 and the note B3_note; `rederive/fewster-osterbrink-qei.py`; `research/warp-drive/docket67-raw/shards/shard04.json`, key `fewster-osterbrink-qei`, `rederivation.outcome` item (ii).
- **Class:** discrepancy (possibly an extraction artefact).

### Eq. (72): the limiting constant and its normalisation (discrepancy)

- **The paper states** (eq. (72), p. 15), as extracted: lim_{κ→∞} ⟨ρ_quant ∘ γ⟩_{Ψκ}(f²) / (⟨H³⟩_{Ψκ} ‖f‖²_{L²}) = **2(2 + 3ξ)/(3π)**.
- **What we find:**
  - Smearing the derived (71) against f² and dividing by ⟨H³⟩ = 3κ³ gives the limit **f(0)² (1 + 4ξ)/(2π)**.
  - A direct numerical evaluation confirms this. At κ = 10³ and ξ = 0.1, the smeared ratio is 0.222817, against 0.222817 from f(0)²(1 + 4ξ)/(2π): the relative difference is about 5 × 10⁻⁷. The printed form gives 0.6117 for the same function.
  - **The ξ-coefficients agree.** Both give 6ξ/(3π). The difference is in the ξ-free part.
  - **The two findings appear to be linked.** The printed ξ-free part, 4/(3π), is what the first term of (71) gives if its exponent is 1. With the exponent 3 that state (70) gives, the ξ-free part is 1/(2π).
  - **The normalisation.** The pulse has width ~1/κ and height ~κ⁴, so its smearing against f² tends to f(0)² times a constant times κ³. The natural normalisation is therefore f(0)², not ‖f‖²_{L²}. The two differ in dimension, by one power of time.
- **Shown by:** `rederive/fewster-osterbrink-qei.out.json`, check B4, B4_note and B5_numeric (κ = 100 and 1000); `shards/shard04.json`, key `fewster-osterbrink-qei`, `rederivation.outcome` item (iii).
- **Class:** discrepancy.

### What these do not touch

- The purpose of (70)-(72) is to show that the left-hand inequality of (69) fails for q < 3. Any positive limiting constant gives that conclusion.
- The q ≥ 3 conclusion is reproduced here (check B4).

## What stands

- **Theorem 4.2 and Theorem 4.3.** The state-dependent QEIs. Eq. (53) agrees numerically with (55) at ξ = 0, 0.1 and 0.25. The coefficient in (57) and the Q_{n,k} properties check.
- **Theorems 5.1 and 5.3, and Cor. 5.4.** Non-triviality. The KMS scaling of Theorem 5.1 is reproduced.
- **Sec. 3, eqs. (26)-(29).** The counterexample showing that no state-independent bound exists for ξ > 0 is reproduced exactly (difference 0 against eq. (28)).
- **The reduction at ξ = 0.** It gives Fewster-Eveson's 1/(16π³).
- **The q ≥ 3 conclusion** of Sec. 5.2.

A missing factor β^{2−n} in eq. (61) is also reproduced here. We do not count it as a finding, since it is already noted in Fewster and Kontou, arXiv:1809.05047 (footnote 4), together with the remark that the final results are correct.

In passing, two constants in Sec. 3 are more generous than the argument needs. The threshold on j is a factor 16 = 2⁴ larger than required, and the lower bound (34) is loose by a factor 2. Both statements remain true as printed, because a larger j and a weaker bound are still sufficient, so nothing in Sec. 3 changes. Shown by: `rederive/0708.2450.py` and `.out` ("printed j exceeds the needed j (sufficient: (33) still holds), by 16 = 2^4"; "FO (34) lower bound … is below 2jk'/3 at their j (true, loose by 2)"); `shards/shard03.json`, key `0708.2450`, `rederivation.outcome`.
