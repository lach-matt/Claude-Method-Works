## Summary

The paper's derivation stands. Every closed-form step re-derives, and so do the wall-thickness bound and the energy formula. The paper's conclusion stands too, for the spherical Alcubierre bubble it treats: the negative energy needed for a macroscopic bubble is unphysically large. We record **three numerical discrepancies**. In each, a printed figure does not follow from the paper's own formulas. All three are discrepancies, not refutations, and none moves the conclusion. Each figure is evaluated at v_b = 1, as the printed figures are.

## Findings

### Eq. (29): the intermediate figure in Planck units (discrepancy)

- **The paper states** (eq. (29), p. 10): for R = 100 m with Δ at the bound (23), E ≤ −6.2 × 10⁷⁰ v_b L_Planck ∼ −6.2 × 10⁶⁵ v_b grams.
- **What we find:**
  - Eq. (28), E = −(1/12) v_b² (R²/Δ + Δ/12), with R = 100 m and Δ = 10² L_P, gives **3.19 × 10⁷⁰** in Planck units. The printed 6.2 × 10⁷⁰ is 1.94 times larger.
  - In grams, the same value is **6.94 × 10⁶⁵ g**. The printed 6.2 × 10⁶⁵ g is close to this (ratio 0.89).
  - The printed step from 6.2 × 10⁷⁰ to 6.2 × 10⁶⁵ g implies 10⁻⁵ g per Planck unit. The Planck mass is 2.18 × 10⁻⁵ g.
  - We do not suggest a cause.

### p. 11: the bubble of one electron Compton wavelength (discrepancy)

- **The paper states** (p. 11): "a bubble with a radius the size of one electron Compton wavelength would require a negative energy of the order E ∼ −400 M_sun."
- **What we find:**
  - The paper's eq. (28), with Δ at the bound (23) and v_b = 1, gives for R = 2.4263 × 10⁻¹² m: E ≈ **−2.06 × 10⁵ M_sun**. That is 514 times the printed figure, 2.7 orders of magnitude.
  - An independent route agrees. Van Den Broeck's eq. (9) (gr-qc/9905084), scaled by R², gives 2.07 × 10⁵ M_sun.
  - The reduced Compton wavelength, 3.862 × 10⁻¹³ m, gives 5.2 × 10³ M_sun. That is still 13 times the printed figure.
  - To give 400 M_sun, the formula needs R = 1.07 × 10⁻¹³ m at v_b = 1, or v_b = 1.95 × 10⁻³ at one Compton wavelength.

### p. 10: a wall one metre thick (discrepancy)

- **The paper states** (p. 10): with R = 100 m and Δ = 1 m, "the total energy required … would be on the order of a quarter of a solar mass".
- **What we find:** eq. (28) at R = 100 m, Δ = 1 m and v_b = 1 gives **0.56 M_sun**, about 2.2 times a quarter.

## What stands

- **The quantum-inequality steps.** Eqs. (10), (12)-(17) and (19) re-derive symbolically, including the contour integral (16).
- **The wall bound (22).** Δ ≤ (3/4)√(3/π) v_b/α² L_P = 0.7329 v_b/α² L_P. At α = 1/10 this is 73.3 v_b L_P, printed as "10² v_b L_Planck" in (23).
- **The energy formula.** Eq. (26) and eq. (28), E = −(1/12) v_b² (R²/Δ + Δ/12).
- **The R = 100 m energy.** 6.94 × 10⁶⁵ g against the printed 6.2 × 10⁶⁵ g. This is 3.5 × 10²⁰ galaxy masses at the paper's 2 × 10⁴⁵ g (printed 3 × 10²⁰), and about 9.7 orders of magnitude above the baryons of the observable universe ("roughly ten").
- **The conclusion.** For the spherical Alcubierre bubble the paper treats, the negative energy needed for a macroscopic bubble is physically unattainable. Later work changes the shape and keeps the wall bound: Van Den Broeck (gr-qc/9905084) and Bobrick and Martire (arXiv:2102.06824). That later work bears on other shapes. It does not bear on the paper's result for its own.
