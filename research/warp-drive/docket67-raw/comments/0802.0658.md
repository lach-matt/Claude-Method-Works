## Summary

Sec. 5.2.1's account of the noise kernel's coincidence limit stands, as far as we checked it (flat space, vacuum, m = 0). Our one finding concerns notation: two definitions of the noise kernel, a factor 4 apart, are both described as the same object. Each section is consistent with itself. It is a discrepancy, not a refutation.

## Finding

### Eqs. (3.11) and (5.17): the normalisation of the noise kernel (discrepancy)

- **The paper states:**
  - Sec. 3.2, p. 10, Eq. (3.11): N_abcd[g; x, y) = ½ ⟨{t̂_ab[g; x), t̂_cd[g; y)}⟩, where t̂ = T̂ − ⟨T̂⟩.
  - Sec. 5.2.1, p. 29: "We now turn our attention to the noise kernel introduced in Eq. (3.11) …". Eq. (5.17) then reads 8N_ab,c′d′(x, y) = ⟨{T̂_ab(x) − ⟨T̂_ab(x)⟩, T̂_c′d′(y) − ⟨T̂_c′d′(y)⟩}⟩.
- **What we find:**
  - Eq. (3.11) gives N = ½⟨{t, t}⟩. Eq. (5.17) gives N = ⅛⟨{t, t}⟩. These differ by a factor of 4, yet Sec. 5.2.1 refers to its N as the kernel of Eq. (3.11).
  - Sec. 5 follows the convention of Phillips & Hu (their Eq. 1.3), and it is consistent with itself.
  - We evaluated the explicit functional (5.29)-(5.32) at R = 0, m = 0 and general ξ on the Minkowski-vacuum Wightman function. It equals ⅛ of the anticommutator computed independently by Wick's theorem, on all eight components checked.

## What stands

- **Sec. 5.2.1's account of the coincidence limit** of the noise kernel, Eqs. (5.17)-(5.22), checked in flat space, in the vacuum, at m = 0. The curved Hadamard case was not checked.
  - C₀₀₀₀ goes as 3(30ξ² − 10ξ + 1)/(2π⁴r⁸). The coefficient is nonzero for every real ξ (discriminant −20), so the coincidence limit diverges pointwise. The kernel exists as a distribution, as Sec. 3.2 says (p. 10: "the limit of coincidence points has meaning only in the sense of distributions").
  - With the Wightman iε rule, the worldline-smeared kernel is finite (1/105 for Gaussian sampling with τ = 1). This is our own computation, consistent with the paper's statement that the kernel is meaningful as a distribution; it is not a claim the paper makes.
- **The explicit functional (5.29)-(5.32)** agrees exactly with the independent Wick calculation on the eight components checked. One term, Ñ_c′d′, was not printed on the pages read and was taken as the x ↔ y mirror of (5.31).
