## Finding

### Eq. (33), the flat-space thermal energy density (p. 6). Class: discrepancy

**What the paper states.**
- Eq. (33): "T_00(T) = −π²(1 + ξ)/(30β⁴)", described as "the well-known Stefan–Boltzmann law for a massless scalar field".
- The text adds that "the additional factor (1+ξ) reflects the contribution of the non-minimal coupling between the scalar field and the curvature". Of the exterior Schwarzschild region it then says that "ξ simply acts as a parameter rescaling the normalization of the stress–energy tensor relative to the minimally coupled case", and that "the same interpretation applies in the flat–spacetime limit".

**What we found.**
- In flat space the ξ-dependent part of the stress tensor in the paper's eq. (4) is ξ(R_γρ − ½g_γρ R + g_γρ□ − ∇_γ∇_ρ)φ². Its curvature terms vanish.
- The derivative terms act on ⟨:φ²:⟩_β = 1/(12β²), which is constant in a homogeneous thermal state, so they vanish too.
- So the flat-space thermal energy density does not depend on ξ.
- The paper's (1 + ξ) therefore does not follow from its own eq. (4). At ξ = 1/6 it would change the Stefan-Boltzmann value by a factor of 7/6.
- arXiv:1804.05382, eq. (15), gives the flat-space value with no ξ.
- On the paper's remark that ξ "rescales the normalization" in flat space: with the stress tensor of eq. (4), we find no such rescaling.

The two agree at ξ = 0. The (1 + ξ) appears to enter through the point-split operator Γ̂_00 of eq. (17), whose spatial-derivative terms carry (1/2 + ξ). We have not traced this further.
