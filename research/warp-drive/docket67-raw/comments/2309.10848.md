## Summary

We found no counterexample to any result in the paper. The following stand as stated:

- the structure of Theorem IV.1, and its validity for any real ξ;
- the difference form (59);
- the DSNEC algebra (94) and (96);
- the ANEC limit (89);
- the frame map;
- the Einstein-frame NEC;
- the Sec. V effective-field-theory argument, taken as an argument.

Our six findings are all **discrepancies**: a curvature coefficient that does not follow from the paper's own stress tensor, a missing pair of absolute-value bars, a printed power whose reading depends on what is held fixed, an unspecified Planck mass, an abstract stated more strongly than Sec. II.C, and a normalisation difference between eqs. (1) and (7). None is a refutation.

We read eq. (9) with the paper's convention □_g := −g^{μν}∇_μ∇_ν (p. 5).

## Findings

### 1. The Ricci coefficient in Q[f]: discrepancy

- **The paper states** (pp. 17-18):
  - (64): ρ_n(f) = (∇φ∇φ)(ℓℓf) − ξφ²[∇∇(ℓℓf) + ½R_{μν}(ℓℓf)];
  - (69b): Q[f] = ∇∇(ℓℓf²) + ½R_{μν}ℓ^μℓ^ν f²;
  - Theorem IV.1, eq. (72), carries the same Q.
- **What we find:**
  - **Eq. (9) is conserved.** We tested the stress tensor (9) (p. 6), off shell, against the Euler-Lagrange expression of action (7). On a generic time-dependent metric diag(−a, b, c, c)(t, x), the Noether identity holds to a residual of 2 × 10⁻¹⁶⁵. An independent test on a flat FRW metric gives 1.4 × 10⁻¹⁶⁶.
  - **Eq. (9) gives −1.** Carried through the Leibniz and integration-by-parts steps (62)-(63), eq. (9) gives Q = ∇∇(ℓℓf²) − R_{ℓℓ}f², a Ricci coefficient of **−1**. We checked this as a pointwise identity, to a residual of 4 × 10⁻¹⁶⁵.
  - **Eqs. (12), (13) and (61) give other values.**
    - Eqs. (12)/(13) carry the R_{μν}φ² term with the opposite sign to (9): (9) − (12) = 2ξR_{μν}φ² exactly. With (12)'s sign the Noether identity fails (residual 0.356).
    - The same steps give a coefficient of **+1** from (13).
    - They give **+2** from (61), as extracted ("−2ξ{… + φ²(R_{μν}ℓℓf)}").
  - **The printed +½ matches none of these.** Compared with the Q that (9) gives, it leaves a residual of exactly −(3/2)ξR_{ℓℓ}f²φ².
- **Weight, in both directions:**
  - The term vanishes in Minkowski space, so the DSNEC, ANEC and SNEC applications in Secs. IV.C-D are untouched.
  - The positive-type step (70) carries no curvature term, so the theorem goes through with the corrected Q.
  - No state is shown that violates the bound as printed.

### 2. The ξ-term of (80), (85) and (93) without absolute-value bars: discrepancy

- **The paper states** (Sec. IV.C): the field-dependent term as ∫ ∂²₋(f²) with no absolute value. This draft's re-read also shows the same unbarred integral ∫ d²s ∂²_{s−}(F(s)²) in (87), p. 21.
- **What we find:**
  - That integral is identically zero: 0 for a Gaussian, and −2.8 × 10⁻¹³ for a compact bump, against ∫|·| = 1.035.
  - (96) needs the bars.
  - The step (79) → (80) needs |⟨:φ²:⟩| ≤ φ²_max, while (79) as extracted is one-sided. Sec. V writes |⟨:φ²:⟩| < φ²_max.
  - Read from the text layer only.

### 3. The power in the ANEC limit: minor discrepancy (a reading ambiguity)

- **The paper states** (p. 21, before (89)): "the first term of Eq.(86) goes as δ₊/(Aαⁿ) while the second term goes as δ₊/(Aα⁴)."
- **What we find:**
  - The power of the second term depends on what is held fixed as δ₊ → 0 with δ₊δ₋ = α².
    - With the dimensionless field bound φ̃²_max = (δ₊δ₋)^{(n−2)/2}φ²_max held fixed, as in (86)-(87), it goes as C|ξ|φ̃²_max δ₊/(Aαⁿ). The printed α⁴ agrees with this reading only at n = 4.
    - With φ²_max held fixed, it goes as δ₊/(Aα²), which the printed α⁴ does not match either.
  - Under every reading the term tends to zero, so the limit (89) holds as stated. One of our audits records this as a minor printed-power discrepancy, the other as a reading ambiguity; we report it as the latter, and it is not an error in the result.

### 4. "Planckian or sub-Planckian ... small": discrepancy

- **The paper states** (p. 4, after eq. (3)): "when M²_cutoff is Planckian or sub-Planckian, the quantity in the exponent is small and the two metrics are close."
- **What we find:**
  - Eq. (2) gives φ²_max ~ M_c^{n−2}, so x = 8πG_N ξφ² = 8πξ(M_c/M_P)^{n−2}.
  - At n = 4, M_c = M_P (the non-reduced Planck mass) and ξ = 1/6: x = 4π/3 = 4.19 > 1. There Ω^{n−2} = 1 − x (eq. (99)) is negative.
  - With the reduced Planck mass, x = ξ = 0.167.
  - The paper does not say which Planck mass it means. The claim holds for the reduced mass and fails at the cutoff for the non-reduced one.

### 5. The abstract states more than Sec. II.C: discrepancy

- **The paper states:**
  - Abstract (p. 1): "the average null energy condition, whose violation is necessary to allow traversable wormholes".
  - Introduction (p. 3): "the effective Averaged Null Energy Condition (ANEC), which must be violated for certain traversable wormholes".
  - Discussion, Sec. VI (p. 29): "traversable wormholes are only possible if the effective average null energy condition (ANEC) is violated".
- **What we find.** Sec. II.C (pp. 8-9) establishes the requirement as a theorem only for short, causality-violating wormholes, via the achronal ANEC [10]. For long wormholes it says:
  - "While there is no relevant theorem, it is obvious that …";
  - it restricts itself to "cases where the connected areas are asymptotically flat";
  - and it ends: "This point requires further consideration, but so far it seems …".

  The abstract does not carry that qualification.
- **Shown by:** `research/warp-drive/docket67-raw/shards/shard04.json`, key `2309.10848-verdict-anec`, field `hypothesis_drift` (the entry "REQUIREMENT WIDENED"); all four passages re-read at source in the drafting and verification stages.
- **Class:** discrepancy (an internal inconsistency between the abstract and the body).

### 6. Eq. (1) against action (7): discrepancy

- **The paper states:** eq. (1), p. 3, δL = ξRφ²; and action (7), p. 5, −½ξRφ².
- **What we find:**
  - Read literally, the two ξ's are related by ξ₍₁₎ = −ξ₍₇₎/2.
  - Barcelo-Visser (2.1) and Fewster-Osterbrink (1) use (7)'s normalisation.
  - The difference does not matter for the pointwise NEC statements, which hold for either sign. It does matter for statements restricted to ξ > 0.
- **Shown by:** `research/warp-drive/docket67-raw/shards/shard02.json`, key `nmc-classical-nec-violation`, field `hypothesis_drift` (the entry "NORMALISATION/SIGN DISCREPANCY").
- **Class:** discrepancy (normalisation and sign, read literally).

## What stands

- **Theorem IV.1:** its structure; its validity for any real ξ (p. 18, read at source); the small sampling domain and the null vector field; and the difference form (59). With the curvature coefficient corrected, nothing in the proof changes.
- **The Minkowski results:** the DSNEC inequalities (94) and (96) (Q₀ and Q₂ re-derived; p₄ = 2/π²), the ANEC limit (89), and (116), (117) and (119).
- **The classical results:**
  - the frame map (21) and (98)-(103), with exact factor Ω² = (1 − x)^{2/(n−2)};
  - the Einstein-frame NEC (24);
  - the classical NEC cases read from (18), confirmed independently at extrema of φ².
- **The effective-field-theory view:** the Sec. V argument stands as an argument.
- No counterexample to the paper was found.
