## Summary

The paper's result stands: a quantum inequality to first order in the Ricci tensor, confirming the premise of Ford and Roman. Our one finding is a sentence on p. 20 that states the condition with the comparison inverted. The paper states the condition correctly on p. 19. It is a misprint, a discrepancy and not a refutation.

## Finding

### p. 20: "the radius of curvature is small as compared to the sampling time" (discrepancy)

- **The paper states** (p. 20): "Ford and Roman [6] have argued that flat-space quantum inequalities can be applied in curved spacetime, so long as the radius of curvature is small as compared to the sampling time. The present paper explicitly confirms this claim …".
- **What we find:**
  - The condition the paper proves, and the one Ford and Roman use, is the reverse: the sampling time must be small compared with the radius of curvature.
  - The paper says so itself on p. 19: the bounds hold "if t₀ is much smaller than any curvature radius". It also says so in the introduction (p. 2): "with a timescale small compared to any curvature radius".
  - The corrections are of order R_max t₀² and its derivative analogues, which are small only in that regime.
  - The p. 20 sentence has the comparison inverted.
- **Shown by:** reading pp. 2, 19 and 20; `research/warp-drive/docket67-raw/shards/shard08.json`, key `ford-roman-sampling-practice`, field `later_literature` (the Kontou & Olum row: "the inverse of what they prove. That is a misprint").
- **Class:** discrepancy (a misprint).

## What stands

- **The inequality.** It holds for a minimally coupled scalar on a geodesic in a spacetime with small curvature, to first order in the Ricci tensor and its derivatives. Corrections are of order R_max t₀².
- **The causal-diamond condition.** The curvature must be small not only on the path but at every point that is both in the causal future of one point of the path and in the causal past of another (p. 20).
- **The renormalisation ambiguity**, parametrised by a and b.
- **Vacuum spacetimes.** Where the Ricci tensor vanishes, the flat-space inequality holds to first order without modification (p. 20).
