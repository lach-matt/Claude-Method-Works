## Summary

The paper's central results stand. They are the model system (5)-(7) in its conserved reading, the throat constraint (8), the boundary data (9), the throat radius r(0) ≈ 0.02 l_P, the ~67 l_P illustration and the asymptotic frequencies ω₁ and ω₂. We record two **coefficient discrepancies** in the text layer of eqs. (5)-(6), M1 and M2. Both vanish at the throat, so no throat number changes. We cannot tell from the text layer alone whether they are in the typeset paper or arise in the extraction. We also note one **wording point** about the scope of the metric form (2), and, in passing, one about footnote [22]. None of these is a refutation.

## Findings

### M1. Eq. (5), the ln f bracket: the coefficient 16 (discrepancy)

- **The paper states** (eq. (5), pp. 4-5): the text layer reads the term **16 f′² f″/f³** inside the ln f bracket of the tt component.
- **What we find:**
  - The printed system (5)-(7) is **not conserved**. That is, d_l T^l_l + (f′/2f)(T^l_l − T^t_t) + (2r′/r)(T^l_l − T^θ_θ) does not vanish identically.
  - We treated the tt and θθ log coefficients as unknowns (a_tt, a_θθ) and asked which values conserve the tensor identically, for arbitrary f(l) and r(l).
    - With the ll log term as printed (see M2), there is **no** solution.
    - With that term repaired to r², the solution is **unique**: (a_tt, a_θθ) = **(116, 21)**. The θθ value is the printed 21. The tt value is 116, so the printed coefficient 16 is exactly 100 below it: the printed term is short by 100 f′² f″/f³.
  - In this reading the ln f bracket is conserved on its own and traceless, as a term multiplying ln μ should be. It equals exactly **24 times the Bach tensor**, the tensor obtained by varying ∫√(−g) C² d⁴x.
  - Popov's later printing of the same source term (hep-th/0302039v2, App. B, eq. (B1)) has 116 here, as transcribed in `research/warp-drive/hpscentre.py` §2. Popov shares an author with this paper, so this is a second printing, not independent corroboration. We did not re-read Popov for this draft.
  - The argument assumes, as is standard, that the effective stress tensor is identically conserved, since it comes from varying a diffeomorphism-invariant action. The analytic approximation of Anderson, Hiscock and Samuel (PRD 51, 4337), which the paper uses, could not be read here.
  - The text layer may have dropped a digit. We have not seen the page image.
- **Shown by:** `research/warp-drive/docket67-raw/rederive/gr-qc_9701064.py` and `.out` (checks C3, C4a-C4e: "printed r^1 slot: NO (a_tt, a_th) conserves"; "r^2 slot: unique conserving pair (a_tt, a_th) = (116, 21)"; "printed system (16, 21, r^1) NOT conserved"); `rederive/gr-qc_9701064_bach.py` and `.out` (C10: "lambda candidates: [24] … residuals 0"); `rederive/ahs-prd-51-4337-hps.py` and `.out` (E/F: of the four readings, only (116, r²) is conserved); `research/warp-drive/docket67-raw/shards/shard01.json`, key `gr-qc/9701064`, fields `hypothesis_drift` and `grade_evidence`.
- **Class:** discrepancy.

### M2. Eq. (6), the ln f bracket: the power of r (discrepancy)

- **The paper states** (eq. (6), p. 5): the text layer reads the term **−4 f′² r′²/(f² r)** inside the ln f bracket of the ll component.
- **What we find:**
  - Every other term of (5)-(7) has dimension length⁻⁴. A dimensional scan of all 31 transcribed terms of (6) finds that this one, alone, has dimension length⁻³.
  - Homogeneity and conservation both call for **r²** in the denominator. That repair is the one M1 depends on.
  - Popov's (B2) prints r² here, as transcribed in `hpscentre.py` §2.
  - As with M1, the text layer may have dropped a superscript.
- **Shown by:** `rederive/gr-qc_9701064.out` (C2c and C3: "exactly one printed term is not length^-4: ll-log -4 f'^2 r'^2/(f^2 r)"); `shards/shard06.json`, key `hps-1997-largest-throat-300lp`, `rederivation.outcome` (B); `research/warp-drive/docket67-raw/adjudications/no-existence-theorem-ssaf.json`, `corrections_to_record` (the HPS eq. (6) item, read this pass, A1).
- **Class:** discrepancy.

### What M1 and M2 do not touch

- Both disputed terms carry a factor f′², which vanishes at the throat (f′(0) = 0).
- So eq. (8) comes out the same, term for term, under either reading. It follows exactly from (6) at l = 0 and does not depend on f‴(0) or r‴(0), as the paper says on p. 6.
- The same holds for the boundary data (9), r(0) = √15/(90√π) = 0.02428 l_P (printed "~0.02 l_P"), 12√(10π) = 67.26 l_P (printed "~67 l_P") and the throat fourth derivatives.
- **Shown by:** `rederive/gr-qc_9701064.out`, C4f, C6a-C6c, C7a-C7c, C8b.

### Wording: the scope of the metric form (2) (discrepancy)

- **The paper states** (p. 4): "The metric for a general static and spherically symmetric spacetime can be cast into the form (2)."
- **What we find:**
  - With f > 0, which the paper requires (p. 6), form (2) covers only the static region outside any Killing horizon, because f → 0 there.
  - The paper itself reports further local solutions "with horizons located far from the throat" (p. 8). Form (2) ends at those horizons.
  - The paper uses (2) only where f > 0, so nothing in it depends on the wider wording.
- **Shown by:** `shards/shard03.json`, key `gr-qc/9701064#eq2-einstein`, `hypothesis_drift` (fourth item); the Einstein-tensor components of (2) re-derived in `rederive/gr-qc_9701064_eq2-einstein.py` (all 24 checks pass).
- **Class:** discrepancy (wording; slightly wider than what is shown).

### In passing: footnote [22]

- **The paper states** (p. 6 and footnote [22], p. 10): "The chain rule also provides an additional constraint: namely r′(0) = 0 → f′(0) = 0." The footnote obtains this from r′(l) df/dr = f′(l) at l = 0.
- **What we find:** this step assumes df/dr is finite at the throat. That is an extra assumption, since r(l) has a minimum there. The reflection symmetry r(l) = r(−l), f(l) = f(−l), described on the same page, supplies f′(0) = 0 independently. So the data (9) are unaffected. The point concerns only how the condition is presented.
- **Shown by:** `shards/shard03.json`, key `gr-qc/9701064#eq2-einstein`, `lacked_data` (item (ii)).
- **Class:** discrepancy (presentation).

## What stands

- **The geometry.** Eq. (2) and its three Einstein-tensor components, in MTW conventions.
- **The model system.** (5)-(7) at ξ = 1/6, m = κ = 0, in the conserved reading. Its non-log trace is the conformal-anomaly form, with the expected coefficient.
- **The throat.** Eq. (8), the data (9), r(0) = 0.02428 l_P, the 67.26 l_P case, and the throat values ρ = −45/L, p_l = −ρ, p_t = 0, with L = ln f(0).
- **The asymptotics.** ω₁ and ω₂, re-derived by linearising (5) and (7) at large l, in both readings.
- **The far zone.** The p. 8 statement that "the metric as a whole is not asymptotically flat", alongside the abstract's two asymptotically spatially flat regions. These are about different functions, f and R(l), so they do not conflict. Our integrations of both readings of (5)-(7) to 10⁵ K bear out the first (f keeps growing), as far as a finite integration can (`shards/shard07.json`, key `gr-qc/9701064#not-af`).

## Not included

- **The large-l law F(l) ≈ (a ln l − b)², a = 5.3, b = 25.5 (p. 8).** Our integrations of our transcriptions of (5)-(7) give a local slope a that is still rising at 10⁵ K (5.57 there), so (5.3, 25.5) fits a window near 2 × 10³-10⁴ K rather than the large-l limit (`adjudications/gr-qc_9701064_ab-fit.json`). We do not put this to the authors as a finding. It rests on which system the paper integrated, which we could not determine, and on the unit of l, which the paper does not state for this law. The grade it carries (NARROWED) records how our own work used the law.
- **The size of the ripples.** In the same integrations the spatial ripples stay of order one out to 10⁵ K, which sits uneasily with "modulo the Planck-scale wiggles" (p. 8). This rests on the same undetermined system, so it is not raised either.
