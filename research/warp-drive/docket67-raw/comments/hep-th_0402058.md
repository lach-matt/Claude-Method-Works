## Summary

The paper's argument stands:

- the Bekenstein bound (2.2) contains no G and is non-trivial at G = 0;
- its ratio to the holographic bound is 2GM/R;
- the X-ray argument reproduces it up to factors of order one.

Our one finding is a **numerical discrepancy** in an order-of-magnitude illustration in Sec. 2.2. It does not touch the argument and is not a refutation.

## Finding

### The holographic bound for an electron: discrepancy

- **The paper states** (Sec. 2.2, p. 5): for a single massive elementary particle, "the particle can be localized to within a Compton wavelength: R ≈ ℏ/M"; the holographic bound "is given by the surface area in Planck units, πR²/l²_Pl"; and "E.g., for an electron, the holographic bound gives S ≤ 10⁴⁴".
- **What we find:**
  - With the paper's own R ≈ ħ/(m_e c), the reduced Compton wavelength, πR²/ℓ_P² = **1.8 × 10⁴⁵**. The printed 10⁴⁴ is low by 1.3 orders of magnitude, a factor of about 18.
  - If the full Compton wavelength h/(m_e c) is used instead, the figure is 7.1 × 10⁴⁶, 2.9 orders above the printed value. The paper's text specifies ħ/M, so we take 1.8 × 10⁴⁵ as the comparison.
  - The constants involved have not changed, so this is not an effect of later data.
  - The illustration's point stands at either value: the holographic bound is a huge number, far weaker than the Bekenstein bound, which the same paragraph shows is "of order one" for the particle.

- **Class:** discrepancy.

## What stands

- **Eq. (2.2).** S ≤ 2πMR/ħ carries no G and stays non-zero at G = 0, whereas A/4Għ diverges as G → 0.
- **The ratio of the bounds.** S_Bek/S_hol = 2GM/R exactly. So the Bekenstein bound is the tighter one precisely when M ≪ R/G.
- **The X-ray argument** reproduces 2πMR/ħ up to order-one factors.
- **The restriction to weakly gravitating regions** is stated and kept. A Schwarzschild black hole saturates the bound but lies outside that regime.
- **Later work.** Casini (2008) supplies the rigorous flat-space formulation the paper says is missing (p. 8). It contains no G, which confirms the conclusion.
