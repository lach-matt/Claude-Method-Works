## Summary

The paper's framework stands. That covers the field equations, the effective-stress rearrangement (2)-(21), the energy-condition analysis, and example (31). We record **two discrepancies** in the reconstruction of f(R) for the examples. Neither is a refutation.

- In example (25), the f(R) obtained from the trace equation does not satisfy df/dR = F, so the quoted matter density is not that of a metric-f(R) solution. The combination ρ + p_r is unaffected.
- Eq. (34) as rendered, at α = −1, does not reduce to the f(R) that example (31) requires.

## Findings

### Example (25): f(R) from the trace equation and df/dR = F (discrepancy)

- **The paper states** (Sec. III.A, pp. 4-5):
  - for b(r) = r₀²/r and a traceless matter tensor, F(r) is given by (25) and ρ by (26) (that is, (16), ρ = F b′/r²);
  - "Substituting these relationships into the consistency equation (3), the specific form f(R) is given by" (29), f(R) = −R{C₁ sinh[…] + C₂ cosh[…]} = −R F;
  - the WEC is shown for C₁ = 0, C₂ = −1 (Fig. 1).
- **What we find:**
  - Metric f(R) gravity requires F = df/dR, with F defined that way just after eq. (2).
  - With r₀ = 1, the f of (29) gives residuals df/dr − F dR/dr of **12.56, 0.685 and 0.0757** at r/r₀ = 1.25, 2 and 3. So (25) and (29) are not a consistent metric-f(R) pair.
  - The field equations (10)-(12) are unchanged under ρ → ρ − λ(r), p → p + λ(r) for any λ(r). The split between matter and effective stress is fixed only by the trace equation (3) together with f_R = F. With f_R = F failing, the quoted ρ is not the matter density of a metric-f(R) solution.
  - Enforcing f = ∫F dR (integration constant set to zero) moves ρ at r = 1.25 r₀ from **0.815 to 2.278**, at r = 2 r₀ from 0.080 to 0.235, and at r = 3 r₀ from 0.0138 to 0.0409.
  - **ρ + p_r is unchanged by the correction.** It is the combination that enters the NEC.
- **Shown by:** `research/warp-drive/docket67-raw/rederive/modified-gravity-effective-stress.py` (re-run in this stage, ALL PASS; output lines "ex.(25): df/dr - F dR/dr with f from trace eq(3), r0=1, r=1.25,2,3: [12.562…, 0.6848…, 0.07565…]" and "published rho(16) = +0.81526; true rho with f=int F dR (c=0) = +2.27780"); `research/warp-drive/docket67-raw/shards/shard11.json`, key `modified-gravity-effective-stress`, `rederivation.outcome`.
- **Class:** discrepancy.

### Eq. (34) at α = −1 against example (31) (discrepancy, possibly extraction)

- **The paper states** (Sec. III.B.1, pp. 5-6):
  - for p_t = αρ and b = r₀²/r, F(r) = C₁(1 − r₀²/r²)^{1/2 + α/2} (eq. (31)), with the WEC shown for C₁ = −1, α = −1 (Fig. 3);
  - f(R) is given by (34): f(R) = C₁ R (1 − √(R/R₀))^{α/2 − 1/2} [√(R/R₀)(α² + 2α + 2) + (α + 2)].
- **What we find:**
  - At α = −1, eq. (31) gives F = C₁, a constant. So df/dR must equal C₁ (= −1 for the plotted case), and the trace equation indeed returns f = −R, which is consistent.
  - Eq. (34) as rendered, at α = −1, is f = C₁ R (1 + √u)/(1 − √u) with u = R/R₀. At R/R₀ = 1/4 this gives df/dR = 5 C₁ = **−5**, not −1.
  - This may be a misprint, or an artefact of the text layer.
- **Shown by:** `rederive/modified-gravity-effective-stress.py` (output: "printed eq(34) as extracted, alpha=-1: df/dR at R/R0=1/4 = -5 (eq (31) says F = -1)"; "ex.(31): f from trace eq(3) obeys df/dR = F (consistent; f = -R …)"); the same `shard11.json` entry.
- **Class:** discrepancy (misprint or extraction).

## What stands

- **The framework.** The field equations (2)-(3) and the effective-stress rearrangement (5)-(21), verified identically on a general static spherical metric.
- **Example (31).** F = −1 at α = −1, matter ρ = r₀²/r⁴ > 0 with ρ + p_r ≥ 0, and ρ_eff + p_r^eff = −2r₀²/r⁴ < 0. The energy conditions hold for the matter while the NEC fails for the effective stress, as the paper says.
- **For example (25), ρ + p_r.** It is invariant under the correction above, so the NEC statements that rest on it are unaffected.
