## Summary

The paper's treatment of the interior field stands. So does its statement that the speed at Earth's centre in a uniform Earth is near 8 km/s. Our one finding is a **discrepancy in eqs. (8)-(9)**. The energy equation takes +GmM(r)/r as the potential energy inside the Earth. That form is correct only when M(r) ∝ r², the constant-gravity case. For a uniform Earth it gives 11.2 km/s at the centre, √2 above the 7.9 km/s the paper's own text quotes. It is a discrepancy, not a refutation.

## Finding

### Eqs. (8)-(9): the interior potential energy (discrepancy)

- **The paper states** (p. 4): "Conservation of energy (E) dictates that the sum of kinetic and gravitational potential energy is constant. For an arbitrary radial mass profile M(r) we have: E = ½mv² + GmM(r)/r (8)", and so v(r) = √(2G(M(R)/R − M(r)/r)) (9).
- **What we find:**
  - The interior potential Φ(r) must satisfy Φ′ = GM(r)/r², the shell-theorem field of which the paper's eq. (1) is the uniform-density case. The function GM(r)/r has derivative GM′/r − GM/r². This equals GM/r² only when M′ = 2M/r, that is, M(r) ∝ r².
  - **Constant gravity.** M(r) = g r²/G, GM(r)/r = g r, and eqs. (8)-(9) are correct up to a constant.
  - **Uniform Earth.** M(r) = M r³/R³. Eq. (9) at r = 0 gives v = √(2GM/R) = **11.19 km/s**. The shell-theorem potential, Φ(R) − Φ(0) = GM/(2R), gives v = √(GM/R) = **7.91 km/s**. The ratio is exactly √2.
  - The paper's own text agrees with the second value: "The peak velocity at the center of the Earth is near 8 km/s" (p. 2), and "The period of oscillation and the peak velocity are the same for a circular orbit at Earth's surface" (p. 2).
  - We have not checked whether the numerical brachistochrone results for the PREM profile (Figs. 5-6) were computed from eq. (9) as printed. Three things limit what the discrepancy could do there:
    - the paper's own power-law fit to the PREM mass profile, α = 1.97 ± 0.02 (p. 5), puts the PREM case very close to M ∝ r², where eqs. (8)-(9) are exact;
    - for uniform density the factor is a constant in v², so the path shape, eq. (14), is unchanged and only the times rescale (by √2);
    - the uniform-density time in the appendix, T = √(S(2πR − S)/(Rg)) (p. 8), gives π√(R/g) ≈ 42 minutes at S = πR, the value of the correct potential, and the paper uses this formula to validate its numerical scheme (Fig. 7, which we saw only as a caption).

## What stands

- **Eq. (1).** The Newtonian interior field from the shell theorem.
- **The uniform-density results.** ω = √(g/R), the 42-minute fall time, and "near 8 km/s" at the centre (p. 2).
- **The constant-gravity case.** Eq. (3), T_g = 2√(2R/g) ≈ 38.0 minutes. Eq. (7): its series agrees with a direct integration of the constant-gravity chord time to the order printed (relative differences 4 × 10⁻⁷ at θ₀ = 0.3 and 7 × 10⁻⁴ at θ₀ = 1.0, consistent with an O(θ₀⁶) remainder; computed in the verification pass). Eqs. (8)-(9) are correct for constant gravity. We have not re-run the PREM numerics, so we offer no view on the comparison with PREM beyond the paper's own fit noted above.
