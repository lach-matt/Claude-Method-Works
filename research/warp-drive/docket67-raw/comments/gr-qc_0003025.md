## Summary

The paper's equations stand, and so do its averaged-energy-condition results. Our one finding concerns the prose next to eq. (2.6). Each of its three case statements names the opposite type of extremum to the one eq. (2.6), as printed, gives. The conclusion the paragraph draws, that pointwise NEC violation is easy for a non-minimally coupled scalar, holds with "maximum" and "minimum" exchanged. It is a discrepancy, not a refutation.

## Finding

### The three case statements after eq. (2.6) (discrepancy)

- **The paper states** (p. 5; the paper's φ_ξ is written φ here): eq. (2.6) gives the NEC as
  - G_μν k^μ k^ν = [1/(κ − ξφ²)] [φ′² − ξ(φ²)″] ≥ 0.
  - It then says: "For ξ < 0 any local minimum of φ² violates the NEC while for ξ > 0 and |φ| small, [meaning |φ| < (κ/ξ)^{1/2}], any local maximum of φ² violates the NEC. Finally for ξ > 0 and |φ| large, … any local minimum of φ² violates the NEC."
- **What we find:**
  - At a strict extremum of φ² with φ ≠ 0, φ′ = 0. So the sign of (2.6) is the sign of −ξ(φ²)″/(κ − ξφ²).
  - **ξ < 0.** The denominator is positive. The NEC fails where (φ²)″ < 0, that is, at a local **maximum**.
  - **ξ > 0, |φ| < (κ/ξ)^{1/2}.** The denominator is positive. The NEC fails where (φ²)″ > 0, that is, at a local **minimum**.
  - **ξ > 0, |φ| > (κ/ξ)^{1/2}.** The denominator is negative. The NEC fails where (φ²)″ < 0, that is, at a local **maximum**.
  - In each case the other type of extremum never violates the NEC.
  - Each prose statement is therefore the exchange of what (2.6) gives. The restatement in arXiv:2309.10848, eq. (18), gives the version consistent with (2.6).

## What stands

- **Eq. (2.2)** is conserved on shell. This was checked identically on a curved metric with arbitrary φ and V.
- **Eq. (2.6)** re-derives exactly, and V drops out of it. A minimally coupled scalar satisfies the NEC classically.
- **The step (2.8) → (2.10)** is a symbolic identity.
- **The ANEC cases.** In cases 1 and 2 the ANEC (of the effective stress tensor, on complete null geodesics with the paper's smooth asymptotic behaviour) holds. Any negative integrand is confined to case 3. The integration by parts (2.8) → (2.10) is exact, and direct numerical integration in case 2 is positive (`rederive/gr-qc_0003025-anec-cases.py`, checks A, B, D).
- **The existence result** for the massless, static, spherically symmetric scalar. We raise nothing against it; we did not re-run it numerically.
- **The paragraph's conclusion.** Pointwise NEC violation is easy for ξ ≠ 0. This holds as stated, with maximum and minimum exchanged.
