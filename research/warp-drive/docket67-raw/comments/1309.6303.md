## Summary

The paper's theorems stand as stated:

- local existence and uniqueness by the Banach fixed-point theorem;
- extension to a unique maximal solution.

They hold for the paper's model: a massive, conformally coupled free scalar field; states adiabatic of order zero; and the stated choice of renormalisation constants. Our one finding is a **misprint in the abstract**: it gives the critical Hubble rate as H_c where the body defines H_c². It is a discrepancy, not a refutation.

## Finding

### The abstract against eq. (1.9): discrepancy

- **The paper states:**
  - Abstract (p. 1): local solutions extend "up to a point where the scale factor a becomes singular or the Hubble function H reaches a critical value **H_c = 180π/G**".
  - Eq. (1.9) and the line after it (p. 4): "**H_c² := 1440π²/(8πG) = 180π/G**".
- **What we find:**
  - The body consistently uses H_c² = 180π/G. It is the denominator H_c² − H² in (1.9) and (3.3), and Sec. 4 speaks of the point "where either H² becomes bigger than H_c²".
  - The paper works with 8πG = c = ħ = 1 (p. 1) but writes G explicitly in (1.9). With c = ħ = 1, 1/G carries the dimension of a squared rate, so 180π/G is a value of H², not of H.
  - The abstract should read H_c² = 180π/G, or equivalently H_c = (180π/G)^{1/2}.

## What stands

- **Theorem 3.2, with Proposition A.7.** For initial data (a₀, H₀) with a₀ > 0 and |H₀| < H_c, a unique local solution of (1.9) exists, by the Banach fixed-point theorem.
- **Theorem 4.2 and Propositions 4.3-4.4.** A regular solution extends uniquely, and the maximal regular solution exists and is unique.
- **The class of validity, as the paper states it:**
  - a massive scalar field with conformal coupling ξ = 1/6;
  - states adiabatic of order zero, which the paper says explicitly are not Hadamard;
  - the renormalisation constants chosen in (1.8).
