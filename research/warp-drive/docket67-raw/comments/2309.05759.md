## Summary

Eqs. (76)-(77) stand within their stated hypotheses. So do the paper's other results that we checked, and we reproduce I_gravity = −ζ(3)/4 exactly from the printed integral. We have two findings. First, the printed integral (76) and the caption of Fig. 7 disagree about what happens as b² → 1. Second, the p. 25 citation of the ~10⁻²¹ laboratory bound describes it as a bound "for the electron", whereas the cited paper bounds a combined electron-photon coefficient. Both are discrepancies, not refutations. The first lies outside the paper's stated domain, b² < 1.

## Findings

### 1. Eq. (76) against the Fig. 7 caption as b² → 1 (discrepancy)

- **The paper states:**
  - Eq. (76), p. 20: I_gravity = (1/(6√π)) Σ_{w≥1} ∫∫ ds dt (9s² − 2st − 11t² + 5sb²) / (w³ t^{1/2} (s + t)⁶) × exp[−s(πmrw)² − t(πμrw)² − (s + t(1 − b²))/(t(s + t))].
  - The Fig. 7 caption, p. 21: "The function decreases rapidly but has a finite limit as b² → 1."
  - Eq. (12), p. 4: b² = β² ∈ (0, 1). Footnote 3, p. 5: 1 − b² > 0 "is important for convergence of the loop integrals".
- **What we find:**
  - Take m = 0 and μ → 0, the case of eq. (77), and substitute s = tx. The w-sum gives ζ(3), and each t-integral is a Gamma function:
    - I_gravity = (ζ(3)/(6√π)) ∫₀^∞ dx [(9x² − 2x − 11) Γ(5/2) a^{−5/2} + 5x b² Γ(7/2) a^{−7/2}] / (1 + x)⁶,
    - where a = (x + 1 − b²)/(1 + x).
  - **At b² = 0** this is −ζ(3)/4 exactly. That reproduces eq. (77).
  - **As b² → 1** the region x ~ 1 − b² dominates, and I_gravity (1 − b²)^{3/2} → **−ζ(3)/2** exactly. So I_gravity grows like −(ζ(3)/2)γ³ and has no finite limit. At b² = 1 the integrand behaves as x^{−5/2} near x = 0 and the integral diverges.
  - **Numerically**, I_gravity (1 − b²)^{3/2} = −0.3771, −0.5136, −0.5870, −0.5994 and −0.6009 at b² = 0.5, 0.9, 0.99, 0.999 and 0.9999. Compare −ζ(3)/2 = −0.6010.
  - The figure itself was not visible to us, only its caption. "Decreases rapidly" agrees with our result. "Has a finite limit" does not, for the integral as printed.
  - We cannot tell which of the two the authors would wish to amend: a term of the printed integrand (for instance the coefficient of sb²) or the caption.
  - b² = 1 is outside the stated domain (eq. 12, footnote 3). So nothing the paper claims within b² < 1 is affected, and eq. (77) is stated only for b² ≈ 0.

The reduction above is all the computation needs. We repeated it on 2026-10-04 and obtained the figures quoted.
- **Class:** discrepancy (an unreconciled internal inconsistency, possibly a misprint).

### 2. p. 25: the ~10⁻²¹ bound "for the electron" (discrepancy)

- **The paper states** (p. 25): "For the electron, for example, laboratory bounds on the dimensionless coefficients c_μν have reached the level of ∼10⁻²¹ [14]." Ref. [14] is Dreissen et al., arXiv:2206.00570.
- **What we find:**
  - Dreissen et al. bound the combined electron-photon tensor c′_μν = c_μν + k_μν/2, "where c_μν and k_μν describe LV for electrons and photons", and "interpret the results as a difference in isotropy between photons and electrons" (their p. 2).
  - They fit the four anisotropic Sun-centred components c_{X−Y}, c_XY, c_XZ and c_YZ, with 1σ uncertainties of (3.9-9.3) × 10⁻²¹ (their Table 1, p. 6). "10⁻²¹ level" is their abstract's phrase, and KN's "∼10⁻²¹" matches it.
  - **The comparison is exact where k = 0.** Reading the bound as one on the electron's c is exact when the photon-sector k vanishes, or in coordinates that move it into c. The paper does not say which it intends.
- **Shown by:** `research/warp-drive/docket67-raw/adjudications/dreissen-sme-lab-bound.json`, `decision` (both sources READ) and `corrections_to_record` ("KN p.25's 'for the electron' is a restatement discrepancy"); Dreissen et al. p. 2 and Table 1, re-read at source on 2026-10-04.
- **Class:** discrepancy (in how a cited result is restated).

## What stands

- **Eqs. (76)-(77)** within b² ≈ 0, m ≈ 0 and μr → 0, with μ the graviton infrared regulator of eq. (69). I_gravity(0) = −ζ(3)/4 is reproduced from the printed integral.
- **Eq. (41)**, the Yukawa coupling of the bulk scalar to the brane fermion.
- **Eqs. (68)-(73) and footnote 8.** G₄ = 1/(8πM̄₄²), and M̄₄ = (2πr)^{1/2} M̄₅^{3/2}. M̄₄ = 2.4 × 10¹⁸ GeV is a rounding.
- **The tensor structure of (76).** b_μb_ν − ¼η_μν b² is symmetric and traceless.
