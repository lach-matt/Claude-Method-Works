## Summary

The review's recommended photospheric composition stands, and so do the conclusions we checked. Our one finding is a small internal inconsistency about cadmium, between the text and Table 1. It has no effect on any result. It is a discrepancy, not a refutation.

## Finding

### Cadmium: pp. 18-19 against Table 1 (discrepancy)

- **The paper states:**
  - p. 18: a 3D-correction procedure "has been implemented for Cd (Youssef, Doenszelmann & Grevesse 1990), W, Os, Ir and Au, all of which have only heavily blended transitions available in the solar spectrum".
  - p. 19: "Grevesse et al. (2009) present the solar analyses of Cu, Zn, Ga, Ge, Rb, Sr, Y, Zr, Nb, Mo, Ru, Rh, Pd, Ag, Cd, Sn, Ba, Pb and Th, which we adopt throughout".
  - Table 1 (p. 42) prints a single Cd entry, 1.71 ± 0.03.
- **What we find:**
  - Elements for which both a photospheric and a meteoritic value are adopted show two entries in Table 1, for example W 0.85 ± 0.12 and 0.65 ± 0.04, or Os 1.40 ± 0.08 and 1.35 ± 0.03. Cd shows one.
  - The value 1.71 ± 0.03 equals the CI value. Asplund, Amarsi & Grevesse 2021 (arXiv:2105.01661) mark Cd as "meteorites".
  - In the text read, the column the single Cd entry sits in cannot be determined.
  - Either way, the text names Cd among the elements analysed photospherically and adopted, but Table 1 shows no separate photospheric Cd value.
  - **The difference is immaterial.** Whichever column holds 1.71, the value is the same.
- **Shown by:** reading pp. 18-19 and 42; `research/warp-drive/docket67-raw/adjudications/asplund-2009-hybrid-column-recommendation.json`, `corrections_to_record` ("A09 internal discrepancy, recorded not repaired") and `scope` (the column cannot be told from the extracted text).
- **Class:** discrepancy.

## What stands

- **The recommended composition.** The photospheric column is the recommended solar composition, with the CI values "also" given for comparison (p. 8 and p. 24).
- **The Si peg.** The meteoritic scale is placed on the photospheric one by enforcing equal Si abundances (p. 24).
- **Volatile depletion.** Volatile elements are depleted in CI chondrites (pp. 2-3).
- **The photosphere-meteorite agreement.** It is very good for the non-volatile elements: a mean difference of 0.00 ± 0.05 dex for 45 elements (p. 25).
- **Lithium.** It is depleted in the photosphere by about a factor 150 relative to meteorites, which requires mixing below the convection zone (pp. 9-10).
