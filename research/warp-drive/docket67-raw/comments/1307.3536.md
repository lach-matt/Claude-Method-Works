## Summary

The paper's physics conclusions stand. These are the metastability of the electroweak vacuum within the SM, the exclusion of absolute stability at the paper's inputs by every reading of the error budget, the instability scale of 10¹⁰-10¹² GeV, and the stated caveats. We record **three discrepancies** in how the stability bound's uncertainties and confidence levels are combined and quoted on pp. 19-20 and 31. None reverses a conclusion. Each is a discrepancy, not a refutation.

## Findings

### Eq. (65): the combined uncertainty ±1.5 GeV (discrepancy)

- **The paper states** (p. 20): with M_t = (173.34 ± 0.76_exp ± 0.3_th) GeV, "combining in quadrature theoretical uncertainties with experimental errors, we find M_h > (129.6 ± 1.5) GeV". The inputs are eq. (64)'s slope 2.0 in M_t, its α₃ term 0.5 GeV per 0.0007 and its own ±0.3 GeV.
- **What we find:**
  - The quadrature of the components the text names is 2.0 × √(0.76² + 0.3²) = 1.63 GeV, combined with 0.5 GeV (α₃) and 0.3 GeV (eq. (64)). It gives **±1.74 GeV**. Without the top-mass theory error it gives ±1.63 GeV.
  - The printed ±1.5 GeV is reproduced only as 2.0 × 0.76 = 1.52 GeV, the experimental top-mass term alone.
- **Shown by:** `research/warp-drive/docket67-raw/rederive/1307.3536.py` and `.out` (C1: "printed +-1.5 reproduces only as 2.0 x 0.76"; "the stated quadrature of the named components gives +-1.74 (or +-1.63 without the 0.3 top theory error)"); `research/warp-drive/docket67-raw/shards/shard05.json`, key `1307.3536`, `rederivation.outcome` C1.
- **Class:** discrepancy.

### Eq. (66) against eq. (64): the central value and the ±0.42 (discrepancy)

- **The paper states** (p. 20): "We can express the stability condition of eq. (64) as M_t < (171.53 ± 0.15 ± 0.23_α₃ ± 0.15_Mh) GeV = (171.53 ± 0.42) GeV", "combin[ing] in quadrature the theoretical uncertainty with the experimental uncertainties on M_h and α₃".
- **What we find:**
  - **The central value.** Inverting eq. (64) at M_h = 125.15 GeV and α₃ = 0.1184 gives M_t,crit = **171.12 GeV**, 0.41 GeV below the printed 171.53 GeV.
    - Eq. (64) is stated as an approximation "in the neighbourhood of the measured values", and 171.5 GeV lies 1.8 GeV from 173.34 GeV. Curvature of the boundary is therefore a possible cause. We have not computed it.
    - Two later analyses give values near the inverted one: AFS (arXiv:1707.08124) give 171.18 GeV, and Hiller (arXiv:2401.08811) gives 171.10 GeV. These are recorded for comparison only.
  - **The uncertainty.** The quadrature of the three printed parts is √(0.15² + 0.23² + 0.15²) = **0.31 GeV**, not 0.42 GeV. It reaches 0.43 GeV only if the 0.3 GeV top-mass theory error of p. 20 is added, and that error is not among those the sentence lists.
- **Shown by:** `rederive/1307.3536.out` (C3: "eq.(64) inverted at M_h = 125.15, a3 = 0.1184: M_t,crit 171.115"; "quadrature of 0.15, 0.23, 0.15 … 0.313"; "… plus the 0.3 top-mass theory error of p.20 0.433"; the AFS comparison); `shards/shard05.json`, key `1307.3536`, `rederivation.outcome` C3.
- **Class:** discrepancy.

### p. 20 against p. 31: the confidence level of the stability exclusion (discrepancy)

- **The paper states:**
  - p. 20: "vacuum stability of the SM up to the Planck scale is excluded at 2.8σ (99.8% C.L. one-sided)";
  - p. 31: "metastability is now preferred at 99.3% CL".
- **What we find:**
  - 2.8σ one-sided is **99.74 %**, which rounds to 99.7 %, not 99.8 %. One-sided 99.8 % corresponds to 2.88σ.
  - Recomputing z = (129.6 − 125.15)/σ gives 2.93 with the printed ±1.5 (and δM_h = 0.24). It gives 2.54 with the full stated budget. So 2.8 lies between the two readings.
  - 99.3 % one-sided is **2.46σ**, a different figure from p. 20's 2.8σ.
- **Shown by:** `rederive/1307.3536.out` (C2: "one-sided CL of 2.8 sigma, % 99.744"; "z with printed +-1.5 … 2.929"; "z with the full stated budget 2.541"; C4: "sigma of the p.31 '99.3% CL' (one-sided) 2.457"); `shards/shard05.json`, key `1307.3536`, `rederivation.outcome` C2 and C4.
- **Class:** discrepancy.

## What stands

- **Absolute stability is excluded at the paper's inputs** at more than 2.5σ on every reading of the error budget recomputed here (2.54σ to 2.93σ). This is a statement about the 2013 inputs. With present inputs the significance depends on the top-mass definition, as the paper's own caveat anticipates: through eq. (64), 2.86σ for the Monte-Carlo mass and 1.67σ for the cross-section mass, both on the metastable side (`rederive/1307.3536.out`, C5).
- **The instability scale.** Λ_I = 10¹⁰-10¹² GeV (eq. (67), Landau gauge, Λ_I ≈ 13 Λ_V). log₁₀ Λ_I = 10.6 at the 2013 inputs, and 11.2-11.4 at today's, all inside the range.
- **The inputs.** M_h = 125.15 ± 0.24 GeV and the rest of Table 2. g = 2M_W/v (eq. (18)).
- **The metastability conclusion and its caveats.** These include the Λ_QCD-size ambiguity in the definition of M_t (p. 6, p. 20) and the assumption that the SM holds up to very high energy scales (p. 31). At present inputs, through eq. (64), the top mass remains on the metastable side under both top-mass definitions.
