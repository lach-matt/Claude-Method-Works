## Summary

The decomposition stands, and so do its inputs and its leading-order treatment. Both columns of Table I add up to the nucleon mass, and the m_s → 0 column rebuilds exactly from the paper's own equations. We have two small findings. In the m_s → ∞ column, one entry does not rebuild to its printed value. In the text layer, the ratio printed in Eq. (31) does not give the strange charge of Eq. (32). Both are discrepancies, not refutations. The second may come from extracting the text.

## Findings

### 1. Table I, m_s → ∞ column: the quark-energy entry (discrepancy)

- **The paper states** (Table I, p. 9): the quark energy 3(a − b)/4 is 270 MeV (m_s → 0) and 300 MeV (m_s → ∞). The quark mass, gluon energy and trace anomaly rows are 160/110, 320/320 and 190/210 MeV. On p. 6, the numbers are "rounded up … to nearest 10 MeV".
- **What we find:**
  - The inputs are a = 0.55 (Eq. 29) and, for the heavy-strange route, bM = 106.7 MeV (from Eq. 37's −740 MeV, giving m_s⟨s̄s⟩ = 61.7 MeV; printed 107 in Eq. 38).
  - With these, 3(aM − bM)/4 = 3(516.45 − 106.7)/4 = **307.3 MeV** at M = 939 MeV (the p. 2 value). Rounded to the nearest 10 MeV this is **310**, not the printed 300.
  - With 300 the column sums to 940 MeV. With 310 it would sum to 950 MeV.
  - The same rebuild gives the m_s → 0 column exactly: 267.0 → 270, 160.5 → 160, 316.9 → 320 and 194.6 → 190. The m_s → ∞ anomaly entry, (M − bM)/4 = 208.1 → 210, also rebuilds.
  - **The difference is small.** It is 7 MeV. On p. 6 the paper gives "about 5 to 10 MeV" as the total effect on individual numbers of the omitted higher-order perturbative effects and of the errors on the σ-term and the current quark masses, so the difference is of the size the paper itself allows. We do not suggest a cause.

The arithmetic above is all that is involved.
- **Class:** discrepancy.

### 2. Eqs. (31)-(32): the strange charge, as extracted (discrepancy, possibly an extraction artefact)

- **The paper states** (p. 6): ⟨P|s̄s|P⟩ ≈ 0.11 ⟨P|ūu + d̄d|P⟩ (31), ≈ 0.77 (32), "where in the second line, I have used (m_u + m_d)/2 ≈ 7 MeV". The σ-term is 45 ± 5 MeV (p. 5).
- **What we find:**
  - ⟨ūu + d̄d⟩ = σ/m̂ = 45/7 = 6.43. Then 0.11 × 6.43 = **0.707**, not 0.77.
  - Reaching 0.77 needs a ratio of **0.1198**.
  - The downstream numbers use 0.77: m_s⟨s̄s⟩ = 0.77 × 150 = 115.5 MeV (p. 7: "115 MeV"), and bM = 45 + 115.5 = 160.5 MeV (Eq. 33: 160).
  - This was established in the alphaXiv text layer only. The printed digit may differ from what the extraction shows.
- **Shown by:** `research/warp-drive/docket67-raw/adjudications/hep-ph_9410274.json`, `corrections_to_record` (the Eq. 31 item). The arithmetic above is all that is involved. - **Class:** discrepancy.

## What stands

- **The decomposition.** Eq. (20), with b dimensionless; Eq. (30), bM = σ_πN + ⟨m_s s̄s⟩; Eq. (33).
- **The inputs.** σ = 45 ± 5 MeV; m̂ = 7 MeV and m_s = 150 MeV at 1 GeV².
- **Both columns of Table I add up to about 940 MeV.**
- **Scale independence.** The mass and anomaly operators are "finite and scale independent" (p. 4).
- **The order of approximation.** The neglect of γ_m and higher-order terms in β(g) is stated (p. 5), and the paper's own caveats on omitted errors are on p. 6.
