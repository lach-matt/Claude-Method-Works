## Summary

The paper's argument and conclusions stand. These are the use of the flat-space quantum inequality (1) in regions small compared with the local curvature radius and the distance to boundaries, and the constraints on wormhole geometries that follow. Our one finding is a **discrepancy in a coefficient** in the Casimir illustration of Sec. 2. Eq. (3) uses the energy density of two field degrees of freedom where the text describes a single massless scalar. Correcting it moves the illustrative cap from 0.46 L to 0.54 L. The qualitative conclusion of the illustration is unchanged.

## Finding

### Eq. (3): the Casimir coefficient π²/45 (discrepancy)

- **The paper states** (p. 5): "Let us consider a massless scalar field with periodicity of length L in the z-direction … ⟨T_μν u^μ u^ν⟩ = −(π²/(45 L⁴))(1 + 3v²)γ²." Combined with the scalar inequality (1), this gives the caps τ₀ ≤ (3L/2π)(5/6)^{1/4} ≈ 0.46 L (eq. (6)) and f ≤ 0.42 (eq. (9)).
- **What we find:**
  - For one real massless scalar field periodic in one direction, we compute ρ = **−π²/(90 L⁴)**, by two regularisations (mode cutoff and zeta function). That is exactly half the printed coefficient.
  - π²/45 is the value for two field degrees of freedom, such as the electromagnetic field. Paired with the electromagnetic inequality (constant 3/(16π²)), it gives the same caps as the matched scalar pair below.
  - With the one-scalar density in (1):
    - the static cap becomes τ₀ ≤ (135/(16π⁴))^{1/4} L = **0.5425 L** (printed 0.46 L);
    - the moving-observer cap becomes 0.5049 l₁ (printed 0.42 l₁).
  - From the printed eq. (3), the paper's own arithmetic re-derives exactly: 0.456190 L and 0.424533.

### What it does not touch

- The illustration's point is that a constant negative Casimir density is compatible with (1) once the sampling time is capped at a fraction of L of order one half. That point holds at 0.46 L and at 0.54 L alike.
- At the cap the density meets the bound by construction, under either coefficient.
- Sec. 4.5 applies the Casimir discussion of Sec. 2 to the Morris-Thorne-Yurtsever wormhole, with τ₀ = f s and f ≈ 0.1. That value lies well inside either cap.

## What stands

- **The flat-space quantum inequality (1)** for the free massless minimally coupled scalar field, and the paper's argument for applying it to sampling times small compared with the minimum local curvature radius and the distance to boundaries.
- **The illustration's arithmetic.** Eqs. (4)-(9) follow exactly from eq. (3) as printed (0.456190 L and 0.424533), and so does the paper's conclusion that once τ₀ < 0.42 l₁ "the Minkowski space quantum inequality also holds in the compactified spacetime" (p. 6).
- **The wormhole constraints.** The constraints of Secs. 4-5 and the conclusion of Sec. 6. Unless one accepts very large discrepancies in length scales, macroscopic static traversable wormholes appear unlikely.
