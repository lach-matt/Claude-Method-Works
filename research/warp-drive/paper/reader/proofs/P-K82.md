**Remark {#rem:vlasov-obstructions}** (What the hypotheses avoid.) Four obstructions are proved in this chapter, and the
theorem is built so that none applies. (O1) At an axis point every invariant null phase is radial, and two of one
orientation have vanishing mixed symbol ([[lem:vlasov-axis-phases]]): the labels are kept off the axes, and only the
radial pair of [[sec:vlasov-radial]], one dust on each null line, reaches them. (O2) Under a C² metric a continuous
orbit density carries no particle to an axis transversally: it would focus there ([[prop:vlasov-axis-focus]]). (O3)
Without a hierarchy of frequencies, an antipodal pair and any third phase resonate exactly ([[lem:vlasov-resonance]]):
the frequencies are 1, Λ, …, Λ^(n−1). (O4) A non-radial wave with a component on the orbit block leaves a term of order
λ⁻¹ in the constraints ([[lem:vlasov-constraint-coefficient]] (c)): the waves of the labels are torus-polarised; those
of the radial pair have an orbit part near the axes, and leave no such term because their phases are radial on Σ_τ0
and they are tangent to the spheres there ([[lem:vlasov-radial-constraints]]).

### Radial dusts reaching the axes {#sec:vlasov-radial}

[[thm:vlasov]] keeps every label at distance at least 2δ from the axes, and by [[lem:vlasov-axis-phases]] at most two of
the dusts present at an axis point are separated, one on each null line. The background of [[thm:two-dusts]] carries
such a pair, an ingoing and an outgoing radial dust, present at the axes. This section adds a small restricted field to
it. Four things change. First, the waves of the pair cannot be torus-polarised. Near axis 2, e^(2ψ2) dφ2² is a_⊥ times
the form (x3 dx4 − x4 dx3)²/ρ2² ([[prop:class-axis]] (a)), which equals dx4² on the points (ρ2, 0) and dx3² on the
points (0, ρ2) of the (x3, x4)-plane and so has no limit at ρ2 = 0; a torus-polarised wave whose amplitude does not
vanish at the axis is not continuous there. The waves of the pair are polarised instead by tensors parallel along their
generators, smooth across the axes ([[lem:vlasov-radial-wave]]). Second, such a wave has an orbit part near the axes,
and it leaves no term of order λ⁻¹ in the constraints because its phase is radial on Σ_τ0 and it is tangent to the
spheres there ([[lem:vlasov-radial-constraints]]); the data of the pair are chosen so. Third, the commutator of the wave
operator with a generator that reaches an axis has smooth coefficients there, because the planar shear of
[[prop:class-axis]] vanishes to second order at the axis ([[lem:vlasov-axis-smooth]], [[lem:vlasov-axis-commutator]]).
Fourth, the background is constructed on the adapted domain of [[cor:class-two-dust]], uniformly in the number of dusts
([[prop:vlasov-radial-background]]), and the high-frequency step runs on a domain of the type [[eq:omega]] inside it
([[prop:vlasov-radial-step]]).

**Definition {#def:vlasov-radial-wave}** (Waves with a parallel polarisation.) Let g0 be a class metric and u an
invariant eikonal of g0, both smooth across the axes, with ℓ := g0⁻¹du future directed, and let F, ν, ξ := F/ν, k :=
ν du and c be as in [[def:vlasov-wave]]. A *parallel polarisation* of u is a smooth G-invariant symmetric 2-tensor field
𝒫 on a neighbourhood of supp F with

    ∇_ℓ 𝒫 = 0,      𝒫(ℓ, ·) = 0,      tr_(g0) 𝒫 = 0,      |𝒫|²_(g0) = 2.

The *wave* of the dust (u, F) with the polarisation 𝒫 at frequency ν is the profile

    W := 2 ξ cos θ 𝒫 + χ k ⊗ k,      χ := ½ c sin 2θ,      θ := ν u/λ.

F vanishes on every generator on which it vanishes initially, and so does c, so W is smooth where g0 and u are. Off the
axes T0 := e^(2ψ1) dφ1² − e^(2ψ2) dφ2² is a parallel polarisation (proof of [[lem:vlasov-wave]]), and the wave with 𝒫 =
T0 is that of [[def:vlasov-wave]].

**Lemma {#lem:vlasov-radial-wave}** (Parallel polarisations.) Let Ω be a domain of the type [[eq:omega]], g0 a smooth
class metric on Ω and u a smooth invariant eikonal of g0 on Ω, with du ≠ 0 and ℓ := g0⁻¹du future directed, such that
every ray of u through a point of Ω, followed to the past, stays in Ω and meets Σ_τ0.

(a) Let 𝒫0 be a smooth G-invariant field on Σ_τ0 ∩ Ω of symmetric bilinear forms on the tangent spaces of the
spacetime, with 𝒫0(ℓ, ·) = 0, tr_(g0) 𝒫0 = 0 and |𝒫0|²_(g0) = 2. Its parallel transport along the rays of u is a
parallel polarisation 𝒫 of u on Ω, smooth on Ω, the axes included, equal to 𝒫0 on Σ_τ0.

(b) The wave W of [[def:vlasov-radial-wave]] satisfies W(ℓ, ·) = 0 and tr_(g0) W = 0, and in the two-scale expansion of
R[g0 + λ W] at the phase θ = ν u/λ, R^(−1) = 0 and R^(0) = Ric(g0) − F² du ⊗ du.

(c) Let ζ := (ρ1² − ρ2²)/r², let s(y) := sin(πy/3)/y, an entire function with s(0) = π/3, put P_⊥ := |dx|² − (x̂·dx)²,
Q1 := (x1 dx2 − x2 dx1)²/r² and Q2 := (x3 dx4 − x4 dx3)²/r², and

    𝒯 := −(2/√3) cos(πζ/3) P_⊥ + 4 s(1 + ζ) Q1 + 4 s(1 − ζ) Q2      on R × (R⁴ ∖ {0}).            {#eq:vlasov-template}

Let S_r := {τ0} × {|x| = r}, Π the metric g0 induces on S_r, π the g0-orthogonal projection of the tangent spaces of the
spacetime at points of S_r onto TS_r, and 𝒯̊ the Π-trace-free part of the restriction of 𝒯 to TS_r. If u|Σ_τ0 is a
function of r and |𝒯̊|_Π > 0 on Σ_τ0 ∩ Ω, then 𝒫0 := √2 |𝒯̊|_Π⁻¹ 𝒯̊(π ·, π ·) satisfies the hypotheses of (a), and it
vanishes on every vector g0-orthogonal to the spheres S_r. If g0 is invariant under O(4) with area radius |x|, as the
backgrounds of [[thm:two-dusts]] are in their chart, then 𝒫0 = 𝒯, and |𝒯̊|_Π ≥ 1 for every metric close enough to g0
in C⁰ on Σ_τ0 ∩ Ω.

*Proof.* (a) Since ∇_ℓ ℓ = ½ d g0⁻¹(du, du) = 0, the rays are null geodesics. A null vector is never tangent to a
spacelike slice, so ℓ(τ) ≠ 0, and Z := ℓ/ℓ(τ) has τ as parameter along its integral curves. Let Φ(x0, t) be the point at
time τ0 + t of the integral curve of Z from x0 ∈ Σ_τ0. Φ is smooth, injective (integral curves do not cross) and has
invertible differential (Z is transverse to Σ_τ0), so it is a diffeomorphism onto its image, which contains Ω by
hypothesis. Along t ↦ Φ(x0, t) let 𝒫 solve the linear system ∇_Z 𝒫 = 0, that is d𝒫_ab/dt = Z^c (Γ^d_ca 𝒫_db + Γ^d_cb
𝒫_ad), with 𝒫 = 𝒫0(x0) at t = 0. Its coefficients are smooth in (x0, t), so 𝒫 is smooth in (x0, t), hence on Ω, and
∇_ℓ 𝒫 = ℓ(τ) ∇_Z 𝒫 = 0. The covector ω := 𝒫(ℓ, ·) satisfies ∇_ℓ ω = (∇_ℓ 𝒫)(ℓ, ·) + 𝒫(∇_ℓ ℓ, ·) = 0 and vanishes on
Σ_τ0, so it vanishes; ℓ(tr 𝒫) = tr ∇_ℓ 𝒫 = 0 and ℓ(|𝒫|²) = 2 ⟨∇_ℓ 𝒫, 𝒫⟩ = 0, so tr 𝒫 = 0 and |𝒫|² = 2. An element of
G is an isometry of g0 that fixes u, hence ℓ; it maps rays to rays and parallel fields along them to parallel fields, so
𝒫 is G-invariant, 𝒫0 being so, by uniqueness for the system. Nothing here refers to the orbit space: g0, u and 𝒫0 are
smooth in the chart X, the axes included, and so is 𝒫.

(b) The proof of [[lem:vlasov-wave]] uses of T0 only ∇_ℓ T0 = 0, T0(ℓ, ·) = 0, tr T0 = 0, |T0|² = 2 and ⟨T0, k ⊗ k⟩ =
ν² T0(ℓ, ℓ) = 0. These hold for 𝒫, and that proof applies with 𝒫 in place of T0.

(c) Off the axes let e1 := (−x2, x1, 0, 0)/ρ1, e2 := (0, 0, −x4, x3)/ρ2, and e_ϑ the Euclidean unit vector tangent to
{|x| = r} in the plane spanned by (x1, x2, 0, 0) and (0, 0, x3, x4): a Euclidean orthonormal frame of the tangent space
of {|x| = r}. P_⊥ is the identity on that space and vanishes on x̂; x1 dx2 − x2 dx1 takes the value ρ1 on e1 and vanishes
on e_ϑ, e2 and x̂; and x3 dx4 − x4 dx3 takes the value ρ2 on e2 and vanishes on e_ϑ, e1 and x̂. With ρ1²/r² = (1 + ζ)/2
and ρ2²/r² = (1 − ζ)/2, 𝒯(x̂, ·) = 0 and 𝒯 is diagonal in the frame, with entries

    λ_ϑ = −(2/√3) cos(πζ/3),      λ_1 = λ_ϑ + 2 sin(π(1 + ζ)/3),      λ_2 = λ_ϑ + 2 sin(π(1 − ζ)/3).

With t := π/2 − πζ/3 these are λ_ϑ = −(2/√3) sin t, λ_1 = cos t + (1/√3) sin t and λ_2 = −cos t + (1/√3) sin t, since
2 sin(t ± π/6) = √3 sin t ± cos t. So λ_ϑ + λ_1 + λ_2 = 0 and λ_ϑ² + λ_1² + λ_2² = 2 cos²t + (1/3 + 1/3 + 4/3) sin²t =
2: 𝒯 restricted to the tangent space of {|x| = r} is trace-free for the Euclidean metric, with Euclidean norm √2, off the
axes and, by continuity, on them. 𝒯 is smooth on R × (R⁴ ∖ {0}), since ζ, P_⊥, Q1 and Q2 are and s is entire; it has no
dτ component; and it is G-invariant, since the rotations of each plane preserve ζ, P_⊥, x1 dx2 − x2 dx1 and x3 dx4 −
x4 dx3, and the reflections x2 ↦ −x2 and x4 ↦ −x4 change the sign of one of these 1-forms only.

TS_r = ker dτ ∩ ker dr is spacelike for g0, so π and Π are smooth and G-equivariant, and so are 𝒯̊ and 𝒫0, the latter
where |𝒯̊|_Π > 0. By construction 𝒫0 vanishes on the kernel of π, the g0-orthogonal complement of TS_r. Since u|Σ_τ0 is a
function of r, du vanishes on TS_r, so ℓ lies in that complement and 𝒫0(ℓ, ·) = 0. The g0-trace and the g0-norm of a
form that vanishes on that complement are its Π-trace and Π-norm on TS_r, so tr_(g0) 𝒫0 = 0 and |𝒫0|² = 2. If g0 is
invariant under O(4) with area radius |x|, Π is the Euclidean metric of {|x| = r}, so 𝒯̊ = 𝒯 on TS_r and |𝒯̊|_Π = √2;
and the g0-orthogonal complement of TS_r is span(∂_τ, x̂·∂_x), because the isotropy group of a point of S_r acts
irreducibly on TS_r and trivially on that span, so that g0(∂_τ, ·) and g0(x̂·∂_x, ·) are invariant linear forms on TS_r,
hence zero; 𝒯 vanishes on that span, so 𝒫0 = 𝒯. The last claim follows by continuity on the compact set Σ_τ0 ∩ Ω. ∎

The waves of the pair are therefore smooth tensors of the spacetime, with no reference to the orbit space. At axis 2
(ζ = 1) the template has the entries (λ_ϑ, λ_1, λ_2) = (−1, 2, −1)/√3, equal on e_ϑ and e2 as smoothness at that axis
requires; at axis 1 (ζ = −1) they are (−1, −1, 2)/√3. In between it has an orbit part λ_ϑ ≠ 0: the polarisation of
[[lem:vlasov-constraint-coefficient]] (c), whose term of order λ⁻¹ the next lemma removes on the initial slice.

**Lemma {#lem:vlasov-radial-constraints}** (Waves tangent to the spheres in the constraints.) Let the data on Σ :=
Σ_τ0 ∩ Ω oscillate as in [[lem:vlasov-constraint-coefficient]], with families A and γ = γ0 + λ Σ_A W_A + λ²(…), κ =
κ0 + Σ_A κ1_A + λ(…), the second and mixed harmonics entering at order λ² in γ and λ in κ, γ0 and every profile
G-invariant, θ_A = φ_A/λ for the phase φ_A of family A on Σ, and p_A := dφ_A. Let each family be either
torus-polarised, as in [[lem:vlasov-constraint-coefficient]] (b), or *tangent to the spheres*: p_A is a multiple of dr
on Σ, W_A and κ1_A vanish on ν0 := γ0⁻¹dr, and tr_(γ0) W_A = tr_(γ0) κ1_A = 0. Then:

(a) for every function α with 1 + α γ0^(rr) > 0 and every k, the terms of Φ(γ + α dr ⊗ dr, κ + k) that carry a second
derivative of some λ W_A or a first derivative of some κ1_A are of order one: λ⁻¹ times λ times a function of the form
[h] of [[sec:vlasov-scaled]], smooth in α. So the constraint map has no coefficient of order λ⁻¹, and the corrections
of [[lem:constraints-evolution]] meet none;

(b) the conclusion of [[lem:vlasov-leaf-curvature]] holds: p, P, A0, A^B and 𝒮 are of class 𝔓, with parts of order λ⁰
those of (γ0, κ0).

*Proof.* (a) By the proof of [[lem:vlasov-constraint-coefficient]] (a), these terms are, at order λ⁻¹,

    Σ_A (W_A″(γ_h⁻¹p_A, γ_h⁻¹p_A) − |p_A|²_(γ_h) tr_(γ_h) W_A″)   in ρ,
    2 Σ_A (−κ1_A′(γ_h⁻¹p_A, ·) + p_A tr_(γ_h) κ1_A′)                 in J,

with γ_h := γ + α dr ⊗ dr, W_A″ := ∂²_(θ_A) W_A and κ1_A′ := ∂_(θ_A) κ1_A; every coefficient is a smooth function of x,
the phases, λ and α. The forms γ0, W_B, κ1_B and the profiles are G-invariant, so they have no mixed orbit–torus
components, and neither has γ_h⁻¹; it maps orbit covectors to orbit vectors. For a torus-polarised family, W_A″ and
κ1_A′ vanish on orbit vectors, so the terms with γ_h⁻¹p_A vanish, and the traces are O(λ), as in the proof of
[[lem:vlasov-constraint-coefficient]] (b). For a family tangent to the spheres, every W_B vanishes on ν0: a
torus-polarised one because ν0 is an orbit vector, the others by hypothesis. Hence γ_h(ν0) = (1 + α γ0^(rr)) dr +
λ²(…), and γ_h⁻¹dr = (1 + α γ0^(rr))⁻¹ ν0 + λ²(…); as p_A is a multiple of dr, W_A″(γ_h⁻¹p_A, ·) and κ1_A′(γ_h⁻¹p_A,
·) are O(λ²). Further, with G := γ0 + α dr ⊗ dr, G⁻¹ = γ0⁻¹ − α (1 + α γ0^(rr))⁻¹ ν0 ⊗ ν0 and γ_h⁻¹ = G⁻¹ − λ G⁻¹
(Σ_B W_B) G⁻¹ + O(λ²), so tr_(γ_h) W_A″ = tr_(γ0) W_A″ − α (1 + α γ0^(rr))⁻¹ W_A″(ν0, ν0) + O(λ) = O(λ), and likewise
tr_(γ_h) κ1_A′ = O(λ). In every case the coefficient of λ⁻¹ is a smooth function of (x, θ, λ, α) that vanishes at λ =
0, hence λ times such a function, by Taylor's formula in λ: the term is of order one and of the form [h]. The
corrections of [[lem:constraints-evolution]] change γ by α dr ⊗ dr and κ by terms that enter no term of order λ⁻¹.

(b) The coefficients of the angular second derivatives of α in ρ, those of the first derivatives of (ϖ, q) in J, and 𝒮
involve γ, its inverse, Π and α undifferentiated, so they are of class 𝔓, as in the proof of
[[lem:vlasov-leaf-curvature]]. In (γ^(rr))^(3/2) H, H = H̃/ν with H̃ = ½ tr_Π(∂_r Π − 𝓛_σ Π), and σ^B = Π^(BA) γ(∂_r,
∂_A). A derivative costing λ⁻¹ falls on a term of order λ only in three places. (1) ∂_r of λ W_A in Π gives ∂_r φ_A
∂_(θ_A) W_A restricted to the spheres. (2) The angular derivatives of λ W_A in σ^C ∂_C Π give σ0^C ∂_C φ_A ∂_(θ_A) W_A
restricted to the spheres, σ0 the shift of γ0. Traced with Π^(AB) = Π0^(AB) + O(λ), (1) and (2) give (∂_r φ_A −
σ0^C ∂_C φ_A) ∂_(θ_A) tr_(Π0)(W_A|TS) + O(λ), and tr_(Π0)(W_A|TS) = tr_(γ0) W_A = 0 for both kinds, the
γ0-trace of a form that vanishes on ν0, the γ0-normal of the spheres, being its Π0-trace. (3) The angular derivatives of
the oscillating part of σ. A torus-polarised W_A vanishes on ∂_r, which has no torus component, so only the families
tangent to the spheres give σ an oscillating part λ σ1_A(x, θ_A) of order λ; its fast angular derivative is ∂_C φ_A
∂_(θ_A) σ1_A = 0, since φ_A is a function of r on Σ. The second and mixed harmonics are of order λ² and contribute
O(λ). So H̃ is H̃0 plus λ times a function of the form [h], and, α entering only through ν_U and γ_U⁻¹ algebraically, the
same holds as functions of α. ∎

The data of a family tangent to the spheres have that form when its wave on Σ_τ0 is 2 λ ξ cos θ 𝒫0, θ = ν φ/λ for an
eikonal φ that is a function of r on Σ_τ0, with 𝒫0 as in [[lem:vlasov-radial-wave]] (c). Indeed W := 2 ξ cos θ 𝒫0
vanishes on the future unit normal n of Σ_τ0 and on ν0, which are g0-orthogonal to the spheres, and its gauge part
vanishes on Σ_τ0 with its θ-derivatives. As W(n, ·) vanishes on Σ_τ0 with its derivatives along Σ_τ0, the oscillating
part of the shift is λ W(∂_τ, ·) = λ W(β, ·) there, β the shift and N the lapse of g0, and that of the lapse is of
order λ², g0^(ττ) changing by λ W(g0⁻¹dτ, g0⁻¹dτ) = 0 at order λ. In κ_ij = (2N)⁻¹(∂_τ γ_ij − D_i β_j − D_j β_i) the
terms of order one oscillating in θ are

    (2N)⁻¹ ν (∂_τ φ ∂_θ W_ij − ∂_i φ β^l ∂_θ W_lj − ∂_j φ β^l ∂_θ W_li +
              β^l (∂_i φ ∂_θ W_lj + ∂_j φ ∂_θ W_li − ∂_l φ ∂_θ W_ij)),

the last group from the Christoffel symbols in D β. So

    κ1 = (2N)⁻¹ ν (∂_τ φ − β(φ)) ∂_θ W = ½ ν dφ(n) ∂_θ W      on TΣ_τ0,

which vanishes on ν0 and is γ0-trace-free. (With the opposite sign convention for κ, κ1 changes sign.)

**Lemma {#lem:vlasov-axis-smooth}** (Invariant functions at an axis.) Near a point of axis 2 with ρ1 > 0 write the chart
as (s, y), y := (x3, x4), s := (τ, x1, x2).

(a) If E(s, t) is smooth and even in t, then E(s, |y|) is smooth.

(b) Let f be smooth off the axis near the point and invariant under the rotations of the y-plane, and let f(s, (t, 0)),
t ≠ 0, extend to a smooth function e(s, t). Then f extends to a smooth function, f(s, y) = e(s, |y|).

(c) Let g be a smooth class metric and u a smooth invariant eikonal of g near the point, with du ≠ 0. Then a_⊥ :=
g(K2, K2)/ρ2², κ2, Θ_h := □_g u − κ1 − κ2 and σ2/ρ2², where σ2 := Θ_h − κ2 is the planar shear of [[prop:class-axis]],
extend to smooth invariant functions near the point, a_⊥ > 0, and ρ2² dψ2 extends to a smooth covector field.

Near axis 1 the same holds with the two planes exchanged.

*Proof.* (a) Let 𝓔 be the set of finite sums of functions p(y) E′(s, |y|), with p a polynomial and E′ smooth and even in
its last variable. Each member of 𝓔 is continuous, on the axis too. For E′ smooth and even, ∂_t E′ is odd, so ∂_t E′(s,
t) = t E″(s, t) with E″(s, t) := ∫_0^1 ∂_t² E′(s, ϑt) dϑ, smooth and even. Off the axis, ∂_s (p E′(s, |y|)) = p
(∂_s E′)(s, |y|) and ∂_(y_i)(p E′(s, |y|)) = (∂_i p) E′(s, |y|) + p y_i E″(s, |y|); so the first derivatives off the axis
of a member of 𝓔 are members of 𝓔. Let f ∈ 𝓔, let z0 be a point of the axis and L the value at z0 of the continuous
extension of the gradient of f. For z near z0 off the axis, the segment from z0 to z meets the axis only at z0, so

    f(z) − f(z0) − L·(z − z0) = ∫_0^1 (∇f(z0 + ϑ (z − z0)) − L)·(z − z0) dϑ = o(|z − z0|),

and by continuity of f the same bound holds for z on the axis. So f is differentiable on the axis with gradient L,
hence C¹, with first derivatives in 𝓔; by induction f is smooth, and E(s, |y|) ∈ 𝓔.

(b) For y ≠ 0 a rotation maps y to (|y|, 0), so f(s, y) = e(s, |y|); the rotation by π gives e(s, −t) = e(s, t), so e is
even, and (a) applies.

(c) On the points (s, (t, 0)), K2 = t ∂_4, so a_⊥ = g(∂_4, ∂_4) there, smooth in (s, t); by (b) a_⊥ extends smoothly,
and it is positive ([[prop:class-axis]]). Off the axis ψ2 = ½ log a_⊥ + log ρ2, so ρ2² dψ2 = ½ ρ2² da_⊥/a_⊥ + ½ d(ρ2²),
smooth, and κ2 = g⁻¹(du, dψ2) = ½ g⁻¹(du, da_⊥)/a_⊥ + g⁻¹(du, d(ρ2²))/(2ρ2²). At (s, (t, 0)), d(ρ2²) = 2t dx3, so the
last term is h(s, t)/t with h := g⁻¹(du, dx3). The rotation by π of the y-plane is an isometry fixing u and changing the
sign of dx3, so h(s, ·) is odd and h/t = ∫_0^1 ∂_t h(s, ϑt) dϑ is smooth. So κ2 restricted to these points extends
smoothly, and by (b) κ2 extends smoothly. κ1 and □_g u are smooth near the point (g(K1, K1) > 0 there), and so is Θ_h.
Then σ2 is smooth and invariant, its restriction e_σ(s, t) is even in t, and e_σ(s, 0) = 0 because σ2 extends by 0 to
the axis ([[prop:class-axis]] (d)). By Taylor's formula e_σ(s, t) = t² E_σ(s, t) with E_σ(s, t) := ∫_0^1 (1 − ϑ) ∂_t²
e_σ(s, ϑt) dϑ, smooth and even, as ∂_t e_σ(s, 0) = 0. By (a), σ2/ρ2² = E_σ(s, ρ2) extends smoothly. ∎

**Lemma {#lem:vlasov-axis-commutator}** (The commutator up to the axes.) Let g5 be a smooth class metric and u a smooth
invariant eikonal of g5 on a G-invariant open set of {r > 0}, the axes allowed, with du ≠ 0 and ℓ := g5⁻¹du future
directed; put L := ℓ·∂ and H := ∇²u. Let n be the future unit normal of the slices, E := −g5(ℓ, n), ν̂ := E⁻¹ℓ − n,
ℓ̄ := (2E)⁻¹(n − ν̂), Θ_h := □5 u − κ1 − κ2 and σ_i := Θ_h − κ_i off the axes, V := g5⁻¹H(ℓ̄, ·) + H(ℓ̄, ℓ̄) ℓ, and Y :=
□5 ℓ + g5⁻¹Ric(g5)(ℓ, ·), □5 ℓ the wave operator of g5 on the vector field ℓ. Then for every invariant function φ, with
∇φ := g5⁻¹dφ,

    [□5, L] φ = 2 Θ_h □5 φ + 𝔐(L φ) + 𝔑 φ,      𝔐 := 2 H(ℓ̄, ℓ̄) ℓ − 4 V + 4 Θ_h ℓ̄,
    𝔑 φ := 4 H(V, ∇φ) − 4 Θ_h H(ℓ̄, ∇φ) − 2 Σ_i σ_i g5⁻¹(dψ_i, dφ) + Y(φ),               {#eq:vlasov-axis-commutator}

and Θ_h and the coefficients of 𝔐 and 𝔑 extend smoothly to the whole set, the axes included. Off the axes this is
[[eq:vlasov-commutator]], with a = 2 Θ_h = 2 □3 u and 𝔐 = 2𝔐′ for the frame (ℓ, ℓ̄, e) used here. The same holds for
□̃ := g5^(ab) ∂_a∂_b in the chart X, with another 𝔑.

*Proof.* For any metric, [□, ℓ^μ ∇_μ] φ = 2 ⟨∇ℓ, ∇²φ⟩ + Y(φ) (proof of [[cor:vlasov-commutator]]), and ∇ℓ is H with
an index raised. E > 0, since ℓ ≠ 0 is null and future directed and n is future timelike; g5(ν̂, n) = 0 and
g5(ν̂, ν̂) = 1, so ℓ = E (n + ν̂), and ℓ̄ is null with g5(ℓ, ℓ̄) = −1. The fields n and ℓ, hence ν̂ and ℓ̄, are smooth,
G-invariant and orthogonal to K1 and K2, as dτ(K_i) = du(K_i) = 0. Off the axes let e be a unit vector orthogonal to
ℓ, ℓ̄, K1 and K2, and e_i := e^(−ψ_i) K_i, so that g5⁻¹ = −ℓ ⊗ ℓ̄ − ℓ̄ ⊗ ℓ + e ⊗ e + Σ_i e_i ⊗ e_i. By
[[prop:class-structure]] (a) and (b), H(ℓ, ·) = 0, H vanishes on the pairs (e_i, e), (e_i, ℓ̄) and (e1, e2), and
H(e_i, e_i) = κ_i; tracing, □5 u = H(e, e) + κ1 + κ2, so H(e, e) = Θ_h. For a symmetric form S, expanding both inverse
metrics in ⟨H, S⟩,

    ⟨H, S⟩ = H(ℓ̄, ℓ̄) S(ℓ, ℓ) − 2 H(ℓ̄, e) S(ℓ, e) + Θ_h S(e, e) + Σ_i κ_i S(e_i, e_i),

and tr S = −2 S(ℓ, ℓ̄) + S(e, e) + Σ_i S(e_i, e_i). Eliminating S(e, e),

    ⟨H, S⟩ = Θ_h tr S + H(ℓ̄, ℓ̄) S(ℓ, ℓ) − 2 H(ℓ̄, e) S(ℓ, e) + 2 Θ_h S(ℓ, ℓ̄) − Σ_i σ_i S(e_i, e_i).

Take S := ∇²φ. Then tr S = □5 φ; S(ℓ, X) = X(Lφ) − (∇_X ℓ)(φ) = X(Lφ) − H(X, ∇φ); S(e_i, e_i) = g5⁻¹(dψ_i, dφ) by
[[prop:class-structure]] (a), whose proof uses of the function only its invariance; and H(ℓ̄, e) e = V, since g5⁻¹H(ℓ̄,
·) = −H(ℓ̄, ℓ̄) ℓ + H(ℓ̄, e) e, the components along ℓ̄ and e_i vanishing. Inserting these, with H(ℓ, ∇φ) = 0, gives
[[eq:vlasov-axis-commutator]] off the axes, and 2(H(ℓ̄, ℓ̄) ℓ − 2V + 2 Θ_h ℓ̄) is 2𝔐′ of the proof of
[[cor:vlasov-commutator]], whose H is Hess_(g3) u, equal to H on orbit vectors.

Near a point of axis 2, Θ_h, σ2/ρ2² and ρ2² dψ2 extend smoothly by [[lem:vlasov-axis-smooth]] (c), and κ1, ψ1 are
smooth (g5(K1, K1) > 0 there), so σ1 g5⁻¹(dψ1, dφ) and σ2 g5⁻¹(dψ2, dφ) = (σ2/ρ2²) g5⁻¹(ρ2² dψ2, dφ) are first-order
operators with smooth coefficients; near axis 1 exchange the planes. H(ℓ̄, ℓ̄), V, ℓ̄ and Y are smooth everywhere. For
smooth φ both sides of [[eq:vlasov-axis-commutator]] are continuous, so the identity holds on the axes as well. For □̃,
□̃φ = □5 φ + H0·∂φ with H0^ρ := g5^(ab) Γ^ρ_ab smooth, and [L, H0·∂] is of first order with smooth coefficients. ∎

So the transport of [[cor:vlasov-commutator]] for Ψ := □̃φ, with ℓ·dφ = f, holds up to the axes with coefficients that
are bounded with all their derivatives on compact sets. On the O(4)-invariant two-dust background σ_i = 0, the
generators being shear-free, and the identity is [[lem:two-dust-commutator]].

**Proposition {#prop:vlasov-radial-step}** (The high-frequency step with a radial pair.) Let Ω be a domain of the type
[[eq:omega]] and g0 a smooth class Einstein–null-dust solution on Ω with n + 2 families: n families (u_A, F_A²) and two
families (ϕ_𝔨, F_𝔨²), 𝔨 ∈ {in, out}, the *radial pair*, each amplitude smooth and transported by
[[eq:vlasov-amplitude]], each generator future directed (after the sign change of [[def:vlasov-label]] where needed).
Assume (i) for all n + 2 families; (ii′) for every pair among them, with the frequencies of all n + 2 families in the
hierarchy of (ii′); (iii) and (iv) for the n families; (V3) on Σ_τ0; and

- (iii′) the wave of family 𝔨 is the wave of [[def:vlasov-radial-wave]] at its frequency with a parallel polarisation
  𝒫_𝔨 that vanishes, on Σ_τ0, on every vector g0-orthogonal to the spheres {τ0, r = const};
- (v) ϕ_𝔨|Σ_τ0 is a function of r with nonzero derivative on Σ_τ0 ∩ Ω.

Then the conclusions of [[thm:vlasov-n-approximant]], [[thm:vlasov-n-uniform]] and [[thm:vlasov-n-data]] hold for the
n + 2 families. In particular, for λ ≤ λ0 there is a smooth metric g_λ on Ω, invariant under T² and both reflections,
with Ric(g_λ) = 0, |g_λ − g0| ≤ C λ and ∂g_λ − ∂g0 = Σ ν du ⊗ [∂_θ W] + O(C λ), the sum over the n + 2 families with
their phases, frequencies and waves; λ0 and C depend on n.

*Proof.* The proofs of the three theorems hold with n + 2 phases and these changes.

(1) *Stage 0.* By [[lem:vlasov-radial-wave]] (b), R^(0)_𝔨 = Ric(g0) − F_𝔨² dϕ_𝔨 ⊗ dϕ_𝔨, with no oscillating part, the
gauge part removing the second harmonic of the quadratic term exactly as for the labels, since |∂_θ W_𝔨|² = 8 ξ_𝔨²
sin²θ by |𝒫_𝔨|² = 2. So stage 0 is that of the proof of [[thm:vlasov-n-approximant]]. The later stages use of the
waves only that each depends on one phase and is annihilated by its own generator, which holds by
[[lem:vlasov-radial-wave]] (b).

(2) *The uniform estimate.* Step (3) of the proof of [[thm:vlasov-n-uniform]] uses of G2^B(ℓ_A, ℓ_A) only that it is a
bounded profile in θ_B alone. For B = 𝔨 it is [−2 ξ_𝔨 cos θ_𝔨 𝒫_𝔨(ℓ_A, ℓ_A) − 2 c_𝔨 sin 2θ_𝔨 ν_𝔨² (dϕ_𝔨(ℓ_A))²],
at the phase of family 𝔨: such a profile, here with a polarisation part besides the gauge part. In step (4) the
commutator of L_𝔨 is that of [[lem:vlasov-axis-commutator]], whose coefficients are smooth on Ω, the axes included,
hence bounded with all their derivatives on the compact Ω; this is the only place where that proof uses (iv), and for
the n families (iv) holds. Step (5) holds for every family by (i). The second derivatives of the phase shift of family 𝔨
are handled as in (L6) of [[lem:two-dust-facts]], the transport field Z_𝔨 := ℓ_𝔨/ℓ_𝔨(τ) being smooth on Ω, the axes
included.

(3) *The data.* In the proof of [[thm:vlasov-n-data]], the gauge parts vanish on Σ_τ0 with their θ-derivatives, so on
Σ_τ0 family 𝔨 contributes λ W_𝔨 = 2 λ ξ_𝔨 cos θ_𝔨 𝒫_𝔨 to γ and, by the identity after
[[lem:vlasov-radial-constraints]], κ1_𝔨 = ½ ν_𝔨 dϕ_𝔨(n) ∂_(θ_𝔨) W_𝔨 on TΣ_τ0 to κ. By (iii′) both vanish on n and on
ν0 = γ0⁻¹dr, which is γ0-orthogonal to the spheres in Σ_τ0 and orthogonal to n, and both are γ0-trace-free, the
γ0-trace of a form that vanishes on n being its g0-trace. By (v), p_𝔨 = ν_𝔨 d(ϕ_𝔨|Σ_τ0) is a multiple of dr, with
nonzero differential. So the radial pair is tangent to the spheres in the sense of [[lem:vlasov-radial-constraints]],
the labels are torus-polarised, and that lemma replaces [[lem:vlasov-constraint-coefficient]] (b) and
[[lem:vlasov-leaf-curvature]] in the structure step: f and g̃ are of class 𝔏, and p, P, A0, A^B and 𝒮 are of class 𝔓
with the parts of order λ⁰ of (γ0, κ0). The phases ν_𝔨 ϕ_𝔨|Σ_τ0 satisfy (C0) as every phase does. The stage data are
those of [[lem:stage-data]] for each of the n + 2 families, with the polarisation map of each, onto by the argument
given there. The correction, the r-derivatives and the datum are unchanged. ∎

**Remark {#rem:vlasov-radial-pair}** (Labels and the radial pair.) By [[eq:vlasov-mixed-angle]], for null covectors
with future-directed generators, |g0⁻¹(du_α, dϕ_𝔨)| = 2 E E′ sin²(δβ/2), δβ the angle between them in the orbit slice,
and the absolute value does not depend on the orientations. So (V2′) holds with c_r = 2 E_* E′_* sin²(η_r/2) when that
angle is at least η_r > 0 on D_α(S) ∩ 𝒯_𝔨, E_* and E′_* the least energies on these compact sets, and it is empty where
the tubes do not meet. It cannot be dropped: where du_α is parallel to dϕ_𝔨 on the overlap of the supports,
g0⁻¹(du_α, dϕ_𝔨) = 0, and every harmonic j supported on that pair has Q_j = j_A² ν_A² · 0 + 2 j_A j_𝔨 ν_A ν_𝔨
g0⁻¹(du_α, dϕ_𝔨) + j_𝔨² ν_𝔨² · 0 = 0, whatever the frequencies, so that no hierarchy removes the resonance. The pair
itself is separated: g0⁻¹(dv, du) = −e^(−2σ) ([[sec:two-dust-phases]]), and c_σ := min over Ω₂ of e^(−2σ) is positive.
So the hierarchy of [[lem:vlasov-hierarchy]] runs over all n + 2 phases, with c := min(½ c0 (2π/n)², ½ c_r, ½ c_σ) in
the proof of [[cor:vlasov-two-dust]].

g0. It lies in Ω_ε at distance at least ι − C ε from its lateral boundary, by the bound on the maps T of
[[def:class-adapted]], so g_ε is smooth on a neighbourhood of it; its lateral boundaries are, near the axes, level sets
of ϕ_in^ε and ϕ_out^ε ([[def:class-adapted]]), so they are adapted to the radial pair of g_ε; and it is admissible for
g_ε, being C¹-close to Ω₂^ι. By [[lem:vlasov-open]], (g_ε, ε F^ε, u^ε) satisfies (V1) on S with the margins
halved, (V2) with δ, and (V3), for ε small; and (H2′) holds for its families on Ω_ε^ι as for those of g0 on Ω₂^ι,
the tubes moving by at most C ε. The datum D_(ε,n) is corrected on Σ_τ0 ∩ Ω₂ by [[prop:vlasov-scaled-correction]] as
in (2), with 𝔉(0) a quadrature error of [[lem:vlasov-trapezoid]], and restricted to Σ_τ0 ∩ Ω₂^ι, where Ω_ε^ι meets
Σ_τ0, T_(τ0,·) being the identity. Now apply [[prop:vlasov-uniform-n]] with the reference (g_ε, ε F^ε, u^ε), its
radial pair and the domain Ω_ε^ι, and d = 0, its proof changed as in (1) of part (a). It gives the developments g^(n)
on their adapted domains for Ω_ε^ι relative to g_ε, and [[eq:vlasov-uniform-n]] on their slices, the families of
g^(n) within C_k n^(−k) of the sampled families of g_ε. The maps T of these adapted domains differ from the identity by
at most C_k n^(−k), the data of the phases being those of g_ε on Σ_τ0, so for n ≥ n0 these domains contain Ω₂′, as in
(4) of part (a). The slices of Ω₂′ are annuli, on which the Sobolev inequality of [[lem:sobolev]] holds with a constant
independent of τ, and the energy contains the τ-derivatives; with m ≥ 6 this gives the bound in C¹(Ω₂′). ∎

**Corollary {#cor:vlasov-two-dust}** (The two-dust background with a restricted Vlasov field.) Let g0 be the two-dust
background of [[thm:two-dusts]] on its domain Ω₂, assume that its outgoing packet does not reach the inner sphere, {u1 ≤
u ≤ u2} ∩ Ω₂ ∩ {R = a} = ∅, and replace the lateral boundaries of Ω₂ by the adapted ones of [[cor:class-two-dust]]
([[def:class-adapted]]). Let (F, u) be a field for g0 satisfying (V1), (V2) and (V2′) on S, as in the setting above, and
fix a′ and V′. Then there is ε1 > 0 such that for 0 < ε ≤ ε1 the class Einstein–restricted massless Vlasov solution g_ε
with the two radial dusts of g0 and the field of amplitude ε F, with the data D_ε corrected by the radial solve, exists
on its adapted domain, which contains Ω₂′ ([[prop:vlasov-radial-background]]), and is a weak limit there of class vacuum
metrics: there are smooth metrics g_i on Ω₂′, invariant under T² and both reflections, with Ric(g_i) = 0,

    g_i → g_ε uniformly on Ω₂′,      ∂g_i ⇀ ∂g_ε weakly in L²(Ω₂′).

*Proof.* Let ε ≤ ε0 of [[prop:vlasov-radial-background]], to be decreased below, and let g_ε and the g^(n) be as there,
restricted to Ω₂′. The solution exists on its adapted domain by [[prop:vlasov-cauchy]] around g0, the radial pair being
governed by [[thm:class-stability]], whose (H2′) holds under the packet hypothesis by the proof of
[[cor:class-two-dust]]: this is [[prop:vlasov-radial-background]] (a). We check the hypotheses of
[[prop:vlasov-radial-step]] for g^(n) on Ω₂′, for odd n large, and then follow the diagonal of the proof of
[[thm:vlasov]].

*(Ω) and (V3).* For g0 on Ω₂′: Σ_τ is spacelike and {v + τ = const} is spacelike with v + τ increasing along future
causal curves, by [[lem:two-dust-domain]] (a) and (c); at R = a′, μ ≥ μ0 > a′², so g0⁻¹(dR, dR) = 1 − μ/a′² < 0 and
g0⁻¹(dR, dτ) = e^(−ψ) − 1 + μ/a′² > 0 (as e^(−ψ) ≥ 1), and {R = a′} is spacelike with R decreasing along future causal
curves near it, as in the proof of [[lem:two-dust-domain]] (b); so a past causal curve from a point of Ω₂′ cannot leave
Ω₂′ through its lateral boundaries and meets Σ_τ0 inside Ω₂′, as in (d) there. On Σ_τ0 the slice of g0 is round, γ =
G dR² + R² γ̂ with G > 0 ([[lem:two-dust-slice]]), and its spheres have mean curvature 3/(R √G) > 0. These are strict
inequalities on compact sets, stable under C¹-small changes of the metric. By [[prop:vlasov-radial-background]], g_ε is
within C ε of g0 and g^(n) within C_k n^(−k) of g_ε in C², with m = 6 there, by Sobolev's inequality on the slices
(H⁷ ⊂ C⁴ in dimension four) and the equations for the τ-derivatives; so (Ω) and (V3) hold for g_ε and for g^(n) on Ω₂′
for ε small and n large, for the generators of every family.

*(ii′).* Two labels: g_ε satisfies (Ω), (V1) and (V2) on Ω₂′ by [[lem:vlasov-open]], so [[lem:vlasov-separation]] holds
for g_ε, and the argument of (ii′) in the proof of [[thm:vlasov]] gives |g^(n)⁻¹(du_A, du_B)| ≥ ½ c0 (2π/n)² on the
overlaps for n large, c0 the constant of g_ε. A label A and a radial dust 𝔨: the function (y, α) ↦ g0⁻¹(du_α,
dϕ_𝔨)(y) is continuous on the compact set of the (y, α) with y ∈ D_α(S) ∩ 𝒯_𝔨, where it is at least c_r in absolute
value by (V2′). Let S′ ⊂ int S be compact with supp F ⊂ int S′. A point y of the overlap of the supports of the two
families of g^(n) lies, for n large, on a ray of u_(α_A) for g_ε from S′_(α_A), and within C_k n^(−k) of the tube for
g_ε of supp q_𝔨 ∩ Σ_τ0; these tubes lie within C ε of the corresponding tubes for g0, the rays depending continuously
on the metric in C¹. For g0, the set of the (y, α) with y on a ray from int S_α is open in Ω₂ × S¹, the flow of the
rays being a diffeomorphism by (V1), and it contains the compact set of the (y, α) with y ∈ D_α(S′); and 𝒯_𝔨 contains
a neighbourhood in Ω₂ of the tube for g0 of supp q_𝔨 ∩ Σ_τ0, which is {v_start ≤ v ≤ v_end} ∩ Ω₂ for 𝔨 = in and
{u1 ≤ u ≤ u2} ∩ Ω₂ for 𝔨 = out. So for ε small and n large, y ∈ D_(α_A)(S) ∩ 𝒯_𝔨 for g0, and, the eikonals of g^(n)
and of g0 with the same data on Σ_τ0 differing in C¹ on the tubes by at most C |g^(n) − g0|_(C²) (proof of
[[lem:vlasov-open]]), |g^(n)⁻¹(du_A, dϕ_𝔨^(n))(y)| ≥ c_r − C (ε + C_k n^(−k)) ≥ ½ c_r. For the pair, the absolute
value of g^(n)⁻¹(dϕ_in^(n), dϕ_out^(n)) is at least c_σ − C (ε + C_k n^(−k)) ≥ ½ c_σ ([[rem:vlasov-radial-pair]]). So
(ii′) holds for all n + 2 families with c := min(½ c0 (2π/n)², ½ c_r, ½ c_σ), and Λ = Λ(n) is chosen as there.

*(iii), (iv) and (iii′).* The waves of the labels are those of the proof of [[thm:vlasov]], and their supports lie at
distance at least δ from the axes for n large. For the pair, ϕ_𝔨^(n) = ϕ_𝔨 on Σ_τ0, functions of R = r with nonzero
derivative, since dv(∂_R) = 1 and du(∂_R) < 0 on Σ_τ0 (proof of [[prop:two-dust-background]] (a)): this is (v). The
metric Π^(n) that g^(n) induces on the spheres of Σ_τ0 is within C (ε + C_k n^(−k)) in C⁰ of the Euclidean one, which
is that of g0; so [[lem:vlasov-radial-wave]] (c) gives 𝒫0 for g^(n), and (a) the parallel polarisations 𝒫_𝔨, its
transports along the rays of ϕ_𝔨^(n), which vanish on Σ_τ0 on the vectors g^(n)-orthogonal to the spheres. The
amplitudes F_𝔨^(n) are the solutions of [[eq:vlasov-amplitude]] with the data F_𝔨|Σ_τ0, smooth, with (F_𝔨^(n))² =
q_𝔨^(n).

So [[prop:vlasov-radial-step]] gives, for each odd n ≥ n0 and λ ≤ λ0(n), vacuum metrics g^(n)_λ on Ω₂′, invariant under
T² and both reflections, with |g^(n)_λ − g^(n)| ≤ C(n) λ and ∂g^(n)_λ − ∂g^(n) = Σ ν du ⊗ [∂_θ W] + O(C(n) λ), the sum
over the n + 2 families. For a label, |ν_A ∂_(θ_A) W_A| ≤ C (F_A + F_A²) with C independent of n and A, as in the proof
of [[thm:vlasov]]. For the pair, ν_𝔨 ∂_(θ_𝔨) W_𝔨 = −2 F_𝔨 sin θ_𝔨 𝒫_𝔨 + ν_𝔨³ c_𝔨 cos 2θ_𝔨 dϕ_𝔨 ⊗ dϕ_𝔨, where the
Cartesian components of 𝒫_𝔨 are bounded independently of n (𝒫0 is, and the transport has coefficients bounded by the
C¹ norm of g^(n)), and ν_𝔨³ c_𝔨 ≤ C F_𝔨² as for the labels; so these two terms are bounded independently of n.

*The diagonal.* It is that of the proof of [[thm:vlasov]], with the n + 2 families and Ω₂′ in place of Ω. The two
radial terms add at most C to ‖Σ ν du ⊗ [∂_θ W]‖²_(L²), independently of n; the cross terms between a radial family
and any other family have the phases ν ϕ_𝔨 ± ν′ u′, with nonzero differentials, the covectors being null and not
parallel on the overlaps by (ii′), and they tend to zero at fixed n by non-stationary phase. With
[[prop:vlasov-radial-background]] (b), |g_i − g_ε| ≤ 1/n_i + C_k n_i^(−k) and |∫ η_j · (∂g_i − ∂g_ε)| ≤ 1/n_i + C_k
n_i^(−k) ‖η_j‖_(L¹) for g_i := g^(n_i)_(λ_(n_i)), and ‖∂g_i‖_(L²) is bounded independently of i. So g_i → g_ε
uniformly and ∂g_i ⇀ ∂g_ε weakly in L²(Ω₂′); each g_i is smooth, vacuum and invariant under T² and both
reflections. ∎

**Remark {#rem:vlasov-radial-scope}** (What the corollary does not give.) The vacuum metrics live on Ω₂′, a domain of the
type [[eq:omega]] inside the adapted domain, for every a′ > a and V′ < V allowed above, ε1 depending on them. On the
adapted domain itself, whose slices after Σ_τ0 are not annuli, the mollifier of [[lem:mollifier]] and the transport
identity of [[lem:two-dust-transport]] are written for annular slices, and the high-frequency step is not carried out
there. Smallness of the field enters only the existence of the background near g0 ([[prop:vlasov-radial-background]]
(a)); [[prop:vlasov-radial-step]] and the lemmas before it use no smallness.
