### Radial dusts reaching the axes {#sec:vlasov-radial}

[[thm:vlasov]] keeps every label at distance at least 2δ from the axes, and by [[lem:vlasov-axis-phases]] at most two of
the dusts present at an axis point are separated, one on each null line. The background of [[thm:two-dusts]] carries
such a pair, an ingoing and an outgoing radial dust, present at the axes. This section adds a small restricted field to
it. The pair is indexed by 𝔨 ∈ {in, out}, as in [[ch:two-dusts]]; its phases are written ϕ_𝔨 and its amplitudes F_𝔨, as
F_A is the amplitude of family A (F_𝔨 is not the phase derivative that [[ch:two-dusts]] writes so). The labels are
localised as in [[lem:vlasov-local-stability]] and [[sec:vlasov-n]]; the pair is not, and its phases are eikonals on the
whole domain. Four things change. First, the waves of the pair cannot be torus-polarised. Near axis 2, e^(2ψ2) dφ2² is
a_⊥ times the form (x3 dx4 − x4 dx3)²/ρ2² ([[prop:class-axis]] (a)), which equals dx4² at the points (ρ2, 0) and dx3² at
the points (0, ρ2) of the (x3, x4)-plane and so has no limit at ρ2 = 0; a torus-polarised wave whose amplitude does not
vanish at the axis is not continuous there. The waves of the pair are polarised instead by tensors parallel along their
generators, smooth across the axes ([[lem:vlasov-radial-wave]]). Second, such a wave has an orbit part, and it leaves no
term of order λ⁻¹ in the constraints because its phase is radial on Σ_τ0 and it is tangent to the spheres there
([[lem:vlasov-radial-constraints]]); the data of the pair are chosen so. Third, the commutator of the wave operator with
a generator that reaches an axis has smooth coefficients there, because the planar shear of [[prop:class-axis]] vanishes
to second order at the axis ([[lem:vlasov-axis-smooth]], [[lem:vlasov-axis-commutator]]). Fourth, the background is
constructed on the adapted domain of [[cor:class-two-dust]], uniformly in the number of dusts
([[prop:vlasov-radial-background]]), and the high-frequency step runs on a domain of the type [[eq:omega]] inside it
([[prop:vlasov-radial-step]]).

**Definition {#def:vlasov-radial-wave}** (Waves with a parallel polarisation.) Let g0 be a class metric and u an
invariant eikonal of g0, both smooth across the axes, with ℓ := g0⁻¹du future directed, and let F, ν, ξ := F/ν, k :=
ν du and c be as in [[def:vlasov-wave]]. A *parallel polarisation* of u is a smooth G-invariant symmetric 2-tensor field
𝒵 on a neighbourhood of supp F with

    ∇_ℓ 𝒵 = 0,      𝒵(ℓ, ·) = 0,      tr_(g0) 𝒵 = 0,      |𝒵|²_(g0) = 2.

The *wave* of the dust (u, F) with the polarisation 𝒵 at frequency ν is the profile

    W := 2 ξ cos θ 𝒵 + χ k ⊗ k,      χ := ½ c sin 2θ,      θ := ν u/λ.

F vanishes on every generator on which it vanishes on Σ_τ0, and so does c, so W is smooth where g0 and u are. Off the
axes T0 := e^(2ψ1) dφ1² − e^(2ψ2) dφ2² is a parallel polarisation (proof of [[lem:vlasov-wave]]), and the wave with
𝒵 = T0 is that of [[def:vlasov-wave]].

**Lemma {#lem:vlasov-radial-wave}** (Parallel polarisations.) Let Ω be a domain of the type [[eq:omega]], g0 a smooth
class metric on Ω and u a smooth invariant eikonal of g0 on Ω, with du ≠ 0 and ℓ := g0⁻¹du future directed, such that
every ray of u through a point of Ω, followed to the past, stays in Ω and meets Σ_τ0.

(a) Let 𝒵0 be a smooth G-invariant field on Σ_τ0 ∩ Ω of symmetric bilinear forms on the tangent spaces of the
spacetime, with 𝒵0(ℓ, ·) = 0, tr_(g0) 𝒵0 = 0 and |𝒵0|²_(g0) = 2. Its parallel transport along the rays of u is a
parallel polarisation 𝒵 of u on Ω, smooth on Ω, the axes included, equal to 𝒵0 on Σ_τ0.

(b) The wave W of [[def:vlasov-radial-wave]] satisfies W(ℓ, ·) = 0 and tr_(g0) W = 0, and in the two-scale expansion of
R[g0 + λ W] at the phase θ = ν u/λ, R^(−1) = 0 and R^(0) = Ric(g0) − F² du ⊗ du.

(c) Put 𝔷 := (ρ1² − ρ2²)/r², let 𝔥(y) := sin(πy/3)/y, an entire function with 𝔥(0) = π/3, put P_⊥ := |dx|² −
(x̂·dx)², and

    𝒳 := −(2/√3) cos(π𝔷/3) P_⊥ + 4 𝔥(1 + 𝔷) (x1 dx2 − x2 dx1)²/r²
          + 4 𝔥(1 − 𝔷) (x3 dx4 − x4 dx3)²/r²      on R × (R⁴ ∖ {0}).                              {#eq:vlasov-template}

Let 𝕊_r := {τ0} × {|x| = r}, Π the metric g0 induces on 𝕊_r, 𝔭 the g0-orthogonal projection of the tangent spaces of
the spacetime at points of 𝕊_r onto T𝕊_r, and 𝒳̊ the Π-trace-free part of the restriction of 𝒳 to T𝕊_r. If u|Σ_τ0 is a
function of r and |𝒳̊|_Π > 0 on Σ_τ0 ∩ Ω, then 𝒵0 := √2 |𝒳̊|_Π⁻¹ 𝒳̊(𝔭 ·, 𝔭 ·) satisfies the hypotheses of (a), and it
vanishes on every vector g0-orthogonal to the spheres 𝕊_r. If g0 is invariant under O(4) with area radius |x|, as the
backgrounds of [[thm:two-dusts]] are in their chart, then 𝒵0 = 𝒳, and |𝒳̊|_Π ≥ 1 for every metric close enough to g0
in C⁰ on Σ_τ0 ∩ Ω.

*Proof.* (a) Since ∇_ℓ ℓ = ½ d g0⁻¹(du, du) = 0, the rays are null geodesics. A null vector is never tangent to a
spacelike slice, so ℓ(τ) ≠ 0, and Z := ℓ/ℓ(τ) has τ as parameter along its integral curves. Let Φ(x0, t) be the point at
time τ0 + t of the integral curve of Z from x0 ∈ Σ_τ0 ∩ Ω, defined while the curve stays in Ω. Φ is smooth, injective
(integral curves do not cross) and has invertible differential (Z is transverse to Σ_τ0), so it is a diffeomorphism onto
its image, which is Ω by hypothesis. Along t ↦ Φ(x0, t) let 𝒵 solve the linear system ∇_Z 𝒵 = 0, that is
d𝒵_ab/dt = Z^c (Γ^d_ca 𝒵_db + Γ^d_cb 𝒵_ad), with 𝒵 = 𝒵0(x0) at t = 0. Its coefficients are smooth in (x0, t), so 𝒵 is
smooth in (x0, t), hence on Ω, and ∇_ℓ 𝒵 = ℓ(τ) ∇_Z 𝒵 = 0. The covector 𝒵(ℓ, ·) satisfies
∇_ℓ (𝒵(ℓ, ·)) = (∇_ℓ 𝒵)(ℓ, ·) + 𝒵(∇_ℓ ℓ, ·) = 0 and vanishes on Σ_τ0, so it vanishes; ℓ(tr 𝒵) = tr ∇_ℓ 𝒵 = 0 and
ℓ(|𝒵|²) = 2 ⟨∇_ℓ 𝒵, 𝒵⟩ = 0, so tr 𝒵 = 0 and |𝒵|² = 2. An element of G is an isometry of g0 that fixes u, hence ℓ, and
maps Σ_τ0 to itself; it maps rays to rays and parallel fields along them to parallel fields, so 𝒵 is G-invariant, 𝒵0
being so, by uniqueness for the system. Nothing here refers to the orbit space: g0, u and 𝒵0 are smooth in the chart X,
the axes included, and so is 𝒵.

(b) The proof of [[lem:vlasov-wave]] uses of T0 only ∇_ℓ T0 = 0, T0(ℓ, ·) = 0, tr T0 = 0, |T0|² = 2 and ⟨T0, k ⊗ k⟩ =
ν² T0(ℓ, ℓ) = 0. These hold for 𝒵, and that proof applies with 𝒵 in place of T0.

(c) Off the axes let e1 := (−x2, x1, 0, 0)/ρ1, e2 := (0, 0, −x4, x3)/ρ2, and e_ϑ the Euclidean unit vector tangent to
{|x| = r} in the plane spanned by (x1, x2, 0, 0) and (0, 0, x3, x4): a Euclidean orthonormal frame of the tangent space
of {|x| = r}. P_⊥ is the identity on that space and vanishes on x̂; x1 dx2 − x2 dx1 takes the value ρ1 on e1 and vanishes
on e_ϑ, e2 and x̂; and x3 dx4 − x4 dx3 takes the value ρ2 on e2 and vanishes on e_ϑ, e1 and x̂. With ρ1²/r² = (1 + 𝔷)/2
and ρ2²/r² = (1 − 𝔷)/2, 𝒳(x̂, ·) = 0 and 𝒳 is diagonal in the frame, with the entries

    𝔢_ϑ = −(2/√3) cos(π𝔷/3),      𝔢_1 = 𝔢_ϑ + 2 sin(π(1 + 𝔷)/3),      𝔢_2 = 𝔢_ϑ + 2 sin(π(1 − 𝔷)/3).

Since 2 sin(π/3 ± π𝔷/3) = √3 c_𝔷 ± s_𝔷 with c_𝔷 := cos(π𝔷/3) and s_𝔷 := sin(π𝔷/3), these are 𝔢_ϑ = −(2/√3) c_𝔷,
𝔢_1 = (1/√3) c_𝔷 + s_𝔷 and 𝔢_2 = (1/√3) c_𝔷 − s_𝔷. So 𝔢_ϑ + 𝔢_1 + 𝔢_2 = 0 and 𝔢_ϑ² + 𝔢_1² + 𝔢_2² = (4/3 + 1/3 +
1/3) c_𝔷² + 2 s_𝔷² = 2: 𝒳 restricted to the tangent space of {|x| = r} is trace-free for the Euclidean metric, with
Euclidean norm √2, off the axes and, by continuity, on them. 𝒳 is smooth on R × (R⁴ ∖ {0}), since 𝔷, P_⊥ and the forms
(x1 dx2 − x2 dx1)²/r² and (x3 dx4 − x4 dx3)²/r² are and 𝔥 is entire; it has no dτ component; and it is G-invariant,
since the rotations of each plane preserve 𝔷, P_⊥, x1 dx2 − x2 dx1 and x3 dx4 − x4 dx3, and the reflections x2 ↦ −x2
and x4 ↦ −x4 change the sign of one of these 1-forms only.

T𝕊_r = ker dτ ∩ ker dr is spacelike for g0, so 𝔭 and Π are smooth and G-equivariant, and so are 𝒳̊ and 𝒵0, the latter
where |𝒳̊|_Π > 0. By construction 𝒵0 vanishes on the kernel of 𝔭, the g0-orthogonal complement of T𝕊_r. Since u|Σ_τ0 is a
function of r, du vanishes on T𝕊_r, so ℓ lies in that complement and 𝒵0(ℓ, ·) = 0. The g0-trace and the g0-norm of a form
that vanishes on that complement, which is a Lorentzian plane, are its Π-trace and Π-norm on T𝕊_r, so tr_(g0) 𝒵0 = 0
and |𝒵0|² = 2. If g0 is invariant under O(4) with area radius |x|, Π is the Euclidean metric of {|x| = r}, so 𝒳̊ = 𝒳 on
T𝕊_r and |𝒳̊|_Π = √2; and the g0-orthogonal complement of T𝕊_r is span(∂_τ, x̂·∂_x), because the isotropy group of a
point of 𝕊_r acts irreducibly on T𝕊_r and trivially on that span, so that g0(∂_τ, ·) and g0(x̂·∂_x, ·) are invariant
linear forms on T𝕊_r, hence zero; 𝒳 vanishes on that span, so 𝒵0 = 𝒳. The last claim follows by continuity on the compact
set Σ_τ0 ∩ Ω. ∎

The waves of the pair are therefore smooth tensors of the spacetime, with no reference to the orbit space. At axis 2
(𝔷 = 1) the template has the entries (𝔢_ϑ, 𝔢_1, 𝔢_2) = (−1, 2, −1)/√3, equal on e_ϑ and e2 as smoothness at that axis
requires; at axis 1 (𝔷 = −1) they are (−1, −1, 2)/√3. On a round slice its orbit part is 𝔢_ϑ q̂ ⊗ q̂, q̂ the unit
covector of the orbit slice orthogonal to dr, and 𝔢_ϑ ≤ −1/√3 everywhere, the axes included, since cos(π𝔷/3) ≥ ½
where the absolute value of 𝔷 is at most 1: the wave is not torus-polarised at any point, and what removes its term of
order λ⁻¹ in the constraints, which [[lem:vlasov-constraint-coefficient]] (c) exhibits for a non-radial phase, is that
its phase is radial on Σ_τ0 ([[lem:vlasov-radial-constraints]]).

**Lemma {#lem:vlasov-radial-constraints}** (Waves tangent to the spheres in the constraints.) Let the data on Σ :=
Σ_τ0 ∩ Ω oscillate as in [[lem:vlasov-constraint-coefficient]], with families A and γ = γ0 + λ Σ_A W_A + λ²(…), κ =
κ0 + Σ_A κ1_A + λ(…), the second and mixed harmonics entering at order λ² in γ and λ in κ, γ0 and every profile
G-invariant, θ_A = φ_A/λ for the phase φ_A of family A on Σ, and p_A := dφ_A. Put 𝔯 := γ0⁻¹dr. Let each family be
either torus-polarised, as in [[lem:vlasov-constraint-coefficient]] (b), or *tangent to the spheres*: p_A is a multiple
of dr on Σ, W_A and κ1_A vanish on 𝔯, and tr_(γ0) W_A = tr_(γ0) κ1_A = 0. Then:

(a) for every function α with 1 + α γ0^(rr) > 0 and every k, the terms of Φ(γ + α dr ⊗ dr, κ + k) that carry a second
derivative of some λ W_A or a first derivative of some κ1_A are of order one: λ⁻¹ times λ times a function of the form
[h] of [[sec:vlasov-scaled]], smooth in α. So the constraint map has no coefficient of order λ⁻¹, and the corrections
of [[lem:constraints-evolution]] meet none;

(b) the conclusion of [[lem:vlasov-leaf-curvature]] holds: p, P, A0, A^B and 𝒮 are of class 𝔓, with parts of order λ⁰
those of (γ0, κ0).

*Proof.* (a) By the proof of [[lem:vlasov-constraint-coefficient]] (a), these terms are, at order λ⁻¹,

    Σ_A (W_A″(γ_h⁻¹p_A, γ_h⁻¹p_A) − |p_A|²_(γ_h) tr_(γ_h) W_A″)   in ρ,
    2 Σ_A (−κ1_A′(γ_h⁻¹p_A, ·) + p_A tr_(γ_h) κ1_A′)                 in J,

with γ_h := γ + α dr ⊗ dr, W_A″ := ∂²_(θ_A) W_A and κ1_A′ := ∂_(θ_A) κ1_A; every coefficient is a smooth function of x,
the phases, λ and α. The forms γ0, W_B, κ1_B and the profiles are G-invariant, so they have no mixed orbit–torus
components, and neither has γ_h⁻¹; it maps orbit covectors to orbit vectors. For a torus-polarised family, W_A″ and
κ1_A′ vanish on orbit vectors, so the terms with γ_h⁻¹p_A vanish, and the traces are O(λ), as in the proof of
[[lem:vlasov-constraint-coefficient]] (b). For a family tangent to the spheres, note first that every W_B vanishes on 𝔯:
a torus-polarised one because 𝔯 is an orbit vector, the others by hypothesis. Hence γ_h(𝔯, ·) = (1 + α γ0^(rr)) dr +
λ²(…), and γ_h⁻¹dr = (1 + α γ0^(rr))⁻¹ 𝔯 + λ²(…); as p_A is a multiple of dr, W_A″(γ_h⁻¹p_A, ·) and
κ1_A′(γ_h⁻¹p_A, ·) are O(λ²). Further, with 𝔊 := γ0 + α dr ⊗ dr, 𝔊⁻¹ = γ0⁻¹ − α (1 + α γ0^(rr))⁻¹ 𝔯 ⊗ 𝔯 and
γ_h⁻¹ = 𝔊⁻¹ − λ 𝔊⁻¹ (Σ_B W_B) 𝔊⁻¹ + O(λ²), so tr_(γ_h) W_A″ = tr_(γ0) W_A″ − α (1 + α γ0^(rr))⁻¹ W_A″(𝔯, 𝔯) + O(λ) =
O(λ), and likewise tr_(γ_h) κ1_A′ = O(λ). In every case the coefficient of λ⁻¹ is a smooth function of (x, θ, λ, α)
that vanishes at λ = 0, hence λ times such a function, by Taylor's formula in λ: the term is of order one and of the
form [h]. The corrections of [[lem:constraints-evolution]] change γ by α dr ⊗ dr and κ by terms that enter no term of
order λ⁻¹.

(b) The coefficients of the angular second derivatives of α in ρ, those of the first derivatives of (ϖ, q) in J, and 𝒮
involve γ, its inverse, Π and α undifferentiated, so they are of class 𝔓, as in the proof of
[[lem:vlasov-leaf-curvature]]. That proof writes (γ^(rr))^(3/2) H with H = H̃/ν and H̃ = ½ tr_Π(∂_r Π − 𝓛_σ Π), σ the
shift and ν the lapse of the foliation of Σ by the spheres, σ^B = Π^(BA) γ(∂_r, ∂_A). A derivative costing λ⁻¹ falls on
a term of order λ only in three places. (1) ∂_r of λ W_A in Π gives ∂_r φ_A ∂_(θ_A) W_A restricted to the spheres. (2)
The angular derivatives of λ W_A in σ^C ∂_C Π give σ0^C ∂_C φ_A ∂_(θ_A) W_A restricted to the spheres, σ0 the shift of
γ0. Traced with Π^(AB) = Π0^(AB) + O(λ), (1) and (2) give (∂_r φ_A − σ0^C ∂_C φ_A) ∂_(θ_A) of the Π0-trace of the
restriction of W_A to the spheres, plus O(λ); and that trace is tr_(γ0) W_A = 0 for both kinds, the γ0-trace of a form
that vanishes on 𝔯, the γ0-normal of the spheres, being its Π0-trace. (3) The angular derivatives of the oscillating part

**Lemma {#lem:vlasov-axis-smooth}** (Invariant functions at an axis.) Near a point of axis 2 with ρ1 > 0 write the chart
as (x′, y), y := (x3, x4), x′ := (τ, x1, x2).

(a) If e(x′, t) is smooth and even in t, then e(x′, |y|) is smooth.

(b) Let f be smooth off the axis near the point and invariant under the rotations of the y-plane, and let f(x′, (t, 0)),
t ≠ 0, extend to a smooth function e(x′, t). Then f extends to a smooth function, f(x′, y) = e(x′, |y|).

(c) Let g be a smooth class metric and u a smooth invariant eikonal of g near the point, with du ≠ 0. Then a_⊥ :=
g(K2, K2)/ρ2², κ2, Θ_h := □_g u − κ1 − κ2 and σ2/ρ2² extend to smooth invariant functions near the point, a_⊥ > 0, and
ρ2² dψ2 extends to a smooth covector field. Here κ_i := g⁻¹(du, dψ_i) are the torus curvatures of
[[prop:class-structure]] (not the parts κ1_A of a second fundamental form), and σ2 := Θ_h − κ2 is the planar shear of
[[prop:class-axis]] (not the shift of the spheres).

Near axis 1 the same holds with the two planes exchanged, with σ1 := Θ_h − κ1.

*Proof.* (a) Let 𝔄 be the set of finite sums of functions p(y) e′(x′, |y|), with p a polynomial and e′ smooth and even
in its last variable. Each member of 𝔄 is continuous, on the axis too. For e′ smooth and even, ∂_t e′ is odd, so
∂_t e′(x′, t) = t e″(x′, t) with e″(x′, t) := ∫_0^1 ∂_t² e′(x′, s t) ds, smooth and even. Off the axis,
∂_(x′) (p e′(x′, |y|)) = p (∂_(x′) e′)(x′, |y|) and ∂_(y_i)(p e′(x′, |y|)) = (∂_i p) e′(x′, |y|) + p y_i e″(x′, |y|);
so the first derivatives off the axis of a member of 𝔄 are members of 𝔄. Let f ∈ 𝔄, let z0 be a point of the axis and
L the value at z0 of the continuous extension of the gradient of f. For z near z0 off the axis, the segment from z0 to z
meets the axis only at z0, so

    f(z) − f(z0) − L·(z − z0) = ∫_0^1 (∇f(z0 + s (z − z0)) − L)·(z − z0) ds,

which is at most |z − z0| times the supremum of |∇f − L| off the axis in the ball of radius |z − z0| about z0, hence
o(|z − z0|); by continuity of f the same bound holds for z on the axis. So f is differentiable on the axis with gradient
L, hence C¹, with first derivatives in 𝔄; by induction f is smooth, and e(x′, |y|) ∈ 𝔄.

(b) For y ≠ 0 a rotation maps y to (|y|, 0), so f(x′, y) = e(x′, |y|); the rotation by π gives e(x′, −t) = e(x′, t) for
t ≠ 0, hence for all t, so e is even, and (a) applies.

(c) At the points (x′, (t, 0)), K2 = t ∂_4, so a_⊥ = g(∂_4, ∂_4) there, smooth in (x′, t); by (b) a_⊥ extends smoothly,
and it is positive ([[prop:class-axis]]). Off the axis ψ2 = ½ log a_⊥ + log ρ2, so ρ2² dψ2 = ½ ρ2² da_⊥/a_⊥ + ½ d(ρ2²),
smooth, and κ2 = g⁻¹(du, dψ2) = ½ g⁻¹(du, da_⊥)/a_⊥ + g⁻¹(du, d(ρ2²))/(2ρ2²). At (x′, (t, 0)), d(ρ2²) = 2t dx3, so the
last term is h(x′, t)/t with h := g⁻¹(du, dx3). The rotation by π of the y-plane is an isometry fixing u and changing
the sign of dx3, so h(x′, ·) is odd and h/t = ∫_0^1 ∂_t h(x′, s t) ds is smooth. So κ2 restricted to these points
extends smoothly, and by (b) κ2 extends smoothly. κ1 and □_g u are smooth near the point (g(K1, K1) > 0 there), and so
is Θ_h. Then σ2 is smooth and invariant, its restriction e_σ(x′, t) is even in t, and e_σ(x′, 0) = 0 because σ2 extends
by 0 to the axis ([[prop:class-axis]] (d)). By Taylor's formula e_σ(x′, t) = t² E_σ(x′, t) with E_σ(x′, t) := ∫_0^1
(1 − s) ∂_t² e_σ(x′, s t) ds, smooth and even, as ∂_t e_σ(x′, 0) = 0. By (a), σ2/ρ2² = E_σ(x′, ρ2) extends smoothly. ∎

**Lemma {#lem:vlasov-axis-commutator}** (The commutator up to the axes.) Let g5 be a smooth class metric and u a smooth
invariant eikonal of g5 on a G-invariant open set of {r > 0}, the axes allowed, with du ≠ 0 and ℓ := g5⁻¹du future
directed; put L := ℓ·∂ and H := ∇²u, the Hessian (as in [[lem:vlasov-no-shear]]). Let 𝔫 be the future unit normal of
the slices, E := −g5(ℓ, 𝔫), ℓ̄ := E⁻¹ 𝔫 − (2E²)⁻¹ ℓ, Θ_h := □5 u − κ1 − κ2 and σ_i := Θ_h − κ_i off the axes, with κ_i
the torus curvatures of [[lem:vlasov-axis-smooth]] (c), 𝔙 := g5⁻¹H(ℓ̄, ·) + H(ℓ̄, ℓ̄) ℓ, and 𝔜 := □5 ℓ +
g5⁻¹Ric(g5)(ℓ, ·), □5 ℓ the wave operator of g5 on the vector field ℓ. Then for every invariant function φ, with
∇φ := g5⁻¹dφ,

    [□5, L] φ = 2 Θ_h □5 φ + 𝔐(L φ) + 𝔑 φ,      𝔐 := 2 H(ℓ̄, ℓ̄) ℓ − 4 𝔙 + 4 Θ_h ℓ̄,
    𝔑 φ := 4 H(𝔙, ∇φ) − 4 Θ_h H(ℓ̄, ∇φ) − 2 Σ_i σ_i g5⁻¹(dψ_i, dφ) + 𝔜(φ),              {#eq:vlasov-axis-commutator}

and Θ_h and the coefficients of 𝔐 and 𝔑 extend smoothly to the whole set, the axes included. Off the axes this is
[[eq:vlasov-commutator]], with a = 2 Θ_h = 2 □3 u and 𝔐 = 2𝔐′ for the frame (ℓ, ℓ̄, e) used here. The same holds for
□̃ := g5^(ab) ∂_a∂_b in the chart X, with another 𝔑.

*Proof.* For any metric, [□, ℓ^μ ∇_μ] φ = 2 ⟨∇ℓ, ∇²φ⟩ + 𝔜(φ) (proof of [[cor:vlasov-commutator]]), and ∇ℓ is H with an
index raised. E > 0, since ℓ ≠ 0 is null and future directed and 𝔫 is future timelike; and
g5(ℓ̄, ℓ̄) = −E⁻² − 2 E⁻¹ (2E²)⁻¹ g5(𝔫, ℓ) = 0 and g5(ℓ, ℓ̄) = E⁻¹ g5(ℓ, 𝔫) = −1, so ℓ̄ is null with g5(ℓ, ℓ̄) = −1. The
fields 𝔫 and ℓ, hence ℓ̄, are smooth, G-invariant and orthogonal to K1 and K2, as dτ(K_i) = du(K_i) = 0. Off the axes
let e be a unit vector orthogonal to ℓ, ℓ̄, K1 and K2, and e_i := e^(−ψ_i) K_i, so that g5⁻¹ = −ℓ ⊗ ℓ̄ − ℓ̄ ⊗ ℓ + e ⊗ e +
Σ_i e_i ⊗ e_i. By [[prop:class-structure]] (a) and (b), H(ℓ, ·) = 0, H vanishes on the pairs (e_i, e), (e_i, ℓ̄) and
(e1, e2), and H(e_i, e_i) = κ_i; tracing, □5 u = H(e, e) + κ1 + κ2, so H(e, e) = Θ_h. For a symmetric form S, expanding
both inverse metrics in ⟨H, S⟩,

    ⟨H, S⟩ = H(ℓ̄, ℓ̄) S(ℓ, ℓ) − 2 H(ℓ̄, e) S(ℓ, e) + Θ_h S(e, e) + Σ_i κ_i S(e_i, e_i),

and tr S = −2 S(ℓ, ℓ̄) + S(e, e) + Σ_i S(e_i, e_i). Eliminating S(e, e),

    ⟨H, S⟩ = Θ_h tr S + H(ℓ̄, ℓ̄) S(ℓ, ℓ) − 2 H(ℓ̄, e) S(ℓ, e) + 2 Θ_h S(ℓ, ℓ̄) − Σ_i σ_i S(e_i, e_i).

Take S := ∇²φ. Then tr S = □5 φ; S(ℓ, X) = X(Lφ) − (∇_X ℓ)(φ) = X(Lφ) − H(X, ∇φ); S(e_i, e_i) = g5⁻¹(dψ_i, dφ) by
[[prop:class-structure]] (a), whose proof uses of the function only its invariance; and H(ℓ̄, e) e = 𝔙, since
g5⁻¹H(ℓ̄, ·) = −H(ℓ̄, ℓ̄) ℓ + H(ℓ̄, e) e, the components along ℓ̄ and e_i vanishing. Inserting these, with H(ℓ, ∇φ) = 0,
gives [[eq:vlasov-axis-commutator]] off the axes, and 2(H(ℓ̄, ℓ̄) ℓ − 2𝔙 + 2 Θ_h ℓ̄) is 2𝔐′ of the proof of
[[cor:vlasov-commutator]], whose H is Hess_(g3) u, equal to H on orbit vectors; and Θ_h = □5 u − ⟨du, dψ⟩ = □3 u by
[[eq:vlasov-box]].

Near a point of axis 2, Θ_h, σ2/ρ2² and ρ2² dψ2 extend smoothly by [[lem:vlasov-axis-smooth]] (c), and κ1, ψ1 are
smooth (g5(K1, K1) > 0 there), so σ1 g5⁻¹(dψ1, dφ) and σ2 g5⁻¹(dψ2, dφ) = (σ2/ρ2²) g5⁻¹(ρ2² dψ2, dφ) are first-order
operators with smooth coefficients; near axis 1 exchange the planes. H(ℓ̄, ℓ̄), 𝔙, ℓ̄ and 𝔜 are smooth everywhere. For
smooth φ both sides of [[eq:vlasov-axis-commutator]] are continuous, so the identity holds on the axes as well. For □̃,
□̃φ = □5 φ + H0·∂φ with H0^ρ := g5^(ab) Γ^ρ_ab smooth, and [L, H0·∂] is of first order with smooth coefficients. ∎

So the transport of [[cor:vlasov-commutator]] for Ψ := □̃φ, with ℓ·dφ = f and φ invariant, holds up to the axes with
coefficients that are bounded with all their derivatives on compact sets. On the O(4)-invariant two-dust background
σ_i = 0, the generators being shear-free, and a = 2 Θ_h = 2 L(R)/R is the coefficient of [[lem:two-dust-commutator]].

**Remark {#rem:vlasov-radial-pair}** (Labels and the radial pair.) By [[eq:vlasov-mixed-angle]], for null covectors
with future-directed generators, |g0⁻¹(du_α, dϕ_𝔨)| = 2 E E′ sin²(δβ/2), δβ the angle between them in the orbit slice,
and the absolute value does not depend on the orientations. So (V2′) holds with c_r = 2 E_* E′_* sin²(β_r/2) when that
angle is at least β_r ∈ (0, π] on D_α(S) ∩ 𝒯̊_𝔨, E_* and E′_* the least energies on these compact sets, and it is empty
where the tubes do not meet. It cannot be dropped: where du_α is parallel to dϕ_𝔨 on the overlap of the supports,
g0⁻¹(du_α, dϕ_𝔨) = 0, and every harmonic j supported on that pair has Q_j = j_A² ν_A² · 0 + 2 j_A j_𝔨 ν_A ν_𝔨
g0⁻¹(du_α, dϕ_𝔨) + j_𝔨² ν_𝔨² · 0 = 0, whatever the frequencies, so that no hierarchy removes the resonance. The pair
itself is separated: g0⁻¹(dv, dϕ_out) is negative and continuous on the compact Ω₂ ([[sec:two-dust-phases]]), so c_vu :=
min over Ω₂ of |g0⁻¹(dv, dϕ_out)| is positive. So the hierarchy of [[lem:vlasov-hierarchy]] runs over all n + 2 phases,
with c := min(½ c0 (2π/n)², ½ c_r, ½ c_vu) in the proof of [[cor:vlasov-two-dust]].

(3) *The limit.* Write U_n for the n-dust development of (1) and Ω_n for its adapted domain, the image of Ω₂ under
Φ_n(τ, x) := (τ, T_(τ,U_n)(x)). For every j the norms of order j of the proof of (1) are bounded uniformly in n, for
n ≥ n0(j), by the clause on m′ of [[prop:vlasov-uniform-n]]; the slices of Ω_n are the images of annuli under maps
whose derivatives are bounded uniformly in τ and n, so the Sobolev inequality holds on them with a uniform constant
(through the extension operator 𝔼 of the proof of [[thm:class-stability]]), and the energy contains the τ-derivatives:
the U_n are bounded in every C^j(Ω_n), uniformly in n. By the bound of [[def:class-adapted]] at every order, and its
form for the τ-derivatives, the Φ_n are bounded in every C^j(Ω₂), with |T_(τ,U_n) − id| ≤ C (sup 𝒩_k + ε) ≤
C (ε + n⁻²), and T_(τ,U_n) = id at distance at least δ from the axes. So the U_n ∘ Φ_n are bounded in every C^j(Ω₂),
and, by the Arzelà–Ascoli theorem and a diagonal argument, along a subsequence Φ_n → Φ = (τ, T_τ) and U_n ∘ Φ_n → U_Φ in
every C^j(Ω₂). The derivative of each T_(τ,U_n) − id is at most ½ in norm ([[def:class-adapted]]), and so is that of
T_τ − id; so Φ is a diffeomorphism onto Ω_∞ := Φ(Ω₂), which maps the lateral boundary of Ω₂ onto that of Ω_∞, and
U := U_Φ ∘ Φ⁻¹ is smooth on Ω_∞. For y in a slice and x := T_τ⁻¹(y), the bound |T_(τ,U_n)(x′) − T_(τ,U_n)(x″)| ≥
½ |x′ − x″| gives |T_(τ,U_n)⁻¹(y) − T_τ⁻¹(y)| ≤ 2 |T_(τ,U_n)(x) − T_τ(x)|; so if y_n ∈ Ω_n and y_n → y ∈ Ω_∞, then
Φ_n⁻¹(y_n) → Φ⁻¹(y), and U_n(y_n) → U(y), with all derivatives. In particular a compact subset of Ω_∞ that avoids its
lateral boundary lies in Ω_n for n large, and U_n → U there in every C^j.

Ω_∞ is the adapted domain of U. On the axes near the points where an adapted boundary B meets them, the axis phase of
U_n is the datum's phase, the same for every n, at the point where the integral curve of the transport field of the
family to which B is adapted, in the axis, meets Σ_τ0; these curves converge to those of U, so the points z_B(τ) of the
U_n converge to the point of U at which its axis phase is c_B(τ), the only one near z̊_B(τ) (the monotonicity in
[[def:class-adapted]]). The leaves h_B of the U_n over D_B are bounded in every C^j by the bound of [[def:class-adapted]]
at every order, and their graphs lie in the lateral boundaries of the Ω_n; along a further subsequence they converge in
every C^j to a function h whose graph lies in the lateral boundary of Ω_∞. The coefficients of [[eq:class-leaf-volterra]]
for U_n, evaluated on the graph of its leaf, converge to those for U on the graph of h, by the convergence just shown;
so h solves that equation for U, with h(0) the point of U above, for every smooth extension of U across its lateral
boundary, and by the uniqueness in [[lem:class-volterra]] h is the leaf of U through that point. So the whole
subsequence of leaves converges, the d_B of the U_n converge to that of U in every W^(j,∞), Stein's operator and the
average over O(2) being linear and bounded on W^(j,∞), and T_(τ,U_n) → T_(τ,U). Hence T_τ = T_(τ,U), and Ω_∞ is the
adapted domain Ω_ε of U: the limit is on its own adapted domain (the paragraph of [[def:class-adapted]] on the
determination of T_(τ,U) by U on the closure of Ω_U).

The rest of the proof of [[prop:vlasov-cauchy]] (the eikonals and amplitudes of the labels, the Riemann sums, the gauge
and the data) is unchanged: the tubes of the labels lie at distance at least δ from the axes, where Ω_n agrees with Ω₂
for every n. The pair of U is the limit of the pairs of the U_n: its covectors are closed and null, as limits of the
dϕ_𝔨^(n), with the spatial parts of d(ϕ_𝔨|Σ_τ0) on Σ_τ0, so they are dϕ_𝔨^ε with ϕ_𝔨^ε an eikonal of g_ε on the simply
connected Ω_ε equal to ϕ_𝔨 on Σ_τ0 ∩ Ω₂; their densities solve (Aug4) with Θ_𝔨 = □ ϕ_𝔨^ε; and the amplitudes F_𝔨^ε,
transported from F_𝔨|Σ_τ0, have squares q_𝔨^ε, both sides solving the same transport with the same data. The limit of
Ric(g^(n)) is T_V plus the stress of the pair, as in [[prop:vlasov-cauchy]].

(4) *Containment.* By [[def:class-adapted]], Ω_ε ∩ Σ_τ = T_(τ,U)(Σ_τ ∩ Ω₂) with |T_(τ,U) − id| ≤ C ε. The points of

(b) [[lem:two-dust-domain]] (a)–(e) hold for g0 on Ω₂^𝔡, of the type of Ω₂ with a + 𝔡 and V − 𝔡 in place of a and V,

does not reach the outer boundary, where v = V − 𝔡 + 1 + τ_end − τ ≥ V − 𝔡 + 1 > v_end + η. Let Ω_ε^𝔡 be the adapted
domain of g_ε for Ω₂^𝔡 relative to g0 ([[def:class-adapted]]). It lies in Ω_ε at distance at least 𝔡 − C ε from its

sets 𝒰_(2δ_B) about the points where they meet the axes its lateral boundaries are the level sets of ϕ_in^ε and
ϕ_out^ε at the values c_B(τ) ([[def:class-adapted]]), so they are adapted to the radial pair of g_ε there, these sets
serving as the 𝒱_B. Ω_ε^𝔡 is admissible for g_ε, being C¹-close to Ω₂^𝔡, which is what the proof of
[[prop:vlasov-uniform-n]] uses of (Ω), together with the statement on rays, which holds by (c) of
[[def:class-admissible]]. By [[lem:vlasov-open]], after the replacement of the labels made there, (g_ε, ε F^ε, u^ε)
satisfies (V1) on S with the margins halved and (V2) with δ/2 in place of δ; the proof of that lemma uses the metric
only along the rays of the labels, at distance at least δ from the axes, where Ω_ε agrees with Ω₂. (V3) holds on

for g_ε, with the compact set S ∩ ((Σ_τ0 ∩ Ω₂^𝔡) × S¹), as in the proof of [[cor:vlasov-two-dust]] for Ω₂′. (H2′) of
[[thm:class-stability]] holds for the families of g_ε on Ω_ε^𝔡 as for those of g0 on Ω₂^𝔡, the tubes moving by at most

Ω₂^𝔡 there, T_(τ0,·) is the identity and the datum on Σ_τ0 ∩ Ω₂^𝔡 suffices (the last paragraph of
[[def:class-adapted]]); its densities satisfy supp q_A ∩ Σ_τ0 ∩ Ω₂^𝔡 ⊂ S_A, the radial ones being those of g_ε, equal to

Sobolev inequality of [[lem:sobolev]] holds with a constant independent of τ, and the energy contains the τ-derivatives;
with m ≥ 6 this gives the bound in C¹(Ω₂′). ∎

**Corollary {#cor:vlasov-two-dust}** (The two-dust background with a restricted Vlasov field.) Let g0 be the two-dust
background of [[thm:two-dusts]] on its domain Ω₂, with the outgoing phase ϕ_out, assume that its outgoing packet does
not reach the inner sphere, {u1 ≤ ϕ_out ≤ u2} ∩ Ω₂ ∩ {R = a} = ∅, and replace the lateral boundaries of Ω₂ by the
adapted ones of [[cor:class-two-dust]] ([[def:class-adapted]]). Let (F, u) be a field for g0 satisfying (V1), (V2) and
(V2′) on S, as in the setting above, and fix a′ and V′ as there. Then there is ε_* > 0 such that for 0 < ε ≤ ε_* the
class Einstein–restricted massless Vlasov solution g_ε of [[prop:vlasov-radial-background]] (a), with the two radial
dusts of g0 and the field of amplitude ε F, developed from the data D_ε, exists on its adapted domain, which contains
Ω₂′, and on Ω₂′ it is a weak limit of class vacuum metrics: there are smooth metrics g_i on Ω₂′, invariant under T² and
both reflections, with Ric(g_i) = 0,

    g_i → g_ε uniformly on Ω₂′,      ∂g_i ⇀ ∂g_ε weakly in L²(Ω₂′).

*Proof.* Let ε ≤ ε0 of [[prop:vlasov-radial-background]], to be decreased below, and let g_ε and the g^(n) be as there,
restricted to Ω₂′. The solution exists on its adapted domain, which contains Ω₂′, by
[[prop:vlasov-radial-background]] (a). We check the hypotheses of [[prop:vlasov-radial-step]] for g^(n) on Ω₂′, for odd
n large, and then follow the diagonal of the proof of [[thm:vlasov]].

*(Ω) and (V3).* For g0 on Ω₂′: Σ_τ is spacelike and {v + τ = const} is spacelike with v + τ increasing along future
causal curves, by [[lem:two-dust-domain]] (a) and (c); at R = a′, μ ≥ μ0 > a′², so {R = a′} is spacelike with R
decreasing along future causal curves near it, as in [[lem:two-dust-domain]] (b) with a′ in place of a; so a past causal
curve from a point of Ω₂′ cannot leave Ω₂′ through its lateral boundaries and meets Σ_τ0 inside Ω₂′, as in (d) there.
On Σ_τ0 the slice of g0 is round and its spheres have positive mean curvature ([[lem:two-dust-slice]]). These are
strict inequalities on compact sets, stable under C¹-small changes of the metric. By
[[prop:vlasov-radial-background]], g_ε is within C ε of g0 and g^(n) within C_k n^(−k) of g_ε in C² on Ω₂′, with m = 6
there, by Sobolev's inequality on the slices (H⁷ ⊂ C⁴ in dimension four) and the equations for the τ-derivatives; so (Ω)
and (V3) hold for g_ε and for g^(n) on Ω₂′ for ε small and n large, for the generators of every family. So Ω₂′ is a
domain of dependence for each of these metrics: a ray of a label from a point of S_α outside Ω₂′ never enters it, and
one that leaves it never returns. The restriction of each field to Ω₂′ is therefore a field in label form there, with the
compact set S ∩ ((Σ_τ0 ∩ Ω₂′) × S¹), whose tubes are those of S intersected with Ω₂′, and (V1), (V2) and (V2′) on Ω₂′
follow from the same conditions on Ω₂.

*(ii′).* Two labels: g_ε, after the replacement of the labels made in [[lem:vlasov-open]], satisfies (Ω), (V1) and (V2)
on Ω₂′ by that lemma and the preceding paragraph, so [[lem:vlasov-separation]] holds for g_ε, and the argument of (ii′)
in the proof of [[thm:vlasov]] gives |g^(n)⁻¹(du_A, du_B)| ≥ ½ c0 (2π/n)² on the overlaps for n large, c0 the constant
of g_ε. A label A and a radial dust 𝔨: fix compact sets S′ and S″ with sections invariant under T² and both reflections,
supp F ⊂ int S″, S″ ⊂ int S′ and S′ ⊂ int S. The rays of a family for two metrics, from the same point of Σ_τ0 with the
future roots over the same spatial covector, and their covectors, differ by at most C times the C¹ distance of the
metrics while both rays are in Ω₂′, by Gronwall's lemma for the geodesic equations; and every point of one in Ω₂′ is
within that distance of a point of the other in Ω₂′, both leaving Ω₂′ transversally through its lateral boundary, as in
the comparison of rays of the proof of [[thm:vlasov]]. Let y ∈ Ω₂′ be a point where the supports of the two families of
g^(n) overlap. It lies on a ray of u_A for g^(n) from a point of S″_(α_A), and on a ray of ϕ_𝔨^(n) from a point of
supp q_𝔨 ∩ Σ_τ0, the data being those of g0. Put C_n := C (ε + C_k n^(−k)), at least C |g^(n) − g0|_(C¹(Ω₂′)). Then
y lies within C_n of a point y′ of D_(α_A)(S″) and of a point y″ of the tube of supp q_𝔨 ∩ Σ_τ0, both for g0, and the
covectors du_A(y) and dϕ_𝔨^(n)(y) lie within C_n of du_(α_A)(y′) and dϕ_𝔨(y″). For g0, u_α is an eikonal on a
neighbourhood of uniform size of D_α(S′) by (V1), so the ray of u_(α_A) for g0 through y, followed to the past, stays in
Ω₂′ and meets Σ_τ0 within C C_n of S″_(α_A); for ε small and n large, y ∈ D_(α_A)(S′) ⊂ D_(α_A)(S). And 𝒯̊_𝔨 contains a
neighbourhood in Ω₂ of the tube for g0 of supp q_𝔨 ∩ Σ_τ0, which is {v_start ≤ v ≤ v_end} ∩ Ω₂ for 𝔨 = in and
{u1 ≤ ϕ_out ≤ u2} ∩ Ω₂ for 𝔨 = out, so y ∈ 𝒯̊_𝔨 for ε small and n large. By (V2′), |g0⁻¹(du_(α_A), dϕ_𝔨)(y)| ≥ c_r;
the eikonals of g0 being smooth on these neighbourhoods, uniformly in α, |du_(α_A)(y′) − du_(α_A)(y)| and |dϕ_𝔨(y″) −
dϕ_𝔨(y)| are at most C C_n; so |g^(n)⁻¹(du_A, dϕ_𝔨^(n))(y)| ≥ c_r − C C_n ≥ ½ c_r. For the pair, by the same comparison
of rays, the absolute value of g^(n)⁻¹(dϕ_in^(n), dϕ_out^(n)) is at least c_vu − C C_n ≥ ½ c_vu
([[rem:vlasov-radial-pair]]). So (ii′) holds for all n + 2 families with c := min(½ c0 (2π/n)², ½ c_r, ½ c_vu), and
Λ = Λ(n) is chosen as there.

*The setting of [[sec:vlasov-n]], (iii), (iv), (iii′) and (vi).* The setting holds for the labels of g^(n) as in the
proof of [[thm:vlasov]], with the sets S_A := S″_(α_A) of that proof and u_A extended off its tube by a cut-off, which
changes neither Ric(g^(n)) nor [[eq:vlasov-amplitude]]; their waves are those of that proof, and their sets 𝒱_A lie
within C n^(−k) of D_(α_A)(S) for g_ε, hence at distance at least δ/2 from the axes for n large. For the pair, S_𝔨 :=
Σ_τ0 ∩ Ω₂′ and 𝒱_𝔨 = Ω₂′, ϕ_𝔨^(n) is an eikonal of g^(n) on the whole of Ω₂′ ([[prop:vlasov-radial-background]] (b)),
and ϕ_𝔨^(n) = ϕ_𝔨 on Σ_τ0, functions of R = r with nonzero derivative, since dv(∂_R) = 1 and dϕ_out(∂_R) < 0 on Σ_τ0
(proof of [[cor:class-two-dust]]: dϕ_out(∂_R) = (1 − R_v)/R_u with R_v ≤ ½, and R_u < 0 by
[[prop:two-dust-background]] (c)): this is (vi). The metric Π^(n) that g^(n) induces on the spheres of Σ_τ0 is within
C (ε + C_k n^(−k)) in C⁰ of the Euclidean one, which is that of g0; so [[lem:vlasov-radial-wave]] (c) gives 𝒵0 for g^(n),
and (a) the parallel polarisations 𝒵_𝔨, its transports along the rays of ϕ_𝔨^(n), which vanish on Σ_τ0 on the vectors
g^(n)-orthogonal to the spheres: this is (iii′). The amplitudes F_𝔨^(n) are the solutions of [[eq:vlasov-amplitude]] with
the data F_𝔨|Σ_τ0, smooth, with (F_𝔨^(n))² = q_𝔨^(n).

So [[prop:vlasov-radial-step]] gives, for each odd n ≥ n0 and λ ≤ λ0(n), vacuum metrics g^(n)_λ on Ω₂′, invariant under
T² and both reflections, with |g^(n)_λ − g^(n)| ≤ C(n) λ and ∂g^(n)_λ − ∂g^(n) = Σ ν du ⊗ [∂_θ W] + O(C(n) λ), the sum
over the n + 2 families. For a label, |ν_A ∂_(θ_A) W_A| ≤ C (F_A + F_A²) with C independent of n and A, as in the proof
of [[thm:vlasov]]. For the pair, ν_𝔨 ∂_(θ_𝔨) W_𝔨 = −2 F_𝔨 sin θ_𝔨 𝒵_𝔨 + ν_𝔨³ c_𝔨 cos 2θ_𝔨 dϕ_𝔨 ⊗ dϕ_𝔨, where the
Cartesian components of 𝒵_𝔨 are bounded independently of n (𝒵0 is, and the transport has coefficients bounded by the
C¹ norm of g^(n)), and ν_𝔨³ c_𝔨 ≤ C F_𝔨² as for the labels; so these two terms are bounded independently of n.

*The diagonal.* It is that of the proof of [[thm:vlasov]], with the n + 2 families and Ω₂′ in place of Ω. The two
radial terms add at most C to ‖Σ ν du ⊗ [∂_θ W]‖²_(L²), independently of n; the cross terms between a radial family and
any other family have the phases ν ϕ_𝔨 ± ν′ u′, with nonzero differentials, the covectors being null and not parallel on
the overlaps by (ii′), and they tend to zero at fixed n by non-stationary phase. With [[prop:vlasov-radial-background]]
(b), |g_i − g_ε| ≤ 1/n_i + C_k n_i^(−k) and |∫ η_j · (∂g_i − ∂g_ε)| ≤ 1/n_i + C_k n_i^(−k) ‖η_j‖_(L¹) for g_i :=
g^(n_i)_(λ_(n_i)), and ‖∂g_i‖_(L²) is bounded independently of i. So g_i → g_ε uniformly and ∂g_i ⇀ ∂g_ε weakly in
L²(Ω₂′); each g_i is smooth, vacuum and invariant under T² and both reflections. ∎

**Remark {#rem:vlasov-radial-scope}** (What the corollary does not give.) The vacuum metrics live on Ω₂′, a domain of the
type [[eq:omega]] inside the adapted domain, for every a′ ∈ (a, √μ0) and V′ < V with a′ − a < δ0/4 and V − V′ < δ0/4,
ε_* depending on them; the last two conditions cost nothing, as Ω₂′ grows when a′ decreases or V′ increases. On the
adapted domain itself, whose slices after Σ_τ0 are not annuli, the mollifier of [[lem:mollifier]] and the transport
identity of [[lem:two-dust-transport]] are written for annular slices, and the high-frequency step is not carried out
there. Smallness of the field enters only the existence of the background near g0 ([[prop:vlasov-radial-background]]
(a)); [[prop:vlasov-radial-step]] and the lemmas before it use no smallness. No uniqueness of g_ε is stated.
