# P-36-c44: Proposition prop:iii-c-4-4 (smallness at order k): verdict, uses, fix

Scope: prop:iii-c-4-4 (chapters/36-s17-null-infinity.md, line 422; assembled as Proposition 27.14), its proof, every
use of it, and the analogues in chapters 38 and 45.

## 1. Verdict: a real gap in the statement. The mathematics was fine, and the gap is now closed

**What the proof needed.** The old hypothesis was ‖h‖_(H^(k,a₀,a_I)_b) < ε₀, "ε₀ small depending on k, a₀, a_I and
on g₁". The old proof used it in one place only: the Neumann series
(g₁ + h)⁻¹ − g₁⁻¹ = Σ_(j≥1)(−1)^j g₁⁻¹(hg₁⁻¹)^j, summed in H^(k,ℓ₀,ℓ_I)_b with term bounds C(Cε₀)^j. Here C is the
constant of the algebra estimate in part (i) of the proof of lem:iii-c-2-1 *at the order k*, so it depends on k. The
proof also relied, without saying so, on L^∞ smallness: lem:iii-c-4-3 needs g = g₁ + h to be Lorentzian with a bounded
inverse. Nothing else in the proof needs smallness. The background part and the counting are smallness-free, and the
order-k membership of the coefficients comes from the products (lem:iii-c-2-1, k − 2 > 5/2).

**Embedding and product lemma, checked against the paper.**
- lem:iii-c-2-1 needs k > d/2 = 5/2 and k′ ≥ k, so its algebra form holds from order 3 on.
- Part (i) of its proof works on unit cubes in logarithmic coordinates, using Sobolev embedding there. The same cubes
  give H³_b ⊂ L^∞ on U. Chapter 45 states this as eq:iv-0-1 (H^s_b ⊂ L^∞ for s > 5/2 on M_♯, whose b-spaces are those
  of U).
- So order 3 is the least fixed order at which the inverse metric can be controlled. The order-k membership then
  follows by induction from the products, with no smallness. Chapter 38 already works this way:
  - prop:iii-i-3-3 asks only for a small H^(3,(1,0,0))_b-norm;
  - it uses its own lemma lem:s19-inverse;
  - its text says the Neumann series of prop:iii-c-4-4 "needs smallness in H^k".

**Why it is a gap.** As written, prop:iii-c-4-4 needs smallness at order k for membership at order k. So do
prop:iii-c-5-2 and cor:iii-c-5-3, which ask for h "small as in prop:iii-c-4-4". But the paper applies these results at
arbitrary orders, or at every order, while having smallness at one fixed order only (H⁵, H⁸, B⁵, or the order-3d
smallness of the exterior solution):
- chapter 45: thm:iv2-3-1 (ii), lem:iv2-2-2, thm:iii-c1-3-1 Steps 1–2, lem:iii-c2-1-1, thm:iii-c2-3-1 (2), and
  sec:iv1-0 through prop:iv1-2-2 and thm:iv2-2-4;
- chapter 46: the Nash–Moser map (ii), part (c), and e_S.

In the Nash–Moser schemes, Φ must be defined on a fixed ball {|x|_(3d) < δ} and satisfy bounds at every order. If ε₀
depends on k, no fixed δ works. Taken literally, these uses were not covered.

**Can it be fixed?** Yes, without changing any result's substance. The fixed form, proved in full below, asks for
smallness at order 3 only, with ε₀ independent of k, and h merely in the order-k space. It also states and proves
that the coefficients depend smoothly on h. This replaces the old proof's implicit reliance on the analytic Neumann
series at order k, which cor:iii-c-5-3 (continuity in s) and thm:iv2-3-1 (the map is C²) need. The paper now says
explicitly that the fixed-order form and the dependence on h are proved in the paper, not taken from [Hin26].

## 3. The fix (chapters 36, 38, 45; no labels added, so no crosswalk change)

### 3.1 The restated proposition and its proof (chapter 36)

Statement: let k ≥ 5, g₁, g₀ ∈ 𝒢, a₀, a_I > 0, and h ∈ H^(k,(ℰ₀,ℓ₀),(ℱ,ℓ_I))_b ∩ H^(k,a₀,a_I)_b with
‖h‖_(H^(3,a₀,a_I)_b) < ε₀, where ε₀ depends on a₀, a_I and g₁, and not on k. Then eq:iii-c-4-4 holds, with
L^(g,g₀) − L^(g₁,g₀) in the H^(k−2,…) class, its coefficients depending smoothly on h and vanishing at h = 0.

The proof is unchanged except for its part *The perturbation*, which now has four steps:
- **Step 1 (order 3).** The algebra estimate at order 3 sums the Neumann series in H^(3,a₀,a_I)_b, with ε₀ independent
  of k. Embedding on the cubes gives sup bounds, so g is Lorentzian with bounded inverse, as lem:iii-c-4-3 needs.
- **Step 2 (order k).** Use V(g⁻¹) = −g⁻¹(Vg)g⁻¹, which gives
  VG = −g₁⁻¹(Vh)g₁⁻¹ − G(Vg₁+Vh)g₁⁻¹ − g₁⁻¹(Vg₁+Vh)G − G(Vg₁+Vh)G.
  Products at order m (part (i) of lem:iii-c-2-1) and induction then give G ∈ H^(k,a₀,a_I)_b, with no smallness.
- **Step 3 (expansions).** Write g⁻¹ = Σ_(j<J)A^jg₁⁻¹ + A^Jg⁻¹. The finitely many Taylor terms lie in the
  polyhomogeneous class by lem:iii-c-2-1. The tail lies in H^(k,Ja₀,Ja_I)_b ⊂ H^(k,ℓ₀,ℓ_I)_b, so it adds no expansion
  term.
- **Step 4 (dependence on h).** Steps 2–3 give polynomial bounds. The identity g′⁻¹ − g⁻¹ = −g′⁻¹(h′ − h)g⁻¹ then gives
  local Lipschitz continuity and the derivative ḣ ↦ −g⁻¹ḣg⁻¹, and by induction the inverse is smooth in h.

A paragraph after the proof says that the smallness is at order 3 and that ε₀ does not depend on k. It says that this
form and the dependence on h are proved in the paper, not taken from [Hin26]. It also defines "small as in
prop:iii-c-4-4" on any domain of h, and checks that suitable a₀, a_I exist for the classes of def:iii-c-3-1 and
def:iii-c-3-3. The attribution in the proposition's heading is unchanged.

In the same chapter:
- the proof of prop:iii-c-5-2 now justifies its coefficients off U (Steps 1–2 without weights);
- the proof of cor:iii-c-5-3 replaces "the Neumann series of (g₀ + sh)⁻¹" by the continuity of Step 4.

### 3.2 Chapter 38 (two sentences, now accurate after the fix)

The proof of prop:iii-i-3-3 now cites Steps 1–3 instead of "the Neumann series … (whose convergence needs smallness in
H^k)". It notes that both arguments ask for smallness at order 3 only. The sentence after lem:s19-inverse now calls
that lemma the counterpart on Ω_* of Steps 1–3, with a tame bound in addition.

### 3.3 Chapter 45

- **sec:iv1-0:** the smallness is now in H^(5,(ℰ₀,ℓ₀),(ℱ,ℓ_I))_b "for every k". This gives the order-3 smallness of
  prop:iii-c-4-4, with the a₀, a_I stated.
- **prop:iv1-1-2 proof:** the "Neumann series" reference now points to Steps 1–3. The *Smallness* paragraph is
  restated at order 3 (embedding) and order 5 (for h), using Step 4 at k = 5.
