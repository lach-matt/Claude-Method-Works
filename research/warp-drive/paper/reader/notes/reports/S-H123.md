# S-H123: can (H1)–(H3) of lem:tl5-continuation be established at 10 l5, and how?

Feasibility study and plan. Read-only on the paper. Nothing here is proved. Labels:
- **deduced**: an argument given here;
- **computed**: floating-point scoping, mine, with scripts and outputs under `scratchpad/sh123/`;
- **record**: a figure from `docket68/n1star/CERTIFY-10L5*.md` or from chapter 87, cited where it stands.

**Short answer.**
- *The record's route.* The route the record pursued is a no-go, and the reasons are proved there. It uses the double-null
  gauge, the (2+1) Einstein frame, axis charts glued to the interior, and absolute-value Grönwall bounds.
- *Two of the record's obstructions are artefacts of that formulation, not properties of the problem.*
  - *The axes.* Written covariantly in five dimensions, with the seed's static Killing field as multiplier, the energy
    constant of the approximant's wave operator on the chart Π is **F² ≈ 10^0.5 up to and including the axes**. That
    is about 38 in a non-degenerate norm. The record has **10^66.9 on |x| ≤ 1/2 alone, and ∞ at the axes** (E2 below).
  - *The derivative loss.* The double-null metric formulation loses a derivative (record Y3–Y5). A wave gauge relative to
    the approximant does not.
- *What is left: the lower-order coupling.* This is the zeroth- and first-order part of the linearised Einstein operator
  (curvature and frame terms). Bounded without sign, as any explicit Grönwall estimate bounds it, it costs
  10^19 on Π on the seed itself, where the true constant is O(1), and about 10^36 over the natural target. The wave adds
  between 2 and 12 orders of magnitude on Π.
- *Verdict.* **Go with conditions, staged.**
  - The weak-field exterior through the whole wave (**D_A = [−10, −2] × [1/2, 7/2]**) is plausibly provable.
  - The full exterior through the whole wave (**D\* = [−10, −11/10] × [1/2, 7/2]**) depends on a computable gate.
  - The horizon region is a no-go at present.

## 2. F: the linear estimate on all of D, axes included

### 2.1 What the record established (record, CERTIFY-10L5-RESULT.md §§6–9)

- **The metric formulation of the double-null system.**
  - It has a 2×2 Jordan block in its principal symbol (Y3). So no estimate holds in any Sobolev scale.
  - The constraint-preserving variant loses one angular derivative through the shift (Y5).
- **The (2+1) Einstein-frame hierarchy.** Theorem 7.L, and 8.W with weights, give F² = 10^138 and 10^66.9 on |x| ≤ 1/2
  over Π. Toward the axes the coefficients grow like 1/(1 − |x|): 10^166843 to the outermost node.
- **No truncated domain avoids the axes.** The angular shrink needed is 5.8 against a half-width ≤ 1 (E5).
- **Axis charts.** Near each axis the class is 4D axisymmetric Einstein–scalar. The reduced hierarchy at [Wu26]'s level
  closes structurally (axhier5), but its inequality, its constants and C_eq are not done.
- **Both small-constant routes are closed by proof.**
  - The finite-rank approximate inverse: Lemma 8.F, the defect is ≥ 1.
  - The Schur block in Chebyshev norms: Lemmas 9.H–9.B. The cut couplings are independent of N, and the block product
    is ≥ 484 on |x| ≤ 1/2.
- **Measured response** (a lower bound): F ≥ 9.2, so F² ≥ 85 (ampresp5). In the frozen-angle transported subsystem the
  signed growth is F² ≈ 2 × 10⁶, against an absolute-value Grönwall bound of 10^87–10^97 (W5).

### 2.4 What F is, as far as it can be said now

- **The true F² over D\*** is unknown. Its lower bound is 85. On the seed it is O(1–10), by ch. 65. The record's
  frozen-angle transported subsystem gives 2 × 10⁶. A plausible range is **10² to 10⁷**.
- **The explicit F²** is what a written lemma can certify.
  - The crude signless bookkeeping above gives 10^45–10^60 over D\*, once the estimated surcharge is added.
  - An optimised bookkeeping might reach 10^15–10^30. That means pointwise splits (u, v)-dependent, consistent
    k-normalisation in a horizon-regular frame, a redshift multiplier near u\*, and the zeroth-order part controlled
    through a Hardy inequality instead of exponential transport.
  - That range is an estimate, not a measurement. Measuring it is the gate of §7.
- **The explicit F² over D_A** is 10^8.1 crude on the seed, plus the surcharge, giving 10^18–10^23 crude. Optimised,
  plausibly **10^8–10^14**.
- **Level 3**, which (H1) needs for L^∞ control of ∂h, adds commutator terms. My estimate is a factor 10²–10⁴ in F².

**How a sharp F could be had, if the explicit one does not close D\*** (deduced; research, not routine).
- *Structured curvature term.* At high angular frequency the curvature coupling is suppressed: |h| ≤ (R/2N)|∇_{S³} h| on
  modes of degree > N. So the zeroth-order terms are small in the tail.
- *What is not suppressed.* The first-order frame terms, which encode the boost of parallel transport against X. Their
  true size is the boost along the null geodesics over D\*, about 10–10² in amplitude² for f ≥ 0.17. Their signless
  bound is far larger.
- *Low modes.* A validated propagator on the low angular modes (a linear 2D Goursat system per mode block, certifiable
  as an ODE problem).
- *High modes.* An energy estimate with a time-dependent symmetrizer that captures that boost.
- This is not the Schur route that the record closed: that one was global in time, in Chebyshev norms, with no
  high-frequency structure.
- No published method certifies such constants for a non-dissipative system with O(1) coefficients. I put its success
  within a year at well under even odds.

## 3. G: the quadratic constant

- *The structure* (deduced). In wave gauge the reduced equation is ĝ^{αβ}∂_α∂_β h + (lower order) = −Ric(ĝ) + N(h).
  - N contains (g⁻¹ − ĝ⁻¹)∂²h, which is quasilinear and is absorbed into the energy of □_g at a cost of |∂h| in the
    rate.
  - N also contains about 20 semilinear terms of the form ĝ⁻¹ĝ⁻¹∂h∂h.
- *The resulting constant.* In the level-3 characteristic norm, G ≈ C_S² · c_Q · max|ĝ⁻¹|²_k.
  - C_S is the trace and Sobolev constant that bounds ‖∂h‖_{L^∞(D)} by the level-2 fluxes on the 2D cone sections
    (v or u, x) with the S³ measure (R³/4) dx. It is O(1) on [−1, 1] in x; the 5D gradient on S³ carries the weight
    (1 − x²).
  - c_Q ~ 10–30 counts the terms. |ĝ⁻¹|_k is O(1) in a horizon-regular frame.
- *Estimate* (computed and deduced): **G ∈ [10, 10³]**, with central value 10². The record's G_low = 382 is a different
  quantity but the same order: the second derivative of the data-to-solution map in data units (ampresp5). Read in
  2(d)'s ODE model it is 0.57 (linest5 E8).
- *How to make it rigorous.* G is a lemma: Sobolev and trace inequalities on the cone sections with explicit constants,
  times interval enclosures of sup|ĝ⁻¹| on boxes. It is cheap. Its size matters less than F's, because it enters
  linearly, not squared.

## 4. ε_r: the residual in the norm of y, over all of D\*

**State.**
- Certified pointwise on G ⊂ Π: 3.73 × 10⁻⁵ (second kind, prop:tl5-points). On five sub-boxes: 3.01 × 10⁻⁴ (first
  kind, prop:tl5-box-first) and ≤ 5.0 × 10⁻⁴ (second kind, prop:tl5-boxes-second).
- Nothing outside Π. Π is about 1/10 of D\*'s (u, v) area and covers neither [−10, −4], nor v > 1.6, nor the axis caps.
- The data error on N₁ is |R_app − R| ≤ 1.9 × 10⁻⁷ (cor:cone-compare). The shift on N₁ (Ric_vx) is untreated
  (def:tl5-problem).

**What a usable ε_r needs.**
1. *A new approximant on all of D\*.* The existing one is a degree 24 × 30 × 15 least-squares fit to a second-order
   scheme. Its residual is the scheme's truncation error, which Richardson extrapolation lowers 65-fold, and it cannot
   go further.
   - Chebyshev decay of that fit on Π (computed, `e5_decay.py`): ρ ≈ 3.5–6 per degree in t and σ, and ρ ≈ 2.7–3.7 in x.
     The solution is analytic there with comfortable Bernstein parameters.
   - The residual carries two derivatives, and level 3 three more. Allowing for those, degrees n ≈ 50–60 per direction
     per Π-sized element give 10⁻¹⁶–10⁻²⁰ in double-double arithmetic, and n ≈ 100–140 give 10⁻³⁵ in quad-double.
   - D\* needs about 9 elements (3 in u, 3 in v) and a global x-expansion of degree 60–100. That is 2 × 10⁶–10⁷
     coefficients for five fields.
   - The solver: Chebyshev collocation in (u, v) per characteristic element, Picard or Newton iteration, axis-regular
     variables (P, Q from m, n), and a constraint-preserving form. One to two person-months, and under 10³ core-hours.
2. *Exact data to the same precision.*
   - R on N₁ solves a linear ODE with analytic w (prop:cone-exact). Chebyshev of degree 80 in r gives 10⁻³⁰ with a
     certificate, as in the cone section of the chapter.
   - The shift solves the linear ODE Ric_vx = 0 along each generator. It needs a lemma, and is certifiable the same way.
   - The wave-gauge data follow from ODEs along the generators (Rendall / [CTG23] §4).
   - Making the approximant exact on N₀ costs nothing: start ĝ from the seed at v = 1/2, the affine-correction trick of
     eq:cone-approximant.
3. *A rigorous bound over a region, not at points.*
   - The record's method, order-3 Taylor models on boxes with the Lagrange remainder, needed about 37,000 evaluations to
     certify 3 × 10⁻⁴ on one Π-box. Extrapolated, it needs 10⁷–10⁸ evaluations for Π at 5 × 10⁻⁴. It cannot reach
     10⁻¹⁵, because its remainder is C h³ with C of the size of the terms, not of the residual (record 6.3).
   - **The method that scales** is the one chapter 87 already uses on the data cone: Chebyshev interpolation of the
     residual at (2n)³ nodes, ball arithmetic at the nodes, plus a polyellipse analyticity bound (lem:cone-structure,
     lem:cone-ellipse, (I1)–(I2), extended to three tensor factors).
   - The level-3 source norm then follows from coefficient sums of the interpolant's derivatives (lem:tl5-chebyshev) and
     the interpolation error.
   - The cost is about 10³ s of 128-bit ball arithmetic per element (computed estimate: fields' jets at nodes by tensor
     evaluation, about 2 × 10⁹ ball operations per element at the record's rate of 3.2 × 10⁶/s). That is hours for D\*,
     not weeks.
4. *Norm conversion.* From the pointwise frame residual in units of √K₀ to 𝒮₃(D\*)^{1/2}: a factor of about 10²
   (measure and R⁻⁵ weight), times 10–10³ for three derivatives of the residual. So ε_r ≈ 10³–10⁵ × sup|f_ab|.

## 6. Regularity

- **The data are smooth within each stage** (deduced).
  - env = sin⁴(π(v − 1/2)/3) is the restriction of an entire function to [1/2, 7/2].
  - φ, θ and P, Q are analytic in r on [r_s, r_e], one-sided at the ends. R of prop:cone-exact solves a linear ODE with
    analytic coefficient there.
  - The shift solves a linear ODE with zero data at v = 1/2.
  - So the data on N₁ ∩ {1/2 ≤ v ≤ 7/2}, and on N₀ (the seed), are smooth up to the corner.
  - In the characteristic problem the only corner conditions are the first-order corner data. These are continuous,
    since the data are C³.
- **Consequence for stage 1.** lem:tl5-continuation, as stated for smooth data, applies to stage 1 with v\* ≤ 7/2.
  **No finite-regularity version is needed.** rem:tl5-beyond's objection ("the data are C³ and not C⁴") is about
  crossing v = 1/2 and v = 7/2. D starts at v = 1/2 and stage 1 ends at 7/2.
- **Stage 2 (v ∈ [7/2, 9/2]).** Its data are the stage-1 solution on {v = 7/2}, which is smooth from below, and
  N₁ ∩ {v ≥ 7/2}, which is affine R and P = Q = 0: smooth.
  - The glued solution is C³ across v = 7/2 and across v = 1/2, with a jump in ∂_v⁴. def:tl5-problem asks for C².
  - Stage 2's data error is stage 1's error trace on {v = 7/2}: δ₂ ≤ y₁ ≤ 2A₁, in the same level-3 norm. So the
    constants multiply: ε_r effectively ≲ 1/(F₁F₂²G).
- **The order of y.** Level 3 means four derivatives of h in L² on the cones. That is the minimum for ‖∂h‖_∞ with margin
  on a 3D quotient. It is also exactly what C³ data with a bounded fourth derivative support: the fifth derivative of env
  has a Dirac mass at the ends.
  - So a single-stage, finite-regularity version across v = 7/2 is possible only at level 3, and it would need (F1)–(F3)
    in H⁴. [CTG23] says this holds "in suitable Sobolev spaces" but does not prove it.
  - **Recommendation: two smooth stages.**
- **The derivatives in (H2).** (H2) needs C^k bounds for every k, but only to pass to the limit in the "I is closed"
  step. They follow from persistence of regularity once y ≤ 2A gives C^{1,1} control. They are non-quantitative, and no
  numerics enter.
- **Coordination with (F1)–(F3).** In the recommended wave-gauge route, the local theory needed is for the gauge-fixed
  reduced system. There:
  - (F2), uniqueness, is a PDE statement: an energy estimate for the difference of two solutions.
  - (F3), continuation, is the standard continuation criterion.
  - (F1) is [CTG23]'s construction itself (Rendall's method in all dimensions).
  - The obstruction the paper names after lem:tl5-continuation, uniqueness up to isometry of the maximal development, is
    needed only to put the solution into double-null form. Here that is done at the end by a construction (Lemma L9
    below), applied to a known solution close to ĝ in C^{1,1}.
  - **So the other agent's (F1)–(F3), if proved in double-null gauge, are not on this route's critical path.** Wave-gauge
    versions are, and they are easier.
  - The lemma can be applied verbatim with "the gauge" read as Υ(g, ĝ) = 0. Its proof does not use which gauge it is.

## 8. Risks

1. **F (decisive).** The optimised explicit constant over D\* may still be ≥ 10^35. Then D\* does not close with any
   affordable approximant. Mitigation: G0 before anything certified, and D_A as the guaranteed deliverable. Likelihood
   for D\*: high. For D_A: low to moderate.
2. **The wave's surcharge beyond Π.** It is measured only on Π (+2 to +12). Over v ∈ [1.6, 3.5], where env peaks at
   v = 2, it is extrapolated. G0's provisional approximant measures it.
3. **Resolution of the solution on D\* at seed 1 for v ∈ [2, 3.5] near u = −1.1.** It has not been shown with
   constraint convergence. The record's constraint-preserving runs stop at v ≈ 2–2.25, and its failures sit at
   u ∈ [−1.04, −1.0]. Mitigation: u\* = −1.1 (or −1.2), or seed 2, which converges to v ≈ 3.5 at every angle.
4. **Level 3 and the quasilinear term** may cost more than 10⁴ in F² (§2.4). Measured in G0.
5. **The double-null conversion (L9).** It needs the absence of conjugate points of ĝ's null foliations on D\*. Their
   expansions are bounded on the approximant, so this risk is low, but it is a new lemma.
6. **Data.** The shift on N₁ (Ric_vx) is untreated in the paper. It is an ODE, but it must be written. The data cone is
   at u = −10, not past null infinity; def:tl5-problem poses it there, so this is no risk to the stated problem.
7. **Appendix B size.** The approximant cannot be printed; see above. This is a presentation risk for a paper without
   code.
8. **Coordination.** If the local theory is supplied only in double-null gauge, the wave-gauge route needs its own (F2)
   and (F3). These are standard but must be written, or y must be defined through a gauge-comparison map, which is
   heavier.
9. **Over-reading the scoping numbers.** E2 and E3 are floating point, on a Π-only approximant, with crude splits.
   E2's 10^0.5 is a floor for the principal part, not a bound for the operator. E3's figures are upper estimates of a
   crude bookkeeping, not of the best explicit estimate. Only the comparisons (formulation against formulation, seed
   against approximant, u\* against u\*) are robust.
