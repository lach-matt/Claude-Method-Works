### 2.1 Setting and notation

- **Region.** D̄ := [−10, u\*] × [1/2, v\*] × S³ with coordinates (u, v, x, φ1, φ2); D_σ := D̄ ∩ {v ≤ σ}.
  N₀ := {v = 1/2}, N₁ := {u = −10}. For a rectangle Q = [u₀, u₁] × [v₀, v₁] ⊂ [−10, u\*] × [1/2, v\*] write
  Q × S³ for the corresponding region.
- **Background.** ĝ is a smooth metric of the class (eq:tl5-metric) on a neighbourhood of D̄:
  ĝ = −4Ω² du dv + e^{2ζ}(dx − b du)² + e^{2A}dφ1² + e^{2B}dφ2², with Ω² = e^{2W}, b = (1 − x²)s, and ζ, A, B
  as in eq:tl5-radii. Then e^{ζ+A+B} = R³/4 exactly (the P, Q and the x-factors cancel).
- **Null pair.** L := ∂_v and L̲ := ∂_u + b∂_x. Both are null, ĝ(L, L̲) = −2Ω², and both are orthogonal to ∂_x, ∂_φ1, ∂_φ2.
- **Measures.** The leaf measure is dμ := e^{ζ+A+B} dx dφ1 dφ2 = (R³/4) dx dφ1 dφ2, and the volume form is
  dvol_ĝ = 2Ω² dμ du dv.
- **Multiplier.** X := L + c L̲, with c = c(u) > 0 smooth. The default is c = f(−u)/2, f(r) = 1 − r⁻². Then X is the
  seed's static Killing field T in the gauge (lem:tl5-seed (b)–(c): T = ∂_v + (f(−u)/2)∂_u). Also Y := L − cL̲, and
  |X|² := −ĝ(X, X) = 4cΩ² = ĝ(Y, Y).
- **Frame.** e₀ := X/|X|, e₁ := Y/|X|, e₂ := e^{−ζ}∂_x, e₃ := e^{−A}∂_φ1, e₄ := e^{−B}∂_φ2. This is ĝ-orthonormal with
  η = diag(−1, 1, 1, 1, 1), and L = √c Ω(e₀ + e₁), L̲ = (Ω/√c)(e₀ − e₁).
- **Fibre metric.** For 2-tensors, k(A, B) := Σ_{M,N} A(e_M, e_N) B(e_M, e_N). This is the metric induced by
  ĝ + 2e₀♭⊗e₀♭; |·| denotes the k-norm.
- **The class subspace.** V ⊂ Sym² is {h(∂_a, ∂_φi) = 0, h(∂_φ1, ∂_φ2) = 0} (a ∈ {u, v, x}). It has dimension 8, and
  g − ĝ ∈ V for any metric g of the class or T²×reflection-invariant (L2). For a T²-invariant h ∈ V, the tensors
  ∇_{e_a}h (a = 0, 1, 2) lie in V, and ∇_{e₃}h (resp. ∇_{e₄}h) lies in the span of the (a, 3) (resp. (a, 4))
  components. These are zeroth-order in h.
- **Curvature operators.**
  - (Riem·h)_{μν} := R̂_{μανβ}h^{αβ}.
  - 𝓡h := Riem·h − ½(Ric∘h + h∘Ric).
  - L_ĝ h := □_ĝ h + 2𝓡h. This is −2 times the linearisation of the reduced Ricci operator Ric − δ\*Υ(·, ĝ) at ĝ; on
    a vacuum ĝ it is the Lichnerowicz wave operator.
  - 𝓒_{αβ}h := [∇_α, ∇_β]h, i.e. (𝓒_{αβ}h)_{μν} = −R̂^ρ_{μαβ}h_{ρν} − R̂^ρ_{ναβ}h_{μρ}.
- **Connection of the frame.**
  - ω_i(V) := ĝ(∇_V e₀, e_i) for i = 1, …, 4. Since e₀ is unit timelike, ∇_V e₀ = Σ_{i≥1} ω_i(V) e_i.
  - a_i := ω_i(e₀) is the acceleration of e₀.
  - K_{γi} := ω_i(e_γ) for γ ≥ 1 is the deformation of the e₀-congruence (expansion, shear and twist).
  - Define D_i(A, B) := 4 Σ_N (A_{iN}B_{0N} + A_{0N}B_{iN}) (frame components). Then (∇_γ k)(A, B) = Σ_i ω_i(e_γ) D_i(A, B),
    because ∇_γ(ĝ^{μα} + 2e₀^μe₀^α) = 2ω_i(e_γ)(e_i^μe₀^α + e₀^μe_i^α).

### 2.2 The current and its divergence

For a C² symmetric 2-tensor field h on D (no symmetry is assumed), put
T[h]_{αβ} := k(∇_αh, ∇_βh) − ½ ĝ_{αβ} ĝ^{γδ} k(∇_γh, ∇_δh).
For positive C¹ functions z_L, z_L̲ of (u, v), put Z := z_L L + z_L̲ L̲ and

  J[h] := −T[h](X, ·)^♯ + |h|² Z.

**The flux densities.**
- e_o := −ĝ(J, L) = T(X, L) + 2Ω² z_L̲ |h|²,
- e_i := −ĝ(J, L̲) = T(X, L̲) + 2Ω² z_L |h|².

Write w_± := ∇_{e₀}h ± ∇_{e₁}h and ∇̸h := (∇_{e₂}h, ∇_{e₃}h, ∇_{e₄}h). Since ĝ^{γδ}k(∇_γh, ∇_δh) =
−Ω^{−2}k(∇_Lh, ∇_L̲h) + |∇̸h|², one has T(L, L) = |∇_Lh|², T(L̲, L̲) = |∇_L̲h|² and T(L, L̲) = Ω²|∇̸h|². Hence

  T(X, L) = cΩ²(|w₊|² + |∇̸h|²),    T(X, L̲) = Ω²(|w₋|² + |∇̸h|²),

and both are ≥ 0, and so are e_o and e_i.

**Lemma F0.1 (divergence; deduced).**

  div J = −½ π^X_{αβ}T^{αβ} − k(L_ĝh, ∇_Xh) + 2k(𝓡h, ∇_Xh) − k(∇^αh, X^β𝓒_{αβ}h)
          − (∇^αk)(∇_αh, ∇_Xh) + ½(∇_Xk)(∇^γh, ∇_γh) + 2k(h, ∇_Zh) + (∇_Zk)(h, h) + |h|² div Z,

with π^X := ℒ_X ĝ.

*Proof.*
- Differentiate both terms of T:
  ∇^αT_{αβ} = (∇^αk)(∇_αh, ∇_βh) + k(□h, ∇_βh) + k(∇^αh, ∇_α∇_βh) − ½(∇_βk)(∇^γh, ∇_γh) − k(∇^γh, ∇_β∇_γh).
- The two second-derivative terms combine to k(∇^αh, 𝓒_{αβ}h).
- Use div(T(X, ·)^♯) = X^β∇^αT_{αβ} + ½π^X_{αβ}T^{αβ} and □h = L_ĝh − 2𝓡h.
- Use div(|h|²Z) = Z(|h|²) + |h|² div Z, with Z(|h|²) = 2k(h, ∇_Zh) + (∇_Zk)(h, h). ∎

The commutator term k(∇^αh, X^β𝓒_{αβ}h) is genuinely present for tensors (it vanishes for scalars). The scoping
bookkeeping E3 does not include it; it is included here.

**Lemma F0.2 (the weighted energy identity; no boundary at the axes; deduced).** Let φ be any C¹ function of (u, v) and
Q = [u₀, u₁] × [v₀, v₁]. Then

  ∫_{v₀}^{v₁}∫_S e^{−φ}e_o(u₁, v) dμ dv + ∫_{u₀}^{u₁}∫_S e^{−φ}e_i(u, v₁) dμ du
   = ∫_{v₀}^{v₁}∫_S e^{−φ}e_o(u₀, v) dμ dv + ∫_{u₀}^{u₁}∫_S e^{−φ}e_i(u, v₀) dμ du
     + ∫∫_Q ∫_S e^{−φ}(2Ω² div J − φ_u e_o − φ_v e_i) dμ du dv.

*Proof.*
- In coordinates, √|ĝ| = 2Ω²·(R³/4) (as densities in du dv dx dφ1 dφ2). Hence
  div(e^{−φ}J) √|ĝ| = ∂_u(√|ĝ| e^{−φ}J^u) + ∂_v(√|ĝ| e^{−φ}J^v) + ∂_x(√|ĝ| e^{−φ}J^x).
- From the metric, −ĝ(J, L) = 2Ω²J^u (since g_{vv} = g_{vx} = 0) and −ĝ(J, L̲) = 2Ω²J^v (the b-terms cancel). So
  2Ω²J^u = e_o and 2Ω²J^v = e_i.
- The x-term integrates to zero. J^x = e^{−ζ} ĝ(J, e₂), and e^{−ζ} = 2√(1−x²) e^{P+Q}/R → 0 at x = ±1, while J is
  bounded (h is C¹ on the manifold D, the axes included). The axes are interior points of S³, so there is no boundary.
- Finally, div(e^{−φ}J) = e^{−φ}(div J − φ_u J^u − φ_v J^v), and 2Ω²(φ_uJ^u + φ_vJ^v) = φ_u e_o + φ_v e_i. ∎

so that e_o = nᵀE_on and e_i = nᵀE_in. Then pointwise

where 𝔅 = 2Ω²𝔅′, and 𝔅′ is symmetric with the entries below. All entries are explicit in the 2-jet of ĝ and the
1-jets of c, z_L and z_L̲.

**Lemma F0.4 (the linear estimate at level 0; deduced).**

*Admissible rates.* Let K(p) := {(a, b) ∈ ℝ²_{≥0} : aE_o(p) + bE_i(p) − 𝔅(p) ⪰ 0}, which is convex and upward closed.
Let a_o ∈ C⁰[−10, u\*] and a_i ∈ C⁰[1/2, v\*] satisfy (a_o(u), a_i(v)) ∈ K(u, v, x) for all (u, v, x). Put
A(u) := ∫_{−10}^u a_o, B(v) := ∫_{1/2}^v a_i and φ := A + B.

*Norms.*
- Y² := sup_{(u₁, v₁)} [∫_{C_{u₁}∩D_{v₁}} e^{−φ}e_o + ∫_{C̲_{v₁}, u≤u₁} e^{−φ}e_i].
- y² := the same without the weight.
- Y_d² := ∫_{N₁} e^{−φ}e_o, the data flux (h = 0 on N₀).

*Statement.* For L_ĝh = S on D_σ,

  Y ≤ Y_d + 2‖S‖_{𝒩,φ},   y ≤ e^{φ(u*,v*)/2} Y,   hence  y ≤ F₀(δ + 2‖S‖_{𝒩,0}),  F₀² := e^{A(u*)+B(v*)},

where ‖S‖_{𝒩,φ} := ∫ du ‖Ω²e^{−φ/2}S‖_{L²(C_u, dμ dv)} + ∫ dv ‖√c Ω²e^{−φ/2}S‖_{L²(C̲_v, dμ du)} and δ := Y_d|_{φ=0}.
In the report's convention, where y sums the two sups separately, the constant is F₀² = 2e^{A+B}; that is the
convention of every number below.

*Proof.*
- By Lemma F0.3 and the choice of rates, the bulk in Lemma F0.2 is ≤ e^{−φ}·2Ω²|X||S||∇₀h|.
- Next, |X||∇₀h| ≤ √cΩ(n₊ + n₋), with √cΩ n₊ ≤ √e_o and Ω n₋ ≤ √e_i. So the bulk is ≤ 2Ω²|S|(√e_o + √c√e_i).
- By Cauchy–Schwarz on each cone, its integral is ≤ 2Y‖S‖_{𝒩,φ}.
- So Y² ≤ Y_d² + 2Y‖S‖, which gives Y ≤ Y_d + 2‖S‖.
- φ is increasing in u and in v, so e^{−φ} ≥ e^{−φ(u*,v*)} on D. ∎

**Remark (what the weight buys).**
- The two-variable integral Grönwall lemma (linest5 E7), used by the scoping report, starts from the same pointwise
  bound but only uses Φ := O + I ≤ D + ∫a_oΦ du + ∫a_iΦ dv.
- The least supersolution of that inequality is D e^{A+B}I₀(2√(AB)), and the report's form is e^{2(A+B)}.
- The weighted identity uses the pointwise structure (each density is paid by its own rate) and gives e^{A+B}: **the
  exponent is halved, rigorously and at no cost.**
- The rates need not be separable either. Any φ with (φ_u, φ_v)(p) ∈ K(p) for all p gives F₀² = e^{φ(u*,v*)}.
  On the seed this gains nothing, since the binding requirements line up along the boundary path v = 1/2 then u = u\*.

**How the rates are chosen (computed in §6; L7 certifies them).**
- *Separable rates by LP.* At each grid node (u_k, v_j), the boundary of K is sampled along rays a_i = t a_o
  (49 values of t ∈ [10⁻⁴, 10⁴]: a_o(t) = λ_max((E_o + tE_i)^{−1/2}𝔅(E_o + tE_i)^{−1/2})), taking the sup over x.
  K is replaced by the inner polygon of the sampled points (secants of a convex curve lie inside it), and
  A(u\*) + B(v\*) is minimised by linear programming (HiGHS).
- *Zeroth-order weights by outer search.* z_L = e^{p₀}R_s^{−p₂}e^{−p₄(v−1/2)} and z_L̲ = e^{p₁}R_s^{−p₃}e^{−p₅(u+10)},
  where R_s is the seed's radius (an explicit function of (u, v)), optimised by Nelder–Mead.
- *For the paper (L7).* Interval enclosures of the entries of 𝔅, E_o and E_i on boxes, and a Cholesky (LDLᵀ) test of
  a_oE_o + a_iE_i − 𝔅 with margin, certify the rates. 10 % slack suffices, as the scoping report says.

## 3. Lemma F at level 3 (L4 of the plan)

### 3.1 Commutation fields

Γ := {T, E₁₂, E₃₄, E₁₃, E₁₄, E₂₃, E₂₄}:
- T = L + (f(−u)/2)L̲ is the seed's static Killing field (it is the default multiplier X).
- E_ab is the rotation x_a∂_b − x_b∂_a of ℝ⁴ ⊃ S³, written in (x, φ1, φ2) through
  (x₁, x₂, x₃, x₄) = (√((1+x)/2)(cos φ1, sin φ1), √((1−x)/2)(cos φ2, sin φ2)). For example
    E₁₃ = −2√(1−x²) cos φ1 cos φ2 ∂_x + √((1−x)/(1+x)) sin φ1 cos φ2 ∂_φ1 − √((1+x)/(1−x)) cos φ1 sin φ2 ∂_φ2,
  and E₁₄, E₂₃, E₂₄ follow by φ_i ↦ φ_i − π/2.
- E₁₂ = ∂_φ1 and E₃₄ = ∂_φ2.

Facts (deduced; the first three checked in floating point: `killing.py`):
- (i) Every Z ∈ Γ is a smooth vector field on D̄ (the E_ab are smooth on S³, axes included).
- (ii) Every Z ∈ Γ is Killing for the seed: T by lem:tl5-seed (c), the E_ab because the seed is SO(4)-invariant.
  E₁₂ and E₃₄ are Killing for every metric of the class.
- (iii) Σ_{a<b} E_ab ⊗ E_ab = γ_{S³}^{−1} (the unit round metric), so the six span TS at every point.
- (iv) On ĝ, π^Z := ℒ_Zĝ measures the departure from the symmetry. For the E_ab it involves only ∂_xR, P, Q, s, W_x and
  their x-derivatives. The sum Σ_{E∈{13,14,23,24}}|π^E|² is T²-invariant.

### 3.2 The commuted equation

For a multi-index I = (Z₁, …, Z_k), put Ψ_I := ℒ_{Z₁}⋯ℒ_{Z_k}h. By naturality of L_ĝ,

  L_ĝ Ψ_I = ℒ_{Z₁}⋯ℒ_{Z_k} S − Σ_j ℒ_{Z₁}⋯ℒ_{Z_{j−1}} (DL_ĝ[π^{Z_j}]) Ψ_{(Z_{j+1},…)},

where DL_ĝ[δ] := d/dt|₀ L_{ĝ+tδ}. Explicitly, with Γ′^ρ_{αβ} := ½ĝ^{ρσ}(∇_αδ_{σβ} + ∇_βδ_{σα} − ∇_σδ_{αβ}):

  (DL[δ]Ψ)_{μν} = −δ^{αβ}∇_α∇_βΨ_{μν} − ĝ^{αβ}Γ′^ρ_{αβ}∇_ρΨ_{μν} − 2ĝ^{αβ}(Γ′^ρ_{αμ}∇_βΨ_{ρν} + Γ′^ρ_{αν}∇_βΨ_{μρ})
                  − ĝ^{αβ}(∇_αΓ′^ρ_{βμ}Ψ_{ρν} + ∇_αΓ′^ρ_{βν}Ψ_{μρ}) + 2(D𝓡[δ])Ψ_{μν}.

Hence, in k-norms, |[L_ĝ, ℒ_Z]Ψ| ≤ |π^Z||∇²Ψ| + c₁|∇π^Z||∇Ψ| + (c₀|∇²π^Z| + c_R|R̂iem||π^Z|)|Ψ|.
- c₁ = (3/2)(√5 + 4) ≈ 9.4, a crude count: |Γ′| ≤ (3/2)|∇δ|, and an η-trace costs at most √5.
- c₀ and c_R are similar explicit counts (≈ 5 and ≈ 6).

**On the seed every commutator vanishes.** Each Ψ_I then satisfies L_ĝΨ_I = Γ^I S, and the level-0 estimate applies to
each of them with the same rates (the Sym² rates, since Ψ_I with an E_ab is not T²-invariant). This is why the
Killing fields are used and not ∂_u, ∂_v or (1 − x²)^{1/2}∂_x:
- ∂_v is not Killing even on the seed.
- (1 − x²)^{1/2}∂_x = −½∂_ϑ is not smooth at the axes, so repeated commutation produces non-L² terms at the second
  step: 1/ρ near a codimension-two axis.

### 3.3 Recovery (elliptic and equation)

**Lemma F3.1 (deduced).** At every point, for every C² tensor Ψ,

  |∇²Ψ|² ≤ K_rec² Σ_{Z∈Γ} |∇ℒ_ZΨ|² + (lower order: |∇Ψ|², |Ψ|², with coefficients |∇Z|, |∇²Z|, |R̂iem|) + 4|L_ĝΨ|²,

  K_rec² := max(4|X|^{−2}, 8λ_M^{−1}),   λ_M := λ_min(Σ_{Z∈Γ} Z^jZ^k) (j, k ∈ {2, 3, 4}, frame components).

*Proof.*
- ∇_A∇_{e₀}Ψ = |X|^{−1}∇_A(ℒ_TΨ) + l.o. (T = |X|e₀).
- ∇_A∇_{e_j}Ψ = Σ_{Z,k}(M^{−1})_{jk}Z^k∇_A(ℒ_ZΨ) + l.o. for j = 2, 3, 4.
- ∇_{e₀}∇_{e₁}Ψ and ∇_{e_j}∇_{e₁}Ψ follow by commuting (𝓒 is zeroth order).
- ∇_{e₁}∇_{e₁}Ψ = L_ĝΨ − 2𝓡Ψ + ∇_{e₀}∇_{e₀}Ψ − Σ_j∇_{e_j}∇_{e_j}Ψ: the equation recovers the one missing direction.
- Count the terms. ∎

On the seed λ_M = R² (checked). Near u\* one has |X|^{−1} = (2√cΩ)^{−1} ≈ 1.9, so K_rec ≈ 4 there.

### 3.4 The level-3 system and its rates

Put y₃² := sup Σ_{|I|≤3} μ^{|I|}[O[Ψ_I] + I[Ψ_I]], with 0 < μ ≤ 1, and Y₃ the same with the weight e^{−φ}. In the bulk
of the identity for Ψ_I, the commutator source splits into two parts.
- **Same level.** The part −π^{αβ}∇_α∇_βΨ_{I′}, with |I′| = |I| − 1. By Lemma F3.1 its contribution, summed over Z by
  Cauchy–Schwarz, is
  ≤ 2Ω²|X| K_rec π_tot (Σ_{Z′}|∇Ψ_{Z′I′}|²)^{1/2}(Σ_Z|∇₀Ψ_{ZI′}|²)^{1/2}, with π_tot := (|π^T|² + Σ_E|π^E|²)^{1/2}.
  This adds to 𝔅 the term 𝔅_comm := 2Ω²|X|K_rec π_tot·½(aaᵀ + D²), with a = (½, ½, 0, 0, 0) and
  D² = diag(½, ½, 1, 1, 0) (Young with weight 1).
- **Lower levels.** The parts with ∇π, ∇²π and R̂iem·π. With the μ^{|I|} weights and Young, they add rates
  ∝ √μ(c₁|∇π| + c₀|∇²π| + c_R|R̂iem||π|) to both families, and a factor μ^{−3/2} to the Sobolev constant of §5
  (polynomial, not exponential). The evaluation uses μ = 10⁻² and the estimate
  |∇π|_tot ≲ |∇π^T| + 3(Σ_E|π^E|²)^{1/2}. |∇π^T| is computed; the E-part is an **estimate** (the wavelength is 1).
- **Source.** The terms with S = −2Ric(ĝ) + N(h) are source terms.

**Lemma F3.2 (the linear estimate at level 3; deduced).** With K₃(p) defined from 𝔅_{Sym²} + 𝔅_comm + 𝔅_cross in place
of 𝔅, Lemma F0.4 holds verbatim for the whole system: Y₃ ≤ Y₃,d + 2‖Γ^{≤3}S‖_{𝒩,φ}, with F₃² = 2e^{A₃+B₃}.
- The data on N₁ are the Ψ_I on N₁. They are computed from the characteristic data by the transport equations along
  N₁ (L1).
- Ψ_I = 0 on N₀.

**On the seed, F₃² = F₀²(Sym²) exactly**, because π_tot = 0 and ∇π = 0. On an approximant, the surcharge is the integral
of the deformation of the seed's Killing fields with respect to ĝ: it measures the wave, not the background curvature.

### 8.1 The single-domain closing and where its F² comes from

- (H1) in the report's form treats the nonlinearity N(h) as a source of the linear estimate:

  local quadratic constant and S the extent (§5).

### 8.3 Chains: the coordinator's model checked

**The model.** Pieces k = 1, …, N in causal order, each a characteristic rectangle or a band. For piece k:
- y_k ≤ F_k(a_{k−1} + ε_k) + F_kG_k y_k²;
- closing: 4F_k²G_k(a_{k−1} + ε_k) ≤ θ_k < 1;
- output: a_k ≤ y_k ≤ (1 + θ_k)F_k(a_{k−1} + ε_k), using y ≤ A + 4A²B = A(1 + θ_k), with flux constant 1 by (2).

The factor (1 + θ_k) is the "up to 2" (θ_k → 1 for a tight piece). What G_k is decides everything.

- **(a) G_k fixed (the coordinator's model).** Every piece is tight (θ = 1) when 4F_k²G·2^{k−1}F₁⋯F_{k−1}a₀ = 1 for all
  k. This gives F_k² = F_{k−1}/2, i.e. L_k := ln F_k + ln 2 halves: **confirmed**, with the factor 2 shifting the
  logarithm by ln 2.

    reach F_tot is **F_tot G a₀ ≲ 2^{−K}/2**: linear in F_tot, up to the factor 2^K ≈ ln F_tot/ln 2.

   - Define x̃ and φ̃ to be constant along the outgoing null generators and equal to x and φ on N₀.
   - Then (ũ, ṽ, x̃, φ̃) is a diffeomorphism of a neighbourhood of D′ onto a region containing D′.
   - In these coordinates g has the form of the class in the gauge (eq:class-metric, with β from the generators'
     x-drift), and its fields R, P, Q, W, β are smooth.
3. **(data)** On N₀ ∪ N₁ the new coordinates coincide with the old (both hypersurfaces are g-null and the definitions
   restrict to the identity). So the converted g induces the data of def:tl5-problem and is a solution on D′.

**What L9 does not need.** L9 needs no uniqueness in double-null gauge and no well-posedness of the double-null
characteristic problem, because it is applied to a metric already known to exist.
- The obstruction the chapter names after lem:tl5-continuation is uniqueness, up to isometry, of the maximal
  development. It enters only if one wants the solution found to be *the* double-null solution: for uniqueness, or to
  identify it with a solution obtained otherwise.

## 10. Risks

1. **The cross-level part of level 3 is the least accurate number in this report, and it moves the decision.**
   - The lower-level commutator terms (∇π, ∇²π, R̂iem·π) are priced with crude term counts (c₁ ≈ 9.4, a factor 2 for
     the zeroth-order part). |∇π^E| is an estimate (3|π^E|); |∇π^T| is computed.
   - Their cost after optimising μ is ≈ 3 log₁₀ R_x + O(1) decades, where R_x is the rate integral of the coupling. A
     factor 4 in R_x moves the result by 1.8 decades.
   - A careful count, and better-adapted angular commutation fields, would lower it. Commuting with angular fields
     co-moving with L̲ might remove the shift's part of π^E (untested).
   - The same-level rows are a floor that the cross-level estimate cannot go below.
2. **G is a conservative explicit product (±1 decade).** C_S is a chain of crude bounds (trace, Kato–Simon, recovery).
   It enters linearly in M₁ and M_w.
3. **The provisional approximant (L6a).**
   - It is a Richardson combination of two runs of the record's unconstrained scheme at M = 16, interpolated in
     Chebyshev series.
   - Second-order convergence holds over all of D\* (difference ratios 3.5–4.0, late v near u\* included). This answers
     S-H123's risk 3 for u\* = −1.1 at the level of the fields.
   - The angular truncation is the limiting error near (u\*, v ≥ 2): M = 24 differs from M = 16 by up to 5 × 10⁻³ in s.
   - The fit's Ricci residual is ≤ 4 × 10⁻³ (in units of √𝒦₀) there and ≤ 10⁻⁴ elsewhere. The curvature-driven
     constants are reliable to a few per cent; the residual is irrelevant to the constants.
   - It is not a certified object, and its coefficients are not pinned.
4. **Floating point and sampling.**
   - Rates are sampled at 64 × 49 × 21 nodes (t Chebyshev–Lobatto, σ Chebyshev–Lobatto, x Gauss up to |x| = 0.997)
     for the approximants, and at 72 × 49 for the seed. The axes themselves are not sampled; the formulas are smooth
     there, and S-H123 E2 found no growth toward them.
   - The LP's inner polygon is exact only at the sampled rays. L7 must certify the rates on boxes with margin.
5. **The tilt requirement (F\*), new here.** The bootstrap domains must have future boundaries that are not timelike for
   g. lem:tl5-continuation has to be restated with tilted D_s, and (F3_W) involves a spacelike–null corner. This is a
   technical cost, not a numerical one.
6. **Data (L1).** The wave-gauge data on N₁ and their transversal derivatives up to order 3 must be computed by
   transport along N₁, with certified enclosures. Since Γ ∋ T, δ is a level-3 norm that includes transversal derivatives.
7. **What precision is achievable.**
   - The decision uses projected residual levels: double precision 10⁻¹², double-double 10⁻¹⁶–10⁻²⁰, quad-double
     10⁻³⁰–10⁻³⁵ in units of the curvature scale, as S-H123 §4 gives them, and they are not measured here.
   - The level-3 conversion factor ε_r/f ≈ 2 × 10³ (level 0: 231, computed; level 3: an estimated factor ≈ 10).
   - If a quad-double D\* approximant stalls at 10⁻²⁵ (the corner near (u\*, 7/2), where even the double-precision runs
     converge most slowly), the weighted margin disappears.
8. **The decisive term is the wave's curvature near u\* at the envelope's peak.** None of the rigorous levers tried
   touches it by more than a fraction of a decade: redshift ≤ 0.33 decades; the k-symmetric trick is blocked by the
   67 % antisymmetric part. Lowering it substantially needs a different idea (§11).
9. **Seed 2 was not evaluated.** Its wave is shorter (λ = 10^{−1/2}), so its strain rates and π^Z are larger and its
   curvature-dominated region is similar. Expect larger constants.

## 11. Open levers, ranked by expected gain (deduced, untested)

1. **Better commutation fields.** Angular fields adapted to ĝ (co-moving with L̲, or the leaf metric's conformal Killing
   approximants) lower π^E and with it both the same-level surcharge and R_x. Expected 1–5 decades at level 3.
2. **Careful commutator counts** (c₁, c₀, c_R) and a computed |∇π^E|. Expected 1–2 decades.
3. **A richer weight family and non-separable φ.** Expected a fraction of a decade at level 0.
4. **The validated low-mode propagator of S-H123 §2.4.** It is the only lever aimed at the curvature coupling itself.
   It is research, not routine.
