# V-87: the residual at the worst point of G, 2.41 × 10⁻³ against 2.42 × 10⁻³

Verifier's report on chapters/87-ten-l5.md (section "The residuals of two explicit approximants") and Appendix B,
chapters/96-tables.md. I made no change to the paper.

## Verdict in brief

- **The distinction is true.** At (t, σ, x) = (−23/24, 23/24, −21/32) the printed first-kind approximant has
  f^(1)_ll = −2.414178936216672616538946 × 10⁻³ (rigorous Arb ball, radius 1.4 × 10⁻²⁸).
  - Over every approximant of the first kind (|C − C̃| ≤ ε₀), f^(1)_ll there lies in
    [−2.41417969, −2.41417818] × 10⁻³. That interval is rigorous, by Arb, and its exact first-order half-width is
    7.54 × 10⁻¹⁰.
  - So |f^(1)_ll| is 2.41418 × 10⁻³ to six figures for every approximant of the first kind. It exceeds
    2.41 × 10⁻³ by 4.18 × 10⁻⁶, so 2.41 × 10⁻³ is not an upper bound. The least three-figure upper bound is
    2.42 × 10⁻³.
- **The printed bound 2.42 × 10⁻³ holds on the whole set it is claimed for.** That set is the 16,128 points of G,
  for every approximant of the first kind.
  - I computed all eight components at all 16,128 points by a generic five-dimensional Ricci computation,
    independent of the edition's closed forms. Adding the first-order spread over all approximants, the maximum is
    2.4141797 × 10⁻³, at the named point. The margin to 2.42 × 10⁻³ is 5.8 × 10⁻⁶.
  - For the second kind the maximum is 3.721721 × 10⁻⁵, at (−23/24, 23/24, x₆). The margin to 3.73 × 10⁻⁵ is
    8.3 × 10⁻⁸.
- **The edition's argument for the maximum is complete for what it claims.**
  - The claim is a maximum over the finite set G, taken from table entries. No derivative bound between grid points
    is used, and none is needed.
  - The edition states that no bound over Π is given (ch. 87 l. 735). That restriction is necessary: off G the
    first-kind |f_ll| reaches 2.4986 × 10⁻³ at the corner (t, σ) = (−1, 1). That is more than 2.42 × 10⁻³.
- **The edition notes the distinction.** The note is in the paper's own terms, with no mention of a source, and it
  is correct (quotes below). No replacement is required. An optional sharpening of one sentence is given at the end.
- **No other place is inconsistent.** No other chapter prints 2.41 × 10⁻³ or 2.42 × 10⁻³ for this quantity. The
  appendix table agrees, and so do the lower bounds 2.414, 2.387, 2.347 and 1.095 × 10⁻³.

## 2. Recomputed values

I used three independent routes.

- **(A) Closed form, mpmath at 60 digits.** The printed approximant is evaluated at each point.
- **(B) Arb ball arithmetic, python-flint at 200 bits.** The printed approximant is evaluated rigorously.
  - The family of all approximants is enclosed by giving each needed field derivative its exact range,
    d^aF̃(c) ± ε₀ Σ|T_i(t)| Σ|T_j^(a)(σ)| Σ|T_k(x)|.
  - The exact first-order half-range ε₀ Σ_ijk |∂f_ll/∂C_ijk| is also computed.
- **(C) Generic Ricci, float64.** Christoffel symbols and Ricci are computed from second-order jets of the metric in
  the coordinates (t, σ, x, φ1, φ2), with no use of eq:tl5-components, eq:tl5-derivatives or eq:tl5-chain. All eight
  f_ab are evaluated at all 16,128 points of G.
  - The spread over all approximants comes from central differences of the 50 field-jet entries, weighted by their
    exact radii.

## 3. Verdict on the distinction

The claim is true.

- 2.41 × 10⁻³ is false as an upper bound. At (−23/24, 23/24, −21/32), every approximant of the first kind has
  |f^(1)_ll| ≥ 2.4141781 × 10⁻³ > 2.41 × 10⁻³.
- The true value is 2.41418 × 10⁻³. It is 2.414178936… × 10⁻³ for the printed approximant, and it rounds to 2.41418
  × 10⁻³ at both ends of the all-approximant enclosure.
- 2.42 × 10⁻³ is the least upper bound with three significant figures over G and all first-kind approximants.

## 4. Verdict on the edition's maximum argument

- **What prop:tl5-points (l. 1081–1105) claims.** The claim concerns the finite set G only: "At every point of G
  and for every ab, every approximant of the first kind satisfies |f^(1)_ab| ≤ 2.42 × 10⁻³".
- **How the proof works.** It takes the maximum of the table integers (242 and 373) and identifies the maximizing
  points from the f_ll intervals (lower ends 2.41411 > 2.39 and 3.71597 > 3.64) against all other entries (≤ 239 and
  ≤ 364).
- **No derivative bound between grid points.** The proof uses none. It is complete for its claim, and I confirmed
  every number it uses by the independent computations above.
- **The claim does not extend off G, and the edition does not extend it.** l. 735 says "no bound for the residual
  over the whole region Π of [[eq:tl5-region]] is stated", and rem:tl5-beyond and ch. 90 l. 217–219 say that the
  bounds hold "at points and on boxes". Off G the printed approximants give:
  - First kind, on the segment t = −23/24, σ = 23/24: max |f_ll| = 2.418908 × 10⁻³ at x ≈ −0.68817. That is below
    2.42 × 10⁻³ but above the grid value.
  - First kind, at the corner (t, σ) = (−1, 1) of Π: |f_ll| = 2.4986 × 10⁻³ at x ≈ −0.69, which is greater than
    2.42 × 10⁻³.
  - Second kind, on the same segment: 3.8251 × 10⁻⁵ at x ≈ −0.9176, which is greater than 3.73 × 10⁻⁵.
  - Second kind, at the corner: 4.03 × 10⁻⁴.
- **So the restriction to G is necessary, and the edition keeps it.** A sentence such as "the residual is at most
  2.42 × 10⁻³ on Π" would be false. No such sentence occurs.
- **The box propositions.** prop:tl5-box-first and prop:tl5-boxes-second are a separate Taylor-and-enclosure argument
  on Π* and Π₁–Π₄, with bounds 3.01 × 10⁻⁴ and 5.0 × 10⁻⁴. They do not concern 2.42 × 10⁻³.
  - Their four lower bounds are 2.414, 2.387, 2.347 and 1.095 × 10⁻³, against the values 2.414179, 2.387595,
    2.347796 and 1.095613 × 10⁻³. They are correct, and the four points do lie in Π₁, Π₂, Π₃ and Π₄.
  - Check: x_10 = −21/32 is the left end of [−21/32, −41/64]. x_9 and x_8 are the right ends of X₉ and X₈. And
    −23/24 ∈ I_t, 23/24 ∈ I_σ, (−11/24, 11/24, 21/32) ∈ Π₄.

  10⁻³ … Finally 2.41411 × 10⁻³ > 2.41 × 10⁻³. ∎"
- Appendix, 96-tables.md l. 10329–10331: "The largest entries are 242 (first kind, p = 0, q = 23, r = 10) and 373 …,
  and every other entry is at most 239 (first kind) and 364 (second kind)."
- Appendix, 96-tables.md l. 11525: "first kind (0, 23, 10) [−2.41424e−3, −2.41411e−3] 2.15e−4".

The note shows that 2.41 × 10⁻³ is not an upper bound: the lower end of an enclosure valid for every approximant of
the kind is above it. It shows why 2.42 × 10⁻³ is used: that is the largest table entry, and the least three-figure
bound. Both halves of "least three-figure upper bound" are proved. The note is correct.
