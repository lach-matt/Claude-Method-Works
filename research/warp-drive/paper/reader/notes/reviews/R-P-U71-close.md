## The decisive question (task 1)

**Which statements fix the index sets, and do they constrain only least real parts?** No. The statements record
memberships and least real parts, but the constructions that define the sets are stated rules with explicit inputs:

- ℰ_ind is, by definition, "the union of the index sets of" named propositions (ch. 40 l. 132–133, 705–706,
  1243–1245), all built from (□^Υ_b)^(0): prop:iii-d2-3-1, whose (F1) takes "ℰ generated by ℱ and the boundary spectrum
  above α" (ch. 32 l. 1303–1309, used with k = 2 and k = 1 at l. 1319 and 1335); prop:iii-f3-2-2 through
  lem:iii-f3-2-1, whose proof names "ℰ′ the index set of lem:iii-a-1-4 built from E(α)" (ch. 34 l. 1169–1175); and
  prop:iii-d3-3-1 through lem:iii-d3-2-1 (ch. 32 l. 1866–1871).
- lem:iii-a-1-4 is a statement (ch. 30 l. 1493–1497): E is generated by the boundary spectrum above α and the source's
  index set. The boundary spectrum of (□^Υ_b)^(0) is explicit: lem:iii-d-4-1 lists every root as a root of an explicit
  quadratic, eq:iii-d-4-2 bounds them, and prop:np-10-52 (i) gives d₁, d₂ in closed form (ch. 32 l. 206–223, ch. 33
  l. 2537–2548). Source exponents are integers (g_b is smooth on X̄) or such roots shifted by integers.
- ℰ^ι_ind collects the index sets of the states of ch. 13 and ch. 15, 2 + ℕ₀ and (2, 1) (ch. 35 l. 1745–1747). The
  physical states of ch. 15 are built by lem:iii-a-1-4 from the roots of L_b^(0), "an index set [that] contains the roots
  ι^C_(·,+) − 1" (ch. 34 l. 1856–1859); prop:iii-f1-2-3 lists those roots, and the ι^C are roots of the explicit
  polynomials P_l, P_0 = −(ι − 2)C and p_(v) of prop:np-10-12 at (v_C, e_C, h) = (1/2, 1/102400, 10⁻⁶).
- The least ℰ^C_I is generated from σ_Υ by (3) of def:iii-c-3-1 (ch. 36 l. 193–199; ch. 62 l. 571–579), and the least
  ℰ_++ by eq:iii-i8-1-1 (ch. 40 l. 711–715).

**Is the added exponent 3 − λ_♯ + t consistent with every statement?** With every membership, yes, and trivially: any
enlargement of an index set is (l. 43). But it is not an exponent of the ℰ_ind the edition constructs. Every root of
(□^Υ_b)^(0) lies within z = 4·10⁻⁶ of an integer (eq:iii-d-4-2 and prop:np-10-52 (i)), and every exponent that the three
constructions above produce below real part 5 is an integer plus a signed sum of a bounded number of such offsets, so it
lies within 10⁻⁴ of an integer. 3 − λ_♯ + t = 1.4901 + t is at least 0.0199 from ℤ for 0 < t < 0.49. So the explicit
indicial-root statements of ch. 13, with lem:iii-a-1-4 as the proofs apply it, exclude it from ℰ_ind. E_t is an
admissible enlargement, not the edition's set.

**Is there a resonance list or a mode-stability statement that settles the rest?** For ℰ^ι_ind the non-integer roots
ι^C − 1 of L_b^(0) enter only the physical states at the root −2, with relative exponents ι^C + 1 > 3, so they do not
reach Λ ∩ {Re ≤ 3 + ε_+} as increments; their location is explicit but not computed in the edition (lem:s14-small-h
gives only their limits as h → 0). No list of spec_ι⁺(L̄) exists: lem:iii-g1-4-2 (a) and ch. 36 l. 981 give only
spec_ι⁺(L̄) ⊂ {Re ≥ 2}. The mode-stability results (thm:iii-e-1-1 (1)–(2), ch. 34 sec:iii-f2) concern σ ≠ 0 and kernels,
not exponents.

**Answer.** The non-reachability argument is not right as written (F1, F2). The conclusion that the edition proves no
integer upper bound survives, for a different reason: l_* bounds the orders of the poles of N_ι⁺(L̄, ·)⁻¹ at real part
at most 5 (ch. 41 l. 154–156), these enter every logarithmic order k̄(λ) and every count n(E, β), and the edition does
not determine them (F3). The exponents of the index sets, by contrast, are determined by the edition and only not
listed.
