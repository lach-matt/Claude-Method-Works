## 1. The smooth-dependence clause against Step 4 (36:431–434, 36:482–488)

The clause asks that the coefficients of L^(g,g₀) − L^(g₁,g₀) be smooth. The domain is
{h ∈ X : ‖h‖_(H^(3,a₀,a_I)_b) < ε₀}, with X := H^(k,(ℰ₀,ℓ₀),(ℱ,ℓ_I))_b ∩ H^(k,a₀,a_I)_b. The target is
H^(k−2,(ℰ₀+2,ℓ₀+2),(ℱ,ℓ_I))_b. This is the second class of eq:iii-c-4-4.

- **The domain is Step 4's.** Step 4 works on {h ∈ X : ‖h‖₃ < ε₀}, ‖·‖₃ being the norm of H^(3,a₀,a_I)_b. The set is
  open in X, as X ⊂ H^(k,a₀,a_I)_b ⊂ H^(3,a₀,a_I)_b continuously (k ≥ 5). ε₀ is the ε₀ of the statement: small
  depending on a₀, a_I and g₁, and not on k.
- **What Step 4 proves.**
  - G = g⁻¹ − g₁⁻¹ is locally Lipschitz into X, by g′⁻¹ − g⁻¹ = −g′⁻¹(h′ − h)g⁻¹. The bound uses the polynomial bound
    of Steps 2–3, and the fact that multiplication by the smooth components of g₁⁻¹ is bounded on X.
  - G is differentiable with derivative ḣ ↦ −g⁻¹ḣg⁻¹. The remainder is −(g′⁻¹ − g⁻¹)(h′ − h)g⁻¹ = O(‖h′ − h‖²).
  - The derivative is a continuous bilinear expression in g⁻¹, so G is C^∞ by induction.
- **The coefficients.** By lem:iii-c-4-3 and the conversion to b-operators, the coefficients are ρ₀² times sums of
  products of four kinds of factor: G, h, b-derivatives of h of order at most 2, and smooth functions. Each product has
  a factor h or a derivative of h.
  - The b-derivatives map X continuously and linearly into H^(k−2,(ℰ₀,ℓ₀),(ℱ,ℓ_I))_b, by (F1).
  - Multiplication is continuous there, by lem:iii-c-2-1 at k − 2 > 5/2. Its last clause states the continuity, and
    the strict weight inequalities hold as Step 3 records.
  - So each coefficient is a continuous multilinear expression composed with the smooth map h ↦ (G, h). It is smooth
    from the open set into H^(k−2,(ℰ₀,ℓ₀),(ℱ,ℓ_I))_b, and so, after the factor ρ₀², into
    H^(k−2,(ℰ₀+2,ℓ₀+2),(ℱ,ℓ_I))_b.
  - It vanishes at h = 0, and the frame change F preserves the class.
- **Step 4's own words.** Step 4 ends "so they depend smoothly on h". The target it means is the class of Step 3, which
  is the space the clause now names. The reviewer's "Step 4 proves exactly this" is right.
- **The "In particular" sentence.** It now carries the clause to h in the structured classes ⟨ℰ_I^C⟩ and ⟨(3,0)⟩₀.
  This goes through the continuous inclusions of lem:iii-c-3-2 (b)–(c), and the existence of a₀, a_I in the paragraph
  after the proof. Composition with a continuous linear inclusion keeps smoothness.

## 3. The tame bound for the inverse metric (45:846–849)

The claim is ‖g⁻¹ − g₀⁻¹‖_(H^j) ≤ C‖h‖_(H^j), with g = g₀ + h (45:837). The constant C depends on a bound for ‖h‖_(H⁴),
and implicitly on j and g₀, as the surrounding constants do. The proof is the induction of lem:s19-inverse
(38:375–398), with the three replacements named.

**The hypotheses of lem:s19-inverse, with g_1 := g₀ and δ := h, on M_♯ (or U, whose b-vector fields are the same, 45:184):**

- The components of g₀ and g₀⁻¹ lie in C^∞_b. g₀ ∈ 𝒢 has Cartesian components, and inverse, smooth on U (36:444–445).
  They are therefore smooth on M_♯ (x_I = ρ_I^(1/2)), so they lie in C^∞_b.
- ϵ sup|g₀⁻¹| ≤ ½, with sup|h| ≤ ϵ. eq:iv-0-1 at s = 3 gives sup|h| ≤ C‖h‖_(H³), and h is small in H⁵ (sec:iv1-0).
- h has components in C¹_b, with ‖h‖_(C¹_b) ≤ B. eq:iv-0-1 applies to h and to Vh, V ∈ 𝒱_b(M_♯), and gives
  ‖h‖_(C¹_b) ≤ C‖h‖_(H⁴). So B is C times a bound for ‖h‖_(H⁴), which is the dependence the text states.

**The replacements:**

- **(a), Moser.** It is used at the order k − 1 for products in which one factor, Ĝ or Vδ, is in H^(k−1) and the rest
  are bounded. lem:iv1-2-3 is that estimate on M_♯ and on its domains, with weights. The L^∞ factors are bounded by
  3 sup|g₀⁻¹| and by CB.
- **(b), the characterisation by one b-derivative.** def:s11-b-analysis (3) defines H^(k,α)_b by testing with
  Diff^k_b. A composition of r ≥ 1 b-vector fields is an element of Diff^(r−1)_b followed by one b-vector field, so
  u ∈ H^(k+1,α)_b exactly when u ∈ H^(0,α)_b and Vu ∈ H^(k,α)_b for every V. Step 2 of the proof of prop:iii-c-4-4 cites
  it the same way (36:471).
- **(c), sup|δ| and ‖δ‖_(C¹_b).** These come from the second clause of (c), ‖u‖_(C^j_b) ≤ C‖u‖_(H^(j+3)_b) at j = 0, 1.
  The paragraph after lem:s19-inverse (38:403–404) uses it so. eq:iv-0-1, applied to h and Vh, is that clause on M_♯.
- **Weights.** lem:iv1-2-3 carries them: one factor takes the weight, and the bounded factors are measured in L^∞,
  their weighted sup bounds again by eq:iv-0-1. The bound therefore holds in the weighted classes of eq:iv2-0-4. The
  polyhomogeneous half of "in these classes" is the separate, pre-existing matter that R-P-36-c44 and the fixer's §5.1
  set aside. The pointer does not claim it.

So the tame bound holds, and "C depending on a bound for ‖h‖_(H⁴)" is the right dependence. The pointer is also what
eq:iv2-0-4 needs. ‖ã_i‖_(H^j) ≤ C‖h‖_(H^(j+2)) follows from lem:iv1-2-3, with the tame bound for the factor g⁻¹. Each
coefficient is rational in h and in two of its b-derivatives.
