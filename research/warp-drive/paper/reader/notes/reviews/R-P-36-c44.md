## Was the gap real?

Yes. It was a gap between the statement and its uses. Nothing in it was false.

- **The old statement.** It asked ‖h‖_(H^(k,a₀,a_I)_b) < ε₀ with "ε₀ small depending on k, a₀, a_I and on g₁".
- **What the old proof used.** Smallness entered in one place: the Neumann series Σ_(j≥1)(−1)^j g₁⁻¹(hg₁⁻¹)^j, summed in
  H^(k,ℓ₀,ℓ_I)_b with term bounds C(Cε₀)^j. Here C is the constant of part (i) of the proof of lem:iii-c-2-1 at the
  order k. Old 36:451–460.
  - The proof also needed L^∞ smallness, so that lem:iii-c-4-3 applies to g. That followed from the order-k smallness
    by embedding, so it was not a separate hole.
  - Under its own hypotheses the old proposition was true.
- **Uses it did not cover.** These apply the result at arbitrary or every order k with smallness at one fixed order only:
  - 45:1334–1336, thm:iv2-3-1 (ii): cor:iii-c-5-3 for x ∈ B^k small in B⁵, on the fixed ball {|x|_(3d) < δ};
  - 45:1012/1029, lem:iv2-2-2: h small in H⁵, h ∈ H^(s+2) with s arbitrary;
  - 45:528 (old) with 45:646, prop:iv1-2-2: "as in sec:iv1-0" asked smallness at order k, while prop:iv1-2-2 has
    smallness in H⁸ only and k ≥ 8 arbitrary;
  - 45:1564/1609, thm:iii-c1-3-1: h ∈ H^∞;
  - 46:815, 46:1287 and 46:1359.

  Literally, ε₀(k) → 0 is not excluded, so no fixed δ works for these.

### 2. The new statement and proof of prop:iii-c-4-4, line by line (36:422–495)

**Order 3.**
- lem:iii-c-2-1 needs k > d/2 = 5/2 (dim Ω_ext = 5), so its part (i), the algebra estimate on unit cubes in logarithmic
  coordinates, holds at the order 3.
- Weights: ρ^a H³_b · ρ^a H³_b ⊂ ρ^(2a)H³_b ⊂ ρ^a H³_b, with a constant depending on a₀ and a_I, as ρ^a is bounded with its
  b-derivatives on U.
- The embedding H³(Q) ⊂ L^∞(Q) on the same cubes (3 > 5/2) gives H^(3,a₀,a_I)_b ⊂ L^∞. Chapter 45's eq:iv-0-1 states
  the same at s > 5/2.
- So order 3 is the least integer order at which both hold, and it suffices.

**Step 1.**
- ‖A‖₃ ≤ C‖h‖₃, since multiplication by g₁⁻¹ is bounded on H^(3,a₀,a_I)_b. So ‖A^jg₁⁻¹‖₃ ≤ C(Cε₀)^j.
- S := Σ_(j≥1)A^j converges in H³ ⊂ L^∞, and (I − A)(I + S) = I because multiplication is continuous. Hence
  g⁻¹ = (I + S)g₁⁻¹ and G = Σ_(j≥1)A^jg₁⁻¹. g = g₁(I − A) is checked.
- sup|G| ≤ C‖G‖₃ ≤ C′‖h‖₃.
- With g₁ uniformly Lorentzian (g₁⁻¹ bounded on U), sup|h| small gives invertibility with |g⁻¹| ≤ 2|g₁⁻¹|. The signature
  is constant along g₁ + th. The splitting frame and the Cartesian frame differ by F₀(ω)(I + ρ²C^∞), so the sup bounds
  carry over to the Cartesian components that lem:iii-c-4-3 needs.

**Step 2.**
- The identity VG = −g₁⁻¹(Vh)g₁⁻¹ − G(Vg₁ + Vh)g₁⁻¹ − g₁⁻¹(Vg₁ + Vh)G − G(Vg₁ + Vh)G is verified by expanding
  −(g₁⁻¹ + G)(Vg₁ + Vh)(g₁⁻¹ + G) + g₁⁻¹(Vg₁)g₁⁻¹.
- The Leibniz rule holds for bounded H³_loc factors.
- Each term is a product of smooth factors with one, two or three factors in H^(m,a₀,a_I)_b (G, and Vh by (F1), using
  h ∈ H^(m+1), m + 1 ≤ k). So it lies in H^(m,a₀,a_I)_b by the algebra estimate at m ≥ 3, with no smallness.
- def:s11-b-analysis (3) then gives G ∈ H^(m+1,a₀,a_I)_b.
- The polynomial bound follows by induction from the base ‖G‖₃ ≤ C‖h‖₃. Smallness is used only in the base.

**Step 3.**
- The identity g⁻¹ = Σ_(j<J)A^jg₁⁻¹ + A^Jg⁻¹ is checked.
- The Taylor terms lie in (jℰ₀, ℓ₀), (jℱ, ℓ_I) by lem:iii-c-2-1 with γ = ℓ: γ ≤ 2ℓ and γ < ℓ + min Re. And
  jℰ₀ ⊂ ℰ₀ by def:iii-c-3-1 (1), jℱ ⊂ ℱ by induction from 2ℱ ⊂ ℱ.
- The tail A^Jg₁⁻¹ + A^JG lies in H^(k,Ja₀,Ja_I)_b ⊂ H^(k,ℓ₀,ℓ_I)_b, which is the remainder class of
  def:s11-b-analysis (5) (E′_H = ∅).
- So G has the stated index sets and remainder.
- The coefficient count and the frame change are as before, and eq:iii-c-4-4 follows.
- rem:iii-c-4-5's (5.11) membership is the case J ≥ 2 (j-fold sums with j ≥ 2 lie in 2ℰ₀ and 2ℱ).

**Step 4.**
- X is an algebra: lem:iii-c-2-1 in the polyhomogeneous part, and weights a + a ≥ a in the conormal part.
- {‖h‖₃ < ε₀} is open in X.
- The identity g′⁻¹ − g⁻¹ = −g′⁻¹(h′ − h)g⁻¹ with the polynomial bounds gives the local Lipschitz bound.
- The remainder of the linearisation is −(G′ − G)(h′ − h)g⁻¹ = O(‖h′ − h‖²), so the derivative is ḣ ↦ −g⁻¹ḣg⁻¹.
- It is a continuous bilinear expression in g⁻¹, so by bootstrapping G is C^∞.
- The coefficients are polynomial in G, in h, ∂h, ∂²h (in H^(k−2), an algebra as k − 2 ≥ 3) and in smooth functions.
  Each term has a factor h (lem:iii-c-4-3, which involves no derivative of g⁻¹). So they are smooth in h and vanish at
  h = 0. The factor ρ₀² and the conjugation by F preserve this.

**The paragraph after the proof (36:489–495).**
- "small as in" is well defined, with ε₀ independent of k.
- The claim that such a₀, a_I exist follows from def:s11-b-analysis (5). The expansion coefficients lie in H^k of the
  face, and every exponent exceeds a, so the classes of def:iii-c-3-1 and def:iii-c-3-3 embed continuously in
  H^(k,a₀,a_I)_b for 0 < a₀ < min(ℓ₀, min Re ℰ₀) and 0 < a_I < 3/2. Every slot set lies in ℰ_I^tot, with min Re = 3/2,
  and ℓ_I > 3/2.
