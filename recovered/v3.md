**Five exclusions. They are not five mechanisms.** One breaks monotonicity; three
are the same resolution failure at different values of one ratio; one is a
data-hygiene condition. §10.1 makes the reduction precise.

**(i) Sign-changing quantities.** Any property built from a matrix element that
passes through zero cannot be monotone. Sr I 5s²¹S₀ → 5s*n*p ¹P₁ reduced
transition probabilities collapse by a **factor of 60** at *n* = 6 and recover —
a Cooper-type node. Bracket coverage across the node: 79%. Away from it: 100%
on 11 cells. This is the only exclusion that attacks the order hypothesis
itself.

**(ii) Unresolvable steps.** Fine-structure splittings reduced by ν³ vary by
~0.5% across a series against a measurement noise of 1.6%. Bracket coverage:
**35.3%**. Zero of 17 adjacent steps are resolvable.

Earlier versions attributed this to non-monotonicity — "a constant is not
monotone." That diagnosis does not fit our own number. If the sequence were flat
and the neighbours carried independent noise, the bracket would catch the target
exactly when the two neighbours straddle it, giving 50% coverage. We observe
35.3%, fifteen points below that floor, which requires systematic structure
running against the assumed direction rather than mere flatness. **We record this
as unresolved** (§15.4). What the paragraph's own numbers do establish is a
resolution failure: the steps are smaller than the error bars on the quantities
being differenced.

**(iii) Systems without interior cells.** Ti I: 23 channels, 26 members, **zero
interior cells**. No channel reaches three members, because the ionisation limit
near 55,000 cm⁻¹ sits below the onset of severe level density, and above
~42,000 cm⁻¹ the levels are 40–50% configuration-mixed and have no assignable
parent. The method declines rather than errs. This is the degenerate case of
(ii): where no interior cell exists, no step exists to resolve.

**(iv) Collapsed orbitals.** Excluded by the defect-departure test of Rule 1. A
data-hygiene condition, not a property of the observable.

**(v) Insufficient measurement precision.** An observable may be monotone, free
of zeros, and vary by orders of magnitude, and the bracket still fails — because
containment is a claim about *true* neighbours and one only ever has *measured*
ones. Tested off-domain on gas-phase kinetics (§10.2): the deductive guarantee
degrades continuously, and does so as a function of a single ratio.

### 10.1 The five reduce to one ratio

Let *r* = (fractional step between adjacent measured cells) / (fractional 1σ
measurement error). Exclusion (ii) shrinks the numerator; (v) inflates the
denominator; (iii) is *r* undefined. Only *r* appears. Simulating three
consecutive monotone true values spaced *r*σ apart, with independent Gaussian
error on the two neighbours, gives a one-parameter containment curve:

| *r* = step/σ | 0.1 | 0.5 | 1.0 | 1.3 | 2.0 | 2.7 | ≥5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| containment | 50% | 57% | 73% | 84% | 96% | 99% | ~100% |

The floor at *r* → 0 is 50%, not 0: two noisy neighbours straddle a flat target
half the time. **This curve is the domain boundary of Part II**, and it is why
*T* = *Z*²*R*/ν² works. Rydberg energies are measured to 10⁻³ cm⁻¹ against
neighbour spacings of hundreds of wavenumbers, putting *r* in the range 10⁴–10⁶ —
five orders of magnitude inside the plateau, which is why 295 of 295 cells hold
with no exceptions. It is not that energies are unusually monotone. It is that
they are unusually well measured relative to how fast they change.

### 10.2 An off-domain test of the ratio

Because the curve is a claim about arithmetic rather than about atoms, it is
testable outside atomic physics. We took the one system meeting §15.2's other
three conditions that we could find with evaluated data: OH radical reaction
rate coefficients with the *n*-alkanes, C₁–C₁₃, at 298 K, from the Atkinson
(2003) evaluation. The series is a single channel, strictly monotone in carbon
number, spans a factor of 2,359, and carries stated uncertainties of ±20–25%.
Eleven interior cells.

Leave-one-out bracket coverage is 11 of 11 — and that figure is worthless, since
the published values are mutually consistent by construction. Resampling the
neighbours at their stated uncertainty gives the honest result:

| C | min fractional step | *r* | containment |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 0.54 | 5.4 | 99.9% |
| 7 | 0.20 | 2.0 | 95.1% |
| 9 | 0.13 | 1.3 | 86.3% |
| 12 | 0.07 | 0.7 | 68.3% |

Pooled containment 90% against a deductive claim of 100%. These points lie on the
curve of §10.1, computed independently of them. Mean bracket width is 95% of the
target value against an experimental interval of 82%, so the bracket is *wider
than simply measuring the quantity*; and log-linear interpolation between the same
two neighbours predicts the withheld value to a median of 3.2%, six times better
than the measurement it replaces. Both comparisons fail, and both fail on *r*.

Two further observations from that test bear on Part II. First, ordering all 24
alkanes by carbon number alone rather than restricting to the straight chain
produces 46 monotonicity violations in 251 comparable pairs (18%) — neopentane at
C₅ falls below propane at C₃. Rule 1's second clause is doing the same work in a
system with no atomic structure that it does for Sc III 3d, which is modest
evidence that the clause is about channels rather than about spectroscopy.
Second, these rate coefficients are largely relative-rate chained against one
another, so their errors are correlated and independent resampling overstates the
failure somewhat; the C₁₁–C₁₃ steps sit below 1σ under any correlation
assumption.

**The method does not transfer to chemical kinetics, and the reason is
quantitative rather than categorical.** This closes the third question of §15.4
as posed in earlier versions.
