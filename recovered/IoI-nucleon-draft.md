## The nucleon index

**The measured nuclide chart, indexed by proton and neutron count.** Chemistry's index is Λ; the nucleus has its own, and it is the one place a second physical index sits beside the atomic one. Register 298, 388, 390.

| coordinate | values | bound |
|---|---|---|
| Z | ℤ₊ | proton number |
| N | ℤ₊ | neutron number |

**E = 9**, on the particle-bound nuclides at low Z, stable across four proton-number cutoffs. The chart taken as nature draws it — the nuclides that do not immediately emit a nucleon — closes to admit nine cells it lacks: **He-5, He-7, Li-10, Be-8, Be-13, B-9, B-16, B-18, C-21**, every one a known unbound nuclide sitting inside the band its own neighbours define. Be-8 is unbound because it is two alpha particles and nothing else — clustering; He-5, He-7, Li-10 and Be-13 are unbound by one neutron against an even-paired core — pairing. **The nine are the pairing and clustering terms of the mass formula, counted** — content the two coordinates cannot carry, because (Z, N) knows how many protons and neutrons a nuclide has and cannot know that four of them prefer to be an alpha. The defect appears by Z ≤ 7 and none is added after, so it is a property of the measurement and not of where the chart was cut. The drawn chart, between its drip lines, is exactly ℛ of the measured one — E = 0 necessarily, a closure that certifies nothing — which is why the drawn band and the real chart were once conflated.
