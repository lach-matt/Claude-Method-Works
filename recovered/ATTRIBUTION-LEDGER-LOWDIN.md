## D · THE FILLING AMBIGUITY (challenge clause 2) — THE INTERPRETIVE LINE
D1  Scerri, E. (2009). Int. J. Quantum Chem. 109:959-971. [SECONDARY]
D2  Scerri, E., Inference Review letter "On the Madelung Rule". PUBLICLY REVERSED his
    long-held position that the rule cannot be derived. Two claims of record:
    (i) the rule does NOT give the order of occupation of orbitals within the
        independent-electron approximation — it gives the resulting configuration;
    (ii) the "anomalies" are the crossing of the s1 and s2 CONFIGURATION energies,
        and those energies are computable from first principles by Hartree-Fock,
        crossing at about the right place along each transition series.
D3  Bent, H.A. & Weinhold, F. (2007). J. Chem. Educ. 85. [SECONDARY]
D4  Scerri & Restrepo (2018), Mendeleev to Oganesson, ch.5: properly interpreted, the
    rule applies across the whole table. [SECONDARY]

## E · WHAT THE PRIOR ART DOES NOT CONTAIN
E1  No prior work computes a parameter-free field AT EACH ATOM and scores the next
    entrant element by element as a walk. B1-B10 derive an ordering from a chosen
    potential or symmetry; C1 reads an ordering off published tables.
E2  The stated ground on which B1 was rejected (B4) is that its V_eff WAS GUESSED.
    B10's own summary disclaims many-electron status and assigns lambda = l - 1/2 by
    minimisation, i.e. the labelling that produces the ordering is chosen after the fact.
E3  No prior work separates the ORDERING clause from the TIE-BREAK clause and gives the
    tie-break its own field-computed domain. B1-B10 obtain "(n+l, n)" as one object.
E4  No prior work states a collapse condition distinguishing plateau, transit and
    complete regimes, nor derives the absence of a g block from a field.
