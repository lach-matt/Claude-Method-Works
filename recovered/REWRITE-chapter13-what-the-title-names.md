## What the title names

Twice the title changed, and both changes recorded findings rather than preferences; a third change, to the subtitle, did the same.

The book was called *The Lach Index Lattice* until its shape was computed. The shape is a cylinder over the transfer — §13.2 gives the reason — so the object's name became *The Lach Cylinder*.

Then the object became the smaller half of the book. The register of withdrawals, the audits, the principles and protocols outgrew the demonstration on Λ, and a book that was mostly method carrying a single worked object could no longer take that object's name as its own.

And a third time, when the languages were gathered. Fifty sections across fourteen chapters made a claim about language and none owned one; Part IV now does, and shows that eight results already in this book are one statement — **a language is a coordinate system, translation is re-coordinatisation, and E is what the translation costs.** The subtitle records it:

> **The Lach Cylinder — a closed index of transitions, and the languages they speak**

Three things changed and each is a finding. *Closed* replaces nothing and adds the theorem: E(Λ) = 0, verified at 976 cells in six languages. *The languages they speak* replaces four terms that had named four Parts — closure, self-reference, refusal, and the price of certainty — with a single clause naming what those Parts have in common.

And the grammar is the claim. Not *the languages it is spoken in*, which would make the index the thing described and the languages descriptions of it. **The transitions speak them.** §12.11.4 is the proof: the exact J multiset is identical in jK, LS, LK and jj, so those are not four accounts of one transition — they are one transition said four ways, and the 2.17-fold spread in cell count is what each saying costs. The atom does not prefer a scheme; the index remembers the route.

Two candidates were rejected, and the reasons are the finding's boundary. *…and the languages that translate into it* asserts Λ as the target, which §12.11.4 forbids — there is no privileged scheme. *…and what translation into it costs* keeps the price and loses the plurality, and the plurality is the subject.

> The title is now *The Method*, and *The Lach Cylinder* is its subtitle.

### 13.1 Why that ordering and not the other
Because the method is what generalises and the object is what tested it. Λ closes; so does the nucleon index, the string partition function, the Kreuzer–Skarke frontier, and the index of this book's own mathematics. What travels between them is not the cylinder. It is: build the index, close it, read E(X), record every failure.

> The method is the book, and the book is the method. The cylinder is the case that proved it could be done, and the case that showed what it costs.

### 13.2 The cells are transitions, not states
Λ is fibred over q, the number of electrons transferred — not over any property of a single configuration, but over the movement between two.

> One end is a parent (n, ℓ, k) with its spin 2S. The other is a target (e, f) with its occupancy g. They meet at q.

Chapter 7 introduces both ends and never says the pair is the object. The shape says it: |Λ| = Σ_q |A_q| · |B_q|, and there is no factorisation that does not pass through the transfer. [MC-18]

### 13.3 Which is why "index of transitions"
An index of states would fibre over a state variable. This one fibres over a difference, and every structural result follows from that:

| finding | where it comes from |
|---|---|
| the fibres are two-body products | the two ends of the tree |
| as q rises the parent family A_q falls and the target family B_q rises | the transfer is conserved between them |
| g ≤ q is the most active constraint | it is the coupling itself |
| rank is modular | the transfer redistributes without loss |

### 13.4 And what the cylinder does not carry
The lattice supplies kinematics and no dynamics. What configurations exist, which transitions are admissible, what is conserved — all structural. Which transition actually occurs is not.

> 1,113,045,672 maximal chains satisfy every constraint in this book (§12.9). The Aufbau ordering is one of them. Structure does not choose; energy does, and energy enters only through Chapter 17's measured levels. [MC-21]

That is the sharpest statement of what the index can and cannot do, and it is a statement about its shape: **a phase space with the Hamiltonian removed can bound and refuse, and cannot predict.**
