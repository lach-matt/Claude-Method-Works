# Preface — the shape of the argument

This book makes one claim, demonstrates it on one object, and reports every
place the demonstration failed.

**The claim.** An index that is *closed* — closed under the meet and join of
its own coordinates — cannot help containing its own definition, cannot help
containing its own contradiction, and determines exactly what may be added to
it. These are not three properties that a well-made index happens to have.
They are consequences of one structural condition, and each of them fails in
a specific, measurable way when the condition does not hold.

**The object.** Λ, an ordering of atomic electron configurations on eight
coordinates. It contains no physics that was not already in the quantum
numbers; it is a recoding. That is deliberate. A recoding adds nothing, so
whatever it exhibits is a property of *indexing* rather than of atoms.

**The demonstration.** A method that brackets Rydberg energy levels using
only measured neighbours, tested on 1,442 interior cells across 35 atomic
systems, spanning ℓ = 0 to 6 and *Z* = 1 to 83. The bracket holds in every
one. That is a weaker result than it sounds, and Chapter 16 explains why —
and then shows what the silence is actually measuring.

**The register.** Forty-eight claims made in the course of this work were
wrong and were withdrawn. They are listed in Chapter 18, with what replaced
them. Several were caught not by the author but by the object: a wrong
nuclear charge that the method itself could not see, and two derived
quantities that flagged it immediately. **That episode is the book's best
evidence, and it is an account of an error.**

## Why the periodic table is the right place to start

The periodic table is the most successful index in the physical sciences. It
is also not closed, and the cost of that is exactly thirty-six cells.

Chapter 1 computes it. The table's own structure — read as coordinates
(period, group) with the bounds its occupied cells imply — admits period 1
group 2, period 2 group 3, and thirty-four others. They do not exist. **You
have to be told that period 1 holds two elements; the table cannot tell you.**

Λ is the same 118 elements. Its external definition cost is zero.

That difference is the whole book. Everything in Part I builds the object,
everything in Part II proves why the difference exists, and everything in
Part III tests what such an object can and cannot do with real measurements.

## How to read this

**If you want the general result**, Part II is self-contained. Chapter 7
defines closure; Chapters 8 to 10 prove the three consequences; Chapter 11
states what none of it can deliver.

**If you want the physics**, Part III stands alone given the definition of the
bracket in Chapter 13.

**If you want to check the work**, Chapter 18 and Appendix C are where the
withdrawals and the provenance live. Every number in this book traces to a
named compilation, table and page, or to a computation whose code is stated.

**And if you are looking for a prediction**, there is one, in Chapter 16, and
it is offered as falsifiable rather than as a result. The rest of the book is
about why an index cannot supply more of them.
