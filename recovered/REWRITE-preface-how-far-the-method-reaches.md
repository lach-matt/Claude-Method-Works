That edge is worth naming plainly, because it is where the method stops. The method reaches everything that can be observed: it says what a thing is, completely, and where it must have been between two observations. It does not say what a thing will become — that is prediction, and this book does not claim it. What lies past the last observation, and what lies past the thirteenth index, are bounded and not built, named and not crossed. Definition comes from observing a thing from outside it — the principle Schrödinger and Löwdin worked from long before this book, arrived at here from the inside, by counting. The method works up to that boundary and reports it honestly rather than reaching across it. That is not the subject's weakness. It is the oldest discipline in it.
