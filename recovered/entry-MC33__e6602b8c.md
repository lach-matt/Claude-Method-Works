### The fourteenth axis, bounded from inside

**The tower stops at thirteen because the physics stopped being supplied, not because the construction failed, and what lies beyond can be bounded without being built — the bound a bracket, and now a sharp one. Read the surplus directly: at each stage the bits the ambient box could carry, log₂ of the box, against the bits the object needs, log₂ of the cells, gives 12.75 against 9.93 at Λ₈ and 25.51 against 17.60 at Λ₁₃, a surplus rising 2.82, 4.06, 5.45, 5.61, 6.23, 7.91 while fill falls 14.12 % to 0.42 %. The falling is a law, not a pattern. A constrained coordinate has mean multiplicity strictly below its own value count — if it did not it would be free and carry no constraint — so each axis multiplies the ambient box by more than it multiplies the object, and fill is monotone decreasing by construction, bounded below by zero. Monotone and bounded gives convergence: the sequence of fills converges to a limit L in [0, fill(Λ₁₃)] = [0, 0.416801 %]. And the value of that limit is exactly what the construction cannot decide. The admissibility criterion this book uses — a new coordinate is admissible when its bounds are lattice morphisms of the stage below, which is what keeps the enlarged index closed — permits continuations of Λ₁₃ built from the tower's own kinds of axis that reach different limits: a chain of caps drives fill to zero, a repeated single cap settles it at 14,880 / 47,775,744 = 0.031146 %, and a translated cap carries it arbitrarily close to fill(Λ₁₃) from below without ever attaining it. So the bracket is tight at both ends and right-open: zero is attained and 0.416801 % is the supremum and is not, since the constrained fourteenth axis has ratio strictly below one. Two such continuations share their fourteenth stage exactly — 468,530 cells — and part only at the fifteenth, which is why the fourteenth stage does not know which limit it is heading for and why no finite number of built stages could fix it. What decides L is the one clause every admissible continuation lacks by construction: a bound that derives from a law independent of the index. That law is the physics that stopped at thirteen. The existence of the limit is the law's; its value is the world's. This is what an index can say about a coordinate it does not have: it cannot name the fourteenth axis, since ℛ never produces a coordinate (§32.1.4), but it can bound the direction and prove the bound unimprovable from inside — and a null sits at the open end, definable in this language, since the empty index satisfies every criterion in this book: ℛ(∅) = ∅ so E(∅) = 0 and it is closed, Q(∅) = ∅ so it is complete by the completeness predicate, strictly stronger; it has zero join-irreducibles and takes zero bits. E(X) = 0 is therefore necessary for completeness and not sufficient for content — every closure test in this book passes on nothing at all — and what distinguishes Λ from the empty index is not closure but its seventeen join-irreducibles and its bits of surplus.**

*the bits carried and needed as log₂ of box and of cells, and the fills, at the caps of §7.4, (n, e, ℓ, k, f) = (3, 3, 1, 3, 1); the convergence a deduction from monotonicity and boundedness; the limit's value proved undecidable from morphism-admissibility by three admissible continuations of Λ₁₃ reaching 0, 0.031146 % and the supremum, all closed at E = 0; the empty index's closure and completeness by definition, and |J(Λ)| = 17 measured.*

> **Prior art: that a monotone bounded sequence converges is the monotone convergence theorem, Weierstrass's foundational analysis; that an infinite product of factors below one tends to zero exactly when the sum of their deficits diverges is the standard product criterion; that a bound preserving a lattice's meets and joins keeps the enlarged relation a sublattice — the admissibility criterion here — is Birkhoff (1940), the closure test resting on Baker & Pixley (1975); a finite distributive lattice is determined by its join-irreducibles, of which the empty lattice has none, Birkhoff (1937); bits as logarithms of counts, Shannon (1948). That the tower's fill provably converges while its value is provably undecidable from the construction's own admissibility criterion — the bracket [0, 0.416801 %] tight and right-open, zero attained and the supremum not, two continuations sharing a fourteenth stage and parting at the fifteenth — so that the limit's existence is the law's and its value the world's, is this book's.**
