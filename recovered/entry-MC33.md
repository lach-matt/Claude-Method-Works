### The fourteenth axis, bounded from inside

**The tower stops at thirteen because the physics stopped being supplied, not because the construction failed, and what lies beyond can be bounded without being built — the bound a bracket, not a value. Read the surplus directly: at each stage the bits the ambient box could carry, log₂ of the box, against the bits the object needs, log₂ of the cells, gives 12.75 against 9.93 at Λ₈ and 25.51 against 17.60 at Λ₁₃, a surplus rising 2.82, 4.06, 5.45, 5.61, 6.23, 7.91 while fill falls 14.12 % to 0.42 %. The falling is a law, not a pattern. A constrained coordinate has mean multiplicity strictly below its own value count — if it did not it would be free and carry no constraint — so each axis multiplies the ambient box by more than it multiplies the object, and fill is monotone decreasing by construction, bounded below by zero. Monotone and bounded gives convergence, and that is the whole of what can be deduced: the sequence converges, its limit lies in [0, 0.42 %], and that the limit is zero does not follow. The stage-to-stage ratios are 0.424, 0.383, 0.893, 0.652, 0.312, not themselves monotone, so nothing in the construction forbids them approaching one and the limit settling above zero; the existence of the limit comes from the law and survives, its value would come from the pattern and does not (§22.1.2, on the tower's own tail). This is what an index can say about a coordinate it does not have: it cannot name the fourteenth axis, since ℛ never produces a coordinate (§32.1.4), but it can bound the direction — an object occupying less and less of its own ambient box while carrying more and more surplus, in the limit a vanishing fraction of the space it is written in defending itself with unbounded redundancy. Whether that limit is a null or merely a small number is exactly the question the construction leaves open, and leaving it open is the correct answer, not a gap. And a null is definable in this language, worth stating where the tower ends: the empty index satisfies every criterion in this book — ℛ(∅) = ∅ so E(∅) = 0 and it is closed, Q(∅) = ∅ so it is complete by the completeness predicate, strictly stronger; it has zero join-irreducibles and takes zero bits. So E(X) = 0 is necessary for completeness and not sufficient for content — every closure test in this book passes on nothing at all — and what distinguishes Λ from the empty index is not closure but its seventeen join-irreducibles and its bits of surplus. The boundary is real, it is reachable from inside, and it is a bracket the method declines to collapse.**
