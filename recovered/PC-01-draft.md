### The multipole is fixed by the orbital jump and the parity, and nothing else the index carries

**Whether a photon connects two configurations is decided by its multipolarity, and the multipole is fixed by two things a transition carries in Λ — the change in orbital angular momentum and the parity of the jump — with the change in total J, which would ordinarily complete the rule, unavailable at any level of the tower. The assignment is the lowest multipole whose parity matches: Δℓ = 0 gives a magnetic dipole M1, Δℓ = 1 an electric dipole E1, Δℓ = 2 an electric quadrupole E2, Δℓ = 3 an octupole E3. It is a one-electron jump read off the source and target subshells, and it refuses rather than guesses when |Δℓ| exceeds three, no multipole being assigned. What the tower cannot supply is |ΔJ|, because Λ builds J on the target side only and carries no source J — so the finer |ΔJ| ≤ multipole-order half of the electric-dipole rule, which needs both endpoints' J, is simply not expressible here, and the map states exactly the part the coordinates support and no more. That the part it states is right was checked before it was used: the assignment was run against nine textbook classifications and reproduced each, and was shown able to refuse — the two conditions §4.6 asks of any rule, that it agree where agreement is known and be capable of returning a failure. On the base index only M1 and E1 occur, since the caps hold |Δℓ| ≤ 1 there; the quadrupole and octupole assignments and the refusal appear as the caps widen, exercised where the orbital jump can reach them.**

*the multipole assignment Δℓ → {0: M1, 1: E1, 2: E2, 3: E3}, lowest matching parity, read from Δℓ = f − ℓ on Λ₉; |Δℓ| ≤ 1 on the base build so only M1 (814 cells) and E1 (840 cells) occur, E2/E3 and the |Δℓ| > 3 refusal exercised at wider caps; the nine-classification validation and the refusal test carried from the record, at the caps of §7.4, (n, e, ℓ, k, f) = (3, 3, 1, 3, 1).*

Computed — M §12.11.8, §4.6; Laporte 1924; Condon & Shortley 1935.

> **Prior art: that the multipole order of a radiative transition is fixed by the change in orbital angular momentum and the parity is Laporte, Z. Phys. 23 (1924) 135, and the full multipole classification is Condon & Shortley, The Theory of Atomic Spectra (1935), ch. IV. That Λ fixes the multipole from Δℓ and parity alone — |ΔJ| being inexpressible because the tower carries no source J — with the map validated against nine classifications and shown able to refuse, is this book's reading of the standard rule.**
