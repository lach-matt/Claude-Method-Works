### The electromagnetic index was inside Λ all along

A cell of Λ is called a transition, and a transition is not an atomic event but an electromagnetic one — whether a photon connects two configurations is settled by multipolarity, parity, and the angular momentum the field carries. None of that is a coordinate of Λ. All of it is a function of Λ's coordinates, and following that function to its end returns a verdict on what the index was calling a transition all along.

Λ's cells give a source (n, ℓ, k, 2S) and a target (e, f, g, 2S′, v, 2J_c, 2K, 2J). There is no source J — the tower builds J on the target side only — so exactly two electromagnetic quantities are available from the coordinates the index carries:

    Δℓ = f − ℓ   at Λ₈        ΔS = 2S′ − 2S   at Λ₉

and |ΔJ| is available at no level of the tower. The multipole is therefore fixed by Δℓ and parity alone — Δℓ = 0 → M1, Δℓ = 1 → E1, Δℓ = 2 → E2, Δℓ = 3 → E3, the lowest whose parity matches — a map validated against nine textbook classifications and shown able to refuse before it was used.

The image is a rectangle. All 1,654 cells of Λ₉ map onto eight:

    multipole   ΔS = 0   ΔS = 1   ΔS = 2   ΔS = 3
    E1             264      342      180       54
    M1             262      337      171       44

E = 0 there, and the zero is worth nothing, because the image is the complete product — 2 × 4 here, 4 × 11 at caps (5,5,3,10,3). This is the vacuous zero named earlier in a new place: a closure test passes on a full box for free. What the full rectangle says is that Δℓ and ΔS are independent in Λ — every multipole occurs with every spin change.

> Λ imposes no electromagnetic constraint at all. 576 of its Λ₉ cells are E1 transitions with ΔS ≠ 0 — intercombination lines, which LS coupling forbids and the index admits.

Half of the missing law can be imposed on the index and half cannot, and a single criterion says which in advance: a rule may be added with closure surviving exactly when its set is convex in range.

    rule               as a set          convex in range?   imposed on Λ₉
    ΔS = 0, spin       σ⁻¹({0})          yes                526 cells, E = 0
    |Δℓ| = 1, parity   δ⁻¹({−1, +1})     no — hole at 0     840 cells, E = 750

The spin rule holds at four cap settings — 1,654, 2,664, 44,153 and 60,164 cells, E = 0 at each. The parity rule is the fourth instance of the second excluded form of §12.11.2, and the first for which the criterion also names the repair that would work: any reparametrisation moving the hole to an endpoint. [MC-36]

It is a quotient, not an extension. Adjoining the electromagnetic coordinates to Λ₉ as new axes gives E = 3,900 against E(Λ₉) = 0 — Theorem 17.1 in the direction that matters, since every one of them is a function of coordinates Λ already carries, and a derived coordinate cannot improve closure. And it is not composability either. The transit condition 2S′ ≤ g admits 1,169 cells; the electromagnetic predicate and the composability predicate share 0.0004 bits of a possible 0.633. One asks whether a target is a legal source; the other asks whether a photon connects them. They are near-independent predicates on the same cells — and the book had been calling the first one a transition. [MC-37]

The same trade appears once more, measured across four coupling schemes rather than two. The tower as built uses jK. The other three standard schemes — LS, LK, jj — rebuild from their recoupling chains, and two reproduce their stated Λ₁₃ counts from the chain alone: jK 199,130 and LK 341,150. LS and jj depend on a looseness convention that is named and not printed, and two committed readings of it bracket the stated figures rather than fixing them: LS ∈ [383,065, 597,325] ∋ 431,050 and jj ∈ [160,380, 244,060] ∋ 206,520. The chains are standard — NIST states LK and jK in exactly these forms, and Cowan and Andrew (JOSA 55, 502, 1965) treat these four as the four types of pure coupling for two-electron configurations, a precedent for the set and not only for its members.

> The exact J multiset is identical in all four schemes, in every configuration tested — fifteen of fifteen, computed independently through the jK, LS and jj chains.

A coupling scheme is a basis change; the atoms do not move. So the four cell counts, spanning a factor of 2.17, price four routes to one object — the same finding the path-dependence section reached one level down, here read across schemes instead of across recoupling orders. The privileged route exists because the outer electron's spin is ½: the final triangle degenerates to the mutual bound |2J − 2K| ≤ 1, admissible on both sides and closed, losing only the parity point 2J = 2K. The smallest quantum of angular momentum is the largest the constraint language can hold exactly — and the route the language prefers is jK, the scheme the atoms realise at high n, for the same structural reason: the weak coupling goes last. [MC-38] [Interpretation, not theorem.]

This is the tower's last word on its own cells. It built J on the target, priced every coupling axis, and then turned the finished index on the one question it had assumed an answer to — what makes a transition — and found that the index carries the electromagnetic law as a function of its coordinates without imposing it, admits the lines LS forbids, and can be made to enforce exactly the half of the law that is convex. The transition was electromagnetic all along; the index simply had not been asked.
