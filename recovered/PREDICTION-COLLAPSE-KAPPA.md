## 2 · THE INDICATOR

Define, with no fitted quantity and no measured input:

    κ(n, ℓ, Z)  =  D_field(n, ℓ, Z)  /  ( −1 / (2n²) )

κ is a pure ratio of the field's own channel depth to the hydrogenic depth of the same n at unit
net charge. **κ = 1 ⟺ the channel is uncollapsed** (δ = 0, no penetration, not in the field).
**κ > 1 ⟺ the channel has collapsed** into the core region and is a genuine competitor.

The −1/(2n²) is not a fitted constant and not a measured one. It is the Coulomb eigenvalue at
Z_eff = 1, which is what a neutral atom presents to an electron outside all of its charge. It
enters as a **denominator, not a parameter**.

## 3 · THE CLAUSES

s50 left one open physics object: the tie-break clause fails at exactly three elements — La(57),
Ac(89), Th(90) — all f-openings, and what is owed is a collapse condition **from the field** that
predicts which f channel is uncollapsed at which Z. If κ is that condition, then:

**PK-1 — κ(4f, 57) ≈ 1 AND κ(4f, 58) > 1.** At La the 4f channel has not collapsed, so it is not
in the field and cannot take the electron; at Ce it has, and it does. Falsified if 4f at 57 is
already well above hydrogenic, or if 4f at 58 is still at hydrogenic.

**PK-2 — κ(5f, 89) ≈ 1 AND κ(5f, 90) ≈ 1 AND κ(5f, 91) > 1.** The same event at the 5f opening,
and it must hold at **both** Ac and Th, because the tie-break fails at both and succeeds at 91.
Falsified by 5f being collapsed at 89 or 90, or uncollapsed at 91.

**PK-3 — κ > 1 AT EVERY STEP WHERE THE TIE-BREAK CLAUSE HOLDS.** Over the 107 sealed rows, at
every step where the tie-break was tested and passed, the winning channel was already collapsed.
This is the converse and it is the load-bearing half: without it κ separates nothing.
Falsified by any passing step whose entrant sits at κ ≈ 1.

**PK-4 — THE COLLAPSE IS SHARP IN Z, NOT GRADUAL.** κ for a given channel jumps from ≈1 to
substantially above 1 within one or two protons rather than drifting up over ten. Falsified by a
slow monotone climb through the region of interest.

## 4 · WHAT WOULD FOLLOW IF PK-1..PK-4 HOLD

The tie-break clause would stop being a rule with three exceptions and become a rule with a
**stated domain**: *lower n first among equal n+ℓ, over the channels that are in the field.* The
three failures would then not be failures of the rule but the rule being applied outside its
domain — and κ, computed from the field alone with no empirical input, would say where that
domain begins. **That is the object item 3 owes.**
