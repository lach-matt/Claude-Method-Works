## The passage

**On priority for *V* = 4ν/3.**

We have not been able to establish that this ratio is new, and we state the
limits of the search rather than the strength of the claim.

*V* = 4ν/3 is two lines of algebra from *T* = *Z*²*R*/ν², in a field worked
continuously since 1885. The prior expectation that nobody wrote it down
should be low. Against that, we can report the following.

**Ritz (1908) does not contain it.** We read the *Astrophysical Journal*
version in full. It gives the series formula ν = *A* − *N*/[*m* + α + β(*A*−ν)]²,
the combination principle, the derivation of new series from known ones, and
Ritz's magnetic-atom model. There is no error bound of any kind. It does
contain the qualitative ancestor: Ritz observes that computing differences and
sums of observed wave-numbers is more accurate than working through fitted
constants — the principle the bracket formalises, unquantified.

**Martin (1980) does not contain it.** Its deliverable is a set of formulas
predicting one-electron levels to a stated ±0.03 cm⁻¹ — a flat figure, not a
ν-dependent ratio.

**Four documents remain unchecked.** They are named here so that a reader with
library access can settle the question:

| document | why it matters |
|---|---|
| Edlén, *Handbuch der Physik* XXVII (1964), §on series formulas | Edlén wrote the standard account; an elementary ratio would plausibly appear as an unremarked aside. Chapter is in English despite the German series title. |
| Ritz, *Physikalische Zeitschrift* **9**, 521 (1908) | Two pages longer than the ApJ version, containing the "further particulars" Ritz promises in a footnote. Reprinted in *Œuvres* (1911), ~pp. 142–150; platform e-rara.ch. |
| Paschen & Götze, *Seriengesetze der Linienspektren* (Springer, 1922) | Book-length treatment of series laws by the two people who measured most of the underlying spectra. |
| Dunz, *Seriengesetze der Linienspektra* (Leipzig, 1911) | The earliest complete compilation of series, chronologically closest to Ritz. |

**We assess the probability that *V* = 4ν/3 is novel at approximately 75%**,
and the residual is concentrated in those four documents rather than spread
across a century.

**One structural argument supports the higher end.** The quantity has no use in
a fitting paradigm. One computes bracket-width-over-interpolation-error only if
one is constructing brackets, and the literature is uniformly predictive. That
is a reason for absence rather than a failure to find.

**And one consideration cuts the other way.** *V* = 4ν/3 could have been
written down and forgotten, in a book nobody cites, by an author who saw no use
for it. That is precisely what the four documents are.

## Why state it this way

A paper that names its own unchecked prior art is in a stronger position than
one that claims priority and is corrected. The four documents are a finite,
specified task; anyone who completes it either confirms the result or improves
the historical record, and both outcomes are better than an unexamined claim.

The same disposition applies to the other novelty assessments in this work:

| claim | assessed | basis |
|---|---|---|
| *V* = 4ν/3 | ~75% | nine searches, Ritz read in full, four documents open |
| ν_V ceiling | ~75% | very specific form, not found |
| cost surface, pole at *p* = 1 | ~50% | the framing is new; the content is elementary |
| bracketing rather than predicting | ~85% | literature uniformly predictive |
| E(X) as external definition cost | — | 𝓡 is a closure operator, so E is a **closure defect** — a known object applied to a question nobody had asked it |

**E(X) is deliberately not claimed as novel.** Closure operators, Galois
connections and formal-concept analysis are established mathematics. What is
offered is the application: computing the closure defect of an index, and
finding that the periodic table's is 36 while Λ's is 0.
