### 18.6.1 What that means for the five indices this book keeps

| index | E(X) | predictions available | and they are |
|---|---|---|---|
| the periodic table | 36 | 36 | **all impossible** |
| the calendar | 7 | 7 | **all impossible** |
| a subnet with a hole | 8 | 8 | **all impossible** |
| **the box ordering, and Λ** | **0** | **0** | — |
| **the bibliography** | **6** | **6** | **all occupiable — two already named** |

 **Every prediction the first three can make is wrong.** Period-1 group-2 and its thirty-five kin. The thirty-first of February and its six. Λ can propose nothing at all. **The fifth row is the exception, and it is the only place in this book where a prediction is both available and true.**

 **The bibliography, indexed on era, retrieval redundancy ρ, access and depth of entry, holds twenty-two sources over seven cells and admits six it does not carry.** Under exhaustive relabelling E falls from six to two and never to zero, so by §16.7.1 it is irreducible in the same sense as the asteroid belt and unlike the periodic table, whose thirty-six vanish under Janet. **The two survivors are a born-digital literature at ρ ≥ 4 read only in abstract, and a digitised pre-digital source read in full and entered once.** Both are occupiable and both name a route, which is exactly what the belt's absences do not: resonance strength enters at *e*^|p−q|, so the belt's empty cells are empty because nothing can sit in them.

    Closure abolishes prediction. It does not abolish the bibliography,
    because a bibliography is never closed — E(search) > 0 always, by §27.1,
    and the cells it admits are documents that exist.

 **Which gives §16.7.1 a third verdict it did not have.** That section sorts a defect into *an artefact of the layout*, removable by re-coordinatisation, and *a fact about the world*, surviving every one. The bibliography is the second kind and its cells are still fillable, so the two verdicts are not exhaustive:

| the defect | reaches zero? | its predictions |
|---|---|---|
| the periodic table | **yes**, under Janet | impossible — the layout's fault |
| the asteroid belt | **no**, 2 → 1 | impossible — nothing can occupy them |
| **the bibliography** | **no**, 6 → 2 | **possible, and unfilled** |

 **Irreducible does not mean unoccupiable.** An index can carry a defect no re-coordinatisation removes whose cells are nevertheless real and empty for want of work — and that is the only shape in which this method predicts anything.
