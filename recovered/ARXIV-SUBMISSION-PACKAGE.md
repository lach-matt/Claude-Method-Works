## 4. ABSTRACT (paste — plain text, trimmed to arXiv conventions, ~1650 chars)
In 1969 P.-O. Löwdin challenged theoretical physics to derive the ordering rules of the periodic table -- the Madelung (n+l) filling rule, the period-length sequence 2, 8, 8, 18, 18, 32, 32, and the Aufbau principle -- directly from the many-electron Schrödinger equation, with no empirical parameters. This paper presents a solution. A sequential, parameter-free construction places the electron differentiating element Z from Z-1 in the self-consistent field of the preceding atom; the orbital channel it occupies is determined by that field alone. Executed with the inverse fine-structure constant c = 137.035999 as the only number supplied, the construction reproduces the observed filling order at all 107 elements with known ground configurations (Z = 2-108), derives the three genuine exceptions to the secondary rule (La, Ac, Th) from the physics of orbital collapse, shows that no g block exists below Z = 121, and demonstrates that the observed table is irreducibly relativistic: at c -> infinity the same construction misplaces eleven elements, silver and mercury among them. Second-order correlation corrections, computed at every closely contested element, widen rather than overturn the derived order. Beyond the last measured element the construction issues twelve falsifiable predictions (Z = 109-120) reproducing the relativistic Dirac-Fock sequence. The derivation is given in both mathematical and algorithmic form; every supporting analysis was pre-registered under a cryptographic hash protocol; and the identity resolving the numerical method's final internal question is Löwdin's own non-orthogonality formula of 1950.
