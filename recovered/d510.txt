
 D.5.10 The re-closure owed for the mathematics of the Löwdin challenge
 §D.5.9 closed with a question: the mathematics of Chapter 34 (registers 1249–1357) is indexed in the
 Mathematical Compendium under THE MATHEMATICS OF THE LÖWDIN WORK, which ends by naming twelve things
 the work introduces, and none had been entered here. §D.5.3 says what an element is, and each of the
 twelve is a stated mathematical object with a stated verification; so they are elements, and this is
 the re-closure. The sixty-five of §D.5.9 were rebuilt from §D.5.2–§D.5.9 and closed under §D.3's two
 constraints before anything was added: E = 0 in all twenty-two fibres. Every fibre and coordinate
 below is read from the compendium's own entry — what it calls standard, what it calls this work's,
 and how it says the thing was checked.

 **Sixty-five elements become seventy-seven over twenty-four fibres, and E = 0 — after the index named
 one cell and the cell was filled by reading one entry honestly.** Two fibres open for the first time,
 measurement · analysis and formula · order, and each closes trivially. The first run gave **E = 1** in
 theorem · analysis, at *verified · exhaustive · none found*: the corridor had been entered with
 precedent *found*, because the compendium names Fourier 1826 and Motzkin 1936 for the solution of a
 linear system in one variable. But that is not the element. The compendium's own division puts the
 method under *standard* and puts under *this work* the two things the list names — that the
 coefficients are node counts, so the endpoints lie in ℚ(√ℕ) and take nineteen values, and that all
 106 intervals are non-empty. A precedent for the method is not a precedent for the consistency, and
 the search found none. Entered on what it is, the corridor occupies the cell the structure had
 named, and the fibre closes. That is the third time this appendix has closed by correcting a
 coordinate of its own — §D.5.6 by proving a thing that was sampled, §D.5.8 by admitting a thing was
 sampled, here by attributing a thing to the work that did it.

 The observability boundary is the second element to enter as *conjectured* (§D.5.7's slack = kernel
 was the first): one confirming instance, falsifiable, tested once — the compendium says so in those
 words, and *conjectured · sampled* is what those words are. Law · physics now holds four elements over
 three cells and closes; the box it spans admits *conjectured · exhaustive* and *proved · sampled*, and
 §6.1's bounds exclude both, which is the closure doing its work rather than the count.
