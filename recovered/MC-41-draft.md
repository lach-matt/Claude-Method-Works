### The most active constraint is the coupling itself

**Of the seven bounds that cut Λ from its box, one does more work than any other, and it is the one that makes the object an index of transitions rather than of states. Rank the constraints by how many otherwise-admissible cells each removes — the cells that satisfy all six of the others and fail only this one — and g ≤ q leads at 673, ahead of q ≤ k at 575, k ≤ 4ℓ+2 at 564, ℓ ≤ n−1 at 308, 2S ≤ k at 300, f ≤ e−1 at 200, and the two that barely bind, k ≥ 1 at 25 and g ≤ 4f+2 at 24. The measure is marginal by design: a constraint's activity is the exclusion no other constraint would make in its absence, so the count is over cells that clear every other bound. And the constraint that binds hardest is not one of the hydrogenic or Pauli caps that shape a single subshell but the one bound that ties the two ends of the transition together — g ≤ q reads placed ≤ removed, the electrons arriving at the target cannot outnumber those the source gave up, and it is the only bound in the whole definition relating a target coordinate to a source coordinate. That is why it is the most active and why it is the coupling: remove it and the two ends of the tree fall apart into an index of source configurations beside an index of target configurations, a product of states; keep it and the object is a set of moves, each cell a transition conserving what it transfers. The constraint that does the most cutting is the one that makes Λ a cylinder over the transfer rather than a rectangle of states, so most active and is the coupling are the same fact about the same bound.**

*the seven constraints ranked by marginal exclusion — cells passing all others and failing only this one — over the value box of Λ₈, g ≤ q first at 673 with q ≤ k 575, k ≤ 4ℓ+2 564, ℓ ≤ n−1 308, 2S ≤ k 300, f ≤ e−1 200, k ≥ 1 25 and g ≤ 4f+2 24, all measured on the rebuilt Λ₈ at the caps of §7.4, (n, e, ℓ, k, f) = (3, 3, 1, 3, 1); that g ≤ q is the sole target-to-source bound is read from the constraint graph.*

Proved — M §13.3; Birkhoff 1940.

> **Prior art: the count of cells a single inequality removes from a product of chains is a direct enumeration, and that a lattice is a cylinder over a base exactly when one coordinate relation binds its two ends is the structure theory of graded posets in Birkhoff's sense (1940). That g ≤ q is the most active of Λ's seven constraints — excluding 673 otherwise-admissible cells, more than any other — and that this most-active bound is precisely the coupling, the one relation between a target and a source coordinate whose removal collapses the index of transitions into a product of states, is this book's.**
