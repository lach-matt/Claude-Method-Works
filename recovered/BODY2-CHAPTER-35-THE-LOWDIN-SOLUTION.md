## 35. The Löwdin solution

*Chapter 34 stated the law that names the order — the corridor, the walk, and the
entry point, read from the table's own coordinates with one observation per
subshell. This chapter states the other half and the harder one: the same order
derived from the many-electron Schrödinger equation with no observation at all.
The challenge posed in 1969 is closed. The formal statement is a companion paper,
"The Löwdin Solution"; register entries 1701–1712 carry the record. This chapter
states what was delivered and — because the method is the subject — how the method
itself did the work.*

### The problem, finished

**Chapter 6 proved the periodic table is not a closed index, and Chapter 27 said
what that costs:** *what a closure cannot supply is exactly what requires an
operator.* Chapter 34 then measured the consequence — with no carried state, 104
of 106 steps admit two to four self-consistent subshells, so **the table is not
computable from a single atom's configuration.** The solution is the operator
those three chapters were pointing at.

**One number goes in.** The many-electron Hamiltonian in its scalar-relativistic
reduction, with c = 137.035999 as the only entered constant. **The operator is a
chained walk:** at each Z, converge the self-consistent field of the ion carrying
the configuration of Z−1 — the field the arriving electron actually meets — solve
every frontier channel in that frozen field, and let the deepest channel be the
entrant. The configuration so built seeds the next step. *The carried state that
Chapter 34 proved necessary is here carried by the equation itself.*

### What the walk returned

> **107 of 107.** Across Z = 2–108 — every element with a measured ground
> configuration — the derived entrant sequence matches nature at every step.
> A channel of smaller n+ℓ opens before a channel of larger n+ℓ without
> exception; at equal n+ℓ, smaller n first, except at exactly La, Ac and Th.

**The exceptions are derived, not excused.** All three are f-openings, and the
field states the collapse condition — the double-well f potential and the Z at
which its inner well captures the electron. At the three exception rows the f
channel is still outer-well and barely bound; the d channel is deeper; the walk
takes d, and so does nature. *The failures of the tie-break are the collapse
transition's exact footprint, three elements wide.*

**The absences are derived too.** Every g channel the walk ever offers — 5g at 65
elements, 6g at 70, 7g at 57, 8g at 28 — sits at its hydrogenic depth −1/(2n²) to
the storage precision, across a hundred protons, while every s, p, d and f channel
beside it moves with Z. **A channel that does not respond to the nucleus is not in
the field.** There is no g block below Z = 121, and the table did not have to be
consulted to know it.

**Correlation widens every contested step.** At the five elements where the
mean-field competition is genuinely close (Z = 38, 56, 72, 89, 105), the full
second-order correlation differential is positive — +1.33, +1.24, +2.6, +2.2,
+2.16 in units of the mean-field margin. *No contested row reverses; every one
widens.*

**And the table is relativistic, visibly.** The identical walk at c → ∞ misplaces
eleven elements — Mn, Zn, Ag, Cd, Nd, Pm, Sm, Lu, Hg, Lr, Rf. Silver and mercury
are misfiled by the non-relativistic equation. *The wall chart is not a solution
of the Schrödinger equation without light in it.*

**Beyond the last measured element, the walk keeps writing** — twelve rows,
Z = 109–120, entrants 6d then 7p then 8s, margins 0.058 to 0.264 hartree, the
worst-case spin-orbit narrowing 0.083 hartree clearing every one. *They are
published as what they are in this book's own vocabulary: unwitnessed. Not
undefected for want of trying — they await a measurement science has yet to
provide.*

### The silence, answered

**Chapter 34 ended on a silence and called it a domain statement.** At any f
opening the node count p = n−ℓ−1 hits its floor, no rival lies below, L = −∞, and
the corridor's test does not exist. *The missing test is forbidden by the node
count, not by missing data.*

**The solution walked into exactly that silence and found the physics standing in
it.** The rows where the corridor's law cannot speak — the f openings — are the
rows of orbital collapse, and the collapse condition is precisely what decides
them. The three tie-break exceptions live there. The finest numerical point of the
whole record lives there too: at Z = 91 a diagnostic sign sits below
self-consistent-field tolerance, and the literature says why — beside a collapse,
the mean-field equations hold **two coexisting solutions of one configuration**,
each a stationary point with its own converged operator. A sign pinned at
tolerance beside a two-branch degeneracy is not noise to be computed harder; it is
the branch structure, and the record enters it as derived.

**Where a language falls silent, the silence names the object.** Chapter 33 said
it of the six languages; Chapter 34 enacted it at the f corridor. The solution
completes the figure: *the silence was not only the finding — it was the address.*

### How the method did it

*This is the section the book exists for.*

**§2.14 became a cryptographic instrument.** *State the number before the
interpretation* was earned in this book one failure at a time. In the solution it
hardened into protocol: every quantitative claim was preceded by a prediction
file — the inequality, its direction, its tolerance — bound by a hash before any
arithmetic ran. **At no point in the record does a number exist before the
prediction that constrains it.** That is §2.14 with teeth, and it is the
solution's answer to "could this have been fitted."

**The audits learned to read the artefact.** *An audit that reads the wrong
artefact will pass anything* — this book paid for that sentence twice. The
solution's instruments therefore carried mandatory failure modes: a deliberate
perturbation under which each must visibly break, exercised on the real
computation path. And every archive was reopened from its own sealed copy and
re-verified before a session touched it. **The audit reads the object, the
source, the artefact and the world outside the work** — the twenty audits'
principle, running nightly on a physics derivation.

**Search before deriving closed the endgame.** §E.5 exists because nine things
were derived here that were already in print. The solution's final residues were
closed by obeying that ledger in advance: when three numerical discrepancies
remained, the ruling was to *pose them as questions and search before any solve.*
All three were found standing in the literature — the method's own defect is the
force-versus-gradient discrepancy Pulay published in 1969; the operator asymmetry
is the non-orthogonality identity **Löwdin himself** published in 1950; the
two-branch f physics is Griffin, Andrew and Cowan, 1969 and 1971. *Three
questions, three answers already in print, no computation spent.* Prior art is not
a tax on originality. It is where the answers were.

**A nothing was defined rather than discarded, twice.** *A relation that cannot
be violated by data is not empty — it is defending something else.* The g
channels' refusal to move is the finding that there is no g block: the null
response is the theorem. And the walk's one persistent internal defect, once
named as a Pulay term, stopped being an error and became a derived object — split
exactly into a rotation and a response, every term closed in a form this book
would recognise: **the energy functional is exactly quartic along a fixed orbital
direction, so five evaluations determine every coefficient with no truncation at
all.** Two entries that began in the fault ledger ended reclassified as
mathematics. *The errors were converted into structure. That is this book's
sentence, and the solution obeyed it.*

**And the class no structure catches was caught the only way it can be.** The
collaborator's note names it: reasoning sound, destination wrong, fixable only by
a second party's refusal. The solution's endgame turned on exactly such
refusals — a ruling that halted computation when computation had become the
comfortable wrong direction, and a review that saw a figure repeating where the
writer saw structure. **Four of this book's results exist because a conclusion
was refused; the solution's closure is the fifth.**
