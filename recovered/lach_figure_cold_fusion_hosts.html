Caption
(A) The conventional two-dimensional periodic table arranges elements by period (n, horizontal) and group (valence configuration, vertical). FCC-structured elements appear scattered across multiple periods and groups with no geometric coherence. The highest-priority cold fusion host, Yb (Z=70), is displaced into the lanthanide strip below the main body — physically separated from the 5d metals (Ir, Pt, Au) with which it shares the key property of high H_cp. The spatial relationship between these elements is entirely invisible in this representation. (B) The Lach (n, ℓ, k) lattice maps the same 118 elements onto three independent quantum-mechanical axes: principal shell n, subshell type ℓ, and electron occupancy k. In this representation, elements cluster by block along the ℓ-axis without manual arrangement. The f-block (pink) and late 5d metals (purple, high-k) form a geometrically contiguous region of high configuration-space distance |Q₀| = √(n²+ℓ²+k²), which is the primary determinant of H_cp. All FCC-structured viable hosts appear in the high-|Q₀| region of the lattice. Yb, the top-ranked host, is immediately visible as the highest-k occupied f-block cell, adjacent to the 5d cluster — a relationship the 2D table cannot express.
