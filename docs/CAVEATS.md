# CAVEATS.md — the site's caveats, reviewed against the evidence (2026-10-17)

plate, in Help, in the console's `caveats` command and in the Ask prompt, and on the plate of every
value they qualify. On 2026-10-17 each of the ten was reviewed. Where a caveat named something wrong,
the evidence was found and the thing was repaired. Where it named something that stays true, it was
measured and stated exactly. Where it named a decision, the record was searched for one. The list is
generated by `tools/webindex.py` (`CAVEATS`). Each entry now carries a `state` and the review date,
and each repaired entry keeps, as `was`, what it said before.

## `above-108` — corrected

**It said** the observed configurations stop at 108 "because measurement does". **That was wrong.**
`LW1-ground.py`'s ground levels are NIST ASD's. On 2026-10-17 NIST's ionisation-energy service listed
neutral spectra through Hs I and none beyond: Mt appears only from Mt V and Ds from Ds VI. The table
stops where that compilation stops. Measurement stops earlier, as the next caveat says. The same
caveat now says that the B column holds 0 throughout the CSV-only rows (see `b-overloaded`).

## `ground-103-108` — new, measured

NIST ASD's energy-levels service was queried for each neutral spectrum from Lr I to Hs I. Each
listing is one level, the ground level, and a limit:

| spectrum | ground level | ionisation energy (NIST's mark) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lr I | 7s²7p ²P°½ | 4.96 eV, measured |
| Rf I | 6d²7s² ³F₂ | (6.02), ab initio |
| Db I | 6d³7s² ⁴F₃⁄₂ | (6.8), ab initio |
| Sg I | 6d⁴7s², J = 0, no term | [7.8], semi-empirical |
| Bh I | 6d⁵7s², J = 5/2, no term | [7.7], semi-empirical |
| Hs I | 6d⁶7s², J = 4, no term | [7.6], semi-empirical |

NIST's help page defines the marks: parentheses for a value from an ab initio calculation, or one not
derived from evaluated experimental data, and brackets for interpolation, extrapolation or another
semi-empirical procedure. For Db to Hs the ground level carries the same reference as the calculated
limit. `populate.GROUND_ONLY` holds these six, and each element record carries them as `ground_basis`.
The element plate shows what the ground level rests on, beside the configuration, which stays `READ`.

## `b-aufbau` — measured to the row

It said the column was built on the withdrawn aufbau table, "patched by hand". Measured
(`python3 tools/populate.py --check-B`):

- On the aufbau table, with n₀ the first entirely unoccupied n, the bound reproduces **all 101,303**
  integer B values with Z ≤ 118. That is plain Madelung order with no exception patched; the hand
  patches register 1306 speaks of did not touch this column.
- On the observed configurations it reproduces 98,583 of the 99,687 rows whose core the observed
  table holds. The **1,104** that differ sit on the cores of La, Pd, Ce, Ac, Th, Gd, Lr, Pa, U, Np
  and Cm.

The site already gives the observed-table B as primary, with the CSV's value `READ` beside it and
every disagreement marked. The CSV is a corpus artefact and is not edited.

## `b-overloaded` — repaired

It said 25 rows carry "a dispersion of the median defect". That was asserted, not measured.
**`tools/seriesdefect.py` measures it**, from NIST's own levels for the nine spectra concerned
(`tables/nist-levels/`, fetched 2026-10-17):

- each row's series is read from its source text (e.g. `NaI 2S n=3-20, 18 members; median defect`);
- the members are the distinct (n, E) of single-valence configurations whose last subshell carries
  the term's ℓ, below the limit and within the printed n range;
- the defect is `n − √(R_M / (limit − E))`, with the limit as printed in cm⁻¹ and R_M the Rydberg
  reduced by the standard atomic weight (²²³Fr's mass for francium, which has none).

**Result: 25 of 25.** The member count and n range, δ as the median and B as the **range**
(max − min) all reproduce to the five decimals printed. With R∞ no δ reproduces. With ¹¹B's mass in
place of boron's standard weight 10.81, boron's ²P° row fails, so the source's mass convention is
fixed as well. Both are in the instrument's selftest as controls.

The same review found a second overload, never noticed before: **all 3,504 rows of Z = 119 and 120
carry B = 0**, which is the bound on neither table. `--check-B` had been reporting them as 1,308 rows
"neither table reproduces". `populate.csv_B_reading()` now names each row `bound`, `range` or `zero`.
`csv_bound()` returns a bound only for the first. The site shows a range as a range (`RECOVERED`) and
a zero as "0, not a bound".

## `lambda8-mapping` — open, and measured

A Λ₈ cell is a transition, so mapping an element to cells is a choice. The review searched the
Register, `DEFERRED.md`, `DOCKET.md` and `RULINGS-R2.md` for one and found none. The mapping stays
`RECONSTRUCTED` and the choice stays the author's. What it does satisfy was measured:

- all **5,778** ladder steps of Z = 1 to 108 satisfy the six of §7.1's seven constraints that can be
  decided without the ion's term;
- **1,107** lie within §7.4's standing caps;
- the seventh constraint, 2S ≤ k, is undecided at every step, because the ion's term is not carried
  and 2S may not be inferred.

## `equation-domain` — repaired

It said the equation is validated in its stated domain and extrapolated outside it, without saying
which channels are which. **Every channel now says**: `populate.channel_domain()`, `DERIVED`, applies
register 1192's four exclusions. A channel is outside the domain where:

- Z > 103;
- the charge is above 10;
- the core is open-shell at 3, 16 or 119 parents, read from the stage's notes;
- the series is unresolved above ng, where nothing in the channel is measured.

`populate.domain_census()` holds the reading against the record:

- register 1192's two largest exclusions reproduce **exactly**, at 24,312 and 61,748 cells;
- the other two are within 1 and 5;
- of the measured rows, the 51 outside are **exactly** register 1193's 51: 24 three-parent cores,
  15 sixteen-parent cores and 12 ions above charge 10;
- the 282 inside that predate the 2026-08-14 fetch give an rms residual of **0.1475**, against the
  0.147 the record gives for the final form.

The details are in `docs/POPULATE.md`, "The equation's domain, per channel".

## `limit-kind` — repaired

It said a series limit "is printed as the csv prints it, with no unit added". The nine limits the CSV
prints are the ground-state ionisation energies NIST prints for B, Na, Al, Ga, In, Cs, Lu, Tl and Fr I,
in cm⁻¹, to every printed digit (NIST's ionisation-energy service, 2026-10-17). The rows' defects
recompute from NIST's levels only in that unit. The site now shows each limit with **cm⁻¹**: the value
is `READ`, the unit `RECOVERED`. The nine are also carried on populate.py's series-limit axis as
`READ`, which takes it from four banked stages to thirteen.

The review of that axis found a fault and repaired it. `limit_deficit()` measured every banked limit
against `Z²R_M`, the one-electron baseline, so B IV and P IV were reported with "deficits" of
−651,301 and −24,274,907 cm⁻¹. It now returns `None` unless the stage is one-electron.

## `n0-reading` — repaired

It said n₀'s reading is `RECONSTRUCTED`, with "first entirely unoccupied n" matching the column at
97.7 %. That figure was measured on the observed configurations, which are not the column's table.
On the table the column was built on (aufbau, above), "first entirely unoccupied n" reproduces
**all 101,303** integer B values with Z ≤ 118, and "first n with room" reproduces 88.86 %. The
reading is measured out of the corpus's own data, so its status is `RECOVERED` (`populate.AXES`,
`n0_of`, the site's status maps, the Pauli solver mode and the glossary).
