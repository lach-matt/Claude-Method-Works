When Charles Janet created his Left-Step Periodic Table in 1928, he designed it to end at **Element 120** (Unbinilium, Z=120). Even though only 92 elements were known at the time, Janet realized that quantum orbital mechanics naturally creates a closed, 120-element shell system.  
Pairing this table with a **disdyakis triacontahedron** (a 120-sided polyhedron, or **d120**) links quantum chemistry directly to 3D geometry.

## **The Quantum Math Behind 120**

In Janet’s Left-Step table, elements are ordered strictly by the filling of electron subshells following the Madelung rule (n \+ l). The periods come in pairs based on orbital blocks (s, p, d, f):

> * **Period 1 & 2 (s-block only):** 2 \+ 2 \= 4 elements (1s, 2s)  
> * **Period 3 & 4 (s, p blocks):** 8 \+ 8 \= 16 elements (2p\\,3s, 3p\\,4s)  
> * **Period 5 & 6 (s, p, d blocks):** 18 \+ 18 \= 36 elements (3d\\,4p\\,5s, 4d\\,5p\\,6s)  
> * **Period 7 & 8 (s, p, d, f blocks):** 32 \+ 32 \= 64 elements (4f\\,5d\\,6p\\,7s, 5f\\,6d\\,7p\\,8s)

\\text{Total Elements} \= 2(1^2 \+ 1^2 \+ 2^2 \+ 2^2 \+ 3^2 \+ 3^2 \+ 4^2 \+ 4^2) \= 120  
Element 120 (8s^2) represents the exact natural boundary where the f-block period sequence completes.
