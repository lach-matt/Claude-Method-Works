# DRAFT for M — the register counts come out of the prose; Part 0 gains a paragraph on the register's purpose. NOTHING APPLIED.

M's ruling, 5 September 2026: *"the register count statements are not necessary to the reader, only the register
citations. … the total register count of entries only needs to be mentioned in the front matter, if mentioned at all.
I'd rather have a paragraph included in the front matter that explains the purpose of our register rather than drawing
specific attention to its numbers."* And on placement: *"it should sit in part 0."*

This replaces the earlier question entirely. The count is not corrected at fifteen sites; it is removed from fourteen
of them, and the fifteenth — the Preface's own "The register." paragraph, which is in Part 0 — becomes the paragraph
M asked for. No number is substituted anywhere.

## 1. The new paragraph, Part 0, Preface (printed page 17)

It replaces the paragraph that now opens *"The register. one thousand six hundred and thirty-five claims made in the
course of this work were wrong and were withdrawn. They are listed in Chapter 28, with what replaced them."* Its last
three sentences are kept verbatim. It also takes the bold lead-in its siblings have — **The claim.**, **The object.**,
**The demonstration.** — which this one alone lacked.

> **The register.** Every correction, withdrawal and finding this work made is entered in a register of its own,
> printed as a separate volume and cited by number throughout this one. It exists because a book that asserts results
> and reports no failures cannot be checked. Its discipline is that a correction never replaces what it corrects: both
> states are kept, and the entry that corrects names the entry corrected, so the record of a mistake survives its
> repair. It is offered as evidence, not as an apology. Several of its entries were caught not by the author but by
> the object — a wrong nuclear charge that the method itself could not see, and two derived quantities that flagged it
> immediately. That episode is the book's best evidence, and it is an account of an error.

## 3. The sixteenth site is left alone, and why

Register entry 1815 quotes the phrase to identify the fifteen sites it deliberately did not touch. It is not a count
statement about the register; it is the record of a deferral, and a seated entry is never edited. It stands. After
this change it reads as history, which is what the Register is for.

## 4. What this also settles, and what it does not

**Settles.** The question put to M on 5 September — whether to reword the noun or print the smaller figure at ten
sites — does not arise: no number is printed. The two sites that needed a ruling are resolved with the rest: page 27's
"Chapter 28 … entries", whose phrase denoted two different objects, loses the count; entry 1815 stands as record.

**Does not settle, and these are recorded not repaired.** Three sites carry a second defect the count is not
responsible for, and removing the number does not reach it:

- **Page 16 and page 17** say the entries are *in* or *listed in* Chapter 28. Chapter 28 itself says, in bold,
  "The entries themselves are not printed here", and routes the reader to the Register volume.
- **Page 217** says one pattern lies behind *all* the failures; the collaborator's own page names three populations.
- **Page 236** says Chapter 28 records errors "found in work of exactly that kind", narrowing to inherited work that
  was not re-run; Chapter 28 records errors across the whole work.

They belong to the prose leg (Phase 3) and are listed so they are not rediscovered.

## 5. How it would be applied

**A guarded build with a Register entry**, not a press-time substitution. A press substitution leaves the source
saying what it said and strips the text only at print; that is right for a build number, and wrong here, because this
is a change of content and the book's rule is that a correction never replaces what it corrects. The Register entry
records both states, so the fifteen sentences as they stand survive their own repair.

**Wanted from M:** the paragraph's wording, and the fourteen replacements. Then one build, one entry, and the reading
copy re-pressed so you can read the result.
