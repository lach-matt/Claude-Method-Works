# DEF-153M — law · physics is proved-unwitnessed, and every mechanical route to closing it is bad.

M's ruling-in-progress: *"if law · physics only closes with observation, then it is
proven-unwitnessed."* Tested four ways at BUILD97. It holds, and the mechanical alternatives are
worse than leaving it open.

## 1. The whole defect is one element

    all four elements, as printed                     cells 3  E = 3
    WITHOUT the observability boundary                cells 2  **E = 0**
    without LS.law (the ordering law)                 cells 2  E = 2
    without the domain prohibition                    cells 3  E = 3
    without rival = donor iff not full                cells 3  E = 3

Remove the observability boundary and the fibre closes. Remove anything else and it does not move.
The boundary's cell `verified · sampled · found` is the odd one in its fibre: every other element is
`exhaustive`, and two of the three are `none found`.

## 2. What the fibre wants is a witness, and only a witness

With the boundary's verification made exhaustive, E falls 3 -> 1 and one want remains:

    holds   proved · exhaustive · none found     (LS.law, the ordering law)
    wants   proved · exhaustive · found

Same status. Same verification. **They differ on precedent alone.** §D.5.2 reads that coordinate as
*"a record of the search, not of the world — none found means this work found none"*, and M has
ruled that outside attribution is what witness means here. So the fibre is proved, exhaustive over
everything the index reaches, and wanting only observation — which is verbatim §D.2's definition of
**unwitnessed**. M's inference is confirmed by measurement, not by argument.

**And the witness it wants is one the book's thesis says cannot exist.** The only `proved` element
in the fibre is LS.law. For the cell to fill, LS.law would need a precedent found — that is, the
Löwdin challenge would have to have been answered before. Chapter 35 exists because it was not.
Closing this fibre through LS.law would falsify the achievement it indexes.

## 3. Re-fibring closes it only by putting the element where it does not belong

The observability boundary moved to each of the other twenty-three fibres, E recomputed everywhere:

    eight hosts close all 24     definition · analysis, formula · combinatorics, formula · order,
                                 formula · physics, measurement · algebraic geometry,
                                 method · combinatorics, theorem · algebraic geometry,
                                 theorem · complexity

Not one of those is a home for it. And the one semantically defensible candidate —
`measurement · physics`, since §D.5.10 describes the boundary as a survey across eleven closed
indexes returning *"nine of eleven are expectation values"*, which is a count and not a law —
gives **E = 2**. The honest re-fibring fails; only the dishonest ones succeed.

That is §D.5.5's own warning instantiated: *"Fibre finely enough and every fibre holds one cell,
which is a box, and E = 0 says nothing."* Re-fibring to close is the fault the section names, and
this measurement shows what it would cost here.

## 4. So the routes, ranked by what they claim

    A  record the fibre as UNWITNESSED          claims least; true as measured; M's move
    B  prove the observability boundary          closes it honestly; nobody has
    C  make its verification exhaustive          E 3 -> 1; leaves the witness want standing
    D  re-fibre it                               closes only into fibres it does not belong to
    E  reorder §D.2's axes                       ruled out at DEF-153K: 0 of 8,640 orderings

**A is what the evidence supports.** It is also the first use of the sixth value on a fibre's
closure rather than on a row, which is a question for M: §D.2 defines `status` for an ELEMENT, and
this would extend it to the closure of a fibre. Recorded, not taken.
