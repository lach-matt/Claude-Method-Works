
## Chat 153D --- the withdrawn-law repair broke a controlled vocabulary in Appendix D, and this one goes to M (staged loose; seat at the next close, then delete)

1. **The finding, MEASURED.** Appendix D's element table carries a **coordinates** column with a controlled grammar — `status · verification · precedent` — drawn from three closed vocabularies: status ∈ {withdrawn, conjectured, measured, verified, proved}, verification ∈ {cited, sampled, exhaustive}, precedent ∈ {none found, found}. **42 rows obey it. Exactly three do not, and they are the three `r3-wl2` rewrote** under R3-CLASS-WL.md item 5: L10858 *verified as a result about the form (1445, 1463)*, L10859 *withdrawn with ν at 1460*, L10864 *stated at 1332; not independently reproducible (1448)*.
2. **This is not a shift artefact and not an instrument fault.** `r2-23a` does not mis-target: its ranges come from `drange`, which resolves by `lettered` and the next heading and follows the shift correctly. It **crashes** — `ValueError: not enough values to unpack (expected 3, got 1)` in `coords()` — because it parses that column against the vocabulary and the three rows no longer parse. The instrument is right and the volume is what changed.
3. **The wording is M's, approved at chat 128, and it was approved as WORDING.** R3-CLASS-WL.md item 5 gave those three status columns in prose. Nothing in the class file records that the column is a controlled vocabulary shared with 42 other rows and read by a seated instrument, so the consequence was not before M when he approved it. **The repair is faithful to the approval; the approval did not know this.**
4. **Not repaired. This is an editorial decision and it belongs to M** (§1). The three routes, with their costs:
   - **(a) Re-express within the grammar.** The surds row is the clean case: *withdrawn* is already a legal status, so `withdrawn · exhaustive · none found` says exactly what M approved in the table's own language, with register 1460 cited where the table puts citations. L10858 (*verified*, qualified) and L10864 (*stated at 1332*) have no exact legal form — *stated* is not in the status vocabulary at all.
   - **(b) Extend the vocabulary** to admit the new statuses, and seat a successor to `r2-23a` that parses them. This changes what the table can say, in all 45 rows, and needs the extension written down as a rule rather than inferred from three cases.
   - **(c) Accept prose in the column.** Cheapest now, and it retires the grammar: the 42 conforming rows become a convention rather than a contract, and the instrument that enforced it is withdrawn rather than succeeded.
5. **Nothing is done to the volume or to `r2-23a` until M rules.** `r2-23a` stays red and is named at every close; it must **not** be re-banked, because its crash is a true report about the volume, and it must not be given a successor that tolerates prose, because that would silence the finding rather than answer it.
6. **The general point, recorded for the remaining 34 R3 classes.** A repair that rewrites a cell can break a column's grammar without touching a single number, and no arithmetic, pointer or extent check would catch it — only an instrument that parses the column. **Before any further class edits a table, the class file states which columns are controlled vocabularies and what the repair does to them.**
