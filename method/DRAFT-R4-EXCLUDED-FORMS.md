# DRAFT — the L/T title collision is not a fault, and reading it found one that is

**NOTHING IN THIS FILE IS IN THE STORE.** No volume byte has moved, no Register entry written.

## The collision, read

Family **L** prints `### Three excluded forms` (MC L1252); family **T** prints `### The three
excluded forms` (MC L1756). The standing note is that *a descriptive title alone does not identify
those two objects.*

**They are not duplicates and they do different work.**

| | family L, L1252 | family T, L1756 |
|---|---|---|
| what it is | **the argument** — why the tower requires a third excluded form, the three machines, the envelope-gap decomposition, and the measured consequences (join-closure and meet-failure at 2,862 / 12,489 / 40,887 / 110,229, the parity collapse at 1,848) | **the naming** — *"three excluded forms: REFLECTION (particle–hole conjugation), CONGRUENCE (fermion parity), TRIANGLE (one sum and one difference)"*, with the standard attributions |
| grade | Proved — M §12.11.2; Pauli 1925; Racah 1942; Wigner 1927; Dilworth 1940 | Computed — M §12.11.2; Racah 1943; Wigner 1931 |

**The same subject indexed in two families, each stating what its family needs, is what a compendium
is for** — the volume's own standard asks for intuitive taxonomy and fluid cross-referencing.

## And the ambiguity has no site

Every citation of the phrase across the six volumes points at **§12.11.2**, never at either object by
its descriptive title:

- main **L3368** is the section's own heading, `### 12.11.2 Three excluded forms, and the fold has a name`
- main **L5590** *"one of §12.11.2's three excluded forms"*; **L9932**, **L9944** *"§12.11.2 went
  further"*, *"§12.11.2 supplied the shape of the answer"*
- Register **6409** *"§12.11.2's three excluded forms term by term"*; Register **1763** is its own headline
- MC **L1264** *"made of the three excluded shapes (§12.11.2)"*

**Not one cross-reference names either object by title.** The collision is latent, not realised, and
both objects cite §12.11.2 themselves. **Nothing here needs repair.**

## But the family-L object's prior art carries two wrong numbers

Reading the two attributions side by side, the same three concepts are credited to different works —
and where they differ, **the corpus contradicts itself, with the majority right.**

**1 — Wigner, *Gruppentheorie*, dated 1927.** The Mathematical Compendium names that book three times:

| site | year |
|---|---|
| MC **L1702** | **1931** |
| MC **L1774** | **1931** |
| MC **L1260** | **1927** |

*Gruppentheorie und ihre Anwendung auf die Quantenmechanik der Atomspektren* is **1931**. Wigner
**1927** is a real and separately cited work — the bibliography carries both rows, and `1927 | Wigner`
serves `EM.image` and `EM.parity` — so the year is not spurious; **it is attached to the wrong title
at this one site.**

**2 — Racah, *Phys. Rev.* 61 (1942).** The volume number is **62**:

| site | volume |
|---|---|
| main References **L11658** | *Racah, G. (1942). **Phys. Rev. 62**, 438–462* |
| MC **L2669** | *Racah, **Phys. Rev. 62** (1942) 438* |
| MC **L1260** | ***Phys. Rev.* 61** (1942) |

**"Phys. Rev. 61" occurs nowhere else in the six volumes.** Racah's *Theory of complex spectra II*
is Phys. Rev. **62** (1942) 438–462.

**Both errors are in the same prior-art quote, MC L1260**, and each is contradicted by the corpus
elsewhere. Unlike the Barette case, here **the majority and the external record agree** — which is
why this one needs no outside source at all.
