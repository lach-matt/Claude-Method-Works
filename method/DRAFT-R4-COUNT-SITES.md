## The headline, and it is not a stale number

**1,635 was the Register's ENTRY count at BUILD90.** Ten of the sixteen sentences take that entry total and attach it
to a different noun — *corrections*, *withdrawals*, *claims that were wrong*, *errors*, *failures*, *times it caught
the author*. The Register's own kinds table, measured live by `kinds.py` over the seated volume, holds:

| kind | live count |
|---|---|
| a finding | 1,360 |
| a measurement | 502 |
| a correction | 186 |
| prior art | 61 |
| a withdrawal | 34 |
| a new protocol | 24 |
| an open question | 23 |
| a fault of mine | 16 |

*(The kinds overlap and are not a partition; each is a count of entries matching that pattern.)*

So the ten sentences overstate by between about 9× and about 102×, depending on the noun each one chose. **This was
already wrong before the count moved.** At BUILD90, when 1,635 was the correct entry total, it was equally untrue that
1,635 claims had been withdrawn. Pressing 1,677 into them would not repair the arithmetic; it would enlarge the gap
and give a false figure the authority of a fresh measurement. That is exactly the case your condition names, so none
of the ten is touched.

The book already knows the distinction. Register 1815's headline is **"THE THREE UNITS THE VOLUME COUNTS IN ARE NAMED
SO THEY ARE NOT MISTAKEN FOR ONE."** These ten sentences are that mistake, made in the main volume.

## The two that need a ruling before anything is pressed

**Site 6 — main L646, §2.12 Withdrawal: "Chapter 28. one thousand six hundred and thirty-five entries."** The first
reader called this RESOLVES; the check refuted it, and I agree with the check. Within this one member the phrase
"Chapter 28 … entries" denotes two different objects: at L7376 it is the Register volume (1,677 entries), while at

Under that reading, substituting 1,677 prints a false claim. **Ruling wanted:** which object §2.12 points at.

**Site 16 — Register L6703, inside entry 1815 itself.** The entry records what it did *not* touch: *"fifteen
main-volume sentences that write the size in words as narrative — one thousand six hundred and thirty-five — at no
named state … left to M."* The number here is **mentioned, not used**: it is the quoted string that identifies the
sites. A press table cannot see the difference, and a bare-string press row would hit this site along with the fifteen.
Substituting would make entry 1815 report its own deferral as discharged, and would be false of the ten sites this
pass declines. The supporting evidence is strong: 1815's own digit-form figures are themselves dated and left standing
under registers 1816 and 1819 ("the figures are left standing at every site, as the book keeps what it corrects").
**Ruling wanted:** mention, and exempt from the press table — my recommendation — or use.

## Two findings recorded, not repaired

1. **Chapter 28 says both that the set is here and that it is not.** L7372 opens "They are listed here because a book
   asserting 1,442 verified cells and no failures is unfalsifiable without them"; L7377, five lines later and in bold,
   says **"The entries themselves are not printed here"** and routes the reader to the Register volume. Whether
   *listed* and *printed* can be reconciled is a reading, and it is yours; the bolded line is the operative one, being
   dated to this build and carrying the live 1,677. Sites 3, 4 and 11 rest their locative claim on the first sentence.

   sum to exactly **273** (5 + 6 + 6 + 8 + 12 + 75 + 40 + 2 + 119, with §28.3 adding an uncounted set), which is the

   493 are accounted for. **§28.9.1 sizes the same register at 319 entries** (L7785, and again at L7808: "Of 319
   entries, 210 are…"), and the chapter's opening line claims **1,635**. The 319 and the 1,635 are not reconciled to
   anything.

Both belong to Phase 3's prose leg and are recorded here so they are not rediscovered.
